## What this area is about

Circuits tell you what voltages and currents do; signals and systems tell you what they *mean* and how they behave over time. A signal is a quantity that carries information; a system transforms signals. This area answers three questions that every computer must settle at its boundary with physics. Why represent information digitally at all, given that the world is analog? How do continuous real-world signals become finite lists of numbers without losing what matters? And how do loops that feed outputs back into inputs create stability, memory and oscillation?

The unifying idea is **controlling information against noise and time**. The digital abstraction defeats noise by discretising values and restoring them at every stage. Sampling and quantisation discretise time and value at the system's edges, with exact rules for when this loses nothing. Feedback uses measurement to correct errors (negative feedback) or to force a clean decision and hold it (positive feedback). Together they turn physical signals into reliable symbols.

## How the children fit together

| Child | Its role in the whole | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| [Analog vs Digital](analog-vs-digital.md) | The founding bargain: discrete levels, the static discipline and restoration make errors non-cumulative | Noise margins, Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor (CMOS) gain |
| [Sampling and Quantization](sampling-and-quantization.md) | Crossing the boundary: Nyquist-Shannon sampling, aliasing, quantisation noise, converters | Analog vs digital, [Fourier analysis](../../01-mathematical-foundations/05-continuous-mathematics/fourier-analysis.md) |
| [Feedback and Control](feedback-and-control.md) | Loops: negative feedback for precision, positive feedback for bistability and oscillation, stability and delay | Circuits, differential equations |

Start with analog versus digital: it explains *why* the rest of the stack is digital. Sampling and quantisation explain *how* information enters and leaves that digital world. Feedback underpins both: restoration relies on gain, every static memory cell is a positive-feedback loop, every clock is generated by an oscillator locked by a feedback loop, and sigma-delta converters use feedback to shape quantisation noise.

```
   physical world (continuous) ──sample & quantise──► digital symbols ──process──► reconstruct
                                         ▲                      │
                                         └──── feedback: measure output, adjust input ────┘
```
