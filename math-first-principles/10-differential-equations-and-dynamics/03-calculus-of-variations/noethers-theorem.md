## Essence

Emmy Noether's theorem (1918) says that each continuous symmetry of the action of a variational problem yields a quantity conserved along every solution. Invariance under translation in space gives conservation of momentum, under rotation conservation of angular momentum, and under translation in time conservation of energy. The theorem turned the conservation laws of physics from separate empirical facts into consequences of symmetry, and its proof takes two lines once the Euler–Lagrange equation is known. It is why modern physics is organised around symmetry groups.

## Core tenets

1. **The theorem and its two-line proof.** Let L(q, q̇, t) depend on coordinates q = (q₁, …, qₙ), and suppose the transformations qᵢ ↦ qᵢ + ε·ξᵢ(q) + O(ε²) leave L unchanged to first order in ε along every path. Then along every solution of the Euler–Lagrange equations

   ```
   Q = ∑ over i of (∂L/∂q̇ᵢ)·ξᵢ(q)   is constant in time.
   ```

   Proof: by the Euler–Lagrange equations, dQ/dt = ∑ (∂L/∂qᵢ·ξᵢ + ∂L/∂q̇ᵢ·ξ̇ᵢ), which is the first-order change in L under the transformation, zero by assumption. If L changes only by a total derivative dF/dt, then Q − F is conserved instead.

2. **Space translations give momentum; rotations give angular momentum.** For particles with L = ∑ ½mₖ|ẋₖ|² − V, where V depends only on differences xⱼ − xₖ, shifting every particle by the same vector leaves L unchanged, and Q is the total momentum ∑ mₖẋₖ. If V depends only on distances, rotations are symmetries too, and the charge is the angular momentum ∑ mₖ·xₖ × ẋₖ. For a planet around the Sun this is Kepler's second law: the radius sweeps out equal areas in equal times.

3. **Time translation gives energy.** If L has no explicit dependence on t, the quantity E = ∑ q̇ᵢ·∂L/∂q̇ᵢ − L is conserved; when L = T − V with T quadratic in the velocities, E = T + V. Remove the symmetry and the law fails: a pendulum whose pivot is shaken has a Lagrangian depending on t, and energy is pumped in, which is how a child on a swing builds up motion. Energy conservation is the statement that the laws do not change with time.

4. **Fields have conserved currents.** For a field theory, a symmetry gives a density ρ and flux J with ∂ₜρ + ∇·J = 0, so the total ∫ρ over space is constant and can change in a region only by flowing through its boundary. The Lagrangian of the Schrödinger equation is unchanged by the phase rotation ψ ↦ e^(iθ)·ψ, and the conserved quantity is the total probability ∫|ψ|²; the same phase symmetry in electrodynamics gives conservation of electric charge.

5. **In Hamiltonian form, conserved quantities generate symmetries.** With the Poisson bracket, every function Q evolves by dQ/dt = {Q, H}, so Q is conserved if and only if {Q, H} = 0, and then the flow generated by Q preserves H. The correspondence runs both ways. The Kepler problem has an extra conserved vector, the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector, which keeps bound orbits closed ellipses; its hidden symmetry is the rotation group of four-dimensional space, which Pauli (1926) used to derive the energy levels of hydrogen.

6. **Only continuous symmetries of variational problems count.** A reflection x ↦ −x yields no conserved quantity in classical mechanics, and friction, which no Lagrangian of this kind describes, breaks the link entirely. Noether's second theorem treats symmetries depending on arbitrary functions of position and time, such as the coordinate freedom of general relativity: these give identities among the equations rather than new conserved quantities. Hilbert and Klein had invited Noether to Göttingen in 1915 partly to clarify energy conservation in general relativity, and the 1918 paper answered them.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Symmetry of the action | A transformation of paths leaving the Lagrangian unchanged, possibly up to a total time derivative |
| Infinitesimal generator | The first-order part ξ of a one-parameter family of transformations |
| Generalised momentum | pᵢ = ∂L/∂q̇ᵢ |
| Noether charge | Q = ∑ pᵢ·ξᵢ, the quantity a symmetry conserves |
| Cyclic coordinate | A coordinate absent from L, whose momentum is therefore conserved |
| Conserved current | A pair (ρ, J) with ∂ₜρ + ∇·J = 0 |
| Poisson bracket | {F, G} = ∑ (∂F/∂qᵢ·∂G/∂pᵢ − ∂F/∂pᵢ·∂G/∂qᵢ) |

## How it fits

- **Below:** the [Euler–Lagrange equation](the-euler-lagrange-equation.md) supplies the equations along which Q is conserved; symmetries are one-parameter subgroups of [Lie groups](../../08-geometry/04-lie-theory/lie-groups-and-lie-algebras.md) acting on configuration space, a case of [group actions](../../05-abstract-algebra/01-groups/group-actions.md).
- **Above:** in [quantum mechanics](../../14-applied-and-computational-mathematics/04-mathematical-modelling/the-mathematics-of-quantum-mechanics.md) a symmetry is an operator commuting with the Hamiltonian, and momentum is the generator of translations; in [general relativity](../../14-applied-and-computational-mathematics/04-mathematical-modelling/the-geometry-of-general-relativity.md) energy is conserved locally, and globally only when spacetime has a time symmetry.
- **Siblings:** the Beltrami identity of the [Euler–Lagrange equation](the-euler-lagrange-equation.md) is the energy case; enough independent conserved quantities make a [Hamiltonian system integrable](../04-dynamical-systems/hamiltonian-systems-and-integrability.md); [existence of minimisers](existence-of-minimisers.md) asks a different question, whether a solution exists at all.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Conservation of energy is an independent law of nature."**: it is the symmetry of the laws under time shifts; where the laws change in time, energy is not conserved.
- **"Every symmetry gives a conservation law."**: only continuous symmetries of variational problems do; reflections and dissipative systems give nothing by this route.
- **"The theorem belongs to physics alone."**: it holds for any variational problem; on a surface of revolution, rotational symmetry gives Clairaut's relation, a conserved quantity along every geodesic.
