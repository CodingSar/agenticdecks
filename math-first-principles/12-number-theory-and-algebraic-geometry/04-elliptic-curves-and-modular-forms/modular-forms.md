## Essence

A modular form is a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane with an enormous amount of symmetry: it transforms in a prescribed way under the group SL₂(ℤ), or one of its subgroups, acting by Möbius transformations. The symmetry is so restrictive that modular forms of a given weight form a finite-dimensional space, so any two expressions that land in the same small space must agree, and identities about divisor sums, sums of squares and partition-like counts drop out by linear algebra. Their Fourier coefficients turn out to be arithmetic: for the right forms they count points on elliptic curves mod p. That link, the modularity theorem, proved Fermat's Last Theorem.

## Core tenets

1. **SL₂(ℤ) acts on the hyperbolic plane.** The group of integer matrices with determinant 1 acts on the upper half-plane ℍ = {z : Im z > 0} by z ↦ (az + b)/(cz + d). These maps are isometries of ℍ as a model of the [hyperbolic plane](../../08-geometry/01-classical-geometry/non-euclidean-geometry.md). The group is generated by T: z ↦ z + 1 and S: z ↦ −1/z, and every orbit meets the region ∣Re z∣ ≤ 1/2, ∣z∣ ≥ 1. Points of ℍ modulo the action correspond to lattices in ℂ up to scaling, so to elliptic curves over ℂ.

2. **A modular form of weight k transforms with a factor.** It is a holomorphic f on ℍ with f((az + b)/(cz + d)) = (cz + d)ᵏ·f(z) for every matrix in SL₂(ℤ), and bounded as Im z → ∞. Invariance under T gives a Fourier expansion f = ∑_(n≥0) aₙqⁿ in q = e^(2πiz). If a₀ = 0, f is a cusp form. Viewed as a function of lattices, a form of weight k is homogeneous of degree −k.

3. **Eisenstein series and Δ are the basic examples.** For even k ≥ 4, summing (mz + n)^(−k) over non-zero integer pairs gives a form of weight k; normalised, E₄ = 1 + 240∑σ₃(n)qⁿ and E₆ = 1 − 504∑σ₅(n)qⁿ, where σ_r(n) is the sum of the r-th powers of the divisors of n. The discriminant Δ = q∏(1 − qⁿ)²⁴ = q − 24q² + 252q³ − ⋯ is a cusp form of weight 12, equal to (E₄³ − E₆²)/1728. Its coefficients τ(n) are Ramanujan's tau function. The quotient j = E₄³/Δ = q⁻¹ + 744 + 196884q + ⋯ has weight 0 and classifies elliptic curves over ℂ.

4. **Finite dimensionality gives identities for free.** Counting zeros around the fundamental domain shows that the weight-k forms make up a space of dimension about k/12, and every form is a polynomial in E₄ and E₆. Weight 8 has dimension 1, so E₄² = E₈; comparing coefficients gives σ₇(n) = σ₃(n) + 120∑_(m<n) σ₃(m)·σ₃(n − m), a statement about divisors with no visible reason to hold.

5. **Hecke operators make coefficients multiplicative.** Hecke defined in the 1930s commuting operators T_n on each space of forms. A simultaneous eigenform, normalised with a₁ = 1, has multiplicative coefficients, and its L-function L(f, s) = ∑ aₙn^(−s) has an Euler product ∏_p (1 − a_p·p^(−s) + p^(k−1−2s))^(−1), with an analytic continuation and a functional equation obtained, as for [ζ](../02-analytic-number-theory/the-riemann-zeta-function.md), from a Mellin transform. Since Δ spans its space, τ is multiplicative, as Ramanujan conjectured and Mordell proved in 1917. Ramanujan's bound ∣τ(p)∣ ≤ 2p^(11/2) was proved by Deligne in 1974 as a consequence of the Weil conjectures.

6. **Levels allow congruence subgroups.** Replacing SL₂(ℤ) by Γ₀(N), the matrices with c ≡ 0 mod N, gives forms of level N. Theta series are examples: θ(z)⁴ = (∑_(n∈ℤ) q^(n²))⁴ has weight 2 and level 4, and computing its space yields Jacobi's formula that n is a sum of four squares in 8 times the sum of its divisors not divisible by 4 ways. Modular forms also settled sphere packing: Viazovska proved in 2016 that the E₈ lattice gives the densest packing in dimension 8, and with collaborators that the Leech lattice does in dimension 24 ([lattices and sphere packing](../../08-geometry/03-convex-and-discrete-geometry/lattices-and-sphere-packing.md)).

7. **Weight 2 forms of level N are differentials on modular curves.** The quotient of ℍ by Γ₀(N), compactified, is the modular curve X₀(N), whose points classify elliptic curves with a cyclic subgroup of order N. A weight-2 cusp form f gives the holomorphic differential f(z)dz on it. When f is a Hecke eigenform with rational coefficients, the Eichler–Shimura construction produces an elliptic curve E with a_p(E) = a_p(f) for all good p.

8. **Modularity: every elliptic curve over ℚ comes from a form.** The modularity theorem says the converse holds: for every elliptic curve E over ℚ of conductor N, there is a weight-2 eigenform of level N with the same a_p. For example q∏(1 − qⁿ)²(1 − q¹¹ⁿ)² = q − 2q² − q³ + 2q⁴ + q⁵ + ⋯ has level 11, and its p-th coefficient is p + 1 − #E(𝔽_p) for E: y² + y = x³ − x², for every prime p ≠ 11. Wiles, with Taylor, proved this for semistable curves, which gave [Fermat's Last Theorem](fermats-last-theorem.md); Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor completed it in 2001.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Upper half-plane ℍ | Im z > 0, with SL₂(ℤ) acting by Möbius maps |
| Weight k | The exponent in the factor (cz + d)ᵏ |
| q-expansion | f = ∑ aₙqⁿ with q = e^(2πiz) |
| Cusp form | A form with a₀ = 0 |
| Δ and τ(n) | The weight-12 cusp form q∏(1 − qⁿ)²⁴ and its coefficients |
| Hecke operator T_n | Commuting operators whose eigenforms have multiplicative coefficients |
| Level N | Symmetry under Γ₀(N) instead of all of SL₂(ℤ) |
| Modular curve X₀(N) | Compactified ℍ/Γ₀(N), a moduli space of elliptic curves |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Modular forms are invariant functions"**: only weight 0 ones are, and the only holomorphic ones are constants; forms of weight k pick up the factor (cz + d)ᵏ.
- **"Modular means arithmetic mod n"**: the name refers to moduli, the parameters of elliptic curves, though levels do bring in congruences.
