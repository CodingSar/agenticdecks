# Mathematics from First Principles

A layered knowledge base of Mathematics. It starts with logic and sets and climbs, one dependency at a time, through the number systems, combinatorics, linear and abstract algebra, analysis, topology and geometry, advanced analysis, differential equations and probability, to the syntheses of number theory and algebraic geometry, the study of mathematics by mathematics, its applications, and mathematics as a human activity.

- **Leaves** (`*.md` files) hold the few load-bearing tenets you need to understand the essence of one topic: what is true, why it is true (the idea of the proof), and what breaks without each hypothesis.
- **Parents** (`README.md` in each folder) explain how their children fit together and how they connect to sibling areas.
- **[CROSS-CUTTING.md](CROSS-CUTTING.md)** follows ideas that recur in every layer (invariants, symmetry, duality, linearisation, local-to-global, impossibility and obstruction…) and links each to where it appears.
- **[_meta/PLAN.md](_meta/PLAN.md)** explains why the tree has this shape, which alternative structures were considered, and what was rejected.

The sibling projects **[Computer Science from First Principles](../cs-first-principles/README.md)** and **[Medicine from First Principles](../medicine-first-principles/README.md)** use the same method and templates.

## The stack

Read from the bottom up. Each layer depends only on layers with lower numbers.

```
 15  Mathematics in Context .............. history, philosophy, practice, computers and proof, open problems
 14  Applied & Computational ............. numerics, optimisation, codes, models, games, data and learning
 13  Structure & Metamathematics ......... categories; what computation, proof and axioms can and cannot do
 12  Number Theory & Algebraic Geometry .. where the branches fuse: primes, curves, zeta functions
 11  Probability & Statistics ............ chance made rigorous: limit theorems, processes, inference
 10  Differential Equations & Dynamics ... laws of change: existence, stability, diffusion, waves, chaos
 09  Advanced Analysis ................... measure, complex, functional and harmonic analysis
 08  Geometry ............................ what is preserved: Euclid to curvature, convexity, Lie groups
 07  Topology ............................ nearness without distance: continuity, holes, manifolds
 06  Real Analysis ....................... the limit: convergence, derivative, integral, metric spaces  <- limits enter
 05  Abstract Algebra .................... groups, rings, fields, Galois theory, representations
 04  Linear Algebra ...................... vector spaces and linear maps: one structure understood completely
 03  Combinatorics & Graph Theory ........ counting, graphs, discrete probability, forced structure
 02  Number Systems ...................... ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ, ℂ built one step at a time; arithmetic  <- roots meet
 01  Sets & Foundations .................. the universe of objects: sets, functions, infinities, axioms
 00  Logic & Proof ....................... the rules of inference: logic, proof, the axiomatic method
```

Mathematics has **two roots**. The *deductive* one (00) says what follows from what: the rules of inference and what counts as a proof. The *ontological* one (01) says what there is to reason about: sets, functions, relations, and the axioms that keep them from contradiction. Each leans on the other: set theory is a theory written in logic, and logic's notion of a model is a set. They meet in **02 Number Systems**, where induction and set-theoretic constructions build ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ and ℂ. A second boundary lies at **06 Real Analysis**: everything below it is finite or algebraic; from 06 upward, limits and completeness do the work.

## How to read this

- **Bottom-up (the intended path).** Start at 00 and read each layer's README, then the leaves that interest you. You will never meet an idea that depends on something you have not seen.
- **Top-down (by need).** Start at the theorem or field you care about. Each leaf's header lists what it **builds on**. Follow those links downward until things feel solid.
- **Across (by principle).** Pick an idea in [CROSS-CUTTING.md](CROSS-CUTTING.md), for example invariants, and follow it from parity arguments through the determinant, the Euler characteristic and homology to the class group.
