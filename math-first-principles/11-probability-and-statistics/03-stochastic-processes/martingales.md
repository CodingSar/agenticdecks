## Essence

A martingale is the mathematical model of a fair game: a process whose expected next value, given everything known so far, is its present value. The definition uses only conditional expectation, yet it captures a remarkable range of processes: sums of independent mean-zero steps, successive predictions of a fixed quantity as information accumulates, likelihood ratios, and the discounted prices of a market without arbitrage. Two theorems, both due to Joseph Doob, carry the theory. Optional stopping says that no stopping rule turns a fair game into a favourable one, under conditions that matter. Martingale convergence says that a martingale bounded in L¹ converges almost surely.

## Core tenets

1. **A martingale has the present as its best forecast.** Given a filtration ℱ₀ ⊆ ℱ₁ ⊆ … (the information available at each time), an adapted integrable process M is a martingale when E[Mₙ₊₁ ∣ ℱₙ] = Mₙ for every n; a submartingale or supermartingale has ≥ or ≤ in place of =. Three examples cover most uses:

   ```
   random walk:     Mₙ = ξ₁ + … + ξₙ, independent steps with E[ξᵢ] = 0
   Doob martingale: Mₙ = E[Y ∣ ℱₙ] for a fixed integrable Y
   product:         Mₙ = Z₁ × … × Zₙ, independent Zᵢ ≥ 0 with E[Zᵢ] = 1
   ```

2. **No betting system beats a fair game.** A strategy that stakes Hₙ on round n, decided using only ℱₙ₋₁ (a predictable process), has winnings (H·M)ₙ = ∑ₖ Hₖ·(Mₖ − Mₖ₋₁). If the Hₖ are bounded, this is again a martingale, so its expectation stays 0. The martingale transform is the discrete ancestor of the Itô integral of [stochastic calculus](stochastic-calculus.md).

3. **Optional stopping: a bounded or well-controlled stopping time keeps the game fair.** A stopping time T is a random time for which {T = n} ∈ ℱₙ: the decision to stop uses no knowledge of the future. If T is bounded, or if E[T] < ∞ and the increments are bounded, then E[M_T] = E[M₀]. For a simple symmetric walk from 0 stopped on hitting −a or b, this gives at once

   ```
   P(hit b before −a) = a/(a + b),      and, using the martingale Mₙ² − n,   E[T] = a·b.
   ```

4. **Without those conditions optional stopping fails, and the failure is instructive.** Stop the symmetric walk the first time it reaches 1. The walk is recurrent, so T is finite almost surely, and M_T = 1, so E[M_T] = 1 ≠ 0. The catch is that E[T] = ∞ and the walk can go arbitrarily far below 0 first. The doubling strategy in roulette has the same structure: it wins one unit almost surely, but only with unbounded credit and unbounded time.

5. **Martingales bounded in L¹ converge almost surely.** Doob's upcrossing inequality bounds the expected number of times a submartingale crosses upwards from below a to above b by E[(Mₙ − a)⁺]/(b − a). If supₙ E∣Mₙ∣ < ∞, every interval with rational endpoints is crossed finitely often, so the path cannot oscillate and converges to a finite limit. The proof is a betting argument: buy below a, sell above b, and use tenet 2.

6. **Almost sure convergence is not convergence in mean, and uniform integrability is the difference.** Double or nothing, Mₙ = product of n independent factors equal to 0 or 2 with probability 1/2 each, is a martingale with E[Mₙ] = 1, yet Mₙ → 0 almost surely, since some factor is eventually 0. The convergence holds in L¹, with E[M_∞] = E[M₀], exactly when the family is uniformly integrable. In L², martingale increments are orthogonal, so E[Mₙ²] = E[M₀²] + ∑ E[(Mₖ − Mₖ₋₁)²], and boundedness in L² gives convergence in L².

7. **Information accumulates to its limit.** For integrable Y, the Doob martingale E[Y ∣ ℱₙ] converges almost surely and in L¹ to E[Y ∣ ℱ_∞], where ℱ_∞ is generated by all the ℱₙ (Lévy's upward theorem). Applied to the indicator of a tail event, independent of every ℱₙ, it gives Kolmogorov's 0–1 law again. In Pólya's urn, which starts with one red and one blue ball and adds a ball of the drawn colour after each draw, the proportion of red is a bounded martingale; it converges, and its limit is uniform on [0, 1]. Doob's maximal inequality, P(max over k ≤ n of ∣Mₖ∣ ≥ λ) ≤ E∣Mₙ∣/λ, controls the whole path by its endpoint.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Filtration | An increasing sequence of σ-algebras ℱₙ: information at time n |
| Martingale | Adapted, integrable, with E[Mₙ₊₁ ∣ ℱₙ] = Mₙ |
| Predictable process | Hₙ is known at time n − 1 |
| Stopping time | A random time T with {T = n} ∈ ℱₙ for every n |
| Optional stopping theorem | E[M_T] = E[M₀] under boundedness or integrability conditions |
| Upcrossing | A passage of the path from below a to above b |
| Uniform integrability | supₙ E[∣Mₙ∣·1{∣Mₙ∣ > K}] → 0 as K → ∞ |
| Doob martingale | E[Y ∣ ℱₙ], the successive best predictions of Y |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A martingale is a strategy for winning"**: in gambling the word once named the doubling strategy, which tenet 4 shows wins only with unbounded credit. In mathematics a martingale is a fair game.
- **"Converging martingales keep their mean"**: double or nothing has mean 1 for ever and limit 0. Uniform integrability is needed.
- **"A martingale has independent increments"**: its increments are uncorrelated with the past but can depend on it strongly, as in Pólya's urn.
