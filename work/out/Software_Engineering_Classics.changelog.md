# Software Engineering Classics — changelog

**Scope.** 218 new notes (guid null) on the enduring ideas of the canonical software engineering books and papers, organised by idea rather than by book; the `source` field names the originating work (e.g. `Ousterhout · Philosophy of Software Design`, `Parnas 1972`). No author/year/edition trivia: every card asks about the idea. 31 contrast cards (`classics::contrast`), 42 clozes for lists, 58 analogies (27%), 6 hand-drawn SVGs (`media/sec_deep_shallow.svg`, `sec_kwic.svg`, `sec_three_ways.svg`, `sec_pipeline.svg`, `sec_stocks_flows.svg`, `sec_dependency_rings.svg`).

**Subdecks.** 01 Complexity & Its Sources (15) · 02 Modules, Abstraction & Information Hiding (18) · 03 Naming, Comments & Readability (14) · 04 Code Evolution: Refactoring, Tidying & Legacy (26) · 05 Design Patterns & Their Critics (18) · 06 Unix Philosophy & Composition (15) · 07 Correctness, Errors & Robustness in Production (19) · 08 Flow & Delivery: Brooks to Accelerate (23) · 09 Programming Craft & Problem Solving (22) · 10 Data, Models & Reality (15) · 11 People, Teams & Craft (18) · 12 Classic Papers in One Line (15).

**Works drawn on.** Ousterhout *A Philosophy of Software Design*; Brooks *Mythical Man-Month* and *No Silver Bullet*; McConnell *Code Complete*; Martin *Clean Code* and *Clean Architecture* (each paired with its standard criticisms, incl. the Ousterhout–Martin prime-generator exchange); Fowler *Refactoring* and *PoEAA*; GoF *Design Patterns* plus Norvig's critique; Feathers *Working Effectively with Legacy Code*; SICP; Bentley *Programming Pearls*; Raymond *Art of Unix Programming* and *Cathedral and the Bazaar* (McIlroy, Pike); DeMarco & Lister *Peopleware*; Forsgren/Humble/Kim *Accelerate*; Kim et al. *Phoenix Project* and Goldratt *The Goal*; Nygard *Release It!*; Humble & Farley *Continuous Delivery*; Freeman & Pryce *GOOS*; Beck *Tidy First?* and *XP Explained*; Kent *Data and Reality*; Norman *Design of Everyday Things* (applied to API design); Winters et al. *SWE at Google*; Meadows *Thinking in Systems*; Graham *Hackers & Painters*; Petzold *Code*; Kernighan & Plauger *Elements of Programming Style*; Moseley & Marks *Out of the Tar Pit*; Hickey *Simple Made Easy*; papers by Parnas (1972, 1979), Dijkstra (1968, 1972), Conway, Lampson, Saltzer/Reed/Clark, Hamming, Naur, Waldo et al., Royce.

**Deliberately left out.** K&R, CLRS, TAOCP, GEB (little transferable engineering content); Nygard's capacity antipatterns and networking chapters; GoF pattern-by-pattern catalogue beyond ~8 patterns-as-ideas; Clean Architecture's component metrics (I, A, D); Peopleware's office-furniture and meeting chapters; Accelerate's 24-capability list and survey method; SICP's register machines/compiler; Google's build/LSC tooling details.

**Overlap avoided (checked with grep over work/out/*.json).**
- *Pragmatic Programmer* deck: no DRY, orthogonality, tracer bullets, broken windows, Law of Demeter or tell-don't-ask cards; DRY appears only in the "DRY vs the wrong abstraction" contrast.
- *Software Architecture & DDD*: no Repository, bounded-context mechanics, hexagonal or layered-architecture cards; Kent's epistemology is given as the root of bounded contexts and points to that deck.
- *Software Testing*: no TDD cycle, test-double taxonomy or pyramid; characterization tests appear only as Feathers' write-it-wrong-then-paste procedure; Beyoncé Rule omitted.
- *Team Topologies*: no inverse Conway or team cognitive-load cards; Conway is covered as the 1968 claim, mechanism and compiler example.
- *Distributed Systems & Data*: no circuit breaker, bulkhead or consensus mechanics; Nygard is kept at the idea level (integration points, slow responses, steady state, governor); Waldo et al. is one summary cloze.
- *Open Agile Architecture*: no Scrum/Kanban, Three Ways cloze, YAGNI or walking-skeleton definitions; those ideas are asked from a different angle (which Way owns post-mortems; why YAGNI needs refactoring; why build the skeleton first).
- *Architect Craft*: Brooks's Law statement, Westrum typology, Meadows stocks/flows definition, leverage points and "optimise the non-constraint" already exist; here they appear as Brooks-vs-Amdahl, Westrum's messenger test, WIP-as-stock, Herbie and Drum–Buffer–Rope.
- *Adoption & Epistemics*: "worse is better" and Chesterton's fence not repeated.
