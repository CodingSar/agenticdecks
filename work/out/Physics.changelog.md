## Deliberately left out
- Pure history/biography (dates, who-published-what); kept only where the name is the vocabulary (Newton, Kepler, Maxwell, Bell).
- Derivations and problem-solving drills; kinematics formula sheets; vector calculus forms of Maxwell (given in words instead).
- Engineering thermodynamics: heat transfer modes, Carnot/COP numbers, exergy, heat capacity of air vs water, cooling, chip power, PUE — all already in Physics for Datacenters.
- Exotic topics with no conceptual payoff at this level: negative temperature, renormalisation details, specific particle zoo beyond the Standard Model anchor.
- Numbers beyond a handful of anchors (c, g, 1 atm, Avogadro, Å/fm, LEO speeds, γ at a few speeds, GPS drift, cosmic budget).

## Overlap with existing decks avoided
- Physics for Datacenters: no cards on energy/power definitions, first/second law statements, heat vs temperature, Carnot, COP, entropy = log Ω, Boltzmann constant/kT, Boltzmann distribution, equipartition, Shannon vs thermodynamic entropy, Landauer, speed-of-light latency. This deck's thermo subdeck takes other angles: kinetic theory, ideal gas, adiabatic compression, why engines need a cold sink (conceptual), arrow of time, free energy, third law, latent heat, Brownian motion, diffusion √t, Maxwell–Boltzmann speed tail, blackbody, ultraviolet catastrophe, reversible vs irreversible, Maxwell's demon (which mentions Landauer's bound as the resolution; the Landauer number itself stays in the datacenter deck).
- Noether: the datacenter deck has "why is energy conserved" (time symmetry); here one cloze covers the general theorem with the momentum/angular-momentum cases (similar.py 0.44, kept as a different angle).
- Quantitative Foundations: no overlap found (similar.py threshold 0.35).
- Square–cube law appears in both decks but framed differently (heat removal there, general scaling argument here).
