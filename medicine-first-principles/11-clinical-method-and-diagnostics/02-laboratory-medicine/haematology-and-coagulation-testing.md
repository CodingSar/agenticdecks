## Essence

Blood is the one tissue that can be sampled from every patient in a minute, and the full blood count is the most frequently performed test in medicine. Haematology testing counts and sizes the cells of the blood, examines their shape, and measures how fast the blood clots under defined conditions. Each number is a readout of a production line (the marrow), a consumption or loss process (bleeding, haemolysis, sequestration), or a cascade (coagulation), so an abnormal count is a question about which side of the balance has moved. The organising idea is that the count tells you *that* something is wrong and the film, the indices and the specialised tests tell you *where in the pathway*.

## Core tenets

1. **A cell count is production minus destruction and loss, and the indices say which.** Anaemia with a high reticulocyte count means the marrow is responding to loss or haemolysis; with a low count, the marrow is failing or lacks a substrate. Red cell size (mean cell volume) sorts the causes: small cells from iron deficiency or thalassaemia (haemoglobin synthesis failing), large cells from vitamin B12 or folate deficiency (Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) synthesis failing, so cells divide late and large), normal-sized cells from acute loss, renal disease or marrow suppression. The mechanism is in [red cell physiology](../../06-physiology/08-blood/red-cells-haemoglobin-and-iron.md); the count reads it out.

2. **Automated analysers count by impedance and light scatter, and they flag what they cannot classify.** Cells passing one at a time through an aperture change its electrical impedance in proportion to their volume; laser scatter and fluorescence sort white cells into neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and basophils. The analyser is fast and precise but blind to morphology: it cannot see a blast, a fragment, a parasite or a clump of platelets. Flags prompt a human to look at the blood film, which remains the test of record for cell shape.

3. **The blood film shows mechanism directly.** Fragmented red cells mean mechanical shearing in small vessels (microangiopathy); spherocytes mean membrane loss from immune attack or an inherited defect; target cells, sickle cells and the pale centres of iron deficiency are each a visible consequence of a molecular fault. Hypersegmented neutrophils accompany delayed DNA synthesis; blasts mean leukaemia until proven otherwise; a left shift with toxic granulation means the marrow is pouring out young neutrophils in response to infection, which is why the white count and its differential are central to [sepsis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md).

4. **The clotting screen tests the cascade in a test tube, not haemostasis in a patient.** Prothrombin time adds tissue factor and measures the extrinsic and common pathways (factors VII, X, V, II, fibrinogen); activated partial thromboplastin time activates the intrinsic pathway on a surface (factors XII, XI, IX, VIII, then common). A prolonged time means a factor is low or inhibited; a mixing study with normal plasma distinguishes deficiency (corrects) from inhibitor (does not). But the tubes omit platelets, endothelium and flow, so a normal screen does not exclude a bleeding tendency, and a prolonged time (lupus anticoagulant) can accompany thrombosis.

5. **Each test maps to a stage of haemostasis.** Platelet count and function tests address primary haemostasis (the plug); prothrombin and activated partial thromboplastin times address secondary haemostasis (fibrin); fibrinogen and D-dimer address the clot and its breakdown. Disseminated intravascular coagulation shows all stages consumed at once: low platelets, prolonged times, low fibrinogen, high D-dimer. D-dimer's role in excluding venous thrombosis is a pure [likelihood-ratio](../01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md) fact: sensitive and non-specific, so a negative result rules out in a low-probability patient and a positive result means little.

6. **Marrow, flow cytometry and genetics define the malignancies.** When counts or film suggest a clonal disorder, a marrow aspirate and trephine show cellularity and architecture; flow cytometry identifies the lineage and maturation stage of the abnormal cells by their surface markers; cytogenetics and molecular tests (a translocation, a mutation) define the entity and its prognosis, and increasingly its treatment, as with the Philadelphia chromosome and tyrosine-kinase inhibition. The classification of [haematological malignancies](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/haematological-malignancies.md) is now written in these results, and the laboratory, not the clinic, makes the diagnosis.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Full blood count | Haemoglobin, red cell indices, white cell count with differential, platelet count |
| Mean cell volume | Average red cell size; sorts anaemia into microcytic, normocytic and macrocytic |
| Reticulocyte count | Young red cells in circulation; a measure of marrow output |
| Blood film | Microscopic examination of stained blood for cell morphology |
| Prothrombin time / INR | Clotting time via tissue factor; standardised as the International Normalised Ratio for warfarin |
| Activated partial thromboplastin time | Clotting time via surface activation; intrinsic and common pathways; heparin monitoring |
| Mixing study | Adding normal plasma to distinguish factor deficiency from an inhibitor |
| D-dimer | Fibrin degradation product; sensitive, non-specific marker of clot formation and lysis |
| Flow cytometry | Identifying cell lineage and maturation by fluorescent surface markers |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A normal clotting screen means the patient will not bleed."**: the screen omits platelets, von Willebrand factor, vessel wall and flow; the history of bleeding is a better predictor than the tubes.
- **"Haemoglobin measures blood volume."**: it measures concentration; immediately after a haemorrhage it is normal, and it falls only as plasma is replaced over hours.
- **"A raised white count means infection."**: it rises with stress, steroids, inflammation and malignancy; the differential and the film say which.
