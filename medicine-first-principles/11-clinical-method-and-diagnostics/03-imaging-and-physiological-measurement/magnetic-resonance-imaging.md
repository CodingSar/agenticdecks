## Essence

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) makes pictures from the hydrogen nuclei in the body's water and fat. A proton has spin and so a magnetic moment; in a strong magnetic field the protons align and precess at a frequency proportional to the field strength (about 64 MHz at 1.5 tesla). A radiofrequency pulse at that frequency tips them away from the field; as they relax back they emit a signal whose strength and timing depend on the molecular environment of the water they sit in. Gradients in the field make the precession frequency depend on position, which encodes space. The idea that makes MRI the most versatile imaging method is that contrast is not fixed by one physical property, as attenuation fixes CT, but can be chosen: by changing the timing of pulses the same tissue can be made bright or dark according to how fast its protons relax, how freely its water diffuses, or how its blood flows.

## Core tenets

1. **The signal is from protons, and the field strength sets the frequency.** Hydrogen is imaged because it is abundant (water is most of the body) and its nucleus has a magnetic moment ([atoms and nuclei](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/atoms-bonds-and-molecular-shape.md)). In the magnet, slightly more spins align with the field than against it, giving a net magnetisation that precesses at the Larmor frequency, 42.6 MHz per tesla. A radiofrequency pulse at exactly that frequency is absorbed (resonance), tipping the magnetisation into the transverse plane where it induces a signal in a receiver coil. No ionising radiation is involved; the energies are those of radio waves ([electromagnetic spectrum](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md)).

2. **Two relaxation times, T1 and T2, describe how the signal recovers and decays, and they differ between tissues.** T1 is the time for longitudinal magnetisation to recover along the field, governed by how efficiently protons hand energy to their surroundings: fast in fat (short T1, bright on T1-weighted images), slow in free water (long T1, dark). T2 is the time for the transverse signal to decay as spins lose phase coherence, governed by local field variation: long in free water (bright on T2-weighted images), short in solid tissue. Fluid is therefore dark on T1 and bright on T2, fat bright on both, and most pathology, which adds water (oedema, inflammation, tumour, infarct), is bright on T2. Choosing the pulse timing (repetition and echo times) chooses which difference dominates.

3. **Diffusion weighting images the random motion of water, and it detects ischaemia within minutes.** Applying paired gradients makes the signal fall where water moves freely and persist where it is restricted. In acute ischaemia, failing ion pumps let water into cells and restrict its motion, so an infarct is bright on diffusion-weighted imaging within minutes to an hour of onset, long before CT shows anything; this is the basis of modern [stroke](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/stroke.md) imaging. Dense tumours and abscesses also restrict diffusion, which separates them from fluid.

4. **Gadolinium contrast shortens T1 where blood goes and where the blood–brain barrier leaks.** Gadolinium is paramagnetic; chelated and injected, it shortens the T1 of nearby water, brightening perfused tissue on T1-weighted images. In the brain it enters only where the [blood–brain barrier](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/README.md) is disrupted (tumour, active inflammation, abscess), making enhancement a marker of barrier breakdown. Active demyelinating plaques enhance and old ones do not, which is how [multiple sclerosis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/demyelinating-and-neuromuscular-disease.md) activity is followed.

5. **Soft-tissue contrast makes MRI the method for brain, spine, joints and pelvis; its physics makes it poor for lung and bone cortex, and slow for the acutely ill.** Grey and white matter, cartilage and ligament, muscle and tendon, uterus and prostate, which are indistinguishable on CT, differ in relaxation times. Cortical bone and aerated lung contain few mobile protons and are black. An examination takes twenty to sixty minutes in a confined, noisy bore, which the restless, the claustrophobic and the unstable cannot tolerate, so CT remains the emergency test and MRI the definitive one.

6. **The hazards are the magnet and the contrast, not radiation.** The static field is always on: ferromagnetic objects become projectiles, and implanted devices (older pacemakers, some aneurysm clips, cochlear implants) can heat, move or malfunction, so screening for metal is a safety procedure with fatalities behind it. Switching gradients can stimulate nerves and are loud; radiofrequency deposition heats tissue within regulated limits. Gadolinium is retained in small amounts in brain and bone, of uncertain significance, and in severe renal failure older agents caused a fibrosing disease, so the agent and the kidneys are checked before injection.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nuclear spin and magnetic moment | The property of the hydrogen nucleus that lets it absorb and emit radiofrequency energy in a field |
| Larmor frequency | Precession frequency, proportional to field strength (42.6 MHz per tesla for protons) |
| T1 relaxation | Recovery of magnetisation along the field; short in fat, long in water |
| T2 relaxation | Loss of transverse signal from dephasing; long in water, short in solids |
| T1- and T2-weighted images | Pulse timings chosen so that T1 or T2 differences dominate the contrast |
| Gradient | A spatially varying field that encodes position as frequency and phase |
| Diffusion-weighted imaging | Sequence sensitive to the random motion of water; restricted in acute ischaemia |
| Gadolinium | Paramagnetic contrast agent that shortens T1 where it accumulates |
| Enhancement | Brightening after contrast, marking perfusion or barrier breakdown |

## How it fits

- **Below:** the signal is nuclear magnetism from the [hydrogen nucleus](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/atoms-bonds-and-molecular-shape.md) in [water](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/water-solutions-and-concentration.md), excited by [radio waves](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md); the regions it images best are the [head, neck and spine](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/head-neck-and-spine.md) and the [limbs](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/upper-and-lower-limbs.md); diffusion restriction is the cellular swelling of [cell injury](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/cell-injury-adaptation-and-death.md).
- **Above:** MRI defines [demyelinating disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/demyelinating-and-neuromuscular-disease.md), selects [stroke](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/stroke.md) patients for reperfusion, stages [tumours](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md) of the pelvis and brain, and shows the soft tissues of [joints and spine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md).
- **Siblings:** [CT](radiography-and-computed-tomography.md) is faster, cheaper, better for bone, lung and the unstable patient, and uses radiation; [ultrasound](ultrasound.md) is real-time and portable but blocked by air and bone; [nuclear medicine](nuclear-medicine-and-molecular-imaging.md) adds molecular function and is fused with both CT and MRI.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Bright on MRI means abnormal."**: brightness depends on the weighting; fat is bright on T1, fluid on T2, and a normal structure may be bright on one sequence and dark on another.
- **"MRI is a more detailed CT."**: its contrast comes from a different physical property (relaxation, not attenuation), so each sees what the other cannot; spatial resolution is similar, and CT is better for bone and lung.
- **"MRI is completely safe."**: it has no ionising radiation, but the magnet has killed through projectiles and device failures, and gadolinium is not inert.
