## Essence

Ultrasound is echolocation. A transducer sends a short pulse of sound at megahertz frequencies into the body, listens for echoes, and places each echo at a depth given by half the round-trip time multiplied by the speed of sound in tissue (about 1,540 m/s). Echoes arise wherever the acoustic impedance (density times sound speed) changes, so the image shows interfaces: between fluid and solid, between organ and fat, between tissue and gas. Because the pulse returns in microseconds, the image is live, and because the frequency of an echo from moving blood is shifted, the same instrument measures flow. It is the only imaging method that is real-time, portable, radiation-free and cheap enough to be a bedside instrument, and its limits are the physics of sound: it cannot cross air or bone, and resolution is bought with penetration.

## Core tenets

1. **Echoes come from impedance mismatch, so ultrasound sees boundaries, not substances.** The fraction of sound reflected at an interface depends on the difference in acoustic impedance across it ([waves and sound](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md)). Soft tissues differ slightly, giving the grey speckle of parenchyma; fluid has no internal interfaces and appears black (anechoic) with brighter tissue beyond; a stone or calcification reflects almost everything and casts an acoustic shadow. Fluid versus solid is the question ultrasound answers best: a cyst from a tumour, an effusion from consolidation, a full from an empty bladder.

2. **Air and bone are walls, because the impedance mismatch is nearly total.** At a tissue–air interface almost all sound is reflected, so the lung, bowel gas and anything behind them are invisible, and gel is needed to exclude air between probe and skin; bone absorbs and reflects, so the adult brain and the interior of bone cannot be imaged. This dictates the windows: the heart is imaged between ribs and from below the diaphragm, the liver through the intercostal spaces, the uterus through a full bladder or from within the vagina. Paradoxically, lung ultrasound works because pathology (fluid, consolidation) removes the air and creates a window.

3. **Frequency trades resolution against depth.** Resolution is of the order of the wavelength, so higher frequency (10–15 MHz) resolves finer detail; but attenuation rises with frequency, so high frequencies penetrate only a few centimetres. Superficial structures (thyroid, vessels, tendons, a child's hip) use high-frequency probes; the deep abdomen uses 2–5 MHz and accepts coarser detail. Image quality also falls with body fat, which attenuates and scatters, so the same examination is excellent in a thin patient and uninformative in an obese one.

4. **The Doppler shift turns the echo into a flow meter.** Sound reflected from moving red cells returns at a frequency shifted in proportion to their velocity along the beam. Colour Doppler paints direction and speed onto the image; spectral Doppler plots velocity against time at one point and, through the simplified Bernoulli relation (pressure drop ≈ 4 × velocity²), converts the jet velocity across a narrowed valve into a pressure gradient ([pressure and flow](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/fluids-pressure-and-flow.md)). This is how the severity of valve stenosis, the pressure in the pulmonary artery and the presence of a clot in a leg vein are measured without a catheter.

5. **Echocardiography is physiology watched in real time.** Because the frame rate is tens per second, ultrasound shows the heart contracting: chamber sizes, wall thickness, the fraction of blood ejected, regional wall motion after infarction, valve opening and leakage, and fluid around the heart ([the cardiac cycle](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/the-cardiac-cycle-and-pump-function.md)). The dynamic view, not the anatomy, is its distinctive contribution: an empty, hyperdynamic ventricle and a full, poorly contracting one are both [shock](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/shock-and-haemodynamic-support.md) and need opposite treatment, and a probe at the bedside distinguishes them in a minute.

6. **Safety and portability made ultrasound the imaging of pregnancy and the bedside.** No ionising radiation and no known harm at diagnostic intensities make it the method for dating, screening and monitoring [pregnancy](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md), and for children. Hand-held machines have made point-of-care ultrasound part of the examination in emergency medicine, critical care and anaesthesia: looking for free fluid after trauma, a collapsed lung, a distended bladder, or guiding a needle into a vessel or a nerve.


## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acoustic impedance | Density × speed of sound; echoes arise where it changes |
| Anechoic, hypoechoic, hyperechoic | Black (fluid), darker than surroundings, brighter than surroundings |
| Acoustic shadow | Absence of signal behind a strong reflector such as stone or bone |
| Acoustic window | A path free of air and bone through which a structure can be imaged |
| Frequency–depth trade-off | Higher frequency gives finer resolution but shallower penetration |
| Doppler shift | Frequency change in echoes from moving blood, proportional to velocity along the beam |
| Simplified Bernoulli equation | Pressure gradient ≈ 4 × (peak velocity)², used to grade valve stenosis |
| Ejection fraction | Proportion of ventricular volume ejected each beat, measured by echocardiography |
| Point-of-care ultrasound | Focused bedside examination by the treating clinician to answer a specific question |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Ultrasound shows everything in the region scanned."**: it shows the planes swept, through the windows available; bowel gas, bone and fat hide what lies behind them, and a negative scan is only as good as the search.
- **"Doppler measures blood flow."**: it measures velocity along the beam; flow needs the vessel's cross-section as well, and the angle of the beam to the vessel changes the reading.
- **"Ultrasound is harmless, so it needs no justification."**: it has no known harm at diagnostic intensities, but a scan that finds an incidental cyst or nodule in a low-risk patient still triggers the cascade of further testing.
