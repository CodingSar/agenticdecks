# Cross-Cutting Principles

The rest of this knowledge base is a tree: sixteen layers, each split into areas and leaves. A tree gives every topic one parent, which is what makes bottom-up reading possible. But medicine is a graph. A small set of ideas explains phenomena in layer after layer, under different names and at different scales. The sodium pump, the countercurrent loop of the kidney and the anti-Trendelenburg tilt of a bleeding patient's bed are one idea (spend energy to hold a gradient against flow) at three distances. The hidden nephron loss behind a normal creatinine, the normal blood pressure of a young adult who has lost a litre of blood, and the decade of silent dopaminergic loss before Parkinson's disease declares itself are one idea too: reserve hides damage until it runs out. Seeing the idea once saves learning it a dozen times, and lets a lesson from one layer ("the compensation becomes the disease") warn you in another.

Each section below states one principle as a claim, explains the force that makes it recur, and follows it up the stack, lowest layer first, with a link to a leaf for each appearance. Each ends with the failure mode: when the principle misleads, and which neighbouring principle pulls against it.

**How to use it.** Read across, not down. Pick a principle, open two leaves from distant layers in its table and compare them. Parent READMEs throughout the tree end with "Cross-cutting threads" that link back here. The last section, [Principles that pull against each other](#principles-that-pull-against-each-other), lists the tensions behind most hard clinical decisions.

## Structure determines function

**Claim: what a molecule, cell, organ or body can do is fixed by its shape, surface and arrangement, so reading the structure predicts the function and a change in structure predicts the failure.** The force behind it is physics: binding needs complementary surfaces, flow through a tube scales with the fourth power of its radius, a lever's advantage is its arm length, diffusion works only across thin walls. Biology has no other material to work with, so every function is a geometry, and most of anatomy, histology, imaging and surgery is the reading or the alteration of that geometry.

| 00 | [Fluids, pressure and flow](00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/fluids-pressure-and-flow.md) | Resistance varies with radius to the fourth power, so calibre is the controlling structure in vessels and airways. |

**When it misleads.** Function can fail with structure intact (channelopathies, functional disorders, a conduction block that no scan shows), and structure can be abnormal without consequence (the incidental finding, the asymptomatic disc bulge). Imaging that finds a lesion does not prove it is the cause; see [cause and mechanism](#cause-and-mechanism) and [probability and uncertainty](#probability-and-uncertainty). The dynamic half of the body, [signalling and information](#signalling-and-information), is where structure explains least.

## Gradients and flow

**Claim: everything in the body moves down a gradient of concentration, pressure, charge or redox potential, and life spends energy only to build the gradients that passive flow will then run down.** The second law makes it so: nothing moves uphill unaided, so every transport the body needs is either a gradient it has paid for (the sodium pump, the heart, the proton gradient of the mitochondrion) or the free fall that follows. The same relation, flow equals driving force divided by resistance, describes a capillary, a bronchus, a nephron and a drug crossing the gut wall, and the same two failures recur: a gradient that collapses, and a gradient that forces flow where it should not go.

| 00 | [Redox and electron transfer](00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/redox-and-electron-transfer.md) | Electrons fall down reduction potentials from food to oxygen; the drop is the body's energy. |
| 02 | [Membrane transport and ion gradients](02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/membrane-transport-and-ion-gradients.md) | The sodium gradient is the cell's battery, charged by one pump and spent by every channel and cotransporter. |
| 02 | [Glycolysis, TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation](02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/glycolysis-tca-cycle-and-oxidative-phosphorylation.md) | The respiratory chain builds a proton gradient; ATP synthase lets it run down. |
| 04 | [Morphogens, patterning and developmental genes](04-genetics-evolution-and-development/03-development/morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md) | A concentration gradient is the embryo's coordinate system. |
| 06 | [Membrane potential and the action potential](06-physiology/02-nervous-system/membrane-potential-and-the-action-potential.md) | Every potential is an ion gradient and every spike is ions running down it. |
| 06 | [Hemodynamics and blood pressure regulation](06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md) | Pressure drives every millilitre, and the arterioles are where most of the gradient is spent. |
| 06 | [Gas exchange and transport](06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/gas-exchange-and-transport.md) | The oxygen cascade: partial pressure falls at every step from air to mitochondrion. |
| 08 | [Edema and fluid imbalance](08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/edema-and-fluid-imbalance.md) | Starling forces: heart failure, liver failure and inflammation each move one term and fluid leaves the vessel. |
| 09 | [Absorption and bioavailability](09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/absorption-and-bioavailability.md) | A drug crosses the gut wall down a concentration gradient; its lipid solubility sets the resistance. |
| 12 | [Valvular and structural heart disease](12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/valvular-and-structural-heart-disease.md) | A stenosis is a pressure gradient, measured by Doppler as velocity. |

**When it misleads.** Flow down a gradient is indiscriminate: the same Starling forces that nourish tissue flood it when venous pressure rises, and a collapsed potassium gradient stops the heart. Gradients also cost energy to hold, so the first thing an ischaemic cell loses is its gradients. The principle is in permanent tension with [barriers and compartments](#barriers-and-compartments), whose whole purpose is to stop gradients equalising, and it sets the terms of [supply and demand](#supply-and-demand).

## Feedback and homeostasis

**Claim: a system that measures its own output and adjusts its input holds a variable near a set point despite disturbances, and its gain and delay decide whether it stabilises or oscillates.** Negative feedback is the only known way to make a precise system out of imprecise parts, and the body is built from imprecise parts in a changing environment. The same mathematics, stated once in layer 01, is instantiated by an enzyme that inhibits its own pathway, a hormone axis, the baroreflex, a drug tolerance and an epidemic. Positive feedback, the loop that amplifies, is used sparingly and for things that must finish once started: the action potential, ovulation, labour, clotting.

**When it misleads.** A loop with too much gain or delay oscillates (Cheyne–Stokes breathing, the swing of an over-corrected glucose), and a loop can drive the wrong way: the kidney's sodium retention in heart failure is a correct response to a wrong signal. A loop also hides the disturbance it is correcting, which is the whole subject of [compensation and decompensation](#compensation-and-decompensation). Loops act on different clocks, so see [time courses](#time-courses).

## Compensation and decompensation

**Claim: the body's reserve and its feedback loops conceal damage until the damage exceeds what they can cover, so disease is silent for most of its course and failure, when it comes, is sudden.** This follows directly from feedback: a controller that works keeps the output normal while the disturbance grows, so the measured variable says nothing until the effector saturates. It is the most clinically consequential principle in the tree, because it explains why normal findings reassure falsely, why the first symptom is late, why stress tests exist, and why the compensation itself (hypertrophy, vasoconstriction, hyperfiltration, neurohormonal activation) so often becomes the disease.

| 02 | [Metabolic integration and the fed–fasted cycle](02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/metabolic-integration-and-the-fed-fasted-cycle.md) | Glycogen, then gluconeogenesis, then ketosis: staged reserves that each buy time. |
| 06 | [The cardiac cycle and pump function](06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/the-cardiac-cycle-and-pump-function.md) | Frank–Starling: stretch raises output, until the over-stretched ventricle no longer answers. |
| 06 | [Glomerular filtration and clearance](06-physiology/05-renal-system/glomerular-filtration-and-clearance.md) | Creatinine rises as the reciprocal of filtration, so it stays near normal until half the nephrons are gone. |
| 06 | [Glucose homeostasis](06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/glucose-homeostasis.md) | The beta cell hypersecretes for years against insulin resistance before it fails and glucose rises. |
| 08 | [Hemorrhage, shock and sepsis](08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md) | Vasoconstriction and tachycardia hold the pressure until roughly a third of the blood volume is lost. |
| 08 | [Atherosclerosis](08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md) | Outward remodelling hides a plaque until it occupies some 40% of the wall; angiography sees a normal lumen. |
| 08 | [Etiology, pathogenesis and natural history](08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/etiology-pathogenesis-and-natural-history.md) | The preclinical phase is reserve hiding a lesion; symptoms begin when it runs out. |
| 11 | [Functional testing: spirometry, exercise and sleep](11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/functional-testing-spirometry-exercise-and-sleep.md) | Loading the system is how a limit that is invisible at rest is found. |
| 12 | [Heart failure](12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/heart-failure.md) | The neurohormonal response to low output sustains it for years and then kills; blocking it is the treatment. |

**When it misleads.** Treating the compensation as the disease can kill: lowering a reflex tachycardia in haemorrhage, or draining the fluid a failing heart has raised its filling pressure to keep. The reverse error, trusting a normal value, is commoner. The principle makes [time courses](#time-courses) decisive (a slow loss is compensated, the same loss fast is shock) and is the dark side of [redundancy and reserve](#redundancy-and-reserve).

## Supply and demand

**Claim: every tissue's oxygen, substrate and perfusion must match its consumption moment by moment, and the mismatch, whatever its cause, is one event: ischaemia, hypoxia, failure.** Cells store almost no oxygen and the brain and heart store almost no fuel, so supply must be continuous and must track demand across a twentyfold range. The matching is done by flow (the heart, local vasodilatation), by content (haemoglobin, ventilation) and by extraction, and each term can fail. The clinician's equation, delivery equals cardiac output times arterial oxygen content, is the whole of shock, respiratory failure, anaemia and coronary disease read in one line.

| 03 | [The cellular stress response](03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/the-cellular-stress-response.md) | The hypoxia response is the cell's own accounting of oxygen against need. |
| 05 | [Vascular and lymphatic architecture](05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/vascular-and-lymphatic-architecture.md) | End arteries infarct; watersheds between two supplies fail first when pressure falls. |
| 06 | [Red cells, haemoglobin and iron](06-physiology/08-blood/red-cells-haemoglobin-and-iron.md) | Haemoglobin raises the oxygen content of blood seventy-fold; anaemia is a delivery deficit the heart must cover with flow. |

| 08 | [Thrombosis, embolism and infarction](08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md) | Infarction is supply cut to zero in a territory with no second route. |
| 12 | [Ischemic heart disease](12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md) | The heart extracts most of its coronary oxygen at rest, so demand can only be met by more flow; a stenosis caps it. |

**When it misleads.** Raising supply is not always right: hyperoxia injures, transfusion carries its own risks, and reducing demand (a beta-blocker, cooling, rest) often serves better. Supply is bounded by [gradients and flow](#gradients-and-flow) and buffered by [redundancy and reserve](#redundancy-and-reserve), and every attempt to raise it is a [harm and benefit](#harm-and-benefit) decision.

## Barriers and compartments

**Claim: life depends on keeping things apart, so the body is a nest of compartments, each bounded by a wall that is selectively permeable, and disease is very often something in the wrong compartment.** A gradient exists only across a barrier, so the first act of life was a membrane, and every later level repeats it: organelle, cell, epithelium, serous sac, fascial sheath, skull, placenta. Each wall decides what crosses, and each closed space obeys a pressure–volume rule, so a few millilitres of blood is harmless in the thigh and fatal in the skull.

**When it misleads.** The barrier that protects also excludes: antibiotics that cannot reach the meninges, a tumour behind the blood–brain barrier, latent virus in a compartment immunity cannot see. And every device that crosses a barrier (catheter, tube, drain) is a hole an organism can use. Barriers exist to resist [gradients and flow](#gradients-and-flow), and they constrain which [signals](#signalling-and-information) can reach which cells.

## Signalling and information

**Claim: the body is coordinated by messages (genetic, neural, endocrine, immune), and every message is encoded, amplified, read against noise, and given its meaning by the receiver, not the sender.** Cells are too many and too far apart to be controlled by contact, so coordination needs signals, and signals obey information theory: a channel has a capacity set by signal-to-noise, redundancy protects against error, and a threshold trades missed signals against false alarms. The clinician is one more receiver: a symptom, a trace or an image is a noisy signal from a mechanism, decoded against a prior.

**When it misleads.** A signal has no meaning without its receiver, so the same hormone does opposite things in two tissues, and a signal that fails to terminate (cholera toxin, an oncogene) is as harmful as one that never arrives. Noise is never zero: every alert threshold is a false-alarm rate. The principle depends on [specificity and recognition](#specificity-and-recognition) at the receiver and on [probability and uncertainty](#probability-and-uncertainty) at the reader.

## Specificity and recognition

**Claim: biology works by molecular recognition, one surface fitting another with enough weak bonds to bind, and because recognition is a ratio of affinities rather than an absolute, every recogniser sometimes binds the wrong thing.** Enzymes, receptors, antibodies, transcription factors and drugs all use the same lock-and-key chemistry, so the same trade-off recurs at every layer: higher specificity costs speed, breadth or time to build, and imperfect specificity costs cross-reaction. Immunology is the principle's home, because it must recognise everything foreign while sparing everything self, and both failures are diseases.

## Redundancy and reserve

**Claim: the body carries far more capacity than rest requires and more than one route to most ends, so a single failure is usually survivable; this is why disease is tolerated, why organs can be donated, and why reserve, once spent, is missed.** Selection favoured survival of injury and infection, both unpredictable, and the cheapest insurance against the unpredictable is spare capacity and parallel paths. Redundancy is in space (two kidneys, collateral vessels), in number (a million nephrons, ten times the marrow output needed) and in design (three complement pathways, overlapping cytokines). Its measure is the gap between resting and maximal function.

## Trade-offs and evolutionary legacy

**Claim: the body is not designed but descended, so every feature is a compromise that was good enough under past selection, and much disease is the bill for an old bargain presented in a new environment.** Selection maximises reproduction, not health or longevity; it cannot redesign from scratch, only modify what exists; and it worked on environments of scarcity, infection and early death. Hence a clotting system that saves wounds and kills by thrombosis, inflammation tuned for acute injury that destroys when chronic, fat storage that became obesity, a pelvis for walking that barely admits a head built for thinking, and ageing itself as the price of early fitness.

## Scale and surface-to-volume

**Claim: when size doubles, surface rises fourfold and volume eightfold, so every process at a surface (diffusion, heat loss, absorption, filtration) falls behind every process in a volume (metabolism, weight, heat production), and size therefore dictates design.** This one geometric fact explains why cells are microscopic, why anything larger than a millimetre needs a circulation, why exchange organs are folded into tens of square metres, why a newborn chills and dehydrates faster than an adult, and why a child's airway, which is narrow, obstructs with an oedema an adult would not notice. Metabolic rate and drug clearance scale with body mass to a power near 0.75, not 1, which is why children are not small adults for dosing.

## Time courses

**Claim: the same insult produces different disease depending on its tempo, so the clock (acute against chronic, latency, half-life, natural history) is a diagnostic axis as important as the lesion itself.** Biological processes have characteristic times: channels in milliseconds, hormones in seconds to weeks, inflammation in days to years, carcinogenesis in decades. A disturbance faster than the relevant control loop is uncompensated and dramatic; one slower is compensated and silent. Half-life, time to steady state and doubling time are the same exponential mathematics applied to a drug, a tracer, a tumour and an epidemic.

## Dose-response

**Claim: the effect of any drug, toxin, nutrient, radiation or exposure is a function of its dose, usually a saturating curve with a threshold below and a plateau above, so the dose decides whether a substance is a poison, a medicine or nothing.** The shape comes from chemistry: binding sites saturate, so occupancy follows a hyperbola, and effect follows occupancy. Quantal curves (the fraction of people responding) are distributions, and their position for benefit and for harm sets the therapeutic index. Because the curve is steep in the middle, small changes in exposure there have large effects, which is where anaesthesia and poisoning both live.

## Probability and uncertainty

**Claim: biological variation, measurement error and incomplete information mean that medicine never knows, only estimates, so every result is a likelihood ratio, every diagnosis a probability and every prognosis a distribution.** No two patients are alike and no measurement is exact, so population data can only shift the odds for an individual. The right formalism is Bayesian: a prior set by prevalence and the patient's features, updated by each finding according to how much more likely it is under one hypothesis than another. Medicine's thresholds for testing and treating are decisions made at a probability, never at certainty.

## Cause and mechanism

**Claim: an association is not a cause, and a cause is not a mechanism; medicine moves from the first to the third by design (randomisation, natural experiments), by the weight of considerations such as gradient and temporality, and by showing the chain of events that connects exposure to disease.** Most disease has component causes, none necessary or sufficient alone, so the useful question is which component can be removed. | 14 | [The mental state and psychiatric formulation](14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/the-mental-state-and-psychiatric-formulation.md) | Formulation is a multi-level causal model: predisposing, precipitating and perpetuating factors. |
| 15 | [Social determinants and health inequity](15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/social-determinants-and-health-inequity.md) | The causes of the causes: why the risk factors are distributed as they are. |

**When it misleads.** Mechanism is seductive: a story that explains the finding feels like proof, and reverse causation and confounding by indication produce associations as strong as any real cause. The remedy is a design that could have shown the opposite. The principle is read through [probability and uncertainty](#probability-and-uncertainty), and in multifactorial disease it dissolves into [the host and its environment](#the-host-and-its-environment).

## The host and its environment

**Claim: disease is an interaction, not a property of the host or of the exposure alone: the same genes, microbe or behaviour produces disease in one setting and health in another.** Heritability is a statement about variation in one environment and changes when the environment does; an infection needs organism, dose, route and a susceptible host; a risk factor's effect depends on the others present. Because the environment includes diet, microbes, work, housing, income and climate, the principle is where biology meets society, and where prevention has its largest lever.

| Layer | Where it appears | How |
|---|---|---|
| 04 | [Complex traits and heritability](04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/complex-traits-and-heritability.md) | Heritability describes variation in a population and environment, not fate in an individual. |
| 04 | [Human evolution and variation](04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/human-evolution-and-variation.md) | The variants that differ in frequency were shaped by infection, diet and climate; most variation is within every population. |
| 06 | [Fetal and neonatal physiology](06-physiology/09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/fetal-and-neonatal-physiology.md) | The intrauterine environment sets growth and later risk: developmental origins of adult disease. |
| 07 | [The human microbiome](07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/the-human-microbiome.md) | Environment internalised: shaped by birth, diet and antibiotics, and shaping immunity in return. |
| 07 | [Transmission and the chain of infection](07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/transmission-and-the-chain-of-infection.md) | Agent, reservoir, route and susceptible host; every control measure breaks one link. |
| 07 | [Hypersensitivity and allergy](07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md) | Allergy has risen with loss of early microbial exposure: the hygiene hypothesis. |

| 12 | [Asthma and COPD](12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/asthma-and-copd.md) | Smoke, dust and allergens meet a variably susceptible airway; the lung is the organ that breathes its environment. |

**When it misleads.** Two opposite errors: blaming the host (behaviour as pure choice, genes as destiny) and blaming the environment (determinism that leaves the patient nothing to do). Interaction means both matter and neither alone predicts. It complicates [cause and mechanism](#cause-and-mechanism), because the cause of a case and the cause of a rate differ, and it is the present-day face of [trade-offs and evolutionary legacy](#trade-offs-and-evolutionary-legacy): a body adapted to one environment living in another.

## Injury and repair

**Claim: the body has one response to damage of any cause (inflammation, then regeneration where the tissue can and fibrosis where it cannot), and that response is itself a major cause of disease.** Damage is continuous at every scale: DNA breaks, proteins misfold, cells die, tissues tear. Each scale has its repair (proofreading, chaperones, stem cells, scar), and health is repair keeping pace. The response evolved for acute wounds and infections, so when it runs too long (chronic inflammation), too far (sepsis) or in the wrong tissue (fibrosis of lung, liver, kidney, heart), repair destroys what it was meant to save.

|---|---|---|

| 03 | [Protein quality control and degradation](03-cell-biology/01-cell-structure/protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md) | Chaperones, proteasome and autophagy are repair at the molecular scale; their exhaustion is degeneration. |

| 05 | [Connective tissue, bone and cartilage](05-tissues-and-anatomy/01-tissues/connective-tissue-bone-and-cartilage.md) | Bone heals without a scar because it remodels anyway; cartilage, without vessels, barely heals at all. |
| 06 | [Bone remodelling and joints](06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/bone-remodelling-and-joints.md) | Continuous excavation and refilling repairs fatigue damage before it becomes a fracture. |

**When it misleads.** Repair is not restoration: a scar is weaker, stiffer and electrically dead, so a healed infarct still fails as a pump and can host an arrhythmia. Suppressing the response trades one harm for another. The principle is [trade-offs and evolutionary legacy](#trade-offs-and-evolutionary-legacy) in its most visible form, and its outcome depends on [time courses](#time-courses): the same response over days heals and over years destroys.

## Harm and benefit

**Claim: every intervention has a cost as well as a gain, both are probabilities, and the decision is their balance for this patient, which is why absolute risk, number needed to treat and number needed to harm are the currency of therapeutics.** The benefit of a treatment scales with the patient's baseline risk while its harm usually does not, so the same drug is a good bet in one patient and a bad one in another. Testing has costs too (radiation, false positives, incidental findings), and so does doing nothing. "First, do no harm" is this principle stated as a duty; a health budget is the same principle stated for a population.

|---|---|---|

| 07 | [Immunological memory and vaccination](07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/immunological-memory-and-vaccination.md) | Vaccines are judged by rare adverse events against the diseases they prevent in the healthy. |
| 07 | [Transplant and tumour immunology](07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md) | Checkpoint blockade releases the brake on tumours and on self-tolerance by one mechanism. |

**When it misleads.** Harms and benefits fall on different people (screening, vaccination, antimicrobial use), at different times (the statin now, the stroke in a decade) and in different units, so no arithmetic settles the balance without a value judgement, and the patient's values are data. Omission is also a choice with harms. The principle is the clinical face of [probability and uncertainty](#probability-and-uncertainty) and the final arbiter of every [dose-response](#dose-response) decision.

## Principles that pull against each other

These principles are not a checklist to apply all at once. Many pull in opposite directions, and most hard clinical decisions are about where to stand between two of them.

| Tension | Why they conflict | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| [Supply and demand](#supply-and-demand) vs [redundancy and reserve](#redundancy-and-reserve) | Reserve costs energy and perfusion that could meet today's demand; the organs with the least reserve are the ones that cannot afford it. | The heart and brain infarct; the kidney's medulla runs hypoxic to hold its gradient; the ICU supplies what reserve no longer can. |
| [Specificity and recognition](#specificity-and-recognition) vs [redundancy and reserve](#redundancy-and-reserve) | A narrow recogniser is escaped by one mutation; a broad one attacks self. | Innate versus adaptive immunity; a targeted drug and the resistance that follows; a sensitive test and its false positives. |
| [Harm and benefit](#harm-and-benefit) vs [probability and uncertainty](#probability-and-uncertainty) | The benefit is probable and in the future; the harm is certain and now, or the reverse. | Anticoagulation in atrial fibrillation; empirical antibiotics; screening a healthy population. |
| [Compensation](#compensation-and-decompensation) vs [time courses](#time-courses) | Compensation needs time; a fast insult overwhelms the same loss a slow one hides. | Acute against chronic anaemia; acute kidney injury against chronic kidney disease; haemorrhage before and after the blood pressure falls. |
| [Feedback and homeostasis](#feedback-and-homeostasis) vs [compensation and decompensation](#compensation-and-decompensation) | A loop that works conceals the disturbance it corrects, and may drive the wrong way. | Normal creatinine with half the nephrons gone; sodium retention in heart failure; a normal glucose from a hypersecreting beta cell. |
| [Gradients and flow](#gradients-and-flow) vs [barriers and compartments](#barriers-and-compartments) | A barrier exists to stop a gradient equalising, and the gradient is always pushing. | Oedema; raised intracranial pressure; the blood–brain barrier that excludes both toxin and antibiotic. |
| [Injury and repair](#injury-and-repair) vs [trade-offs and evolutionary legacy](#trade-offs-and-evolutionary-legacy) | The repair response was tuned for acute wounds and runs the same way when the stimulus never ends. | Chronic inflammation; fibrosis of lung, liver and kidney; sepsis. |
| [Structure determines function](#structure-determines-function) vs [signalling and information](#signalling-and-information) | A lesion predicts a deficit; a signalling fault produces a deficit without a lesion. | Channelopathies and functional disorders; the normal scan in a patient who is ill; the incidental finding in one who is well. |
| [Cause and mechanism](#cause-and-mechanism) vs [the host and its environment](#the-host-and-its-environment) | A clean mechanism explains the case; the distribution of cases needs the causes of the causes. | Tuberculosis as a bacillus and as poverty; obesity as physiology and as food supply. |
| [Dose-response](#dose-response) vs [scale and surface-to-volume](#scale-and-surface-to-volume) | The curve is written in concentration at the target; size and age change what dose produces that concentration. | Paediatric and geriatric dosing; the obese patient; the neonate's immature clearance. |

[Probability and uncertainty](#probability-and-uncertainty) referees most of these: it is the language in which each side of a trade-off is measured. [Harm and benefit](#harm-and-benefit) is where they are settled, one patient at a time, and its answer depends on values the principles cannot supply.
