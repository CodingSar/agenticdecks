## What this area is about

A neoplasm is a clone of the body's own cells that has escaped the controls governing its neighbours and grows autonomously. Cancer is the malignant case, in which the clone also invades and spreads, and it is the second commonest cause of death worldwide (roughly 10 million deaths a year in the 2020s). This area asks how a normal cell becomes such a clone, what it must acquire to kill, what causes the changes, and how pathologists name and measure the result so that it can be treated.

The unifying idea is **somatic evolution**. Mutation and selection, the forces that shaped species, operate among the cells of one body over a lifetime. Every capability a cancer has was selected because it let a sub-clone outgrow its neighbours; every cause of cancer either supplies mutations or rewards their carriers with division; every treatment is a new selective pressure, and relapse is the survival of a fitter sub-clone. Seen this way, the hallmarks, the genes, the spread, the causes and the classification are five views of one process.

Read the hallmarks first; they are the map. Oncogenes and tumour suppressors fill in the genetic mechanism behind each hallmark and introduce the two-hit logic that explains inherited cancer. Invasion and metastasis take the lethal hallmarks apart step by step and show that a tumour is an organ with a stroma. Causes of cancer steps back to ask what produces the mutations, which is the bridge to prevention. Classification, grading and staging is the output: how the pathologist turns a biopsy into the categories on which oncology, surgery and radiotherapy act.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cell and tissue injury](../01-cell-and-tissue-injury/README.md):** metaplasia and dysplasia are adaptations that precede neoplasia; failed apoptosis is a hallmark; chronic inflammation is both a cause of cancer and the model for the tumour stroma; the healing wound and the growing tumour use the same fibroblasts, macrophages and angiogenesis.
- **[Haemodynamic disorders](../02-hemodynamic-disorders/README.md):** cancer is a leading acquired cause of venous thrombosis; tumour emboli and lymphatic obstruction are routes to harm; tumours need angiogenesis as infarcts need collaterals.
- **[Genetic, metabolic and environmental disease](../04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/README.md):** inherited cancer syndromes are single-gene disorders; radiation and chemical carcinogens are environmental injury; obesity is a promoter; aging supplies the time and the accumulated mutations.
- **[Disease as process](../05-disease-as-process/README.md):** latency, natural history and the multifactorial interaction of exposure and host are illustrated most sharply by cancer, and the biases of screening belong to both areas.
