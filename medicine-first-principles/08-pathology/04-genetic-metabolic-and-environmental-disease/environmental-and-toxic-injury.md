## Essence

The environment injures through chemicals, physical agents and the substances people choose to consume. The World Health Organization attributes roughly a quarter of global deaths to modifiable environmental factors, air pollution and tobacco foremost. Whatever the agent, the damage reaches the cell by a small number of routes already described in cell injury: free radicals, covalent binding to proteins and DNA, depletion of ATP, membrane disruption, and inflammation. What toxicology adds is the arithmetic of exposure: dose, duration, route, and the body's own metabolism, which detoxifies most xenobiotics but converts some into the very species that harm. The organ that is hit is usually the one that absorbs, concentrates or metabolises the agent.

## Core tenets

1. **The dose makes the poison, and the body's metabolism sets the effective dose.** Paracelsus's rule holds for every agent: oxygen, water, iron and vitamin A are toxic in excess, and cyanide is harmless below a threshold. Most foreign compounds (xenobiotics) are lipid-soluble; the liver's cytochrome P450 enzymes oxidise them (phase I) and conjugating enzymes attach glucuronate, sulphate or glutathione (phase II) to make them water-soluble for excretion. For some agents phase I creates the toxin: paracetamol becomes a reactive quinone-imine that is harmless while glutathione lasts and necroses the liver when it runs out; carbon tetrachloride becomes a free radical; benzo[a]pyrene becomes a DNA-binding epoxide. Enzyme induction (alcohol, anticonvulsants) and genetic variants in these enzymes change individual susceptibility.

2. **Tobacco is the single largest preventable cause of disease, and it injures by several mechanisms at once.** Smoke delivers some 7,000 compounds, including polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons and nitrosamines that form DNA adducts (cancers of lung, mouth, larynx, oesophagus, bladder, pancreas and more), carbon monoxide that displaces oxygen from haemoglobin, oxidants and irritants that provoke chronic bronchial inflammation and protease-mediated destruction of alveoli (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), and nicotine, which causes the dependence that sustains exposure. Smoke also activates platelets and injures endothelium, accelerating [atherosclerosis](../02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md). Tobacco kills more than 8 million people a year worldwide (2020s), about half of long-term smokers, and the risk falls after cessation on a timescale of years.

3. **Alcohol injures the liver, brain and fetus, and promotes cancer.** Ethanol is oxidised to acetaldehyde, a reactive aldehyde that binds proteins and DNA, and generates excess reducing equivalents that divert liver metabolism to fat synthesis (steatosis) and lactate. Continued exposure gives alcoholic hepatitis, then fibrosis and cirrhosis, through the inflammation and repair pathways of layer 08. The brain suffers directly (intoxication, dependence, withdrawal) and through thiamine deficiency (Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome). Alcohol crossing the placenta causes fetal alcohol spectrum disorder, the commonest preventable cause of intellectual disability. Acetaldehyde and the solvent effect on mucosa make alcohol a cause of cancers of the mouth, throat, oesophagus, liver and breast. People who flush because of an inactive aldehyde dehydrogenase variant are at higher risk because acetaldehyde accumulates.

4. **Air pollution and inhaled particles injure the lung and, through it, the heart.** Fine particulate matter under 2.5 micrometres reaches alveoli, provokes inflammation and oxidative stress, and enters the blood, raising cardiovascular as well as respiratory mortality; ambient and household air pollution together account for several million deaths a year. Mineral dusts cause the pneumoconioses: silica (silicosis) and asbestos (asbestosis, mesothelioma, lung cancer) are engulfed by macrophages that cannot digest them, die, and release fibrogenic cytokines, so the lung scars around a particle it cannot remove, the same logic as a [foreign-body granuloma](../01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md). Latency is decades, and a cause can be banned long before its deaths stop.

5. **Heavy metals poison by binding the body's own thiols and mimicking its ions.** Lead displaces calcium and zinc, inhibits haem synthesis (anaemia with basophilic stippling), and damages the developing brain at concentrations once considered safe, so no threshold is now recognised for children; removal from petrol and paint measurably raised population intelligence. Mercury binds sulphydryl groups and, as methylmercury in fish, concentrates in the fetal and adult brain (Minamata disease). Arsenic in groundwater causes skin lesions, peripheral vascular disease and cancers of skin, bladder and lung. Cadmium damages kidney tubules and bone. Each is persistent, bioaccumulates, and injures the organ where it concentrates.

6. **Physical agents injure by transferring energy to tissue.** Mechanical force tears and crushes (trauma, the leading cause of death under 45). Heat denatures protein: burns destroy the skin barrier, so fluid leaks and infection follows, and the depth of a burn decides whether it regenerates from surviving appendages or must scar; heatstroke fails thermoregulation and causes multi-organ injury. Cold freezes tissue (frostbite) or lowers core temperature until the heart arrests (hypothermia). Electricity fibrillates the heart and burns along its path. Ionising radiation breaks DNA and generates radicals: acute high doses kill dividing cells first (marrow, gut, skin), and lower doses leave a lifelong excess of cancer, as [causes of cancer](../03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md) describes; ultraviolet light photo-ages skin and causes its cancers. Noise destroys hair cells, and the loss is permanent.

7. **Drugs and poisons follow the same rules as any xenobiotic, and antidotes reverse a named step.** Therapeutic drugs injure by predictable dose-related toxicity (paracetamol, aminoglycosides) or by idiosyncratic immune and metabolic reactions, the subject of [adverse drug reactions](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md). Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin 200 times more tightly than oxygen; cyanide blocks cytochrome oxidase; organophosphates inhibit acetylcholinesterase; methanol is oxidised to formic acid. Each antidote targets the mechanism: oxygen for carbon monoxide, acetylcysteine to restore glutathione for paracetamol, atropine and oximes for organophosphates, fomepizole to block alcohol dehydrogenase for methanol. Toxicology at the bedside is this leaf applied with a stopwatch.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Xenobiotic | A foreign chemical not produced by or normally present in the body |
| Phase I and II metabolism | Oxidation by cytochrome P450 enzymes, then conjugation to make compounds water-soluble |
| Metabolic activation | Conversion of an inert compound to a reactive, toxic metabolite |
| Threshold and no-threshold agents | Agents harmless below a dose versus those (carcinogens, lead in children) with risk at any dose |
| Pneumoconiosis | Lung fibrosis from inhaled mineral dust the macrophage cannot digest |
| Particulate matter (PM2.5) | Airborne particles under 2.5 micrometres, which reach alveoli and the circulation |
| Bioaccumulation | Concentration of a persistent agent up a food chain or in a tissue over time |
| Acetaldehyde | Reactive first product of ethanol oxidation, responsible for much of alcohol's harm |
| Latency | Interval between exposure and disease, often decades for dusts and carcinogens |
| Antidote | An agent that reverses a poison's specific mechanism |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Natural substances are safe and synthetic ones dangerous."** Aflatoxin, arsenic, radon and tobacco are natural; dose and mechanism, not origin, decide toxicity.
- **"The liver detoxifies poisons."** It also creates them: paracetamol, carbon tetrachloride and many carcinogens are harmless until the liver's enzymes activate them.
- **"If exposure stops, risk stops."** Asbestos, radiation and tobacco carry latencies of decades; the mesotheliomas of 2020s workers reflect exposures of the 1970s–1990s.
