## What this area is about

The first three areas of this layer describe how tissue responds to injury, how the circulation fails and how a clone escapes control. This area asks where the injury comes from when it is built into the organism or delivered by the world around it. Some disease is written in the genome: a single changed gene, a missing enzyme, an extra chromosome. Some is written by the environment: too much or too little food, a toxin, a dust, a dose of radiation. And some is written by time: the accumulated damage and lost reserve of aging, which selection never bothered to prevent. The five leaves move from the fully inherited to the fully acquired, and end with the process that is both.

The unifying idea is **the host meets its environment over time**. Genes set what the body can do; exposures set what is asked of it; years accumulate the mismatch. Phenylketonuria is harmless without dietary phenylalanine, obesity genes are harmless without cheap calories, and lead is harmless to a body that never meets it. Even the "pure" categories are interactions: an inborn error presents when feeding begins, a carcinogen needs a susceptible host, and aging is genes that were tuned for an environment of early death now running in one of long life.

```
   GENOME ----------------------------------------------- ENVIRONMENT
   single-gene / chromosomal      nutrition        toxins, dusts,
   inborn errors                  (excess, lack)   radiation, physical
          \                           |                   /
           \        exposure x susceptibility x time     /
            \                         |                 /
             +------------------> AGING <--------------+
                  damage accumulates, reserve declines,
                  degeneration appears in permanent tissues
```

Read single-gene disorders first for the logic of dosage, dominance and penetrance. Inborn errors apply that logic to enzymes and show how a pathway diagram predicts a disease, a test and a treatment. Nutritional disease is the acquired counterpart: the same pathways starved or flooded. Environmental injury catalogues the agents and returns to the dose–response rule and metabolic activation. Aging closes the area because it is where genes, exposures and time integrate, and because it is the risk factor shared by nearly every disease in the specialty layers.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cell and tissue injury](../01-cell-and-tissue-injury/README.md):** toxins and radiation are the insults that leaf describes in general; misfolded protein accumulation and inflamed fat are intracellular accumulation and chronic inflammation; senescent cells sustain inflammation without a pathogen.
- **[Haemodynamic disorders](../02-hemodynamic-disorders/README.md):** familial hypercholesterolaemia and the thrombophilias are single-gene causes; obesity, smoking and aging are the main drivers of atherosclerosis; stiffening arteries raise systolic pressure with age.
- **[Neoplasia](../03-neoplasia/README.md):** inherited cancer syndromes, chemical and radiation carcinogens, obesity as a promoter, and age as the accumulation of mutations are all shared ground; clonal haematopoiesis sits between aging and leukaemia.
- **[Disease as process](../05-disease-as-process/README.md):** the gene–environment interaction stated here for specific diseases is generalised there as multifactorial causation and risk.
