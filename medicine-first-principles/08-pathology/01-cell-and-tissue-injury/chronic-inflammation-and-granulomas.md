## Essence

Chronic inflammation is inflammation that lasts weeks to years because its cause is never removed: a microbe the body cannot kill, a foreign material it cannot digest, a self-antigen it cannot stop attacking, or a low-grade metabolic irritant it cannot switch off. Neutrophils give way to macrophages, lymphocytes and plasma cells, and three processes run at once: continuing injury, continuing repair, and a conversation between macrophages and T cells that keeps both going. The granuloma, a wall of macrophages built around something that cannot be eliminated, is its most distinctive product. It is the shared mechanism behind tuberculosis, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, atherosclerosis and much of the damage of aging.

## Core tenets

1. **Persistence, not intensity, defines chronicity.** Acute inflammation resolves because neutrophils die within days and the stimulus is cleared. When the stimulus persists, macrophages take over, and they do not die on schedule: they live for months, divide locally and are continuously replenished from blood monocytes. The histological picture (mononuclear cells, tissue destruction and fibrosis side by side) is the signature of a response that cannot finish.

2. **The macrophage is the central cell, and it has two opposed programmes.** Activated by interferon gamma from T cells or by microbial products, the "classically activated" macrophage becomes a killer: more lysosomal enzymes, more nitric oxide and oxidants, more interleukin-1, interleukin-12 and tumour necrosis factor. Activated by interleukin-4 and interleukin-13, the "alternatively activated" macrophage becomes a repairer: it secretes growth factors (transforming growth factor beta, platelet-derived growth factor) that recruit fibroblasts and lay down matrix. The two are ends of a spectrum, and in chronic inflammation both run at once, which is why destruction and scarring appear together.

3. **A positive feedback loop between macrophages and T cells sustains the lesion.** Macrophages present antigen to T helper cells and secrete interleukin-12; T cells respond with interferon gamma, which further activates macrophages. Each population keeps the other switched on. Interrupting one arm damps the whole loop, which is why antibodies against tumour necrosis factor change the course of rheumatoid arthritis and Crohn's disease but also reactivate latent tuberculosis: the same cytokine that destroys the joint is the one that holds the mycobacterium inside its granuloma.

4. **A granuloma is a strategy of containment when killing fails.** When macrophages cannot destroy what they have engulfed, they fuse into multinucleated giant cells, transform into epithelioid cells and form a compact ball surrounded by lymphocytes and, later, fibroblasts. Immune granulomas (tuberculosis, leprosy, sarcoidosis, Crohn's disease) are driven by T cells and interferon gamma; foreign-body granulomas (sutures, talc, implant debris) need no immune stimulus. Central caseous necrosis, where the hypoxic core dies, is the hallmark of tuberculosis. The granuloma walls off the threat at the cost of the tissue it sits in, and leaves a nidus: if cell-mediated immunity later weakens (HIV infection, anti-tumour necrosis factor therapy, old age), the organism reactivates.

5. **Chronic inflammation causes disease by destroying architecture and replacing it with scar.** Persistent release of proteases and oxidants, and the ongoing fibrotic response, remodel the organ: alveoli become fibrous septa, hepatic lobules become cirrhotic nodules, joint cartilage is eroded by inflamed synovium (pannus). Function fails because structure has gone: the lung has lost its exchange surface, the liver its sinusoidal flow. This is why chronic inflammatory disease reaches organ failure even when no single episode is severe.

6. **Low-grade, non-infectious chronic inflammation drives the common diseases of affluence.** Oxidised lipoprotein in the arterial wall, urate crystals in a joint, saturated fatty acids and adipokines from excess visceral fat, amyloid in the brain and persistent senescent cells all act as damage signals. The resulting smouldering inflammation (raised C-reactive protein and interleukin-6 without any infection) contributes to [atherosclerosis](../02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md), insulin resistance, some cancers and the frailty of aging. Inflammation is therefore not only a response to disease but a cause of much of it.

7. **Chronic inflammation is a field for neoplasia.** Repeated cycles of injury, proliferation and oxidant exposure, in a microenvironment rich in growth factors, select for cells that grow regardless of signals. Hepatitis B and C and hepatocellular carcinoma, ulcerative colitis and colorectal cancer, Barrett's oesophagus and adenocarcinoma, *Helicobacter pylori* and gastric cancer are all of this type. The link is a cause of about one-fifth of cancers worldwide and is treated in [causes of cancer](../03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mononuclear infiltrate | The macrophage, lymphocyte and plasma-cell population that replaces neutrophils in persistent inflammation |
| Classically activated macrophage | Interferon-gamma-driven microbicidal and pro-inflammatory programme |
| Alternatively activated macrophage | Interleukin-4- and interleukin-13-driven repair and fibrosis programme |
| Granuloma | Organised aggregate of epithelioid macrophages, often with giant cells and a lymphocyte rim |
| Caseous necrosis | Cheese-like central necrosis of a granuloma, characteristic of tuberculosis |
| Pannus | Inflamed, proliferating synovium that erodes cartilage and bone in rheumatoid arthritis |
| Delayed-type hypersensitivity | T-cell- and macrophage-mediated reaction developing over 24–72 hours, the immunology behind immune granulomas |
| Metaflammation | Low-grade chronic inflammation driven by metabolic excess |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Chronic inflammation is acute inflammation that went on too long."** The cells, mediators and consequences are different: macrophages and lymphocytes rather than neutrophils, growth factors rather than vasoactive amines, fibrosis rather than exudate. Many chronic inflammatory diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, tuberculosis) begin insidiously with no acute phase.
- **"A granuloma means tuberculosis."** Granulomas form around any indigestible material: fungi, parasites, sutures, silica, beryllium, and in sarcoidosis and Crohn's disease with no identified agent. Caseation points to tuberculosis; the granuloma itself does not.
- **"Chronic inflammation is only a consequence of disease."** In atherosclerosis, type 2 diabetes and several cancers it is a cause: the inflammatory cells and cytokines drive the lesion forward.
