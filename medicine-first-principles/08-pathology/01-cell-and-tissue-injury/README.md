## What this area is about

This area answers the first question of pathology: what happens to living tissue when something goes wrong, and how does the body respond? Whatever the insult (lack of oxygen, a microbe, a toxin, a physical force, an immune attack, a mutation), the tissue has a small repertoire of responses, and the four leaves describe it in the order it unfolds. A cell adapts, is injured or dies. Death and microbes trigger acute inflammation, which delivers plasma and leucocytes to the site. If the cause persists, inflammation becomes chronic and the macrophage replaces the neutrophil. Finally the lost tissue is replaced, by the same cells if they can divide and the scaffold survived, otherwise by scar.

The unifying idea is that **the response to injury is stereotyped and is itself a major cause of disease**. Inflammation and repair evolved to deal with wounds and infections that killed before reproduction; applied for decades to a cholesterol deposit, an autoimmune target or a smoker's airway, the same programmes destroy the organ they are protecting and fill it with collagen. Most chronic disease is injury plus the body's response to it, and the response is usually the larger part.

Read them in order. Cell injury supplies the vocabulary (hypoxia, free radicals, necrosis versus apoptosis) and the trigger: necrotic cells release the damage signals that start inflammation. Acute inflammation is the fast, neutrophil-driven response and its three possible endings. Chronic inflammation is the ending in which nothing ends, and introduces the macrophage–T cell loop that drives most immune-mediated disease. Repair closes the loop: regeneration restores function, scar restores only continuity, and fibrosis is repair that cannot stop. The pattern "injury → inflammation → fibrosis" will recur in nearly every specialty leaf, and these four files are where it is stated once.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Haemodynamic disorders](../02-hemodynamic-disorders/README.md):** ischaemia is the commonest cause of cell injury, and infarction is coagulative necrosis of a territory; the leaky vessel of acute inflammation is one cause of oedema, and the systemic version of acute inflammation is septic shock. Atherosclerosis is chronic inflammation in an artery wall.
- **[Neoplasia](../03-neoplasia/README.md):** failure of apoptosis lets mutated cells survive; chronic inflammation is a field for cancer (hepatitis and liver cancer, colitis and colon cancer); the tumour microenvironment uses the same macrophages, fibroblasts and angiogenesis as a healing wound, which is why tumours have been called wounds that do not heal.
- **[Genetic, metabolic and environmental disease](../04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/README.md):** toxins, radiation and nutritional excess are the insults; inborn errors cause intracellular accumulations; aging is injury accumulating faster than repair, with senescent cells sustaining low-grade inflammation.
- **[Disease as process](../05-disease-as-process/README.md):** the aetiology → pathogenesis → morphology → clinical picture chain is this area's sequence stated in general form.
