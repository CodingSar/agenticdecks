# Disease as process

> **Layer:** [08 Pathology](../README.md) › Disease as process

## What this area is about

The four areas before this one describe mechanisms. This area describes the form a disease takes when those mechanisms are strung together in one patient over time, and how one thinks about a disease that has no single cause. It answers two questions: what is the general shape of any disease, from cause through mechanism and lesion to symptom and outcome, and how does one reason about the common diseases whose causes are many and probabilistic?

The unifying idea is that **disease is a process in time, not a state**, and that it is described at two levels: the chain of events in one body, and the distribution of liability and outcome in a population. The first level gives the grammar every specialty leaf uses (aetiology, pathogenesis, morphology, clinical features, natural history). The second gives the language of risk that prevention, screening and the treatment of common disease depend on. Together they are the bridge from pathology, which explains mechanism, to epidemiology and clinical method, which measure and act.

```
                  |          (mechanisms of areas 01-04)        |
                exposure  latent  preclinical  clinical  recovery / chronic / death

```

Read aetiology and natural history first: it defines the vocabulary and shows that each phase of a disease is a different opportunity to intervene. Multifactorial disease and risk then takes the component-cause idea and makes it quantitative, introducing relative and absolute risk, heritability, gene–environment interaction and the prevention paradox. The first leaf is about any one disease; the second is about the common diseases that make up most of medicine's work.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cell and tissue injury](../01-cell-and-tissue-injury/README.md):** supplies the pathogenesis step for nearly every chain; acute and chronic inflammation are the archetypes of the acute and chronic tempos described here.
- **[Haemodynamic disorders](../02-hemodynamic-disorders/README.md):** atherosclerosis is the model multifactorial disease, and its risk factors (Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL), blood pressure, smoking, diabetes) are the examples through which risk arithmetic is usually taught.
- **[Neoplasia](../03-neoplasia/README.md):** cancer's long latency, preclinical phase and staging illustrate natural history and the biases of screening; its causes range from single carcinogens to polygenic liability.
- **[Genetic, metabolic and environmental disease](../04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/README.md):** single-gene disorders are the deterministic contrast to multifactorial disease; environmental and nutritional exposures are the modifiable components; aging is the slowest natural history of all.

## Cross-cutting threads

- [Cause and mechanism](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#cause-and-mechanism): necessary, sufficient and component causes; from association to cause through mechanism and experiment.
- [Probability and uncertainty](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#probability-and-uncertainty): risk, liability distributions, heritability and prognosis are all probability statements about populations applied to one person.
- [Time courses](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#time-courses): induction, latency, preclinical and clinical phases; acute against chronic; natural history as the baseline for every treatment claim.
- [The host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment): gene–environment interaction and the social distribution of risk.
- [Compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation): the preclinical phase is reserve hiding a lesion; symptoms begin when it runs out.
- [Harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit): absolute risk determines how much a patient gains from any intervention, and the prevention paradox sets individual against population benefit.
