## Essence

In the third week the flat two-layered disc rearranges itself into three layers by gastrulation, and in the following five weeks those three layers fold, bud, migrate and hollow into every organ of the body. Ectoderm gives skin and nervous system; mesoderm gives muscle, bone, blood, heart, kidney and gonad; endoderm gives the lining of gut and airways and the glands that bud from them. By the end of week 8 the embryo is ~3 cm long and every organ is present in rudimentary form; the remaining 30 weeks are growth and maturation. The one idea is **a small repertoire of cell behaviours (migrate, fold, bud, branch, fuse, die) applied in sequence to sheets of cells** produces all anatomical structure, and because the same sequence runs in every vertebrate the body plan is conserved and its failures are predictable. Where a tissue comes from determines what it can become and what tumours it can form; when it forms determines when it can be damaged.

## Core tenets

1. **Gastrulation converts a disc into three layers by cell migration through the primitive streak.** Epiblast cells at the streak lose their epithelial character (an epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition driven by loss of E-cadherin; [adhesion](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/cell-adhesion-and-the-extracellular-matrix.md)), ingress, and spread between the epiblast and hypoblast as mesoderm or displace the hypoblast as definitive endoderm; the epiblast cells that remain on the surface are ectoderm. The order and place of ingression set a cell's fate, and the streak's position defines the head–tail axis and the midline, so left–right and dorsal–ventral are fixed in the same act ([patterning](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md)). Cells that migrate through the streak and stop at the midline form the notochord, the organiser of the axis that later induces the nervous system and becomes the nucleus of each intervertebral disc.

2. **Ectoderm makes the nervous system by folding a plate into a tube, and failures of that fold are the commonest serious malformations.** Signals from the notochord induce the overlying ectoderm to thicken into the neural plate; its edges rise, meet and fuse into the neural tube (days 22–28), closing from the middle toward both ends. The tube becomes brain and spinal cord; the cells at the fused crest detach as neural crest, a migratory population that forms peripheral nerves, melanocytes, adrenal medulla and most of the skull and face. Failure of closure at the head end is anencephaly, at the tail end spina bifida; together neural tube defects affect ~1 in 1,000 pregnancies and are reduced by about 70% by folate taken around conception, which is why flour is fortified in many countries. The rest of the ectoderm becomes epidermis, hair, nails, lens, inner ear and the enamel of teeth.

3. **Mesoderm is partitioned by distance from the midline, and each partition has a destiny.** Next to the notochord, paraxial mesoderm segments into ~42 pairs of somites in strict head-to-tail order (one pair every ~5 hours in humans), each forming a vertebra, the muscles of one segment and the dermis over it; this segmentation is why the body has repeating vertebrae, ribs and spinal nerves and why a dermatome maps to a spinal level ([segmental innervation](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)). Intermediate mesoderm forms kidney and gonad, which is why renal and genital anomalies travel together. Lateral plate mesoderm splits into a layer lining the body wall and a layer covering the gut, with the body cavity between: the origin of pleura, pericardium and peritoneum and their visceral and parietal layers ([body cavities](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/body-cavities-fascia-and-compartments.md)). Blood, vessels and heart arise from lateral and extraembryonic mesoderm.

4. **The heart forms first, folds, and septates, and each step can fail.** Two endothelial tubes fuse into a single beating tube by day 22, before any other organ works, because the embryo is now too large for diffusion ([scale](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/scale-and-allometry.md)). The tube loops to the right, bringing the future atria behind the ventricles; endocardial cushions grow to divide the atrioventricular canal; septa partition atria and ventricles; the single outflow is divided by a spiral septum into aorta and pulmonary trunk, with neural crest cells contributing to it. Congenital heart disease (~8 per 1,000 births, the commonest major malformation) is this sequence interrupted: septal defects when septa fail to meet, tetralogy of Fallot and transposition when the outflow septum is misaligned, and a persistent patent foramen ovale in a quarter of adults when the fetal shunt never seals.

5. **Endoderm forms the gut tube and everything that buds from it.** Folding of the embryo wraps the endoderm into a tube open only at the yolk sac. Along its length, patches of endoderm bud outward in response to signals from the surrounding mesoderm: thyroid, pharyngeal pouches (which form parathyroid, thymus and part of the ear), lung, liver, gall bladder, pancreas. Each bud then branches by repeated tip-splitting, a process in which the mesoderm induces the epithelium and the epithelium instructs the mesoderm (reciprocal induction): the airway tree completes ~23 generations of branching, the nephron ~10^6 copies from the ureteric bud. Failed recanalisation of the solid gut tube gives atresia; a persistent connection between trachea and oesophagus gives fistula; the embryonic yolk stalk persists in ~2% of people as a Meckel's diverticulum.

6. **Organogenesis is a window, and the time of an insult predicts the malformation.** Each organ has a critical period when its primordium is forming, mostly between weeks 3 and 8 (embryonic period): heart days 20–50, neural tube 22–28, limbs 24–36, palate up to week 10, external genitalia to week 12. An insult before week 3 usually kills the embryo or is repaired; one after week 8 slows growth or damages function (brain, which keeps developing to birth and beyond) but does not alter gross form ([teratogens](congenital-malformations-and-teratogens.md)). This is the reason the first trimester is the period of teratogenic risk, and why a drug's safety in pregnancy depends on when it is taken.

7. **Where a tissue comes from predicts what tumours it forms and how pathologists name them.** Carcinomas arise from epithelia of ectodermal and endodermal origin (skin, gut, lung, breast) and from some mesodermal epithelia (kidney); sarcomas from mesodermal connective tissue, muscle and bone; gliomas and neuroblastomas from neural ectoderm and neural crest; germ cell tumours from the primordial germ cells, which migrate from the yolk sac to the gonadal ridge and occasionally stop en route, which is why teratomas occur in the midline (sacrum, mediastinum, pineal). Tumours also express the markers of their lineage (keratins, vimentin, neural markers), which is how an unknown primary is traced ([tumour classification](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/tumour-classification-grading-and-staging.md)). Embryology is the classification system of pathology.

8. **The body plan is deeply conserved, which makes the embryo an evolutionary document and animal models valid.** Every vertebrate passes through a stage with pharyngeal arches, a notochord, a tail and a segmented trunk (the phylotypic stage). Human pharyngeal arches become jaw, ear bones, larynx and the muscles of face and throat rather than gills; the arch arteries explain the asymmetric adult aorta and why the left recurrent laryngeal nerve loops under it. The transient kidneys (pronephros, mesonephros) that form and regress before the permanent one recapitulate the kidneys of fish and amphibians. The same genes build the same structures across vertebrates ([developmental genes](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md)), so a mutation studied in a zebrafish or mouse predicts a human malformation, and the embryo's detours are the constraints described in [evolutionary medicine](../02-evolution/evolutionary-medicine.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gastrulation | Formation of three germ layers by cell ingression through the primitive streak |
| Ectoderm / mesoderm / endoderm | Outer, middle and inner germ layers and their derivatives |
| Notochord | Midline rod that organises the axis and induces the neural plate |
| Neurulation / neural crest | Folding of the neural plate into a tube / migratory cells released from its crest |
| Somite | Segmental block of paraxial mesoderm forming vertebra, muscle and dermis |
| Lateral plate mesoderm | Source of body wall and gut linings and the body cavity between them |
| Reciprocal induction | Epithelium and mesenchyme instructing each other to form a branching organ |
| Critical period | The window during which an organ's form can be disrupted |
| Phylotypic stage | The embryonic stage at which all vertebrates look most alike |
| Pharyngeal arches | Segmental structures of the neck forming face, jaw, ear, larynx and great vessels |

## Common misconceptions

- **"The embryo develops by growing bigger."** During organogenesis form is produced by rearrangement, folding and local proliferation; the embryo is ~3 cm at the end of it. Growth is the fetal period's work.
- **"Each germ layer makes a separate set of organs."** Nearly every organ is a collaboration: endodermal epithelium with mesodermal stroma in the lung, neural crest with mesoderm in the heart's outflow; this is why malformations cluster.
- **"Embryonic structures disappear."** Many persist as adult remnants (ligamentum arteriosum, foramen ovale, urachus, Meckel's diverticulum) and are sources of adult disease when they fail to close or become inflamed.
