## Essence

Every embryo begins with the same bipotential gonad, two sets of ducts and one set of external folds, and could become either sex. A single gene on the Y chromosome tips the gonad toward testis; the testis then makes two hormones, and those hormones, not the chromosomes, build the rest of the male body. Without them the female pattern forms. Sex is therefore a chain of events, chromosomal → gonadal → hormonal → anatomical, and each link can fail independently, producing people whose chromosomes, gonads and anatomy do not match. The one idea is **a genetic switch followed by a hormonal cascade**, and it explains the differences of sex development, why they cluster into a few patterns, and why sex in medicine is a set of variables rather than a single fact.

## Core tenets

1. **The gonad is bipotential until a switch is thrown in week 6–7.** Intermediate mesoderm forms a genital ridge ([organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md)) colonised by germ cells migrating from the yolk sac. The ridge contains precursors that can become either Sertoli cells or granulosa cells. SRY, a transcription factor gene on the short arm of the Y, is expressed briefly in these cells and switches on SOX9; SOX9 commits them to Sertoli fate and they organise the ridge into testis cords. Without SRY, WNT4 and β-catenin signalling, then FOXL2, drive the ovarian programme and suppress SOX9. The two programmes repress each other and must be maintained throughout life; loss of the maintenance factors in adult mice partly converts one gonad type to the other.

2. **SRY is necessary and sufficient for a testis, and the proof is in humans and mice.** A 46,XX person whose paternal X carries SRY, translocated by an X–Y crossover in the father's meiosis, develops testes; a 46,XY person with a deleted or mutated SRY develops streak gonads and a female body (Swyer syndrome). A female mouse made transgenic for *Sry* alone develops as a male (Koopman, 1991). The Y's other genes matter for sperm production, not for sex determination.

3. **The testis masculinises the body with two hormones acting on two duct systems.** Both Wolffian (mesonephric) and Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts are present in every embryo. Sertoli cells secrete Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH), which makes the Müllerian ducts regress. Leydig cells, driven first by placental human chorionic gonadotropin, secrete testosterone, which acts locally at high concentration to turn the Wolffian duct into epididymis, vas deferens and seminal vesicle; the action is paracrine, so a single testis masculinises only its own side. In the ovary's absence of both hormones the Wolffian ducts regress and the Müllerian ducts become tubes, uterus and upper vagina. Jost's fetal castration experiments (1940s) established this: remove the gonads and the ducts and genitalia develop female regardless of chromosomes.

4. **External genitalia need a stronger androgen, made locally.** The genital tubercle, urethral folds and labioscrotal swellings are identical in both sexes until week 8–9. In their cells 5α-reductase type 2 converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), which binds the androgen receptor more tightly ([receptors](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/receptors-and-ligands.md)) and drives tubercle growth into a penis, fold fusion into a penile urethra and swelling fusion into a scrotum, finished by about week 12. Without DHT the tubercle becomes the clitoris and the folds stay open as labia. Later, androgens and the gubernaculum guide testicular descent; about 3% of term boys are born with an undescended testis, most resolving within a year.

5. **Each link can fail on its own, and the failure pattern names the link.** A 46,XY embryo with a non-functioning androgen receptor (complete androgen insensitivity, X-linked) has testes and makes testosterone and AMH, so it has no uterus, yet its external genitalia are female because no androgen signal is received; it presents at puberty with normal breasts (from aromatised testosterone) and no menstruation. 5α-reductase deficiency leaves Wolffian structures intact but external genitalia female or ambiguous, and virilisation arrives at puberty when testosterone itself rises enough to act. A 46,XX fetus with 21-hydroxylase deficiency (congenital adrenal hyperplasia, about 1 in 15,000 births) has normal ovaries, uterus and tubes, because there is no testis to make AMH, but adrenal androgens virilise the external genitalia ([adrenal steroid synthesis](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md)). In each case the anatomy is read as a record of which hormones acted where.

6. **Sex chromosome aneuploidy affects the gonad's maintenance more than its determination.** In 47,XXY (Klinefelter syndrome) SRY is present, so a testis forms and the body is male; the extra X impairs the seminiferous tubules, giving small testes, infertility and low testosterone in adulthood. In 45,X (Turner syndrome) there is no SRY, so ovaries form and the body is female; but oocytes need two X chromosomes to survive, so the ovary degenerates into a streak before puberty and the person needs oestrogen replacement. Their frequencies and chromosomal mechanisms are in [cytogenetics](../01-inheritance/chromosomes-and-cytogenetics.md); gonadotropins are high in both because the gonad, not the pituitary, has failed ([gonadal disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/adrenal-pituitary-and-gonadal-disorders.md)).

7. **Differences of sex development are managed as a diagnosis first and a decision second.** Genital ambiguity at birth occurs in about 1 in 4,500 births; counting hypospadias and undescended testes the figure is far higher. The first task is to rule out salt-wasting adrenal hyperplasia, which kills within weeks, then to establish karyotype, gonad type and hormone pattern. Irreversible genital surgery in infancy is now widely deferred where it is not medically necessary, following the 2006 consensus that replaced "intersex" and "hermaphrodite" with descriptive terms and multidisciplinary care, because adult outcomes and the person's own identity could not be predicted from the anatomy ([paediatric genetics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/congenital-and-genetic-disease-in-childhood.md)).

8. **In medicine, sex is several variables and gender is another.** Chromosomal, gonadal, hormonal and anatomical sex usually align but can be dissociated at every step above; gender identity is a person's own sense of being male, female or neither and is not read from any of them. Clinical reasoning uses the specific variable that matters: an X chromosome count for X-linked disease, gonads for gonadal cancer risk, a uterus or prostate for screening, current sex steroid levels for reference ranges such as haemoglobin and creatinine, and recorded gender for how the person is addressed. Sex-linked differences in disease are real (autoimmune disease is commoner in people with two X chromosomes; X-linked recessive disease in people with one) and are mechanisms, not stereotypes; the same hormonal cascade reawakens at [puberty](growth-puberty-and-aging.md) to add secondary sex characteristics, and is the substrate of [reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bipotential gonad | Genital ridge able to become testis or ovary until SRY acts |
| SRY / SOX9 | Y-linked switch gene and the transcription factor it activates to make Sertoli cells |
| Wolffian / Müllerian ducts | Paired duct systems forming male internal genitalia (under testosterone) or female (by default) |
| Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) | Sertoli-cell hormone that causes Müllerian duct regression |
| Dihydrotestosterone (DHT) | Potent androgen made locally by 5α-reductase; required for external male genitalia |
| Androgen insensitivity | X-linked loss of androgen receptor function; 46,XY with female external anatomy and no uterus |
| Difference of sex development (DSD) | Any condition in which chromosomal, gonadal or anatomical sex is atypical or discordant |
| Sex / gender | Biological variables (chromosomal, gonadal, hormonal, anatomical) / a person's identity |

## How it fits

- **Below:** the Y and X and their aneuploidies are [cytogenetics](../01-inheritance/chromosomes-and-cytogenetics.md); the ridge, ducts and folds are [organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md); SRY is a switch of the kind described in [developmental genes](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md); androgen action is a nuclear [receptor](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/receptors-and-ligands.md).
- **Above:** the same axis runs [reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md); its failures present to [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/adrenal-pituitary-and-gonadal-disorders.md), [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/congenital-and-genetic-disease-in-childhood.md) and [gynaecology](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/gynaecological-disease.md); the ethics of consent in infancy belong to [medical ethics](../../15-medicine-in-society/README.md).
- **Siblings:** a worked case of induction and a genetic switch from [patterning](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md); the gonad and ducts are formed during [organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md); DSDs are the [malformations](congenital-malformations-and-teratogens.md) of this system, and some are hormonal rather than structural; the cascade resumes at [puberty](growth-puberty-and-aging.md).

## Common misconceptions

- **"The Y chromosome makes a male."** SRY makes a testis; the testis's hormones make the male body. A 46,XY person without androgen action has female external anatomy.
- **"Female is the default that needs nothing."** The female duct and genital pattern forms without testicular hormones, but the ovary itself is built by an active programme (WNT4, FOXL2) that also keeps the testis programme switched off.
- **"A karyotype tells you a person's sex."** It tells you one of four biological variables, which can disagree with the others, and nothing about gender identity.
