## Essence

Every embryo begins with the same bipotential gonad, two sets of ducts and one set of external folds. A single gene on the Y chromosome tips the gonad toward testis; the testis then makes two hormones, and those hormones, not the chromosomes, build the rest of the male body. Without them the female pattern forms. Sex is a chain, chromosomal → gonadal → hormonal → anatomical, and each link can fail on its own, producing people whose chromosomes, gonads and anatomy do not match. The one idea is **a genetic switch followed by a hormonal cascade**; it explains why differences of sex development fall into a few patterns and why sex in medicine is a set of variables rather than one fact.

## Core tenets

1. **The gonad is bipotential until a switch is thrown in week 6–7.** Intermediate mesoderm forms a genital ridge ([organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md)) colonised by germ cells from the yolk sac; its somatic cells can become Sertoli or granulosa cells. SRY, a transcription factor gene on the short arm of the Y, is expressed briefly and switches on SOX9, which commits the cells to Sertoli fate and organises testis cords. Without SRY, WNT4/β-catenin signalling and then FOXL2 drive the ovarian programme and suppress SOX9. The two programmes repress each other and must be maintained throughout life.

2. **SRY is necessary and sufficient for a testis.** A 46,XX person whose paternal X carries SRY, moved there by an X–Y crossover in the father's meiosis, develops testes; a 46,XY person with SRY deleted or mutated develops streak gonads and a female body (Swyer syndrome); a female mouse transgenic for *Sry* alone develops as a male (Koopman, 1991). The rest of the Y matters for sperm production, not for sex determination.

3. **The testis masculinises the ducts with two hormones.** Both Wolffian (mesonephric) and Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts exist in every embryo. Sertoli cells secrete Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH), which makes the Müllerian ducts regress. Leydig cells, driven first by placental human chorionic gonadotropin, secrete testosterone, which acts locally at high concentration to turn the Wolffian duct into epididymis, vas deferens and seminal vesicle; because the action is paracrine, one testis masculinises only its own side. With no testis, the Wolffian ducts regress and the Müllerian ducts become tubes, uterus and upper vagina. Jost's fetal castration experiments (1940s) showed this: remove the gonads and the ducts develop female whatever the chromosomes.

4. **External genitalia need a stronger androgen, made locally.** Genital tubercle, urethral folds and labioscrotal swellings are identical in both sexes until week 8–9. There, 5α-reductase type 2 converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), which binds the androgen receptor more tightly ([receptors](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/receptors-and-ligands.md)) and drives tubercle growth into a penis and fusion of the folds and swellings into urethra and scrotum by about week 12. Without DHT the tubercle becomes the clitoris and the folds stay open as labia. Androgens later guide testicular descent; about 3% of term boys have an undescended testis at birth, most descending within a year.

5. **Each link fails separately, and the anatomy records which hormones acted where.** A 46,XY embryo with a non-functioning androgen receptor (complete androgen insensitivity, X-linked) has testes, testosterone and AMH, so no uterus, but female external genitalia because no androgen signal is received; it presents at puberty with breasts (from aromatised testosterone) and no menstruation. 5α-reductase deficiency leaves Wolffian structures intact but external genitalia female or ambiguous, with virilisation at puberty when testosterone itself rises enough. A 46,XX fetus with 21-hydroxylase deficiency (congenital adrenal hyperplasia, about 1 in 15,000 births) has normal ovaries and uterus, since no testis made AMH, but adrenal androgens virilise the external genitalia ([adrenal steroid synthesis](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md)).

6. **Sex chromosome aneuploidy spares determination but not gonadal maintenance.** In 47,XXY (Klinefelter syndrome) SRY is present, a testis forms and the body is male, but the extra X impairs the seminiferous tubules, giving small testes, infertility and low adult testosterone. In 45,X (Turner syndrome) there is no SRY, ovaries form and the body is female, but oocytes need two X chromosomes to survive, so the ovary becomes a streak before puberty. Frequencies and mechanisms are in [cytogenetics](../01-inheritance/chromosomes-and-cytogenetics.md); gonadotropins are high in both because the gonad, not the pituitary, has failed ([gonadal disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/adrenal-pituitary-and-gonadal-disorders.md)).

7. **Differences of sex development are a diagnosis first and a decision second.** Genital ambiguity at birth occurs in about 1 in 4,500 births; counting hypospadias and undescended testes the figure is far higher. The first task is to exclude salt-wasting adrenal hyperplasia, which kills within weeks, then to establish karyotype, gonad type and hormone pattern. Irreversible genital surgery in infancy is now widely deferred where not medically necessary, following the 2006 consensus that replaced "intersex" with descriptive terms and multidisciplinary care, because adult identity and outcome cannot be predicted from infant anatomy ([paediatric genetics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/congenital-and-genetic-disease-in-childhood.md)).

8. **In medicine, sex is several variables and gender is another.** Chromosomal, gonadal, hormonal and anatomical sex usually align but can dissociate at every step above; gender identity is a person's own sense of being male, female or neither and is not read from any of them. Clinical reasoning uses the variable that matters: X chromosome count for X-linked disease, gonads for gonadal cancer risk, a uterus or prostate for screening, current sex steroids for reference ranges such as haemoglobin, and recorded gender for how the person is addressed. Sex-linked differences in disease (autoimmunity commoner with two X chromosomes, X-linked recessive disease with one) are mechanisms, not stereotypes. The same cascade reawakens at [puberty](growth-puberty-and-aging.md) and becomes [reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bipotential gonad | Genital ridge able to become testis or ovary until SRY acts |
| SRY / SOX9 | Y-linked switch gene and the transcription factor it activates to make Sertoli cells |
| Wolffian / Müllerian ducts | Paired ducts forming male internal genitalia (under testosterone) or female (without a testis) |
| Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) | Sertoli-cell hormone that causes Müllerian duct regression |
| Dihydrotestosterone (DHT) | Potent androgen made locally by 5α-reductase; required for external male genitalia |
| Androgen insensitivity | X-linked loss of androgen receptor function; 46,XY with female external anatomy and no uterus |
| Difference of sex development (DSD) | Any condition in which chromosomal, gonadal or anatomical sex is atypical or discordant |
| Sex / gender | Biological variables (chromosomal, gonadal, hormonal, anatomical) / a person's identity |

## How it fits

- **Below:** the sex chromosomes and their aneuploidies are [cytogenetics](../01-inheritance/chromosomes-and-cytogenetics.md); ridge, ducts and folds come from [organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md); SRY is a switch of the kind in [developmental genes](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md); androgen action is a nuclear [receptor](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/receptors-and-ligands.md).
- **Above:** the axis runs [reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md); its failures present to [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/adrenal-pituitary-and-gonadal-disorders.md), [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/congenital-and-genetic-disease-in-childhood.md) and [gynaecology](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/gynaecological-disease.md); consent for surgery in infancy belongs to [medical ethics](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/medical-ethics.md).
- **Siblings:** a worked case of the switches in [patterning](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md); DSDs are the [malformations](congenital-malformations-and-teratogens.md) of this system, some hormonal rather than structural; the cascade resumes at [puberty](growth-puberty-and-aging.md).

## Common misconceptions

- **"The Y chromosome makes a male."** SRY makes a testis; the testis's hormones make the male body. A 46,XY person without androgen action has female external anatomy.
- **"Female is the default that needs nothing."** The female duct pattern forms without testicular hormones, but the ovary is built by an active programme (WNT4, FOXL2) that also keeps the testis programme off.
- **"A karyotype tells you a person's sex."** It gives one of four biological variables, which can disagree with the others, and nothing about gender identity.
