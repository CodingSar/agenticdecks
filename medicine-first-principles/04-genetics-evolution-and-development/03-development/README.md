## What this area is about

This area answers how one cell becomes a body, and how that body then grows, matures and declines. It is the genome of the inheritance area being read, in order, by the cellular machinery of layer 03: differentiation, migration, adhesion, signalling and death, applied to sheets of cells over weeks. The unifying idea is **position becomes fate, and timing becomes form**: cells decide what to be from where they sit in gradients of a few reused signals, and an organ can only be built or broken during the window in which it is forming. Everything clinical follows from those two facts. Malformations cluster by pathway and date by window; tumours are named by the germ layer they came from; the dermatome map, the body cavities and the course of the great vessels are embryology frozen in the adult; and the schedule of growth, puberty and ageing is the organism's life history, set by selection and read on a growth chart or in a frailty score.

Read fertilisation and early embryogenesis, then germ layers and organogenesis, as a chronological pair. Morphogens and patterning can be read before or after them; it is the mechanism they both assume and is placed third so that the morphology is familiar when the signals are explained. Growth, puberty and ageing extends the timeline from week 9 to death and links back to the senescence and stem cell leaves of layer 03 and the trade-offs of the evolution area.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Inheritance](../01-inheritance/README.md):** the embryo reads the inherited genome; epigenetic reprogramming and imprinting are its first acts; chromosomal abnormalities are the leading cause of early loss and a major cause of malformation; single-gene disorders of patterning pathways produce the dysmorphic syndromes; heritability governs height and the timing of puberty.
- **[Evolution](../02-evolution/README.md):** the developmental programme is conserved across vertebrates, which validates animal models and explains the detours (pharyngeal arches, transient kidneys, the recurrent laryngeal nerve) that are historical constraints; the schedule of growth, reproduction and ageing is a life-history trade-off; the developmental origins of adult disease are mismatch between a fetal forecast and the environment that arrived.
