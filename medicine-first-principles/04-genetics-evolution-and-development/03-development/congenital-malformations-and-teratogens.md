## Essence

About 3% of live-born infants have a major structural abnormality, a leading cause of infant death in high-income countries. Causes are chromosomal (~10%), single-gene (~10–15%), teratogenic (~5–10%), and in roughly half of cases unknown or multifactorial. A teratogen is any agent that disturbs development; its effect depends on what it does, how much reaches the embryo, and above all when. The single idea is **the embryo's response to an insult is determined by its developmental stage more than by the nature of the insult**, which is why timing dominates every tenet below.

## Core tenets

1. **Timing determines outcome: the all-or-none period, the embryonic period and the fetal period.** In the first two weeks an insult usually kills the conceptus or is repaired by totipotent cells, so it rarely yields a malformed survivor. From week 3 to week 8, when organs form ([organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md)), the embryo is most vulnerable, and the organ affected is the one being built at the time. From week 9 to birth, exposure produces growth restriction and functional deficits (especially of the brain) but not gross malformation. Thalidomide produced limb defects only when taken between about days 20 and 36 after conception.

2. **Classification by mechanism tells you about recurrence and cause.** A malformation is intrinsically abnormal development (septal defect, cleft palate, spina bifida) and may be genetic or teratogenic. A deformation is mechanical (club foot from oligohydramnios) and often corrects after birth. A disruption is destruction of normal tissue (amniotic band amputation) with essentially no recurrence risk. A sequence is one primary anomaly producing a cascade; a syndrome is a recurring pattern with one cause.

3. **Malformations cluster because patterning pathways are shared, and the pattern names the cause.** One pathway builds several organs ([morphogens and developmental genes](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md)), so a lesion affects them together: heart, thymus, parathyroid and face in 22q11.2 deletion, all from the pharyngeal arches; the VACTERL association (vertebral, anal, cardiac, tracheo-oesophageal, renal, limb) because these form at the same time. A dysmorphologist reads the combination as a fingerprint of stage and pathway.

4. **Teratogens act through a small number of mechanisms.** Folate antagonism and failure of neural tube closure (valproate, methotrexate, maternal folate deficiency); disturbed retinoid signalling and Hox boundaries (isotretinoin); disrupted limb bud angiogenesis (thalidomide); blockade of the renin–angiotensin system in the fetal kidney (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors); neuronal apoptosis in the developing brain (alcohol); infection of fetal tissue (rubella, cytomegalovirus, Zika, toxoplasma, syphilis). The mechanism predicts both the window and the organ.

5. **Maternal disease and nutrition are teratogens too.** Poorly controlled maternal diabetes doubles or triples the malformation rate (heart, neural tube), probably through hyperglycaemia-induced oxidative stress; control before conception removes most of the excess. Iodine deficiency is the world's commonest preventable cause of intellectual disability. Folate before conception prevents most neural tube defects. Alcohol is the commonest preventable teratogen in high-income countries; fetal alcohol spectrum disorder affects on the order of 1% of births in some populations, mostly as cognitive impairment.

6. **Proving teratogenicity is hard, and the methods are those of causal inference.** Randomised trials are impossible; animal tests have poor species concordance (thalidomide was safe in rats); the 3% background rate means any widely used drug will be followed by malformations by chance; and the disease being treated (epilepsy, diabetes) is itself a risk ([causal inference](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/02-inference-and-causation/causal-inference.md)). Evidence comes from case series with a distinctive pattern (phocomelia is otherwise vanishingly rare), pregnancy registries, and cohort and case–control studies ([pharmacovigilance](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md)). Most drugs have never been adequately studied in pregnancy; absence of evidence is routinely misread as evidence of safety or harm.

7. **Dose and genotype modify susceptibility.** Teratogens show thresholds: below an exposure no effect is detectable, above it risk and severity rise with dose. Variation in mother, fetus and placenta explains why one exposed fetus is affected and another not. Most teratogens raise the absolute risk of a specific malformation from under 1% to a few percent, so even after a known exposure most infants are unaffected: the key message in counselling.

8. **Detection, counselling and prevention are the clinical applications.** Preconception care (folate, glucose control, medication review) prevents a share of malformations. First-trimester ultrasound and cell-free fetal Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) identify chromosomal risk; the anomaly scan at 18–22 weeks finds most major structural defects ([pregnancy and its complications](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md)). The newborn examination catches the rest ([the newborn](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/the-newborn-and-neonatology.md)). Recurrence risk depends on category: near zero for disruptions, ~3–5% for most isolated multifactorial malformations, Mendelian ratios for single-gene syndromes.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Malformation / deformation / disruption / dysplasia | Error of formation / mechanical distortion / destruction / abnormal tissue organisation |
| Sequence / syndrome / association | A cascade from one primary defect / a pattern with one cause / anomalies co-occurring more often than chance |
| Teratogen | Any agent that disturbs embryonic or fetal development |
| Critical period | The window in which an organ's formation can be disrupted |
| Recurrence risk | Probability of the anomaly in a subsequent pregnancy; depends on category |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A drug taken in pregnancy that was followed by a malformation caused it."** The background rate is 3%; causation needs a specific pattern, exposure in the window and controlled comparison.
- **"Teratogens are dangerous throughout pregnancy."** Most structural risk is confined to weeks 3–8; later exposure has different and usually lesser effects, though the brain stays vulnerable.
