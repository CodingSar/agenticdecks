## Essence

How does a cell in an embryo know where it is? A handful of secreted signalling molecules form concentration gradients across fields of cells, and cells read their position from the local concentration, switching on different transcription factors at different thresholds. The signals are morphogens; the transcription factors they control, above all the Hox genes, give each region its identity; and the same few pathways (Wnt, Hedgehog, Notch, Bone Morphogenetic Protein (BMP), Fibroblast Growth Factor (FGF), retinoic acid) are reused at every stage and in every organ. The one idea is **position encoded as concentration, decoded by thresholds, locked in by gene regulation**. Because the toolkit is small and reused, one disturbed pathway malforms several organs, and reactivation in adults is a recurring cause of cancer.

## Core tenets

1. **A morphogen gradient turns a source into a coordinate system.** A morphogen is secreted from a localised source, spreads by diffusion and is degraded or bound as it goes, so its concentration falls with distance ([diffusion](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/diffusion-osmosis-and-transport.md)). Cells express different genes above and below thresholds (Wolpert's French flag model, 1969), so one gradient specifies several cell types. In the neural tube, Sonic hedgehog from the notochord rises ventrally and BMPs dorsally; motor neurons form where hedgehog is high, sensory neurons where BMP is high.

2. **The three body axes are set by three gradient systems.** Head–tail: the primitive streak secretes Wnt, FGF and Nodal while the anterior endoderm secretes their inhibitors ([early embryogenesis](fertilization-and-early-embryogenesis.md)). Dorsal–ventral: BMP opposed by the organiser's inhibitors. Left–right: cilia at the node beat in one direction, sweep signalling molecules to the left and confine Nodal expression there, so the heart loops and the gut rotates accordingly. Defective ciliary motility randomises asymmetry, which is why half of people with primary ciliary dyskinesia have situs inversus ([cytoskeleton](../../03-cell-biology/01-cell-structure/the-cytoskeleton-and-cell-motility.md)).

3. **Hox genes give segments their identity, and their order on the chromosome matches their order in the body.** The 39 human Hox genes lie in four clusters, each expressed in order from head to tail (colinearity). A cell's Hox combination is a positional code: one code makes a somite a cervical vertebra, another a thoracic one with ribs. Retinoic acid sets Hox boundaries, which is why excess retinoids (isotretinoin) malform head, heart and thymus ([teratogens](congenital-malformations-and-teratogens.md)).

4. **The same few pathways are reused, which is why single mutations cause syndromes.** Sonic hedgehog patterns the neural tube, the limb, the face and the gut; its loss in the midline gives holoprosencephaly (one cerebral hemisphere, cyclopia at the extreme). FGF receptor gain-of-function mutations cause achondroplasia (growth plates) and craniosynostosis (skull sutures) because one receptor family controls both. A syndrome is a pathway's footprint.

5. **Patterning uses a small set of cell behaviours triggered by position.** Once a cell knows where it is it proliferates, migrates, changes shape, changes adhesion so it sorts from neighbours, or dies. Programmed death sculpts form: fingers separated by apoptosis of the interdigital webs, ducts hollowed, half the neurons made in the brain removed ([apoptosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/cell-death-apoptosis-necrosis-and-autophagy.md)). Lateral inhibition through Notch spaces cell types in a field ([cell communication](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/cell-to-cell-communication-and-junctions.md)).

6. **Induction and competence make development a sequence of conversations.** An inducing tissue changes a neighbour's fate only if the neighbour is competent, expressing the receptor and transcription factors to respond at that moment. Competence windows explain why one morphogen does different things in different places and times, and why an insult affects only the structures being induced then ([critical periods](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md)).

7. **The toolkit is kept and reused in the adult, in repair and in cancer.** Wnt maintains intestinal stem cells; hedgehog maintains hair follicles; wound healing reactivates embryonic migration and matrix programmes ([tissue repair](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)). Mutations that lock these pathways on are oncogenic: APC loss activating Wnt initiates most colorectal cancers, PTCH1 loss activating hedgehog drives basal cell carcinoma ([skin cancer](../../12-organ-system-specialties/11-dermatology/skin-cancer-and-skin-infections.md)) and medulloblastoma, Notch mutations drive T-cell leukaemia. [The hallmarks of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/hallmarks-of-cancer.md) are embryonic behaviours in the wrong place and time.

8. **Conservation of the toolkit makes evolution a matter of regulation and model organisms informative.** Homeobox genes were found in flies and then in every animal with the same order and function; fly *eyeless* and human PAX6 are interchangeable. Species differ mainly in when and where shared genes are expressed. This is why a knockout mouse predicts the human phenotype and why the body plan is as conserved as [organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md) shows.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Morphogen | Secreted molecule whose concentration gradient specifies cell fates by position |
| French flag model | Fates assigned by concentration thresholds along a gradient |
| Hox genes | Clustered homeobox transcription factors giving head-to-tail segmental identity |
| Colinearity | Correspondence between a Hox gene's position in the cluster and its expression boundary in the body |
| Induction / competence | A signal changing a neighbour's fate / the neighbour's ability to respond at that time |
| Lateral inhibition | Notch-mediated suppression of the same fate in adjacent cells |
| Positional information | A cell's knowledge of its location, encoded in morphogen levels and transcription factors |
| Regulatory evolution | Change in species form by altering expression of conserved genes |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Each organ has its own genes."** A dozen pathways and a few hundred transcription factors build everything; identity comes from combinations, timing and place.
- **"Developmental genes switch off at birth."** They maintain adult stem cells, run wound repair and are the commonest oncogenic targets.
