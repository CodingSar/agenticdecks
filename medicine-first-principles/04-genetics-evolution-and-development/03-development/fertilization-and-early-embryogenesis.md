## Essence

A human begins as one cell formed when a sperm fuses with an egg, restoring the diploid number and triggering a programme that in two weeks turns that cell into a hollow ball, implants it in the uterine wall, sets aside the cells that will become the placenta from those that will become the body, and lays down the first axis. Nothing in this fortnight looks like a person, yet the most important decisions of development are made here: which cells are embryo and which are support, which end is head, and whether the pregnancy continues at all. Roughly half of all conceptions are lost, most before the woman knows she is pregnant, and most of those for chromosomal reasons. The single idea is **symmetry broken by position**: identical cells become different because of where they sit, inside or outside, and each decision is locked in by the epigenetic machinery of the previous area. Fertility treatment, contraception, early pregnancy loss, ectopic pregnancy and twinning all take their logic from these two weeks.

## Core tenets

1. **Fertilisation is a species-specific recognition event followed by a block to further sperm.** Of ~10^8 sperm ejaculated, a few hundred reach the oviduct, where the egg waits arrested in metaphase of meiosis II ([meiosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/mitosis-meiosis-and-chromosome-segregation.md)). Sperm acquire the ability to fertilise (capacitation) only after hours in the female tract, bind the zona pellucida, release enzymes that digest a path, and fuse with the egg membrane via the receptor pair Izumo1–Juno. Fusion triggers calcium oscillations that release cortical granules, hardening the zona so no second sperm enters (polyspermy produces triploidy and is lethal), and that complete meiosis II, expelling the second polar body. The two haploid nuclei replicate their Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) and the first mitosis mixes the genomes.

2. **Cleavage divides the zygote without growth, and the early cells are totipotent.** The zygote divides every ~12–24 hours into blastomeres of halving size within the unchanged zona; genome activation (switching from stored maternal messenger RNA to the embryo's own transcription) occurs at the 4- to 8-cell stage. Up to about the 8-cell stage each blastomere can form a whole embryo: this is why a single cell can be removed for preimplantation genetic testing without harm and why monozygotic twins arise when the early embryo splits. The timing of the split determines the twins' arrangement: before day 3 gives separate placentas and sacs; days 4–8, a shared placenta; after day 8, a shared sac and the hazard of cord entanglement; after day 13, conjoined twins.

3. **The first fate decision is inside versus outside.** At the 16- to 32-cell stage the ball compacts; cells on the outside become polarised, express the transcription factor Cdx2 and become trophectoderm, the future placenta; cells on the inside express Oct4 and Nanog and become the inner cell mass, the future embryo. Position decides fate through the Hippo signalling pathway, which is active in outer cells with a free surface and silenced in enclosed ones ([cell communication](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/cell-to-cell-communication-and-junctions.md)). The resulting blastocyst, a fluid-filled sphere of ~100–200 cells with the inner cell mass at one pole, hatches from the zona at day 5–6. The inner cell mass is the source of embryonic stem cells, which are pluripotent but no longer totipotent, since they cannot make trophectoderm ([stem cells](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/stem-cells-differentiation-and-regeneration.md)).

4. **Implantation is an invasion controlled by both sides and timed to a window.** The blastocyst attaches to the endometrium around day 6–7, during a receptive window of a few days set by progesterone from the corpus luteum. Trophoblast cells fuse into a multinucleate syncytiotrophoblast that erodes the uterine epithelium and secretes human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), the signal that rescues the corpus luteum, sustains progesterone and so prevents menstruation; hCG in urine or blood is the pregnancy test. Invasive cytotrophoblast later remodels the maternal spiral arteries into wide, low-resistance vessels; inadequate remodelling is the root of pre-eclampsia and fetal growth restriction. Implantation outside the uterus, almost always in the fallopian tube, is ectopic pregnancy, which ruptures and bleeds because the tube cannot accommodate invasion ([pregnancy and its complications](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md)).

5. **Epigenetic reprogramming makes the new genome a blank slate, then the embryo and the placenta write different programmes.** Within the first days most methylation inherited from sperm and egg is erased, except at imprinted loci ([epigenetic inheritance](../01-inheritance/epigenetic-inheritance-and-imprinting.md)), and in female embryos one X chromosome is inactivated. Imprinting is why the two parental genomes are not interchangeable: an embryo with two maternal genomes forms a fetus with no placenta, two paternal genomes form a placenta (hydatidiform mole) with no fetus. The trophoblast lineage keeps lower methylation than the embryo throughout, and errors of reprogramming at this stage are the suspected mechanism behind the slight excess of imprinting disorders after assisted reproduction.

6. **The second week forms two layers and the first axis.** The inner cell mass splits into epiblast (which will form the entire body) and hypoblast (which forms the yolk sac and contributes to extraembryonic membranes). The amniotic cavity opens above the epiblast. At the end of the second week a patch of hypoblast thickens at one end to form the anterior visceral endoderm, the first marker of where the head will be; at the beginning of the third week the primitive streak appears at the opposite end and gastrulation begins ([germ layers](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md)). The embryo at day 14 is a two-layered disc ~0.2 mm across. The appearance of the primitive streak is the point beyond which twinning is no longer possible and the legal limit for embryo research in many jurisdictions.

7. **Early loss is common, mostly chromosomal, and is the clinical face of this leaf.** About 30% of fertilised eggs fail to implant, and ~10–20% of recognised pregnancies miscarry, most in the first trimester; across all conceptions the loss is probably 50% or more. Over half of first-trimester losses are aneuploid (trisomies, 45,X, triploidy), with maternal age the dominant risk factor because of the long meiotic arrest described in [meiosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/mitosis-meiosis-and-chromosome-segregation.md). Early loss is a filter removing embryos incompatible with life, which is why the live-birth incidence of aneuploidy (~0.3%) is so much lower than the conception incidence.

8. **Each step is a target for assisted reproduction and for contraception.** In vitro fertilisation (first birth 1978) replaces the oviduct for fertilisation and cleavage, cultures the embryo to the blastocyst and transfers it during the implantation window; intracytoplasmic sperm injection bypasses sperm–zona binding; preimplantation genetic testing biopsies trophectoderm cells. About 2–8% of births in high-income countries (2020s) follow assisted conception. Hormonal contraception prevents ovulation or thickens cervical mucus; the copper intrauterine device is toxic to sperm and inflames the endometrium so implantation fails; emergency contraception delays ovulation. A progesterone receptor antagonist (mifepristone) ends the corpus luteum's support of an implanted pregnancy ([fertility and contraception](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capacitation | Changes in sperm in the female tract that enable fertilisation |
| Zona pellucida / cortical reaction | Glycoprotein coat of the egg / its hardening after fertilisation to block polyspermy |
| Cleavage / blastomere | Mitotic divisions without growth / the resulting cells |
| Totipotent / pluripotent | Able to form embryo and placenta / embryo only |
| Compaction and the inner–outer decision | First fate choice: trophectoderm outside, inner cell mass inside |
| Blastocyst | Hollow ball of trophectoderm with an inner cell mass; the implanting stage |
| Trophoblast (cyto-, syncytio-) | Placental lineage; the syncytium invades and secretes hCG |
| Implantation window | Few days when the endometrium accepts a blastocyst |
| Epiblast / hypoblast | Two layers of the second-week disc; body versus yolk sac |
| Primitive streak | Structure marking the start of gastrulation and the end of possible twinning |

- **Below:** the egg's meiotic arrest and completion are [meiosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/mitosis-meiosis-and-chromosome-segregation.md); totipotency and pluripotency are [stem cell](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/stem-cells-differentiation-and-regeneration.md) concepts; the reprogramming and imprinting are [epigenetic inheritance](../01-inheritance/epigenetic-inheritance-and-imprinting.md); the inside–outside decision is positional [signalling](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/cell-to-cell-communication-and-junctions.md).

## Common misconceptions

- **"Pregnancy begins when the egg is fertilised."** Clinically it is dated from the last menstrual period (about two weeks before fertilisation), and the pregnancy is not established until implantation; a third of fertilised eggs never implant.
- **"The first cell divisions make the embryo grow."** Cleavage subdivides a fixed volume; the embryo is no larger at the blastocyst stage than the egg was.
- **"Identical twins run in families."** Monozygotic twinning is a random splitting event at a near-constant rate (~1 in 250 births) worldwide; it is dizygotic twinning that is heritable and varies with maternal age and ancestry.
