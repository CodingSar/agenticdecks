## Essence

A human begins as one cell formed when a sperm fuses with an egg; in two weeks that cell becomes a hollow ball, implants, separates placenta from body, and lays down the first axis. The most important decisions of development are made here: which cells are embryo and which are support, which end is head, and whether the pregnancy continues at all. Roughly half of conceptions are lost, mostly for chromosomal reasons. The single idea is **symmetry broken by position**: identical cells become different because of where they sit, and each decision is locked in by the epigenetic machinery of the previous area.

## Core tenets

1. **Fertilisation is a recognition event followed by a block to further sperm.** Of ~10^8 sperm ejaculated, a few hundred reach the oviduct, where the egg waits arrested in metaphase of meiosis II ([meiosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/mitosis-meiosis-and-chromosome-segregation.md)); one binds the zona pellucida, digests a path and fuses with the egg membrane. Fusion triggers calcium oscillations that harden the zona so no second sperm enters (polyspermy gives lethal triploidy) and complete meiosis II.

2. **Cleavage divides the zygote without growth, and the early cells are totipotent.** The zygote divides every ~12–24 hours into blastomeres of halving size inside the unchanged zona. Up to about the 8-cell stage each blastomere can form a whole embryo, which is why one cell can be removed for genetic testing and why monozygotic twins arise when the embryo splits. A split before day 3 gives separate placentas, days 4–8 a shared placenta, after day 8 a shared sac, after day 13 conjoined twins.

3. **The first fate decision is inside versus outside.** At the 16- to 32-cell stage the ball compacts; outer cells with a free surface express Cdx2 and become trophectoderm, the future placenta; enclosed cells express Oct4 and Nanog and become the inner cell mass, the future embryo. Position decides fate through the Hippo pathway, active only in cells with a free surface ([cell communication](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/cell-to-cell-communication-and-junctions.md)). The blastocyst hatches at day 5–6; its inner cell mass is the source of embryonic stem cells, pluripotent but no longer totipotent ([stem cells](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/stem-cells-differentiation-and-regeneration.md)).

4. **Implantation is an invasion controlled by both sides and timed to a window.** The blastocyst attaches around day 6–7 during a receptive window set by progesterone. Trophoblast fuses into a syncytium that erodes the uterine epithelium and secretes human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), which rescues the corpus luteum and prevents menstruation; hCG is the pregnancy test. Trophoblast later remodels the maternal spiral arteries; inadequate remodelling is the root of pre-eclampsia. Implantation in the fallopian tube is ectopic pregnancy, which ruptures because the tube cannot accommodate invasion ([pregnancy and its complications](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md)).

5. **Epigenetic reprogramming makes the new genome a blank slate, then embryo and placenta write different programmes.** Within days most methylation inherited from sperm and egg is erased, except at imprinted loci ([epigenetic inheritance](../01-inheritance/epigenetic-inheritance-and-imprinting.md)). Imprinting is why the two parental genomes are not interchangeable: two maternal genomes form a fetus with no placenta, two paternal genomes a placenta with no fetus (hydatidiform mole).

6. **The second week forms two layers and the first axis.** The inner cell mass splits into epiblast (the entire future body) and hypoblast (yolk sac); a patch of hypoblast thickens to mark the future head, and at the start of the third week the primitive streak appears at the opposite end and gastrulation begins ([germ layers](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md)). The primitive streak marks the point beyond which twinning is impossible and the legal limit for embryo research in many jurisdictions.

7. **Early loss is common, mostly chromosomal, and is the clinical face of this leaf.** About 30% of fertilised eggs fail to implant and ~10–20% of recognised pregnancies miscarry, most in the first trimester. Over half of first-trimester losses are aneuploid, with maternal age the dominant risk factor because of the long meiotic arrest. Early loss filters out embryos incompatible with life.

8. **Each step is a target for assisted reproduction and contraception.** In vitro fertilisation (first birth 1978) replaces the oviduct and transfers a blastocyst during the implantation window; about 2–8% of births in high-income countries (2020s) follow assisted conception. Hormonal contraception prevents ovulation; the copper intrauterine device is toxic to sperm and prevents implantation; a progesterone receptor antagonist (mifepristone) ends the corpus luteum's support of an implanted pregnancy ([fertility and contraception](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zona pellucida / cortical reaction | Glycoprotein coat of the egg / its hardening after fertilisation to block polyspermy |
| Cleavage / blastomere | Mitotic divisions without growth / the resulting cells |
| Totipotent / pluripotent | Able to form embryo and placenta / embryo only |
| Blastocyst | Hollow ball of trophectoderm with an inner cell mass; the implanting stage |
| Trophoblast | Placental lineage; its syncytium invades and secretes hCG |
| Implantation window | Few days when the endometrium accepts a blastocyst |
| Primitive streak | Structure marking the start of gastrulation and the end of possible twinning |

## How it fits

- **Below:** the egg's arrest is [meiosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/mitosis-meiosis-and-chromosome-segregation.md); totipotency is a [stem cell](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/stem-cells-differentiation-and-regeneration.md) concept; reprogramming is [epigenetic inheritance](../01-inheritance/epigenetic-inheritance-and-imprinting.md); the inside–outside decision is positional [signalling](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/cell-to-cell-communication-and-junctions.md).
- **Above:** the trophoblast becomes the [placenta](../../06-physiology/09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/pregnancy-placenta-and-lactation.md); implantation failure, ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage and pre-eclampsia open [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md); every step is a lever for [fertility treatment and contraception](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md).
- **Siblings:** this leaf ends where [organogenesis](germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md) begins; the axis-setting signals are in [morphogens and patterning](morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md); chromosomal loss is the earliest [congenital abnormality](congenital-malformations-and-teratogens.md).

## Common misconceptions

- **"Pregnancy begins at fertilisation."** Clinically it is dated from the last menstrual period, and it is not established until implantation; a third of fertilised eggs never implant.
- **"Identical twins run in families."** Monozygotic twinning is a random splitting event at a near-constant rate (~1 in 250 births); dizygotic twinning is the heritable kind.
