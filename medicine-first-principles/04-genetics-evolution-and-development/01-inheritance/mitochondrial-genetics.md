## Essence

Every cell carries a second genome. Mitochondrial Deoxyribonucleic Acid (mtDNA) is a circle of ~16.6 kilobases with 37 genes, present in hundreds to thousands of copies per cell and inherited only from the egg. Those facts break the rules of the previous leaves: no paternal allele, no segregation, no diploid dosage. A cell carries a *population* of genomes. The single idea is **inheritance of a population, not a pair**: a mother passes each child a sample of her mitochondrial genomes, each tissue inherits a sample of each cell's, and phenotype follows the mutant load against a threshold.

## Core tenets

1. **mtDNA encodes a small, essential part of the respiratory chain; the rest comes from the nucleus.** The 37 genes are 13 subunits of the oxidative phosphorylation complexes, 22 transfer Ribonucleic Acids (tRNAs) and 2 ribosomal RNAs ([oxidative phosphorylation](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/glycolysis-tca-cycle-and-oxidative-phosphorylation.md)). The other ~1,500 mitochondrial proteins, including those that copy and repair mtDNA, are nuclear and follow [Mendelian rules](mendelian-inheritance-and-pedigrees.md). "Mitochondrial disease" therefore has two genetics: mtDNA mutations with maternal inheritance, and nuclear mutations, mostly autosomal recessive, presenting identically. The pedigree decides which.

2. **Inheritance is strictly maternal.** The egg contributes ~10^5 mitochondrial genomes; the sperm's few hundred are destroyed after fertilisation. A mother with an mtDNA mutation can transmit it to every child of either sex; an affected father transmits it to none. Transmission only through women, with no carrier daughters of affected men, distinguishes it from X-linked inheritance.

3. **Heteroplasmy and threshold decide whether a mutation causes disease.** Most pathogenic variants coexist with normal genomes in the same cell (heteroplasmy). Function fails only when the mutant fraction passes a threshold, typically 60–90%, because normal genomes complement the mutant until too few remain. The threshold is lowest in tissues with the highest Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) demand and least capacity to divide (brain, retina, cochlea, heart, skeletal muscle, endocrine pancreas), which is why the diseases are neuromuscular, visual, auditory, cardiac and metabolic.

4. **Mitotic segregation makes heteroplasmy drift, so phenotype varies by tissue and with time.** Mitochondria are shared out at random at each division, so daughter cells inherit different mutant fractions ([probability](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/01-probability-and-measurement/probability.md)). In blood the load of the common m.3243A>G variant falls with age as mutant-rich stem cells are selected against; in non-dividing muscle fibres and neurons mutant genomes expand clonally and the load rises. So the disease progresses, and a blood test can understate it.

5. **A germline bottleneck resets the sample each generation.** During oogenesis mtDNA copy number falls to a few hundred molecules per primordial germ cell before expanding again, so each egg's mutant fraction is a small random sample of the mother's. One woman's eggs span a wide range, and recurrence risk is not a Mendelian fraction but a distribution around her load, with the most damaging variants also removed by germline selection. Hence siblings range from unaffected to severely ill, and a mutation can vanish or become fixed within a few generations.

6. **mtDNA mutates fast and does not recombine, so it records history.** Without histones, beside the main source of reactive oxygen species and repaired less efficiently, mtDNA accumulates substitutions roughly 10–20 times faster per base than nuclear DNA ([mutation](genomic-variation-and-mutation.md)). Passed unmixed down the maternal line, it is a molecular clock whose haplogroups support the recent African origin of [modern humans](../02-evolution/human-evolution-and-variation.md). Somatically, deletions accumulate with age in muscle and substantia nigra neurons, one hallmark of [ageing](../03-development/growth-puberty-and-aging.md).

7. **The phenotypes are syndromes of energy failure, and a few variants recur.** m.3243A>G in a tRNA gene causes MELAS (Mitochondrial Encephalopathy, Lactic Acidosis and Stroke-like episodes) at high load and maternally inherited diabetes with deafness at lower load. Leber hereditary optic neuropathy is usually homoplasmic with incomplete penetrance; single large deletions (Kearns–Sayre syndrome) are usually sporadic. Lactate rises because electrons cannot reach oxygen; muscle shows ragged red fibres of proliferating mitochondria. Clinically affected mitochondrial disease of either origin affects about 1 in 5,000 people (high-income countries, 2010s); pathogenic mtDNA variants are carried by roughly 1 in 200 ([single-gene disorders](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/single-gene-and-chromosomal-disorders.md)).

8. **The only way to prevent transmission is to replace the population.** No treatment restores a mutant respiratory chain; care is supportive, and some drugs are avoided (aminoglycosides cause deafness in carriers of m.1555A>G). Preimplantation testing can select low-load embryos but cannot promise safety, because load drifts. Mitochondrial replacement moves the parents' nuclear genome into a donor egg stripped of its nucleus, so the child inherits donor mtDNA; it was legalised in the United Kingdom in 2015 and the first births there were reported in 2023. A small carry-over of maternal mtDNA can in principle expand ([fertility treatment](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| mtDNA | The 16.6-kilobase circular mitochondrial genome: 13 proteins, 22 tRNAs, 2 ribosomal RNAs |
| Maternal inheritance | Transmission from mother to all children of either sex; never from father |
| Heteroplasmy / homoplasmy | Mixture of mutant and normal mtDNA in a cell / a single type only |
| Threshold effect | Mutant fraction above which respiratory function fails; lowest in high-demand tissues |
| Germline bottleneck | Fall in mtDNA copies per germ cell that makes each egg a small sample of the mother's population |
| Mitochondrial replacement | Transfer of the parental nuclear genome into an enucleated donor egg |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Mitochondrial disease is inherited from the mother."** Only mtDNA disease is; most mitochondrial disorders in children are nuclear and autosomal recessive, from both parents.
- **"The mutation load in a blood test is the patient's."** Heteroplasmy differs by tissue and changes with age; blood can be nearly clear while muscle and brain are heavily affected.
