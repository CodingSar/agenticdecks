## Essence

The human genome is packaged into 46 chromosomes, 22 autosomal pairs and two sex chromosomes, ~3.1 billion base pairs in all, each chromosome a single Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) molecule wound around histones. Cytogenetics studies chromosomes as visible objects: their number, their structure and the large rearrangements a microscope or an array can see. The organising idea is **dosage**: most genes are expressed in proportion to copy number, so one chromosome or segment too many or too few disrupts hundreds of genes at once. Chromosomal abnormalities are found in roughly half of early miscarriages, in ~0.5–1% of live births, and in most cancers.

## Core tenets

1. **A chromosome is one DNA molecule folded hierarchically, and the folding is regulatory.** DNA wraps around histone octamers, the beads coil into fibres, the fibres loop onto a scaffold, and at mitosis the loops condense ([nucleic acids](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/01-macromolecules/nucleic-acids.md); [chromatin](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/03-genetic-information-flow/gene-regulation-and-epigenetic-mechanisms.md)). A gene moved next to a strong enhancer by a rearrangement is overexpressed, one way translocations cause cancer.

2. **The karyotype is read by number, then by structure.** Normal is 46,XX or 46,XY. Numerical abnormalities are whole chromosomes gained or lost (aneuploidy from [nondisjunction](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/mitosis-meiosis-and-chromosome-segregation.md)) or whole sets (triploidy). Structural abnormalities are deletions, duplications, inversions and translocations, from breaks repaired to the wrong partner. A balanced rearrangement leaves its carrier healthy but produces unbalanced gametes; an unbalanced one changes dosage.

3. **Autosomal aneuploidy survives only when the chromosome is gene-poor, and the phenotype is dosage-wide.** Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome, ~1 in 700 births, rising with maternal age) is viable because chromosome 21 is the smallest, with ~230 protein-coding genes; trisomies 18 and 13 are usually fatal within the first year; all other autosomal trisomies and monosomies are lost before birth. Down syndrome's features (intellectual disability, heart defects in ~40–50%, early Alzheimer pathology from three copies of the amyloid precursor protein gene) are the summed effect of 1.5-fold expression across the chromosome.

4. **Sex chromosome dosage is buffered by X inactivation, so its abnormalities are mild.** Early in female development each cell randomly silences one X by coating it in the long non-coding Ribonucleic Acid (RNA) XIST; the choice is inherited by all descendants, so every female is a mosaic of two cell populations ([epigenetic inheritance](epigenetic-inheritance-and-imprinting.md)). Extra X chromosomes are inactivated and the Y carries few genes, so 47,XXY (Klinefelter syndrome, ~1 in 600 males) and 45,X (Turner syndrome, ~1 in 2,500 females) permit near-normal life. One X is not quite enough: genes escaping inactivation (SHOX, for height) are haploinsufficient in Turner syndrome, and ~99% of 45,X conceptions are lost.

5. **Structural rearrangements cause disease by breaking genes, changing segment dosage or fusing genes.** The 22q11.2 deletion (~1 in 4,000) removes ~40 genes and gives heart defects, thymic hypoplasia, hypocalcaemia and psychiatric disease. A Robertsonian translocation fuses two acrocentric chromosomes; the carrier has 45 chromosomes and is healthy, but a 14;21 carrier can have a child with translocation Down syndrome, the inherited ~4% of cases, with recurrence risk up to 10–15% from a carrier mother.

6. **Acquired chromosomal changes define and drive cancer.** The Philadelphia chromosome, t(9;22), fuses BCR to ABL and makes a constitutively active kinase: it defines chronic myeloid leukaemia, and the kinase inhibitor imatinib (2001) turned a fatal disease into a chronic one. t(8;14) in Burkitt lymphoma puts MYC next to the immunoglobulin enhancer; t(15;17) in acute promyelocytic leukaemia fuses the retinoic acid receptor and is treated with the receptor's ligand ([haematological malignancies](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/haematological-malignancies.md)). Cancer cytogenetics is a diagnostic test that is also a mechanism.

7. **Resolution determines what a test can see.** A banded karyotype resolves ~5–10 million base pairs and finds aneuploidy and balanced rearrangements. Chromosomal microarray finds copy-number variants down to ~50–100 kilobases genome-wide but misses balanced rearrangements, and has replaced the karyotype as the first test for developmental delay. Sequencing finds structural variants at base-pair resolution ([molecular techniques](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/03-genetic-information-flow/molecular-techniques.md)). Non-invasive prenatal testing infers trisomy from a small excess of chromosome 21 fragments in maternal plasma; it is a screen (~99% sensitivity for trisomy 21), not a diagnosis.

8. **Mosaicism means one tissue's karyotype may not be the person's.** A post-fertilisation error produces cell lines whose proportions differ by tissue. Mosaic trisomies are milder, and confined placental mosaicism gives false positives on prenatal tests with a normal fetus.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Karyotype | The ordered display of a cell's chromosomes; written as count, sex chromosomes, abnormalities |
| Aneuploidy / polyploidy | Gain or loss of individual chromosomes / of whole sets |
| Balanced / unbalanced rearrangement | Structural change with no net change in DNA content / with gain or loss |
| Robertsonian translocation | Fusion of two acrocentric chromosomes at the centromere |
| Copy-number variant | Deletion or duplication of a segment, from megabases down to tens of kilobases |
| X inactivation | Random silencing of one X in each female cell; dosage compensation |
| Mosaicism | Two or more cell lines of different karyotype in one person |
| Chromosomal microarray | Genome-wide copy-number detection at sub-microscopic resolution |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Chromosomal disorders are inherited."** Most are new events in a parent's meiosis or after fertilisation; only translocation carriers transmit a predictable risk.
- **"A normal karyotype excludes a chromosomal cause."** It excludes changes above ~5 million base pairs; microdeletions need an array or sequencing.
