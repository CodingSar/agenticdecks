## Essence

Modern humans arose in Africa within the last ~300,000 years, left it in small numbers ~60,000–70,000 years ago and spread across the planet, picking up a few percent of archaic ancestry on the way. Humans are genetically homogeneous: any two people differ at ~0.1% of positions, most variation is found within every population, and differences between continental groups are gradients. The variation that does differ in frequency was shaped mostly by infection, diet, climate and chance, and much of it matters clinically: malaria resistance alleles, lactase persistence, drug-metabolising variants. Ancestry is a crude proxy for genotype, and race a social category that correlates imperfectly with it. The one idea is **recent African origin, serial founder effects and local adaptation**.

## Core tenets

1. **Human genetic diversity is low and greatest in Africa.** The effective population size through most of human history was ~10,000, and non-African populations descend from a subset of Africans, so each step out of Africa lost diversity ([drift and founder effects](natural-selection-and-population-genetics.md)). Reference databases and association studies built on European-ancestry samples therefore miss African variation, so variants of uncertain significance and false pathogenic calls are more frequent in people of African ancestry, and polygenic scores transfer poorly ([complex traits](../01-inheritance/complex-traits-and-heritability.md)).

2. **Most variation is within populations; between-population differences are clinal.** About 85–90% of human genetic variance lies between individuals within any one population and only ~10–15% between continental groups (Lewontin, 1972, confirmed genome-wide). Allele frequencies change gradually with geography; clusters appear when sampling is sparse. Ancestry predicts a single genotype only weakly, and a test for the variant always beats inferring it from appearance.

3. **Infection, especially malaria, has been the strongest selective force on recent human genomes.** Haemoglobin S, C and E, the thalassaemias, glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency and the Duffy-null blood group each reach high frequency where *Plasmodium* has been endemic, each by heterozygote advantage and each at the cost of a recessive or X-linked disease ([anaemias](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/anaemias.md); [haemoglobin](../../06-physiology/08-blood/red-cells-haemoglobin-and-iron.md)). These make ancestry clinically informative for haemoglobin disorders and for haemolysis risk with oxidant drugs such as primaquine.

4. **Diet and climate left further signatures.** Lactase persistence arose independently in European and several African pastoralist populations in the last ~10,000 years and is the strongest selective sweep known in humans; lactose intolerance is the ancestral majority state, not a disease. Skin pigmentation tracks ultraviolet exposure as a trade-off between folate protection and vitamin D synthesis. Each adaptation becomes a vulnerability when the environment changes ([evolutionary medicine](evolutionary-medicine.md)).

5. **Archaic admixture contributed functional variants.** Non-Africans carry ~1–2% Neanderthal ancestry and Oceanians up to ~5% Denisovan. Some introgressed segments were favoured (immune genes such as the Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) loci; the Tibetan EPAS1 allele is Denisovan in origin), and a Neanderthal haplotype on chromosome 3 roughly doubled the risk of severe COVID-19 (reported 2020). Most genomes are mosaics of several ancestral populations.

6. **Pharmacogenetic variation differs in frequency between populations and matters for prescribing.** CYP2D6 poor metabolisers are ~5–10% of Europeans and under 2% of East Asians; CYP2C19 loss-of-function alleles that blunt clopidogrel activation reach ~30–50% in East Asia; HLA-B*15:02, which predicts carbamazepine-induced Stevens–Johnson syndrome, is found mainly in South and South-East Asian populations and is screened for there ([pharmacogenomics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/pharmacogenomics-and-individual-variation.md)). Ancestry shifts the prior probability of a genotype, but the genotype test should guide the dose.

7. **Reference ranges and risk equations that include race need to be examined for what the variable captures.** Creatinine-based estimates of kidney function used a race coefficient until 2021 that overestimated function in Black patients and delayed referral; it was removed in the United States when shown to encode an unsupported biological claim. Where a difference is real and genetic (haemoglobin disorders, G6PD, pharmacogenes), test for the variant; where it reflects environment, access or discrimination, race is a proxy for a social exposure ([reference ranges](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/clinical-chemistry-and-reference-ranges.md)). Most outcome disparities between racial groups are explained by social and economic factors, not allele frequencies.

8. **The body shows its evolutionary history in its anatomy and vulnerabilities.** Bipedalism remodelled pelvis, spine, knee and foot and left the lower back, knee ligaments and childbirth as weak points; a large brain and narrow pelvis produced a helpless infant and a long childhood; processed food produced the small jaw and crowded teeth. These are the historical constraints of [evolutionary medicine](evolutionary-medicine.md) made specific to our species, and they frame the anatomy of [layer 05](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/README.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recent African origin | Emergence of modern humans in Africa and dispersal within the last ~70,000 years |
| Serial founder effect | Progressive loss of diversity as small groups colonise new territory |
| Cline | Gradual change in allele frequency across geography |
| Apportionment of diversity | The finding that most human genetic variance lies within, not between, populations |
| Heterozygote advantage | Carrier fitness exceeding both homozygotes; maintains malaria-resistance alleles |
| Archaic admixture | Neanderthal and Denisovan ancestry in modern genomes |
| Ancestry versus race | Genetic descent, continuous and measurable / a social classification correlated with it |
| Pharmacogene | Gene whose variants alter drug handling, often with population-specific frequencies |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Races are genetically distinct groups."** Human variation is clinal and mostly within populations; statistical clusters depend on sampling and do not match the social categories in records.
- **"Ancestry tells you a patient's genotype."** It shifts the probability; for any variant that changes treatment, testing is cheaper than being wrong.
