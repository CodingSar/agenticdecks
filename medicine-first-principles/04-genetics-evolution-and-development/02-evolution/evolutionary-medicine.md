## Essence

Most of medicine asks *how* a disease happens. Evolutionary medicine asks *why* the body is built so that it can fail that way. Selection does not produce health; it produces reproductive success in past environments, with whatever variation and history it has. Six reasons for vulnerability follow: mismatch between past and present environments; trade-offs; the declining force of selection with age; constraints of history; arms races with pathogens that evolve faster than we do; and defences (fever, pain, anxiety) that are adaptive but often mistaken for the disease. The one idea is that **the body is not designed but descended**, and much pathology is the shadow of adaptation.

## Core tenets

1. **Mismatch: a body tuned for scarcity meets abundance.** For almost all of human history food was intermittent, salt scarce, effort unavoidable and infection constant, so genes favouring eating when food was available, conserving salt and storing fat were advantageous ([fed–fasted metabolism](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/metabolic-integration-and-the-fed-fasted-cycle.md)). With continuous cheap calories the same genes produce obesity, type 2 diabetes and hypertension, rare in hunter-gatherers and rising within a generation of an industrial diet ([nutritional disease](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/nutritional-disease-obesity-and-malnutrition.md)). Mismatch also explains myopia (indoor childhoods) and probably the rise of allergic disease as early microbial exposure fell.

2. **Trade-offs: every design is a compromise, so improving one thing worsens another.** The pelvis that allows upright walking narrows the birth canal while the brain enlarged, making birth dangerous. A strong inflammatory response clears infection and causes septic shock. A high set point for clotting prevents haemorrhage and causes thrombosis. The sickle allele protects against malaria and kills homozygotes ([balancing selection](natural-selection-and-population-genetics.md)). A drug that suppresses one side of a trade-off reveals the other: anticoagulation and bleeding.

3. **Antagonistic pleiotropy and the declining force of selection explain ageing.** Because selection weakens after reproduction, an allele that helps early and harms late is favoured (Williams, 1957). Cellular [senescence](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/senescence-and-telomeres.md) suppresses cancer in the young and drives degeneration in the old. Ageing is not programmed; it is the sum of damage selection never had reason to prevent (the disposable soma: resources spent on repair are not spent on reproduction). Hence no single ageing gene and the clustering of late-life diseases in one person ([diseases of aging](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/diseases-of-aging-and-degeneration.md); [frailty](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/frailty-multimorbidity-and-polypharmacy.md)).

4. **Constraints of history: evolution modifies, it does not redesign.** The vertebrate eye has its photoreceptors behind the retinal wiring, giving a blind spot. The recurrent laryngeal nerve loops under the aortic arch because it did in a fish without a neck. The shared passage for food and air lets us choke. No mutation can remove these without breaking everything built on top of them.

5. **Arms races: pathogens evolve in days, hosts in millennia.** A bacterium divides every half hour; a human generation is 25 years. The host cannot win by sequence change alone, which is why the adaptive immune system generates diversity somatically, within a lifetime, and why the Major Histocompatibility Complex is the most polymorphic region of the genome ([host–pathogen coevolution](host-pathogen-coevolution-and-resistance.md)). Virulence is itself under selection: a pathogen spread by contact is pushed toward mildness, one spread by vectors or water can afford severity.

6. **Defences are not defects.** Fever impairs many pathogens more than the host at a metabolic cost of ~10% per degree; sequestering iron starves bacteria; the nausea of early pregnancy peaks when the embryo is most vulnerable to toxins. These responses obey the smoke-detector principle (Nesse): when a false alarm is cheap and a missed threat fatal, selection sets the threshold low, so most activations are, in hindsight, unnecessary. The question before suppressing a defence is what it is for.

7. **Reproductive and developmental biology carry the clearest evolutionary logic.** Menopause, gestation length, puberty timing and the thrifty response of a fetus to a poorly nourished mother are life-history traits shaped by trade-offs between current and future reproduction. The developmental origins of adult disease, low birth weight predicting later diabetes and heart disease, are a mismatch between a forecast made in utero and the environment that arrived ([growth and ageing](../03-development/growth-puberty-and-aging.md)).

8. **Evolutionary reasoning generates hypotheses; it does not replace mechanism or trials.** It predicts that treating fever may prolong some infections, that diseases of mismatch will respond to environmental change more than to gene-targeted drugs, and that combination therapy beats sequential monotherapy. Each must still be tested ([multifactorial disease](../../08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/multifactorial-disease-and-risk.md)); the value is to make the body's vulnerabilities expected rather than mysterious.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proximate / ultimate explanation | How a mechanism works / why selection built it that way |
| Mismatch | Disease from a trait tuned to a past environment meeting a present one |
| Trade-off | A gain in one function paid for by a loss in another |
| Antagonistic pleiotropy | An allele beneficial early in life and harmful late |
| Declining force of selection | Weakening of selection on traits expressed after reproduction |
| Historical constraint | A vulnerability inherited from ancestral body plans that cannot be redesigned |
| Smoke-detector principle | Defences set to a low threshold because a missed threat costs more than a false alarm |
| Thrifty phenotype | Fetal metabolic adaptation to scarcity that becomes harmful in abundance |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Evolution should have made us healthy."** Selection maximises reproduction in past environments; health after reproduction and in novel environments was never its target.
- **"Fever and inflammation are malfunctions."** They are regulated defences with costs; the pathology is in their calibration or target, not their existence.
