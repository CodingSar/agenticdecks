## Essence

Most of medicine asks *how* a disease happens: which mechanism fails. Evolutionary medicine asks *why* the body is built so that it can fail that way. The answer is never that selection produces health; selection produces reproductive success in past environments, and it works with whatever variation and history it has. From that follow six reasons for vulnerability: mismatch between the environment we evolved in and the one we live in; trade-offs in which every gain costs something; the declining force of selection with age; constraints of history that no redesign can undo; arms races with pathogens that evolve faster than we do; and defences (fever, pain, anxiety, cough) that are costly but adaptive and are often mistaken for the disease. The one idea is that **the body is not designed but descended**, and much pathology is the shadow of adaptation.

## Core tenets

1. **Mismatch: a body tuned for scarcity meets abundance.** For almost all of human history food was intermittent, salt scarce, physical effort unavoidable and infection constant. Genes that favoured eating when food was available, conserving salt and storing fat were advantageous ([fed–fasted metabolism](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/metabolic-integration-and-the-fed-fasted-cycle.md)). In an environment of continuous cheap calories, added salt and sedentary work those same genes produce obesity, type 2 diabetes and hypertension: the diseases that now kill most people in high-income countries, and that were rare in hunter-gatherer populations and rise within a generation when such populations adopt an industrial diet. Mismatch also explains myopia (indoor childhoods), dental crowding (soft food) and probably the rise of allergic and autoimmune disease as early-life microbial exposure fell ([nutritional disease](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/nutritional-disease-obesity-and-malnutrition.md)). Mismatch is not a claim that the past was healthy; it is a claim that the trait was tuned to a different input.

2. **Trade-offs: every design is a compromise, so improving one thing worsens another.** The upright pelvis that allows bipedal walking narrows the birth canal while the brain enlarged, making human birth dangerous and infants helpless. A strong inflammatory response clears infection and causes septic shock and autoimmunity. A high set point for blood clotting prevents haemorrhage and causes thrombosis. The sickle allele protects against malaria and kills homozygotes ([balancing selection](natural-selection-and-population-genetics.md)). Where a trait seems badly designed, the question is what it was traded against, and that trade is usually still active: a drug that fully suppresses one side of a trade-off often reveals the other (immunosuppression and infection, anticoagulation and bleeding).

3. **Antagonistic pleiotropy and the declining force of selection explain ageing.** Because selection weakens after reproduction, an allele that helps early and harms late is favoured (Williams, 1957). Cellular [senescence](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/senescence-and-telomeres.md) suppresses cancer in the young and drives degeneration in the old; strong growth signalling (insulin-like growth factor, mTOR) builds a reproductive adult quickly and accelerates ageing afterwards; high androgen levels raise early fertility and later prostate disease. Ageing is not programmed; it is the sum of damage that selection never had reason to prevent (the disposable soma: resources spent on repair are resources not spent on reproduction). This is why there is no single ageing gene and why the diseases of late life cluster in one person ([diseases of aging](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/diseases-of-aging-and-degeneration.md); [frailty](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/frailty-multimorbidity-and-polypharmacy.md)).

4. **Constraints of history: evolution modifies, it does not redesign.** The vertebrate eye has its photoreceptors behind the retinal wiring, producing a blind spot and a retina that can detach. The recurrent laryngeal nerve loops under the aortic arch because it did so in a fish without a neck. The appendix, the vulnerable lumbar spine, the shared passage for food and air that lets us choke, and the descent of the testes through the inguinal canal that leaves a path for hernia are all legacies of ancestors with different bodies. Each is a vulnerability no engineer would build and no mutation can now remove without breaking everything built on top of it.

5. **Arms races: pathogens evolve in days, hosts in millennia.** A bacterium divides every half hour and a virus produces 10^10 genomes a day in one patient; a human generation is 25 years. The host cannot win by sequence change alone, which is why the adaptive immune system evolved to generate diversity somatically, within a lifetime, and why the Major Histocompatibility Complex is the most polymorphic region in the genome ([host–pathogen coevolution](host-pathogen-coevolution-and-resistance.md)). The same asymmetry explains antibiotic resistance, influenza drift and the need for annual vaccines. Virulence itself is a trait under selection: a pathogen spread by close contact is selected toward mildness, one spread by vectors or water toward severity.

6. **Defences are not defects: fever, pain, cough, vomiting, anxiety and low iron during infection are adaptations with costs.** Fever raises the temperature a few degrees, which impairs many pathogens more than the host, at a metabolic cost of ~10% per degree; sequestering iron starves bacteria of a nutrient they need; cough clears airways; the nausea of early pregnancy peaks when the embryo is most vulnerable to toxins. These responses are tuned by the smoke-detector principle (Nesse): when the cost of a false alarm is small and the cost of a missed threat is death, selection sets the threshold low, so most activations are, in hindsight, unnecessary. That is why anxiety and pain are common when nothing is wrong, and why suppressing a defence is sometimes safe and sometimes not; the question to ask is what the defence is for.

7. **Reproductive and developmental biology carry the clearest evolutionary logic.** Menstruation, menopause, the length of gestation, the timing of puberty and the thrifty-phenotype response of a fetus to a poorly nourished mother (set metabolism for scarcity, suffer when the world turns out rich) are life-history traits shaped by trade-offs between current and future reproduction. The developmental origins of adult disease (low birth weight predicting later diabetes and heart disease) are a mismatch between a forecast made in utero and the environment that arrived ([growth and ageing](../03-development/growth-puberty-and-aging.md)). Pre-eclampsia and gestational diabetes can be read as conflicts between maternal and fetal (paternally expressed) genes over resource transfer.

8. **Evolutionary reasoning generates hypotheses and explains patterns; it does not replace mechanism or trials.** It predicts that treating fever may prolong some infections (supported in some studies, not others), that polygenic diseases of mismatch will respond to environmental change more than to gene-targeted drugs, that antibiotic cycling will fail where combination therapy succeeds, and that defences should be suppressed only when their cost clearly exceeds their benefit. Each prediction must still be tested ([multifactorial disease](../../08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/multifactorial-disease-and-risk.md)). Its value is to make the body's vulnerabilities expected rather than mysterious, and to keep the clinician asking whether a sign is the disease or the response.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proximate / ultimate explanation | How a mechanism works / why selection built it that way |
| Mismatch | Disease from a trait tuned to a past environment meeting a present one |
| Trade-off | A gain in one function paid for by a loss in another |
| Antagonistic pleiotropy | An allele beneficial early in life and harmful late |
| Declining force of selection | Weakening of selection on traits expressed after reproduction |
| Disposable soma | Ageing as the result of resources allocated to reproduction over repair |
| Historical constraint | A vulnerability inherited from ancestral body plans that cannot be redesigned |
| Smoke-detector principle | Defences set to a low threshold because a missed threat costs more than a false alarm |
| Life-history trade-off | Allocation of resources among growth, maintenance and reproduction over a lifetime |
| Thrifty phenotype | Fetal metabolic adaptation to scarcity that becomes harmful in abundance |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Evolution should have made us healthy."** Selection maximises reproduction in past environments; health after reproduction and in novel environments was never its target.
- **"Fever and inflammation are malfunctions."** They are regulated defences with costs; the pathology is in their calibration or their target, not their existence.
- **"Evolutionary explanations are untestable stories."** They predict patterns (allele distributions tracking malaria, ageing rates tracking extrinsic mortality across species, defence thresholds) that have been tested; the discipline is in testing them.
