## What this area is about

Most of what medicine does to patients is not a drug. It cuts, irradiates, implants, infuses and persuades, and each of these has a mechanism as definite as a receptor's. This area holds the five families of non-pharmacological treatment, written as mechanisms so that the specialties above can cite them the way they cite a drug class. Surgery is controlled injury that depends on the biology of repair; radiotherapy is DNA damage aimed by physics and spared by fractionation; a device bears load or carries signal while the body encapsulates it; fluids, nutrients and blood are drugs with compartments; and behaviour change and placebo act through the brain's prediction machinery on the behaviours and symptoms that drugs cannot reach.

The unifying idea is that **every therapy is an intervention in a physiological process and is judged by the same questions as a drug**: what does it change, where does it go, what is its dose–response, what is its toxicity, and what does the body do in reply. A scalpel has a therapeutic index (the tumour removed against the tissue lost), radiation has a quantal curve for tumour control and another for complication, a litre of saline has a volume of distribution, and a conversation has a nocebo effect. Writing them in this layer, beside the drugs, makes the comparison explicit and lets trials ([10](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/README.md)) compare them fairly.

```
   local, structural           systemic, replacement          the patient's brain
   surgery  <-> radiotherapy    fluids / nutrition / blood      behaviour change
        \        /                       |                      placebo / nocebo
         devices                  (carried by devices)          (controls every trial)
```

Read surgery and radiotherapy together: they are the two local treatments, they compete and combine in cancer, and irradiated tissue heals badly, so each constrains the other. Devices are what surgery leaves inside and what delivers fluids, dialysis and ventilation. Fluids and blood are the systemic replacement therapies and the commonest treatments in any hospital. Behaviour change and placebo come last because they apply to every treatment above: adherence to rehabilitation after surgery, the sham-surgery trials that measured how much of an operation's benefit is expectation, and the nocebo that information about any treatment can generate.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Pharmacokinetics](../01-pharmacokinetics/README.md):** fluids and nutrients obey the same compartment logic as drugs; the volume of distribution of sodium is extracellular water, of glucose total body water, of red cells the vascular space. Dialysis is clearance by a machine, and its ability to remove a drug follows from the drug's Vd and protein binding.
- **[Pharmacodynamics](../02-pharmacodynamics/README.md):** radiotherapy's tumour-control and normal-tissue-complication curves are the quantal dose–response pair under another name; the placebo effect is the response the dose–response curve must be measured against; transfusion reactions and device infections are adverse events that registries track as pharmacovigilance tracks drugs.
- **[Drug classes by mechanism](../03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/README.md):** cytotoxics and radiation share the DNA-damage mechanism and are combined as radiosensitisers; antimicrobials fail on devices because of biofilm; anticoagulants and antiplatelets exist largely to make blood tolerate devices; diuretics are the antidote to fluid; nicotine partial agonists are a drug in the service of behaviour change.
- **[Drug development and toxicology](../05-drug-development-and-toxicology/README.md):** devices and surgical procedures are evaluated by a weaker evidence pipeline than drugs (registries, learning curves, difficulty of blinding), which the development leaf contrasts; radiation and nutritional excess are toxicology with a different agent.
