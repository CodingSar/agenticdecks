## Essence

Inflammation is one response with many triggers, and its chemistry runs through a few hubs: arachidonic acid metabolites (prostaglandins, leukotrienes), the cytokines that coordinate leukocytes (tumour necrosis factor (TNF), the interleukins), the glucocorticoid-sensitive transcription programme that drives most of it, and the T-cell activation and lymphocyte proliferation that sustain adaptive attack. Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs block one of those hubs, and their power and their danger scale together: the further upstream the block, the more of inflammation it removes and the more of host defence goes with it. The single idea is that inflammation and immunity are the same machinery, so every drug in this class trades the harm of inflammation against the harm of infection, impaired healing and, with long use, cancer surveillance.

## Core tenets

1. **Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) block cyclo-oxygenase, and their side effects are the prostaglandins they remove.** Cyclo-oxygenase (COX) converts arachidonic acid into prostaglandins. COX-2 is induced at sites of inflammation and makes the prostaglandins that sensitise nociceptors, dilate vessels and reset the hypothalamic temperature set point; blocking it (ibuprofen, naproxen, the selective celecoxib) is analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory. COX-1 is constitutive and makes the prostaglandins that protect gastric mucosa, maintain renal blood flow when it is threatened, and in platelets make thromboxane; blocking it causes ulcers, acute kidney injury in the volume-depleted, and, for aspirin, antiplatelet effect. Selective COX-2 inhibitors spare the stomach but remove endothelial prostacyclin while leaving platelet thromboxane, which tilts the balance towards thrombosis. Paracetamol is analgesic and antipyretic with little anti-inflammatory action, by a central mechanism still incompletely defined.

2. **Glucocorticoids suppress inflammation at the level of transcription, which makes them the broadest anti-inflammatory and the most expensive in side effects.** Cortisol and its synthetic analogues (prednisolone, dexamethasone) bind a cytoplasmic receptor that enters the nucleus and represses the transcription factors (NF-kB, AP-1) driving cytokine, chemokine, COX-2 and adhesion-molecule genes, while inducing anti-inflammatory proteins such as annexin A1. Nearly every arm of inflammation is turned down, which is why glucocorticoids work in asthma, arthritis, colitis, transplant rejection, cerebral oedema and leukaemia. The same receptor regulates glucose (hyperglycaemia), bone (osteoporosis), skin and muscle (atrophy), mood, and the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis (suppression, so that abrupt withdrawal is dangerous, as [tolerance and withdrawal](../02-pharmacodynamics/tolerance-dependence-and-withdrawal.md) explains). Onset takes hours because transcription must change.

3. **Antihistamines and leukotriene antagonists block single mediators and so are safe and limited.** H1 antihistamines (cetirizine; the sedating chlorphenamine) block the histamine released from mast cells in [allergy](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md) and relieve urticaria and rhinitis, but not asthma or anaphylaxis, in which other mediators dominate; adrenaline, acting physiologically against all of them, is the drug for anaphylaxis. Leukotriene receptor antagonists (montelukast) block the bronchoconstrictor products of the lipoxygenase pathway. Mast-cell stabilisers prevent degranulation. Each removes one message from a conversation with many speakers.

4. **Conventional immunosuppressants stop lymphocytes proliferating or signalling, and they were discovered as anticancer and transplant drugs.** Antimetabolites block the nucleotide synthesis that dividing lymphocytes need: methotrexate (dihydrofolate reductase, though at low dose its anti-inflammatory action is probably through adenosine release), azathioprine (a purine analogue activated and inactivated by enzymes including thiopurine methyltransferase, the [pharmacogenomic](../02-pharmacodynamics/pharmacogenomics-and-individual-variation.md) example), mycophenolate (inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase, on which lymphocytes uniquely depend). Calcineurin inhibitors (ciclosporin, tacrolimus) block the phosphatase that lets T-cells transcribe interleukin-2 after antigen recognition; they are the basis of [transplantation](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md) and are nephrotoxic through vasoconstriction of the kidney they protect. Alkylating agents (cyclophosphamide) kill lymphocytes outright and are reserved for life-threatening vasculitis.

5. **Cytokine blockade removes one signal from the cascade and is specific enough to reveal which signal drives which disease.** Antibodies and receptor fusion proteins against TNF (infliximab, adalimumab, etanercept) transformed rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease and psoriasis, and their chief hazard, reactivation of latent tuberculosis, shows that TNF is what holds granulomas together. Blocking interleukin-6 (tocilizumab), interleukin-17 (secukinumab), interleukin-4/13 (dupilumab), interleukin-1 or interleukin-23 each helps a different set of diseases, which has redrawn the classification of inflammation by mechanism rather than by organ. Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib) are small molecules that block the intracellular signalling shared by many cytokine receptors, broader than one antibody and narrower than a glucocorticoid. These are the [biologics](biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md) of inflammation.

6. **Immunomodulation now runs in both directions: suppressing the immune system in autoimmunity and releasing it in cancer.** Checkpoint inhibitors (antibodies against CTLA-4, PD-1 and PD-L1) remove the brakes that T-cells use to avoid attacking self, so that they attack the tumour; the predictable cost is autoimmunity in any organ (colitis, hepatitis, thyroiditis, hypophysitis), which is treated with the glucocorticoids and cytokine blockers above. Vaccines, interferons and the stimulatory cytokines are the older end of the same spectrum. The field is one dial, turned either way, and the diseases of under- and over-activity are mirror images, as [autoimmunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md) and tumour immunology show.

7. **Infection is the common price, and it is proportional to how much of the defence is removed.** Glucocorticoids, calcineurin inhibitors and antimetabolites, especially in combination, open the door to the organisms that cell-mediated immunity contains: tuberculosis, *Pneumocystis*, cytomegalovirus, fungi, and the reactivation of hepatitis B. Screening for latent infection before starting, prophylaxis during, and vaccination before immunosuppression (live vaccines being contraindicated once it starts) follow directly. Long-term immunosuppression also raises the incidence of skin cancer and lymphoma, a direct demonstration of [tumour immunosurveillance](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cyclo-oxygenase (COX-1, COX-2) | Enzymes converting arachidonic acid to prostaglandins; constitutive and inducible isoforms with different roles |
| Prostaglandins and leukotrienes | Lipid mediators from arachidonic acid; the targets of NSAIDs and leukotriene antagonists |
| Glucocorticoid receptor | Nuclear receptor whose activation represses most pro-inflammatory transcription and alters metabolism |
| Antimetabolite | Drug blocking nucleotide synthesis to stop proliferating cells, including lymphocytes |
| Calcineurin | Phosphatase required for T-cell activation; target of ciclosporin and tacrolimus |
| Cytokine blockade | Antibodies or receptor decoys that neutralise one cytokine or block its receptor |
| Janus kinase (JAK) | Intracellular kinases shared by many cytokine receptors; targets of small-molecule inhibitors |
| Immune checkpoint | Inhibitory receptor (CTLA-4, PD-1) restraining T-cells; blocked in cancer immunotherapy |
| Disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) | Immunomodulator that slows joint damage, as opposed to relieving symptoms |
| Opportunistic infection | Infection by an organism normally contained by the immunity the drug removes |

## How it fits

- **Below:** The mediators are those of [acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md) and the [inflammatory cascade](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/complement-phagocytes-and-the-inflammatory-cascade.md); the cytokines are in [cytokines and interferons](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/cytokines-interferons-and-nk-cells.md); the lymphocyte targets in [T-cells](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/t-cells.md); cortisol's physiology in [adrenal hormones](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md). The assumption is that inflammation can be turned down without the infection or injury it was answering getting worse.
- **Above:** These are the therapeutic tools of [rheumatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md), [gastroenterology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/inflammatory-bowel-disease-and-malabsorption.md), [dermatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/11-dermatology/inflammatory-skin-disease.md), [pulmonology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/asthma-and-copd.md), [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/demyelinating-and-neuromuscular-disease.md) and transplant medicine; NSAID harm is a leading cause of admission in [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/frailty-multimorbidity-and-polypharmacy.md).
- **Siblings:** The cytokine antibodies are made and behave as [biologics](biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md); the antimetabolites and alkylating agents are [anticancer drugs](anticancer-drugs.md) at lower dose; glucocorticoids are also [endocrine drugs](endocrine-and-metabolic-drugs.md); infection under immunosuppression is treated with [antimicrobials](antimicrobials.md).

## Common misconceptions

- **"Selective COX-2 inhibitors are safe NSAIDs."** They spare the stomach but tilt the prostacyclin–thromboxane balance towards thrombosis; rofecoxib was withdrawn for cardiovascular events.
- **"Steroids suppress the immune system and NSAIDs do not."** NSAIDs act on one enzyme; glucocorticoids act on transcription of most of inflammation, and only the latter cause opportunistic infection. The names do not say which is the stronger drug.
- **"Immunosuppression is the opposite of immunotherapy."** Both adjust the same checkpoints and cytokines; checkpoint inhibitors cause the autoimmunity that immunosuppressants treat.
