## Essence

The circulation is controlled by a handful of signals (noradrenaline and adrenaline on adrenoceptors, acetylcholine on muscarinic receptors, angiotensin II and aldosterone, nitric oxide) acting on a handful of effectors (heart rate, contractility, vascular tone, blood volume). Every autonomic and cardiovascular drug either mimics or blocks one of those signals, or acts directly on an ion channel or transporter in the heart, vessel or kidney. The single idea is the pressure equation: mean arterial pressure = cardiac output × systemic vascular resistance, and cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume, where stroke volume depends on preload, contractility and afterload. Each drug class moves one term, and the body's baroreceptor and renin–angiotensin feedback then responds, which is why a drug's net effect is often not its direct one.

## Core tenets

1. **Adrenoceptor subtypes map onto the terms of the pressure equation.** Beta-1 receptors in the sinoatrial node and myocardium raise rate and contractility through cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP); beta-2 receptors relax bronchial and vascular smooth muscle; alpha-1 receptors constrict vessels through inositol trisphosphate and calcium; alpha-2 receptors on presynaptic terminals inhibit noradrenaline release. A beta-1 antagonist such as bisoprolol lowers rate and contractility (and renin release); an alpha-1 antagonist such as doxazosin lowers resistance; a beta-2 agonist such as salbutamol dilates the airway and, as a side effect, causes tremor and tachycardia through the same receptor elsewhere. Selectivity is relative: at high dose every "selective" drug reaches the other subtype.

2. **Muscarinic receptors are the brake, and blocking or stimulating them has consequences in every parasympathetically innervated organ.** Acetylcholine at M2 receptors slows the sinoatrial node and atrioventricular conduction; at M3 it contracts bronchial, bladder and gut smooth muscle and drives salivary, bronchial and sweat secretion. Atropine blocks all of these, which is why it treats bradycardia and organophosphate poisoning and causes dry mouth, urinary retention and confusion. Anticholinesterases (neostigmine, and the organophosphate insecticides and nerve agents) raise acetylcholine everywhere by stopping its breakdown; the therapeutic use is reversal of neuromuscular block and myasthenia, the toxic effect is cholinergic crisis.

3. **The renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system can be interrupted at four points, with a shared logic and a shared hazard.** Renin inhibitors, angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors such as ramipril, angiotensin II receptor blockers such as losartan, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists such as spironolactone all reduce vasoconstriction and sodium retention and the trophic effects of angiotensin II and aldosterone on heart and vessels. The shared hazard follows from the physiology: angiotensin II constricts the efferent arteriole to preserve glomerular filtration when renal perfusion falls, so blocking it in a patient with renal artery stenosis or hypovolaemia drops filtration; and aldosterone is the main route of potassium excretion, so blocking it causes hyperkalaemia. ACE inhibitors also raise bradykinin, hence their cough and angio-oedema.

4. **Diuretics lower volume by blocking a specific transporter at a specific nephron site, and the site predicts both potency and electrolyte effects.** Loop diuretics (furosemide) block the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter in the thick ascending limb, where ~25% of filtered sodium is reabsorbed, so they are the most powerful and they abolish the medullary gradient, causing potassium, magnesium and calcium loss. Thiazides block the Na-Cl cotransporter in the distal tubule (~5% of sodium), are weaker, lose potassium but retain calcium. Potassium-sparing agents act at the collecting duct's epithelial sodium channel (amiloride) or the aldosterone receptor. Osmotic diuretics (mannitol) hold water in the tubule by force. The [tubular map](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/tubular-transport-and-the-countercurrent-system.md) is the pharmacology.

5. **Vasodilators act on vascular smooth muscle through one of three final paths: calcium entry, cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP), or potassium channels.** Dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers (amlodipine) block L-type channels in arterioles and lower resistance; non-dihydropyridines (verapamil, diltiazem) also block cardiac L-type channels and slow the heart. Nitrates (glyceryl trinitrate) release nitric oxide, raising cGMP and relaxing veins more than arteries, which lowers preload; phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors protect the same cGMP from breakdown. Reflex tachycardia from the baroreceptor is the price of any pure arterial dilator, which is why they are often paired with a beta-blocker.

6. **Antiarrhythmics act on the ion channels that shape the cardiac action potential, and each class trades one arrhythmia for another.** The Vaughan Williams classes are: I, sodium-channel block (slows conduction; flecainide); II, beta-blockade (slows nodes); III, potassium-channel block (prolongs repolarisation and refractoriness; amiodarone, sotalol); IV, calcium-channel block (slows the nodes; verapamil). Prolonging the action potential lengthens the QT interval and can provoke torsades de pointes; slowing conduction can create the re-entry it was meant to prevent, which is why sodium-channel blockers increased mortality after myocardial infarction (the CAST trial, 1989). Digoxin inhibits the Na-K-ATPase, raising intracellular calcium (contractility) and increasing vagal tone (slowing the atrioventricular node), with a therapeutic index near 2.

7. **Antithrombotic drugs target one of the three stages of haemostasis.** Antiplatelet agents block platelet activation: aspirin acetylates cyclo-oxygenase irreversibly and stops thromboxane A2 synthesis for the platelet's lifetime; P2Y12 antagonists (clopidogrel) block the adenosine diphosphate receptor. Anticoagulants block the coagulation cascade: heparins potentiate antithrombin; warfarin starves the liver of reduced vitamin K so that factors II, VII, IX and X are made inactive; direct oral anticoagulants inhibit thrombin (dabigatran) or factor Xa (rivaroxaban) stoichiometrically. Fibrinolytics (alteplase) convert plasminogen to plasmin to dissolve formed clot. Arterial thrombi are platelet-rich and venous thrombi fibrin-rich, which is why antiplatelets dominate in [atherosclerosis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md) and anticoagulants in venous [thromboembolism](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md) and atrial fibrillation.

8. **Lipid-lowering drugs work by making the liver take up more low-density lipoprotein (LDL).** Statins (atorvastatin) inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting step of cholesterol synthesis; the hepatocyte responds to its own cholesterol deficit by expressing more LDL receptors, and plasma LDL falls. Ezetimibe blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption and produces the same hepatic response; antibodies and small interfering RNA against PCSK9 prevent the degradation of LDL receptors. All three act by the same feedback loop, which is why their effects add.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Adrenoceptors (alpha-1, alpha-2, beta-1, beta-2) | G-protein-coupled receptors for noradrenaline and adrenaline with distinct tissue distributions and second messengers |
| Muscarinic receptors (M1–M5) | G-protein-coupled receptors for acetylcholine at parasympathetic effectors |
| Baroreceptor reflex | Feedback that raises sympathetic tone when pressure falls; the source of reflex tachycardia with vasodilators |
| Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system | Hormonal loop that raises pressure by vasoconstriction and sodium retention |
| Preload and afterload | Ventricular filling (venous return) and the resistance the ventricle ejects against |
| Vaughan Williams classification | Grouping of antiarrhythmics by the ion channel or receptor they act on |
| QT prolongation | Lengthening of ventricular repolarisation by potassium-channel block; predisposes to torsades de pointes |
| Antiplatelet, anticoagulant, fibrinolytic | Drugs acting on platelet activation, the coagulation cascade, and formed fibrin respectively |
| LDL receptor upregulation | The hepatocyte's feedback response to cholesterol depletion; the common mechanism of lipid-lowering drugs |

## How it fits

- **Below:** The receptors are those of the [autonomic nervous system](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/the-autonomic-nervous-system.md); the terms of the pressure equation are in [hemodynamics](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md); the ion channels in [cardiac electrophysiology](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/cardiac-electrophysiology.md); the nephron sites in [tubular transport](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/tubular-transport-and-the-countercurrent-system.md); clotting in [haemostasis](../../06-physiology/08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md). The assumption is that blocking a signal removes only that signal's contribution; feedback then adds its own.
- **Above:** These classes are the therapeutic half of [heart failure](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/heart-failure.md), [hypertension](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/hypertension-and-vascular-disease.md), [arrhythmias](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/arrhythmias.md) and [ischaemic heart disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md); vasopressors and inotropes are the tools of [haemodynamic support](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/shock-and-haemodynamic-support.md); beta-2 agonists and antimuscarinics are the bronchodilators of [asthma and COPD](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/asthma-and-copd.md).
- **Siblings:** The same receptor families appear in [central nervous system drugs](central-nervous-system-drugs.md) (muscarinic and adrenergic effects of antidepressants and antipsychotics); the renal and endocrine actions of aldosterone antagonists overlap with [endocrine drugs](endocrine-and-metabolic-drugs.md); PCSK9 antibodies belong equally to [biologics](biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md).

## Common misconceptions

- **"A beta-blocker lowers blood pressure by vasodilation."** It lowers cardiac output and renin release; non-selective agents may even raise resistance by leaving alpha-1 unopposed.
- **"Antiarrhythmic drugs reduce the risk of dying from arrhythmia."** Most increase it in structural heart disease by creating new re-entry or torsades; the CAST trial is the canonical warning.
- **"Anticoagulants dissolve clots."** They prevent clot extension and new clot while the body's own fibrinolysis clears what exists; only fibrinolytics dissolve clot directly.
