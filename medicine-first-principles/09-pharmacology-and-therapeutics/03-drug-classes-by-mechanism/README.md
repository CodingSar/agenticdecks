## What this area is about

There are several thousand licensed drugs and a few dozen mechanisms. This area organises drugs by what they do to the body, not by what they are prescribed for, because a mechanism predicts the drug's main effect, its side effects, its interactions and its failure mode, whereas an indication predicts none of these. A beta-blocker is the same drug in angina, heart failure, migraine, thyrotoxicosis and anxiety; a glucocorticoid is the same drug in asthma, arthritis, leukaemia and transplant. Learn the receptor or enzyme once and every indication follows. The leaves therefore follow the physiology they act on: the autonomic and cardiovascular signals, the synapses of the brain, the structures that microbes have and we do not, the mediators of inflammation, the vulnerabilities of the cancer cell, the feedback loops of hormones, and finally the class defined by what the drug is made of rather than what it hits.

The unifying idea is that **every drug is a borrowed signal**. It occupies a receptor, enzyme, channel or transporter that the body already uses, and the response is the body's own response to that signal, turned up or down. Side effects are the same signal in a second tissue; feedback is the body's reply; and tolerance, resistance and immunogenicity are the three ways the body or the target learns to ignore the drug. Specific drugs appear only as examples, named by their international nonproprietary names, and nothing here is a treatment recommendation: which drug to give, at what dose, is settled by trials ([10](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/README.md)) and by the patient in front of the clinician ([11](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/README.md)), not by mechanism alone.

Read the first six in any order; each stands on its own physiology. Read biologics last, because it cuts across the others: anti-TNF antibodies belong to inflammation, checkpoint inhibitors to cancer, insulin and denosumab to endocrinology, and the biologics leaf holds what they share, the behaviour of a protein drug. Three threads recur in every leaf and are worth watching for: the side effect that is the main effect in another tissue, the feedback reply of the system (reflex tachycardia, rising TSH, rebound), and the evolution of the target (microbial resistance, tumour resistance, anti-drug antibodies).

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Pharmacokinetics](../01-pharmacokinetics/README.md):** each class carries kinetic consequences of its chemistry. Polar antimicrobials do not enter the brain; lipid-soluble anaesthetics redistribute; levothyroxine's week-long half-life sets its titration; antibodies last a month and are never metabolised by CYPs. Dosing of cytotoxics and aminoglycosides is kinetics at the limit of toxicity.
- **[Pharmacodynamics](../02-pharmacodynamics/README.md):** supplies the vocabulary every leaf uses (agonist, partial agonist, competitive antagonist, allosteric modulator, therapeutic index) and the two phenomena that change a class over time, tolerance and type B adverse reactions. Pharmacogenomics decides who gets which drug in oncology and who is harmed by which in metabolism.
- **[Non-drug therapeutics](../04-non-drug-therapeutics/README.md):** cytotoxics and radiotherapy share the DNA-damage mechanism and the fractional-kill arithmetic; antimicrobials fail on devices because of biofilm; endocrine replacement and nutrition both supply what the body lacks; the placebo response is subtracted from every drug trial in this area.
- **[Drug development and toxicology](../05-drug-development-and-toxicology/README.md):** every class here began as a target in that pipeline; toxicology is these mechanisms at excess, and most antidotes are competitive antagonists or enzyme interventions described in these leaves.

## Cross-cutting threads

- [Specificity and recognition](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#specificity-and-recognition): selectivity between receptor subtypes, between pathogen and host, between tumour and marrow, and the perfect specificity of an antibody.
- [Feedback and homeostasis](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#feedback-and-homeostasis): the baroreceptor reflex, rising TSH, adrenal suppression and the GnRH agonist paradox are the system replying to the drug.
- [Trade-offs and evolutionary legacy](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#trade-offs-and-evolutionary-legacy): resistance in microbes and tumours is evolution driven by the drug; aminoglycoside ototoxicity is the mitochondrion's bacterial ancestry.
- [Harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit): the side effect in the second tissue, infection under immunosuppression, the cytotoxic therapeutic index near one.
- [Injury and repair](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#injury-and-repair): anti-inflammatory drugs delay healing; bisphosphonates suppress the remodelling that repairs microdamage.
