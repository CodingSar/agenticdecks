# Triage and the ABCDE approach

## Essence

When many patients arrive with unknown problems, two sorting decisions must be made before any diagnosis: which patient first, and within that patient, which problem first. Triage answers the first by estimating time to harm from a few physiological variables. The ABCDE approach (Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure) answers the second by ordering the search for failures by how fast each kills: an obstructed airway in minutes, failed breathing in minutes to tens of minutes, failed circulation in tens of minutes to hours, brain injury over hours. Each failure is treated as it is found before moving on, and the sequence is repeated after every intervention. It is a priority queue built from physiology, and its power is that it works before anyone knows what the disease is.

## Core tenets

1. **Treat the physiology before the diagnosis, because physiology is what kills on the clock.** A patient dies of hypoxia, hypoperfusion or raised intracranial pressure, not of the name of the disease. Hypoxia damages the brain within about four minutes of arrest; exsanguination kills in minutes to an hour; sepsis and an expanding haematoma over hours. The ABCDE sequence orders the search by these time constants, so the clinician addresses what would kill soonest with an intervention that does not depend on knowing why. Diagnosis follows once the patient is stable enough for it to be useful.

2. **Triage estimates time to harm from vital signs and a single question.** Triage systems grade patients into a handful of categories by respiratory rate, pulse, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, temperature and consciousness, plus the presenting complaint. Vital signs work because they are the controlled variables of [homeostasis](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/homeostasis-and-set-points.md): a change means a controller is working hard, and a respiratory rate above about 25 or a new confusion means it is near its limit. Scores such as the National Early Warning Score (NEWS, UK, 2012; revised 2017) sum these deviations and have predictive validity for death within 24 hours across diagnoses. The cost of triage is error in both directions: under-triage harms the patient, over-triage harms the queue.

3. **Each letter is a question with a reversible answer.** *Airway*: is gas moving through an open passage? If not, open it ([airway management](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/airway-and-ventilation-management.md)). *Breathing*: is ventilation and oxygenation adequate (rate, effort, saturation, chest expansion, air entry, percussion)? If not, give oxygen, drain the tension pneumothorax, support ventilation. *Circulation*: is perfusion adequate (pulse, pressure, capillary refill, skin, consciousness, urine output)? If not, find and stop bleeding, give fluid or blood, and treat the mechanism of [shock](../03-critical-care/shock-and-haemodynamic-support.md). *Disability*: level of consciousness, pupils, glucose, lateralising signs; treat hypoglycaemia, seizures and raised intracranial pressure. *Exposure*: undress, look everywhere, measure temperature, then cover. Each question has a bedside test and a bedside action, which is why the sequence can be taught to anyone and run under stress.

4. **Compensation hides the diagnosis, so the signs of compensation are the signs that matter.** A young adult maintains a normal blood pressure until a third of the blood volume is gone ([trauma](../01-surgery/trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md)); a child compensates even longer and then collapses abruptly; an older patient on a β-blocker cannot mount a tachycardia. Triage therefore weights tachycardia, tachypnoea, cool peripheries, slow capillary refill and agitation over the pressure, and an apparently normal set of observations in a patient who looks unwell is read as [compensation about to fail](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation), not as reassurance.

5. **Reassess after every intervention, because the system is dynamic.** Each action changes the state: fluid given to a bleeding patient raises pressure and may restart bleeding; a sedative given for agitation may remove the airway; oxygen that corrects saturation may hide a rising carbon dioxide. The sequence is therefore a loop, not a checklist: A to E, intervene, A to E again. Deterioration after an intervention is information about the diagnosis (a patient who worsens with fluid may have cardiogenic or obstructive shock).

6. **Structure defends against cognitive failure under pressure.** The emergency clinician works with high uncertainty, interruptions, time pressure and incomplete information, the conditions in which anchoring, premature closure and availability bias thrive ([clinical reasoning](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/clinical-reasoning-and-cognitive-bias.md)). A fixed sequence, a shared vocabulary and explicit handover (situation, background, assessment, recommendation) externalise the reasoning so that it can be checked by the team and does not depend on one person's memory. The same logic produced the [surgical checklist](../../15-medicine-in-society/01-health-systems/patient-safety-and-quality-improvement.md).

7. **The second sort is a risk decision: admit, investigate, or send home.** Once stable, most patients do not need a hospital bed. Validated decision rules (for head injury imaging, for pulmonary embolism, for chest pain) combine history and findings into a [pre-test probability](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md) and set a threshold below which the risk of a missed diagnosis is lower than the harm of further testing or admission. Safe discharge is as much a skill as resuscitation, and the department's error rate is measured in unexpected returns and deaths after discharge.

8. **Who else is involved.** Triage decides who the department calls and how fast: [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md) for the occluded coronary artery, [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/stroke.md) for the stroke within the thrombolysis window, [surgery](../01-surgery/README.md) for the bleeding patient, [anaesthesia](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) for the airway, [critical care](../03-critical-care/README.md) for the failing patient who is not yet dead, [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/common-childhood-illness.md) for the child whose normal values differ, [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) once the overdose is medically safe, and [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) for the majority who were never going to die today.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Triage | Sorting patients by urgency, estimated from physiology and presenting complaint |
| ABCDE | Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure: the order of assessment and treatment |
| Early warning score | A sum of vital-sign deviations that predicts deterioration and death across diagnoses |
| Primary and secondary survey | The ABCDE search for immediate threats, then the head-to-toe search for everything else |
| Capillary refill time | Time for colour to return to blanched skin; a bedside measure of peripheral perfusion |
| Under- and over-triage | Assigning too low or too high an urgency; each has a cost |
| Reassessment loop | Repeating ABCDE after every intervention because each changes the state |
| Decision rule | A validated combination of findings that sets a probability and a threshold for testing or discharge |
| Structured handover | A fixed format (situation, background, assessment, recommendation) that transmits reasoning |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Triage is about finding the diagnosis quickly."** It is about estimating time to harm; the diagnosis can wait, the airway cannot.
- **"Normal observations mean the patient is safe."** Compensation normalises the pressure and sometimes the pulse; a patient who looks unwell with normal numbers is compensating, and the collapse is sudden.
- **"ABCDE is for trauma."** It is the universal first assessment of any acutely unwell patient, medical or surgical, adult or child, because every patient dies through the same final pathways.
