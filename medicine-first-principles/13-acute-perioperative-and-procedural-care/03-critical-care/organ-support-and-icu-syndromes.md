## Essence

Once the circulation and the lungs are supported, the rest of the body still has to be kept alive for days or weeks: the kidney that stopped, the gut that will not absorb, the brain that must tolerate a tube, the muscles that waste while the patient lies still. Each support (dialysis, feeding, sedation, immobilisation) replaces a function and causes a syndrome of its own, so an Intensive Care Unit (ICU) stay is a second illness layered on the first. The organising idea is the bridge: support is justified only while there is a far side, recovery of the function being replaced, and the longer the bridge the more it costs in infection, weakness, delirium and lasting disability. Deciding when the far side has disappeared, and saying so, is as much a part of critical care as starting the machine.

## Core tenets

1. **The kidney is supported by replacing filtration, not by restoring it.** Roughly half of intensive care patients develop [acute kidney injury](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/acute-kidney-injury.md), from hypoperfusion, sepsis and nephrotoxins; a minority need renal replacement, which the unit delivers continuously through a venous catheter as haemofiltration or haemodialysis, driven by [diffusion and convection](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/diffusion-osmosis-and-transport.md) across a membrane. It removes potassium, acid, urea and water, and is started for those (life-threatening hyperkalaemia, refractory acidosis, fluid overload, uraemic complications) rather than at a creatinine threshold; trials of early versus delayed initiation have not shown that earlier is better. It does nothing to heal the tubules, which recover on their own in most survivors over days to weeks, and it removes drugs, nutrients and heat as readily as toxins.

2. **The gut is the organ the unit most often starves, and starving it harms the barrier.** Critical illness is catabolic: the stress response breaks down muscle protein at a rate that can exceed 1% of lean mass a day, and no amount of feeding stops it. Enteral feeding through the stomach or jejunum maintains the mucosa and its [barrier](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/the-gut-barrier-and-microbiome.md), reduces infection compared with the intravenous route, and is started early at modest rates; parenteral nutrition is reserved for a gut that cannot be used, because its line infections and overfeeding carry their own harm ([nutrition](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/fluids-nutrition-and-blood-products.md)). Refeeding a starved patient shifts phosphate, potassium and magnesium into cells and can stop the heart; glycaemic control matters, but the tight control tried in the 2000s caused hypoglycaemia and deaths, and the target was loosened.

3. **Drug handling changes by the hour, so dosing is a moving target.** Capillary leak and litres of fluid expand the volume of distribution of water-soluble drugs (aminoglycosides, β-lactams) so that standard doses underdose; low albumin frees highly bound drugs; the hyperdynamic young septic patient clears renally excreted drugs faster than normal, while the patient on renal replacement or with a failing liver clears them more slowly ([elimination and half-life](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/elimination-half-life-and-dosing.md)). The same patient can need a higher dose on day one and a lower one on day three. Measuring levels where possible and thinking in volumes and clearances rather than fixed doses is the only safe approach.

4. **Sedation is a necessary poison, and less of it is better.** A ventilated patient needs analgesia and often sedation to tolerate the tube, but the [anaesthetic drugs](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/general-and-regional-anaesthesia.md) accumulate (context-sensitive half-time), suppress breathing and coughing, lower blood pressure and, especially the benzodiazepines, cause delirium. Daily interruption or light targeted sedation, analgesia first, and avoiding benzodiazepines shorten ventilation and stay in trials. The best sedative is often a treated cause of distress: pain, a full bladder, hypoxia, withdrawal from alcohol or nicotine, fear.

5. **Delirium is brain failure, and it predicts everything bad.** An acute fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness affects a majority of ventilated patients at some point; it is driven by the inflammatory state, sedatives, sleep deprivation, immobility, pain and metabolic derangement acting on a brain whose reserve may already be low. It is often hypoactive and missed. Each day of delirium is associated with higher mortality and with cognitive impairment a year later that can resemble a mild dementia. The treatments with evidence are not drugs but the removal of causes: light, sleep, glasses and hearing aids, early mobilisation, family presence and minimal sedation, the same bundle that works on a [geriatric ward](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md).

6. **The unit's own interventions cause a recognisable set of complications.** Lines cause bloodstream infection; the tube causes ventilator-associated pneumonia; immobility and inflammation cause venous thrombosis; stress and ventilation cause gastric mucosal bleeding; pressure causes skin ulcers; corticosteroids, paralysis and inflammation cause a critical illness polyneuropathy and myopathy that leaves patients too weak to breathe or stand. Each is prevented by a bundle (hand hygiene and line care, head-up positioning and early extubation, prophylactic anticoagulation, acid suppression where indicated, turning, early mobilisation), and these bundles, more than any new drug, are what lowered mortality in intensive care between the 1990s and the 2010s ([healthcare-associated infection](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/infection-control-and-healthcare-associated-infection.md), [patient safety](../../15-medicine-in-society/01-health-systems/patient-safety-and-quality-improvement.md)).

7. **Survival is not the endpoint; the post-intensive care syndrome is.** Survivors of a prolonged stay leave with wasted muscle, neuropathy, cognitive impairment, anxiety, depression and post-traumatic stress, and a substantial fraction have not returned to work or independent living a year later. The determinants are the duration of ventilation, delirium, sedation and immobility, so the prevention starts in the unit: physiotherapy on the ventilator, sleep, orientation, diaries. [Rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md) and follow-up clinics are where the real outcome of critical care is measured, and the question before admission is whether the patient could survive to a life they would want.

8. **Stopping is a medical decision, and who else is involved.** A large fraction of deaths in intensive care follow a decision to withhold or withdraw support, made when the far side of the bridge has gone: the cause cannot be reversed, the organs are not recovering, or the patient would not want the life that survival would bring. The decision is made under uncertainty ([prognosis](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/prognosis-and-shared-decision-making.md)), with a patient who usually lacks [capacity](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md), so it rests on prior wishes, family, and the treating team's consensus, and it shifts the goal from cure to comfort without abandoning care ([end-of-life decisions](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/death-dying-and-end-of-life-decisions.md)). The team is [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md) for the kidney, [gastroenterology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) and dietetics for the gut, [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) for prognostication in brain injury and for the weakness, [microbiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md) for the infections the unit creates, [geriatrics and palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) for frailty and the dying, [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) for the aftermath, and [health economics](../../15-medicine-in-society/01-health-systems/health-economics-and-priority-setting.md) for the cost of the most expensive bed in the hospital and who gets it when there are not enough.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Renal replacement therapy | Extracorporeal removal of solutes and water by diffusion (dialysis) or convection (filtration) |
| Catabolic state | Net protein breakdown driven by the stress response; muscle loss that feeding slows but cannot stop |
| Enteral versus parenteral nutrition | Feeding through the gut versus intravenously; the gut route protects the barrier |
| Refeeding syndrome | Dangerous shifts of phosphate, potassium and magnesium when a starved patient is fed |
| Volume of distribution and clearance | The two quantities that change hourly in critical illness and set the dose |
| Context-sensitive half-time | Time for drug concentration to halve after an infusion; grows with infusion length |
| Delirium | Acute fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness; brain failure in the unit |
| Critical illness polyneuromyopathy | Nerve and muscle injury from inflammation, immobility and drugs; profound weakness |
| Care bundle | A small set of evidence-based measures delivered together to prevent a complication |
| Post-intensive care syndrome | Physical, cognitive and psychological impairment persisting after critical illness |
| Withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment | Stopping support when the goal of recovery cannot be met; a shift to comfort, not abandonment |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Dialysis treats kidney failure."** It replaces filtration while the kidney recovers or does not; it does not speed recovery and removes drugs and nutrients along with toxins.
- **"A sedated patient is a comfortable patient."** Deep sedation prolongs ventilation, causes delirium and hides pain; the comfortable patient is lightly sedated, analgesed and oriented.
- **"If the patient survives the unit, the treatment succeeded."** Many survivors are left with weakness, cognitive impairment and psychological injury; outcome is measured a year later, in function.
- **"Withdrawing treatment is giving up."** It is the recognition that the bridge has no far side, and the redirection of care towards comfort; continuing futile support harms the patient and denies the bed to another.
