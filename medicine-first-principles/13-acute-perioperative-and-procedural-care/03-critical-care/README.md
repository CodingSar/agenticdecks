## What this area is about

Critical care, delivered in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU), is the branch of medicine that keeps a patient alive while the cause of their collapse is found and treated. It was born in the 1952 Copenhagen poliomyelitis epidemic, when anaesthetists kept paralysed patients alive by ventilating them through tracheostomies, by hand, for weeks, and mortality from respiratory failure fell from around 80% to about 25%. The lesson generalised: a failing organ can be replaced temporarily by a machine, a drug or another person's hands, and if the underlying insult is reversible the patient recovers. The question this area answers is **what fails first when supply cannot meet demand, how is each failure recognised and supported, and what does the support itself cost?**

The unifying idea is **oxygen delivery against oxygen consumption**. Delivery is cardiac output times arterial oxygen content (haemoglobin, saturation, a little dissolved); consumption is set by metabolism, fever, work of breathing and the inflammatory state. Shock is delivery failing from the pump, the pipes or the volume; respiratory failure is content failing at the lung; sepsis is the inflammatory response raising consumption, leaking the pipes and poisoning the mitochondria all at once. Every monitor in the unit (arterial pressure, central venous saturation, lactate, urine output) reads one term of that balance, and every intervention (fluid, a vasopressor, a ventilator, a transfusion, dialysis) moves one term.

   oxygen delivery  =  cardiac output  x  [Hb x SaO2 x 1.34 + dissolved O2]
                          |                       |
                     shock (01)          respiratory failure (02)
                          \                       /
                      sepsis (03): consumption up, pipes leaking,
                      mitochondria poisoned, organs failing in sequence
                                      |
                 organ support (04): kidney, gut, brain, muscle, and the
                 price of each day; when the bridge has no far side

Read shock first: it introduces the delivery equation and the four-way classification used throughout. Respiratory failure supplies the oxygen content term and the ventilator. Sepsis combines both and adds the inflammatory mechanism that explains why organs fail together. Organ support closes with the kidney, the gut and the brain, the syndromes caused by the unit itself, and the question of when support should stop.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Surgery](../01-surgery/README.md):** sends the patient after [damage-control surgery](../01-surgery/trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md) and faecal peritonitis, and receives requests for [source control](../01-surgery/the-acute-abdomen.md) when a septic patient does not improve.
- **[Anaesthesia and perioperative medicine](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md):** the parent specialty historically; the same airway, ventilator and sedative skills over days; the [high-risk operation](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/perioperative-risk-and-complications.md) is admitted to the unit by plan.
- **[Emergency medicine](../04-emergency-medicine/README.md):** the first hour of every critical care admission; [resuscitation](../04-emergency-medicine/resuscitation-and-cardiac-arrest.md) and the care after cardiac arrest, including temperature control and prognostication, are shared.
- **[Ophthalmology and Ear, Nose and Throat (ENT)](../05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md):** ENT performs the tracheostomy that lets a patient be weaned and manages the upper-airway obstruction that becomes an intensive care admission.

## Interfaces with other specialties

- **[Cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md):** cardiogenic shock after [myocardial infarction](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md) and acute [heart failure](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/heart-failure.md) are shared patients; mechanical circulatory support and the [arrhythmias](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/arrhythmias.md) of the critically ill are joint decisions.
- **[Pulmonology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/README.md):** [respiratory failure](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/pulmonary-vascular-disease-and-respiratory-failure.md) from pneumonia, exacerbated obstructive disease and pulmonary embolism; non-invasive ventilation sits on the border, and long-term ventilation is handed back.
- **[Nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md):** [acute kidney injury](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/acute-kidney-injury.md) affects roughly half of intensive care patients; renal replacement in the unit is run jointly, and [electrolyte and acid–base disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/electrolyte-and-acid-base-disorders.md) are hourly problems.
- **[Infectious diseases](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md):** [sepsis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md) is the commonest admission diagnosis; [antimicrobial stewardship](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/antimicrobial-stewardship.md) and [healthcare-associated infection](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/infection-control-and-healthcare-associated-infection.md) (ventilator-associated pneumonia, line infection) are where the unit most needs the microbiologist.
- **[Gastroenterology and hepatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md):** variceal [bleeding](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/acid-peptic-disease-and-gi-bleeding.md), acute [liver failure](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md) and severe [pancreatitis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/pancreatic-and-biliary-disease.md) are managed together; nutrition through a failing gut is a daily question.
- **[Haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/README.md):** disseminated intravascular coagulation, massive [transfusion](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/transfusion-medicine.md), thrombocytopenia from drugs and sepsis, and the neutropenic patient from [haematological malignancy](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/haematological-malignancies.md).
- **[Neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md):** [status epilepticus](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/epilepsy.md), neuromuscular [respiratory failure](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/demyelinating-and-neuromuscular-disease.md), large [strokes](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/stroke.md) and prognostication after brain injury and cardiac arrest, including the diagnosis of death by neurological criteria.
- **[Endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md):** diabetic emergencies, glycaemic control in the critically ill, and the relative adrenal insufficiency of septic shock.
- **[Obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md):** severe pre-eclampsia, obstetric haemorrhage and sepsis in [pregnancy](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md) are among the commonest reasons a young woman is admitted.
- **[Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md):** paediatric and [neonatal](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/the-newborn-and-neonatology.md) intensive care apply the same equation to bodies with different reserve, physiology and causes.
- **[Geriatrics and palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md):** whether to admit the frail older patient, the ceiling of treatment, and [withdrawal of support](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/death-dying-and-end-of-life-decisions.md) are decided together; a large fraction of intensive care deaths follow a decision to limit treatment.
- **[Psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) and [rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md):** the post-intensive-care syndrome of weakness, cognitive impairment, anxiety and post-traumatic stress is where survivors go.

## Connections across layers

Critical care takes from [06 Physiology](../../06-physiology/README.md) the [oxygen cascade](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/gas-exchange-and-transport.md), [haemodynamics](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md), [acid–base balance](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/acid-base-physiology.md) and [fluid compartments](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/body-fluid-compartments-and-electrolytes.md); from [08 Pathology](../../08-pathology/README.md) the mechanisms of [shock and sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md) and [cell death](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/cell-injury-adaptation-and-death.md); from [09 Pharmacology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/README.md) the vasoactive drugs, sedatives and antimicrobials whose [elimination](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/elimination-half-life-and-dosing.md) changes hourly as organs fail; from [10](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/README.md) the large [randomised trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md) that have repeatedly overturned physiologically plausible treatments. It gives to [14](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/README.md) the model of organ support adapted to each age and the end-of-life decision in its starkest form, and to [15](../../15-medicine-in-society/README.md) the most expensive bed in the hospital, the ethics of [capacity](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md) in the unconscious patient, and the [resource allocation](../../15-medicine-in-society/01-health-systems/health-economics-and-priority-setting.md) that a pandemic makes explicit.

## Cross-cutting threads

- [Supply and demand](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#supply-and-demand): the delivery equation is the specialty.
- [Compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation): the unit sees the patient after compensation has failed and must supply what the body no longer can.
- [Feedback and homeostasis](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#feedback-and-homeostasis): every infusion titrated against a monitor is a closed loop with a human controller.
- [Injury and repair](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#injury-and-repair): sepsis and multi-organ failure are the inflammatory response running without limit; recovery is repair given time.
- [Harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit): the ventilator injures the lung, the sedative causes delirium, the catheter causes infection; support is never free.
- [Time courses](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#time-courses): the first hour decides shock; the first week decides the organs; the first year decides the recovery.
- [Probability and uncertainty](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#probability-and-uncertainty): admission, prognosis and withdrawal are decisions under uncertainty about a single patient.
