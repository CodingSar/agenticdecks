## Essence

Shock is not low blood pressure. It is the state in which oxygen delivery to tissues falls below their consumption, so cells switch to anaerobic metabolism, lactate accumulates and organs begin to fail ([pathology of shock](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md)). Pressure is only a surrogate: the body defends it so well that pressure falls late, and a vasoconstricted patient can be dying with a normal reading. Delivery has three parts, cardiac output, haemoglobin and saturation, and cardiac output in turn depends on the volume returning to the heart, the heart's ability to pump it, and the resistance it pumps against. Every form of shock is a failure of one of these, every bedside sign and monitor reads one of them, and every drug or fluid moves one of them. Haemodynamic support is the discipline of identifying which term has failed and restoring it without breaking another.

## Core tenets

1. **Oxygen delivery is cardiac output times content, and shock is delivery below demand.** Delivery (DO2) ≈ cardiac output × haemoglobin × saturation × 1.34 mL/g; at rest about 1,000 mL/min against a consumption (VO2) of about 250 mL/min, so a quarter is extracted. When delivery falls, extraction rises first (mixed venous saturation falls from ~75%), then consumption becomes supply-dependent and lactate climbs. Lactate and the base deficit therefore measure the size of the oxygen debt, and their clearance over hours measures whether treatment is working; a single normal pressure measures neither.

2. **Four mechanisms, one equation.** Hypovolaemic shock (haemorrhage, burns, the acute abdomen's third-space loss) empties the circuit, so preload and stroke volume fall. Cardiogenic shock (infarction, arrhythmia, valve failure, [heart failure](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/heart-failure.md)) fails the pump, so output falls with a full circuit. Obstructive shock (tension pneumothorax, pericardial tamponade, massive pulmonary embolism) blocks flow into or out of the heart. Distributive shock (sepsis, anaphylaxis, spinal cord injury, vasodilator overdose) dilates and leaks the circuit, so resistance collapses and volume is lost into tissue while output may be high. The first three are states of low output with high resistance and cold skin; the last is high or normal output with low resistance and warm skin, until the heart fails too.

3. **Compensation defends pressure, so the early signs are the signs of compensation.** Baroreceptors drive sympathetic outflow ([blood pressure regulation](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md)): tachycardia, venoconstriction that returns stored blood, arteriolar constriction that diverts flow from skin, gut and kidney to brain and heart, and renin, antidiuretic hormone and aldosterone that retain salt and water. The patient is pale, cool, sweaty, thirsty and passing little urine, with a narrow pulse pressure and a slow capillary refill, long before the pressure drops. Hypotension means the reserve is spent, and the sudden fall is [decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation). The old, the β-blocked and the pregnant compensate differently and deceive accordingly.

4. **The bedside and the ultrasound separate the mechanisms.** Jugular venous pressure and the inferior vena cava on [ultrasound](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md) are low and collapsing in hypovolaemia, high and fixed in cardiogenic and obstructive shock. The echocardiogram shows the empty hyperdynamic ventricle, the dilated poorly contracting one, the compressed heart in a pericardial effusion, the dilated right ventricle of a pulmonary embolus, or the underfilled vigorous heart of early sepsis. The skin and the lactate measure delivery; the central venous saturation measures extraction. Together these replace the pulmonary artery catheter, which measured the same quantities invasively and, in trials, improved none of the outcomes it was meant to.

5. **Fluid works only if the heart is on the steep part of its curve.** Giving volume raises preload; by the Frank–Starling relation ([pump function](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/the-cardiac-cycle-and-pump-function.md)) that raises stroke volume only while the ventricle is underfilled. A patient on the flat part gains oedema, not output: in the lung (worse oxygenation), the gut (ileus), the kidney (raised venous pressure lowers filtration) and the tissues. Fluid responsiveness is therefore tested, by a small bolus or a passive leg raise, and measured as a change in stroke volume, rather than assumed. Crystalloid is the default; albumin has no survival advantage in most settings; in haemorrhage the fluid is blood ([blood products](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/fluids-nutrition-and-blood-products.md)). Positive fluid balance over days is associated with worse outcome, and de-resuscitation is part of recovery.

6. **Vasopressors raise resistance, inotropes raise contractility, and both have a price.** Noradrenaline, the default vasopressor in distributive shock, acts on α1-receptors to constrict arterioles and veins and on β1-receptors to modestly increase contractility, restoring the perfusion pressure that autoregulation needs ([autonomic drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/autonomic-and-cardiovascular-drugs.md)); vasopressin acts on V1 receptors and is spared the catecholamine tachyphylaxis. Inotropes (dobutamine, a β1 agonist; milrinone, a phosphodiesterase inhibitor) raise contractility and output in cardiogenic shock at the cost of oxygen demand and arrhythmia. Constricting a vessel raises pressure but can lower flow to the gut, kidney and digits; the aim is a mean arterial pressure at which organs autoregulate (around 65 mmHg in most adults, higher in the chronically hypertensive) and urine, lactate and mentation improving, not a number alone.

7. **The treatment is the cause; support only buys time.** No vasopressor fixes a bleeding spleen, a tamponade, an occluded coronary artery or an abscess. Haemorrhage needs [haemostasis](../01-surgery/trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md); tamponade needs a needle; tension pneumothorax a drain; massive embolism thrombolysis or embolectomy; an infarcted myocardium reperfusion ([ischaemic heart disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md)); septic shock antimicrobials and source control ([sepsis](sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md)); anaphylaxis adrenaline, which is the one case where the drug is the cure, acting on α1 to constrict, β2 to open bronchi and β2 on mast cells to stop the release. When the pump cannot recover fast enough, mechanical support (an intra-aortic balloon, a percutaneous ventricular assist device, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation) is a bridge to recovery, to a decision, or to transplantation, and the decision about what lies on the far side is made before it is started.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md) owns cardiogenic shock and the reperfusion that reverses it; [surgery](../01-surgery/README.md) and interventional radiology stop the bleeding and drain the source; [haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/transfusion-medicine.md) runs the massive transfusion; [emergency medicine](../04-emergency-medicine/README.md) begins the resuscitation and makes the first classification; [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/acute-kidney-injury.md) inherits the kidney that recorded the hypoperfusion; [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) and [neonatology](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/the-newborn-and-neonatology.md) apply the same classification to bodies that compensate differently.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Oxygen delivery (DO2) | Cardiac output × arterial oxygen content; about 1,000 mL/min at rest |
| Oxygen extraction ratio | Consumption divided by delivery; about 25% at rest, rising as delivery falls |
| Lactate and base deficit | Markers of anaerobic metabolism; their size and clearance measure oxygen debt and response |
| Hypovolaemic, cardiogenic, obstructive, distributive | The four mechanisms of shock, by which term of the delivery equation has failed |
| Preload, contractility, afterload | Filling, pump strength and resistance: the determinants of stroke volume |
| Fluid responsiveness | A rise in stroke volume with added preload; present only on the steep part of the Starling curve |
| Vasopressor | A drug that raises vascular resistance (noradrenaline, vasopressin) |
| Inotrope | A drug that raises contractility (dobutamine, milrinone) |
| Mean arterial pressure | Average perfusion pressure; the target below which organ autoregulation fails |
| Mechanical circulatory support | Devices that pump or oxygenate blood when the heart cannot: a bridge, not a treatment |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Shock means hypotension."** Shock is inadequate delivery; pressure is defended by compensation and falls late. Lactate, skin, urine and mentation show shock before the pressure does.
- **"Fluid is always the first treatment."** It helps only the fluid-responsive patient; in cardiogenic and some distributive shock it adds oedema without output, and in haemorrhage clear fluid dilutes clotting factors.
- **"A normal central venous pressure means adequate filling."** Static pressures predict fluid responsiveness poorly; a dynamic test (passive leg raise, a measured bolus) does better.
- **"Vasopressors damage the kidney."** Restoring perfusion pressure in a vasodilated patient improves renal blood flow; it is hypotension that injures the kidney.
