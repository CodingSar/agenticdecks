## Essence

The nose, pharynx and larynx are the shared entrance to the lungs and the gut: air and food cross in the pharynx, and the larynx exists to keep them apart while also producing the voice. The space is narrow, lined with lymphoid tissue that meets every inhaled and swallowed antigen, and surrounded by fascial planes along which infection spreads towards the mediastinum and the skull base ([head and neck anatomy](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/head-neck-and-spine.md)). The specialty's unifying problem is a tube that is too narrow: by Poiseuille's law a small reduction in radius causes a large rise in resistance, so swelling that would be trivial elsewhere obstructs breathing here, worst in the child. Its other great subjects are bleeding from the nose, infection of the sinuses, tonsils and deep neck spaces, the cancers of a mucosa exposed to tobacco, alcohol and human papillomavirus, and the loss of voice and swallowing that disease and its treatment leave behind.

## Core tenets

1. **Resistance rises with the fourth power of narrowing, so the upper airway obstructs catastrophically.** Flow through a tube falls with radius to the fourth power ([flow](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/fluids-pressure-and-flow.md)); halving the radius of the airway needs sixteen times the pressure for the same flow, and when flow becomes turbulent the penalty is worse. A 1 mm ring of oedema in an infant's subglottis (diameter about 4–5 mm) removes most of its cross-section, while the same oedema in an adult's larynx (about 15 mm) is a hoarse voice ([scale](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#scale-and-surface-to-volume)). Stridor, a harsh inspiratory noise, means turbulent flow through a critical narrowing at or above the larynx; it is a sign of impending obstruction, and the causes are oedema (croup, epiglottitis, anaphylaxis, inhalational burn), a foreign body, an abscess, a tumour or paralysed vocal cords. The treatment restores the radius (adrenaline and corticosteroids shrink oedema), bypasses the narrowing (intubation past it) or creates a new opening below it ([airway management](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/airway-and-ventilation-management.md)).

2. **The tracheostomy is a hole below the problem, and it changes the physiology of breathing.** A tube placed through the neck into the trachea bypasses the mouth, pharynx and larynx: it rescues the obstructed upper airway, lets a patient be [ventilated for weeks](../03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md) without a tube through the larynx, allows them to be awake, eat and eventually speak through a valve, and gives access for clearing secretions a weak patient cannot cough up. The price is loss of the nose's humidification and filtration, so secretions thicken; loss of the larynx's role in swallowing and the cough; a stoma that is a wound and a route for infection; and a tube that can block or displace, which is the commonest way a tracheostomy kills.

3. **The mucosa is the barrier, and its infections follow the anatomy.** The upper airway lining traps particles in mucus moved by cilia and presents antigens to the tonsils and adenoids ([barriers](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md)), so it is the body's most frequently infected surface. Viral rhinitis obstructs the sinus ostia and the Eustachian tube; the closed, mucus-filled sinus or middle ear then becomes a bacterial abscess (sinusitis, [otitis media](the-ear-hearing-and-balance.md)). Tonsillitis spreads to the peritonsillar space (quinsy), and from there, or from a tooth, into the deep fascial spaces of the neck, where it can compress the airway, thrombose the jugular vein or track down into the mediastinum; the ethmoid sinus is separated from the orbit by paper-thin bone, so sinusitis becomes orbital cellulitis that threatens the [eye](the-eye-and-vision-loss.md) and the cavernous sinus. Most upper respiratory infections are viral and self-limiting, which makes them the main battleground of [antimicrobial stewardship](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/antimicrobial-stewardship.md); the few bacterial complications are surgical.

4. **Epistaxis is a bleed from a vascular mucosa in a space that can be packed.** The nasal septum carries an anastomosis of branches from both carotid systems a few millimetres below a dry, exposed surface, so most nosebleeds are anterior, in Little's area, and stop with pressure or cautery. Posterior bleeds from the sphenopalatine artery are heavier, commoner in older, hypertensive and anticoagulated patients, and may need packing, endoscopic ligation or embolisation; in an anticoagulated patient a nosebleed can be a life-threatening haemorrhage ([haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/bleeding-and-clotting-disorders.md)).

5. **The larynx separates air from food and makes the voice, and both fail when its nerves do.** Swallowing is a reflex sequence in which the larynx rises, the epiglottis tilts and the vocal cords close while the bolus passes; the recurrent laryngeal nerves, which loop under the aorta and the subclavian artery and run beside the thyroid, move the cords. Damage to one nerve (thyroid surgery, a lung cancer at the apex, an aortic aneurysm) gives a breathy voice and aspiration; damage to both leaves the cords near the midline and the airway obstructed. Dysphagia after [stroke](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/stroke.md) or in [neurodegenerative disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/neurodegenerative-disease.md) causes the aspiration pneumonia that is often the terminal event.

6. **Head and neck cancer is a disease of exposed mucosa with two causes and a joint treatment.** Squamous carcinoma of the mouth, pharynx and larynx was historically a disease of tobacco and alcohol, which act synergistically on the whole exposed field; since the 1990s a rising fraction of oropharyngeal cancers in high-income countries is caused by Human Papillomavirus (HPV) type 16, occurs in younger non-smokers and has a better prognosis ([causes of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md), [common solid tumours](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md)). Because the organs involved carry speech, swallowing and the face, treatment is a trade between cure and function decided by a multidisciplinary team: surgery with reconstruction by a free flap, [radiotherapy](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/radiotherapy-and-radiation-biology.md), chemotherapy, and increasingly immunotherapy, with speech and swallowing therapy afterwards.

7. **The nose is also the organ of smell and of sleep.** During sleep, loss of pharyngeal muscle tone narrows the same collapsible tube described under [airway management](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/airway-and-ventilation-management.md), and in obstructive sleep apnoea it closes repeatedly, fragmenting sleep and driving hypertension, arrhythmia and daytime sleepiness ([sleep physiology](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/sleep-and-circadian-rhythms.md)); obesity, a small jaw and large tonsils are the risk factors, and a positive-pressure mask splinting the airway open is the treatment, with surgery on the tonsils and adenoids curing most children.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Anaesthesia](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) and [critical care](../03-critical-care/README.md) share the airway and the tracheostomy; [emergency medicine](../04-emergency-medicine/README.md) sees the nosebleed, the quinsy, the foreign body and the stridulous child first; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) owns most of the [common infections](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/common-childhood-illness.md); [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md), [radiotherapy](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/radiotherapy-and-radiation-biology.md) and [plastic surgery](../01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) share the cancer; [pulmonology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/README.md) and [sleep medicine](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/functional-testing-spirometry-exercise-and-sleep.md) share apnoea; [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) the dysphagia and the facial nerve; [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/thyroid-disease.md) the thyroid that Ear, Nose and Throat (ENT) surgeons remove; [allergy](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/allergy-and-immunodeficiency-in-practice.md) and [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) the rhinitis that fills most clinics.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stridor | Harsh inspiratory noise from turbulent flow through a critically narrowed upper airway |
| Croup and epiglottitis | Viral subglottic oedema and bacterial supraglottic swelling; the child's obstructing infections |
| Tracheostomy | A surgical airway through the neck below the larynx; bypasses obstruction, permits long ventilation |
| Deep neck space infection | Spread of tonsillar or dental infection along fascial planes towards the airway and mediastinum |
| Epistaxis | Nasal bleeding; anterior from the septal anastomosis, posterior from the sphenopalatine artery |
| Recurrent laryngeal nerve | Motor nerve to the vocal cords, running beside the thyroid and under the great vessels |
| Dysphagia and aspiration | Failed separation of food from the airway; pneumonia as its consequence |
| HPV-associated oropharyngeal cancer | Virally driven cancer of the tonsil and tongue base in younger non-smokers; better prognosis |
| Obstructive sleep apnoea | Repeated pharyngeal collapse in sleep; fragmented sleep and cardiovascular consequences |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Stridor means the airway is partly blocked, so there is time."** Stridor means the narrowing is already critical; the next step is obstruction, and the airway plan is made now.
- **"A sore throat or sinusitis needs an antibiotic."** Most are viral and self-limiting; antibiotics shorten symptoms by less than a day on average and drive resistance. The bacterial complications are surgical.
- **"A neck lump in an adult is probably benign."** A persistent painless node in an adult, especially a smoker or drinker, is metastatic cancer until the primary is sought in the mucosa.
- **"A tracheostomy is a safe airway."** It is a reliable one while it is in place and patent; blockage and displacement are common, and a displaced tube in a patient whose upper airway is obstructed is a crisis.
