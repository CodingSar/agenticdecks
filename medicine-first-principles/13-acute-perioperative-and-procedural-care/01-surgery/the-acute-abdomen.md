## Essence

The acute abdomen is sudden abdominal pain severe enough to raise the question of an operation. The abdomen is a closed [cavity](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/body-cavities-fascia-and-compartments.md) lined by peritoneum and filled with hollow tubes under pressure, solid organs under perfusion and a litre of bacteria behind a single epithelial layer. Four things go wrong: a tube is blocked, a wall is breached, a blood supply is cut off, or an organ inflames. Each declares itself through two different nervous systems, visceral and somatic, and the whole art is to read from the pattern of pain, tenderness and physiology which process is running, how fast, and whether it has crossed into the territory where delay costs bowel, blood or life.

## Core tenets

1. **Two kinds of pain map two kinds of injury.** Visceral peritoneum and hollow organs are innervated by autonomic afferents that signal stretch and ischaemia, not cutting; the pain is dull, poorly localised, felt in the midline at the embryological level of the organ (foregut epigastric, midgut periumbilical, hindgut suprapubic) and comes in waves (colic) when a muscular tube contracts against a block. The parietal peritoneum is innervated by somatic nerves of the overlying body wall, so pain from inflamed parietal peritoneum is sharp, localised and worse on movement. Appendicitis tells the story in order: periumbilical visceral pain as the lumen distends, then right iliac fossa somatic pain when the inflamed serosa touches the parietal peritoneum ([referred pain](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)).

2. **Peritonitis is inflammation of a large surface, and it behaves like a burn.** The peritoneum has an area approaching that of the skin. When it is inflamed by bacteria, bile, blood or enzymes, [acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md) makes its capillaries leak litres of protein-rich fluid into the cavity and the bowel wall, so the patient is hypovolaemic before any vomiting. The abdominal wall muscles contract reflexly over the inflamed segment (guarding) and then everywhere (rigidity); the patient lies still, because movement hurts. Generalised peritonitis from a perforated viscus is a [septic](../03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md) process with a clock measured in hours, and antibiotics alone cannot clear a cavity that is being continuously re-contaminated: source control is surgical.

3. **Obstruction is a pressure problem upstream of a block.** A blocked bowel continues to secrete and swallow air; proximal distension raises wall tension, which first provokes colic and vomiting, then, by the same Starling logic as any [oedema](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/edema-and-fluid-imbalance.md), impairs venous return from the wall, then arterial inflow. A simple obstruction kills slowly by dehydration and electrolyte loss; a closed-loop or strangulating obstruction (volvulus, an incarcerated hernia, an adhesive band) kills quickly by ischaemia and perforation. The clinical distinction is constant rather than colicky pain, peritonism, fever, tachycardia and a rising lactate: the signs of dying tissue.

4. **Ischaemia hurts out of proportion to what the hand finds.** When the mesenteric arterial supply fails (embolus, thrombosis, low flow in shock), visceral afferents signal severe pain while the parietal peritoneum is still untouched, so the abdomen is soft. The clue is severity against an unimpressive examination, often with atrial fibrillation or vascular disease, metabolic acidosis and a raised lactate ([infarction](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md)). Bowel tolerates only a few hours of warm ischaemia; by the time peritonism arrives the bowel is dead.

5. **Imaging has replaced the hand for localisation, but not for the decision to operate.** Erect radiography shows free gas under the diaphragm (perforation) and dilated loops with fluid levels (obstruction). [Computed tomography](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/radiography-and-computed-tomography.md) with intravenous contrast shows where and why: the transition point, the inflamed appendix, the non-enhancing ischaemic wall, free fluid. [Ultrasound](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md) is the first test for the gallbladder, the pregnant woman and the child. Blood tests measure consequence, not cause: white cell count and C-reactive protein for inflammation, lactate and base deficit for tissue hypoperfusion, amylase or lipase for the pancreas. The physiology decides urgency; the image decides the operation.

6. **Most acute abdomens do not need an operation, which is why the decision matters.** Pancreatitis, uncomplicated diverticulitis, pyelonephritis, biliary colic, inferior myocardial infarction, diabetic ketoacidosis and lower-lobe pneumonia all present with abdominal pain and are harmed by a laparotomy. The surgeon's task is to separate the processes that resolve (inflammation that will settle, obstruction that will relieve itself) from those that will not (perforation, strangulation, ischaemia, abscess), and to resuscitate during the interval: fluid to replace third-space loss, analgesia (which does not mask the signs, a long-standing misconception), antimicrobials once the diagnosis is infective.

7. **Who else is involved.** [Gastroenterology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) shares the [bleeding ulcer](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/acid-peptic-disease-and-gi-bleeding.md), [pancreatitis and biliary obstruction](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/pancreatic-and-biliary-disease.md), where endoscopy drains, stents and stops bleeding without an incision. Radiology both diagnoses and treats: a percutaneous drain cures many abscesses. [Gynaecology](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) owns the ectopic pregnancy and ovarian torsion that mimic appendicitis. [Critical care](../03-critical-care/README.md) receives the patient with faecal peritonitis, whose operation is the beginning rather than the end of treatment. [Vascular surgery](surgical-subspecialties.md) takes the ruptured aortic aneurysm that presents as back and abdominal pain.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Visceral pain | Dull midline pain from stretch or ischaemia of a hollow organ, carried by autonomic afferents |
| Somatic (parietal) pain | Sharp, localised pain from inflamed parietal peritoneum, carried by body-wall nerves |
| Colic | Pain in waves from a muscular tube contracting against an obstruction |
| Peritonism | Guarding, rigidity, rebound and percussion tenderness: signs of parietal peritoneal inflammation |
| Third-space loss | Fluid sequestered in inflamed peritoneum, bowel wall and lumen, lost to the circulation |
| Strangulation | Obstruction with compromised blood supply; the surgical emergency within obstruction |
| Perforation | Breach of a hollow viscus; free gas and contents in the cavity |
| Source control | Removing or draining the origin of infection; the surgical half of sepsis treatment |
| Transition point | On imaging, the junction between dilated and collapsed bowel that marks the site of obstruction |

## How it fits

- **Below:** embryological innervation explains the pain map ([segmental innervation](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)); [abdominal anatomy](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/abdomen-and-pelvis.md) gives the compartments; [inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md) and [infarction](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md) are the processes; the [gut barrier](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/the-gut-barrier-and-microbiome.md) is what perforation breaches.
- **Above:** the peritonitic patient becomes the [septic](../03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md) patient; the emergency department's [triage](../04-emergency-medicine/triage-and-the-abcde-approach.md) decides how fast the surgeon is called; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) and [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) see atypical presentations in bodies that localise pain poorly.
- **Siblings:** it is [the surgical decision](the-surgical-decision.md) under a clock set from within; [trauma](trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md) is the same cavity injured from without; the [subspecialties](surgical-subspecialties.md) take over once the organ is known.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Give no analgesia until the surgeon has examined the patient."** Trials since the 1990s show that opioid analgesia does not obscure peritoneal signs or reduce diagnostic accuracy; withholding it adds suffering without information.
- **"A soft abdomen means nothing serious."** Early mesenteric ischaemia, retroperitoneal processes (pancreatitis, a leaking aneurysm) and the abdomen of an older or immunosuppressed patient can be soft while tissue dies.
- **"A normal white cell count excludes a surgical cause."** Inflammatory markers lag the process and are blunted in the old, the very young and the immunosuppressed; physiology (pulse, lactate, urine output) is the better early guide.
