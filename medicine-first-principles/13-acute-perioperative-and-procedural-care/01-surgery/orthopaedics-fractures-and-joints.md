## Essence

Orthopaedics is surgery of the load-bearing frame: bone, cartilage, ligament, tendon and the joints they form. Its two halves are the fracture, a structural failure of a living material that can rebuild itself if held in the right position under the right load, and the worn or destroyed joint, which cannot and is replaced with an engineered one. Both halves rest on one idea from [bone physiology](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/bone-remodelling-and-joints.md): bone is a tissue that senses mechanical strain and remodels to it. Fixation that is too rigid, too loose or that starves the bone of blood fails for mechanical and biological reasons that are entirely predictable, and the emergencies of the specialty (the compartment syndrome, the open fracture, the septic joint, the hip fracture in the frail) are races between tissue viability and time.

## Core tenets

1. **A fracture is a material failure, and the pattern records the force.** Bone is a composite of mineral (stiff, strong in compression) and collagen (tough, strong in tension) ([mechanics and materials](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/mechanics-and-materials.md)). Bending produces a transverse fracture with a butterfly fragment on the compression side; torsion a spiral; axial compression a crush; and repetitive sub-threshold loading a stress fracture by fatigue. A fracture through bone that should have withstood the load (a hip from standing height, a vertebra from a cough) is pathological until proved otherwise: osteoporosis, metastasis or myeloma has weakened the material ([bone and calcium disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/bone-and-calcium-disorders.md)).

2. **Bone heals by regeneration, not scar, if the conditions are met.** A fracture haematoma becomes inflammatory granulation tissue, then a cartilaginous soft callus that is mineralised into hard callus and finally remodelled along the lines of stress over months to years ([tissue repair](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)). This indirect healing needs a little movement at the fracture (micromotion stimulates callus) and an intact blood supply, which comes from the periosteum and the medullary vessels. Rigid anatomical compression lets bone heal directly, without callus, by osteons crossing the gap. Non-union follows when the gap is too wide, the movement too great, the blood supply stripped, or the patient smokes, is malnourished, infected or on drugs that suppress osteoblasts.

3. **Reduce, hold, move: fixation is a mechanical and a biological choice.** The fragments are returned to alignment (reduction) and held (a cast, a plate and screws, an intramedullary nail, an external frame) long enough for callus to take the load, while adjacent joints are moved to prevent stiffness. Each device loads the bone differently: a plate shields it from stress and can cause the bone beneath to resorb, a nail shares load and permits early walking, a frame holds an infected or open fracture without putting metal in the wound ([implants and biomaterials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/devices-implants-and-biomaterials.md)). The surgeon's choice is which device gives the bone the strain it needs without failing before the bone can take over, in a patient who may or may not comply with restricted weight-bearing.

4. **Articular cartilage does not regenerate, so a joint that is destroyed is replaced.** Cartilage is avascular and its chondrocytes do not divide usefully in adults; damage from wear ([osteoarthritis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/osteoarthritis-gout-and-regional-pain.md)), inflammation ([rheumatoid arthritis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/inflammatory-arthritis.md)), infection or a fracture into the joint surface is permanent and leads to pain, stiffness and loss of function. Arthroplasty replaces the surfaces with metal, polyethylene or ceramic; the implant fails by wear debris provoking a macrophage reaction that loosens the bone around it, by infection on a foreign surface where biofilm defeats the immune system, or by dislocation. Hip and knee replacement are among the most cost-effective operations in medicine when measured in quality-adjusted life years, which is why their indication is pain and function, not the radiograph.

5. **The compartment syndrome is a pressure problem with a clock of hours.** Muscles lie in inextensible fascial [compartments](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/body-cavities-fascia-and-compartments.md). Bleeding or oedema after a fracture, crush or reperfusion raises the pressure inside until it exceeds capillary perfusion pressure; the muscle becomes ischaemic, swells further, and the loop closes. The cardinal sign is pain out of proportion and on passive stretch; pulses are preserved until far too late, because the arterial pressure is higher than the compartment pressure long after the capillaries have closed. Muscle survives only a few hours; the treatment is to open the fascia, and the cost of delay is a dead, contracted limb and myoglobin in the kidney.

6. **An open fracture and a septic joint are infections waiting to happen, and time is the dose.** An open fracture is contaminated bone with a stripped blood supply; infected bone (osteomyelitis) is hard to clear because bacteria sit in dead bone and biofilm where antimicrobials and phagocytes cannot reach, so early debridement of dead tissue, soft-tissue cover and antimicrobials together decide the outcome. A septic joint destroys cartilage within days through bacterial and neutrophil enzymes; a single hot, painful, immobile joint is aspirated and treated as infected until the fluid proves otherwise, because crystal arthritis and sepsis look identical from the outside.

7. **The hip fracture is the model of surgery in a frail body.** It is common (roughly 1 in 3 women and 1 in 5 men over 50 will sustain an osteoporotic fracture; the hip is the most serious), it follows a fall in a patient with little reserve, and its mortality at one year has been around 20–30% in most series, mostly from medical complications rather than the fracture. Early operation (within a day or two) lets the patient sit, breathe and walk, which prevents the pneumonia, pressure sores and thromboembolism that immobility causes. The service that achieves this is orthopaedics plus [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md) plus [anaesthesia](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/perioperative-risk-and-complications.md), and the secondary prevention (bone protection, falls assessment) belongs to all of them.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Rheumatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) shares every joint until it needs replacing and every hot joint until the aspirate is back; [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md) treats the osteoporosis behind the fracture; [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md) for the pathological fracture through a metastasis; [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) and neurosurgery for the spine, where a fracture or a disc can compress the cord; [plastic surgery](surgical-subspecialties.md) for soft-tissue cover of the open tibia; [sports medicine](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/sports-occupational-and-lifestyle-medicine.md) and [rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md) for the long tail, because an operation on the frame only works if the patient then uses it.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fracture pattern | Transverse, oblique, spiral, comminuted, compression: the geometry that records the force |
| Pathological fracture | Fracture through bone weakened by disease, under a load normal bone would bear |
| Callus | The cartilaginous then bony bridge that stabilises a fracture healing indirectly |
| Reduction and fixation | Restoring alignment and holding it; closed or open, internal or external |
| Non-union | Failure of a fracture to heal in the expected time from mechanical or biological causes |
| Stress shielding | Bone loss under an implant that carries the load the bone would otherwise sense |
| Arthroplasty | Replacement of a joint surface with an implant |
| Compartment syndrome | Pressure in a closed fascial compartment exceeding capillary perfusion pressure; ischaemia of muscle and nerve |
| Open fracture | A fracture communicating with the skin wound; contaminated and at risk of osteomyelitis |
| Septic arthritis | Bacterial infection of a joint; cartilage destruction within days |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A palpable pulse excludes compartment syndrome."** The compartment pressure closes capillaries long before it exceeds arterial pressure; pain on passive stretch is the sign, and pulselessness is a terminal one.
- **"Stronger fixation is better fixation."** Absolute rigidity abolishes the micromotion that drives callus and shields the bone from the strain it needs; the right construct matches stiffness to the biology.
- **"Joint replacement is indicated by the radiograph."** Radiographic severity correlates poorly with symptoms; the indication is pain and lost function that conservative measures have failed to relieve.
- **"Osteomyelitis is cured by antibiotics."** Dead bone and biofilm are beyond the reach of drugs and phagocytes; without removing them the infection recurs.
