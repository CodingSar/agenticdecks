## Essence

A burn is a wound that becomes a systemic disease when it is large enough. Heat, chemicals or electricity kill skin in proportion to temperature and time, and dead skin is a [barrier](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/the-skin-as-barrier-and-sensor.md) lost to water, heat and bacteria. Beyond roughly a fifth of the body surface the [inflammatory response](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md) stops being local: every capillary bed leaks, plasma volume collapses within hours, and the body enters the most prolonged hypermetabolic state in medicine. The organising idea is **area and depth**: area predicts the systemic illness and the fluid needed; depth predicts whether the skin will heal itself or must be replaced.

## Core tenets

1. **Depth decides whether the skin can regrow.** The epidermis is renewed from stem cells in its basal layer and in the hair follicles and sweat glands that reach into the dermis. A superficial partial-thickness burn (blistered, blanching, very painful) re-epithelialises from surviving follicles within two weeks; a deep partial-thickness burn (mottled, slow to blanch) heals slowly from few appendages and scars badly; a full-thickness burn (white or charred, leathery, insensate because the nerve endings are dead) cannot re-epithelialise except from its edges and needs a graft. Depth is underestimated on day one because the zone of stasis around the dead core reperfuses or dies over 48 hours.

2. **Area decides the systemic response, and it is estimated, not measured.** Burned area is a percentage of total body surface area, by the rule of nines (head and each arm 9%, each leg and each side of the trunk 18%) or by charts corrected for a child's larger head. Above about 15–20% in adults (10% in children) the burn needs intravenous fluid; mortality climbs with area, age and inhalation injury.

3. **Burn shock is a capillary leak, and the fluid logic follows from it.** Injured and inflamed capillaries leak protein-rich fluid for the first day ([capillary exchange](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/microcirculation-and-capillary-exchange.md)); in a large burn the leak is everywhere, so litres are lost into oedema and more by evaporation, and hypovolaemic [shock](../03-critical-care/shock-and-haemodynamic-support.md) follows hours later. Resuscitation formulas scale crystalloid to weight and area over 24 hours, front-loaded while the leak is fastest, then adjusted against urine output. Too little deepens the burn and injures the kidney; too much ("fluid creep") swells the airway, limbs and gut ([dose–response](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#dose-response)).

4. **The hypermetabolic response outlasts the wound.** From a few days onward catecholamines, cortisol and glucagon drive a metabolic rate up to double normal, with muscle catabolism, insulin resistance and a raised temperature set point ([stress hormones](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md)), persisting for months after closure. Treatment reverses the mechanism: early high-protein enteral feeding, a warm room so energy is not spent on heat, early wound closure to remove the stimulus, and β-blockade to blunt the catecholamine drive.

5. **Inhalation injury is three injuries and the first to kill.** Hot gas burns the upper airway, which swells over hours into obstruction, so a burned face, singed nasal hair or stridor means the tube goes in before the larynx closes ([airway management](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/airway-and-ventilation-management.md)). Smoke injures the lower airway chemically, leading to pneumonia and [acute respiratory distress syndrome](../03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md). And combustion gases poison: carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin with about 200–250 times the affinity of oxygen while the pulse oximeter reads normal, and cyanide from burning plastics blocks mitochondrial respiration ([poisoning](../04-emergency-medicine/poisoning-and-overdose.md)). Inhalation injury roughly doubles mortality for any burn size.

6. **Dead skin is a culture medium and a tourniquet.** Eschar is avascular protein that bacteria colonise within days; [sepsis](../03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md) is the leading cause of death after the first days, and because antimicrobials cannot reach dead tissue the treatment is excision, not prophylaxis. A circumferential full-thickness burn also contracts as it dries while oedema rises beneath it, until a limb loses perfusion or the chest cannot expand; an escharotomy, an incision through the burn, releases it, by the logic of the [compartment syndrome](orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md).

7. **The treatment of a deep burn is to replace the skin.** Early excision and cover with a split-thickness autograft (a shaving of epidermis and upper dermis, meshed to cover a larger area) cut stay and mortality from the 1970s onward. The graft survives by diffusion, then by vessel ingrowth, so it fails on infected or poorly perfused beds ([plastic surgery](surgical-subspecialties.md)); dermal substitutes bridge the gap when donor sites run out.

8. **The scar is the lifelong disease, and who else is involved.** Deep burns heal as hypertrophic scars that contract across joints ([fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)); pressure garments, splinting and release surgery continue for years. [Emergency medicine](../04-emergency-medicine/README.md) cools, covers and estimates; [anaesthesia](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) secures the airway and runs the many operations; [critical care](../03-critical-care/README.md) carries the systemic illness; [infectious diseases](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md) manage the unit's resistant flora; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) sees scalds and considers [non-accidental injury](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/adolescent-health-and-safeguarding.md); [rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md) and [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) own the years after. 

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Burn depth | Superficial, superficial partial, deep partial or full thickness; decides healing versus grafting |
| Total body surface area | Percentage of skin burned, by the rule of nines or a chart; drives fluid and prognosis |
| Burn shock | Delayed hypovolaemia from generalised capillary leak and evaporative loss |
| Hypermetabolic response | Months of catecholamine-driven raised energy expenditure and muscle catabolism |
| Inhalation injury | Upper airway thermal, lower airway chemical, and carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning |
| Escharotomy | A releasing incision through constricting dead burned tissue |
| Split-thickness skin graft | Epidermis and upper dermis shaved from a donor site and laid on the excised wound |

## Common misconceptions

- **"The most painful burn is the worst burn."** A full-thickness burn is painless because the nerve endings are dead; the exquisitely painful burn is superficial.
- **"A burned patient in shock on arrival is in burn shock."** Burn shock takes hours; shock in the first hour means another injury, usually haemorrhage.
- **"Normal oxygen saturation excludes carbon monoxide poisoning."** The oximeter cannot tell carboxyhaemoglobin from oxyhaemoglobin.
