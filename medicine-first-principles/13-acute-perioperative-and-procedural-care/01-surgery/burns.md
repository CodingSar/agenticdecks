## Essence

A burn is a wound that becomes a systemic disease when it is large enough. Heat, chemicals, electricity or radiation kill skin in proportion to temperature and time, and the dead skin is a [barrier](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/the-skin-as-barrier-and-sensor.md) lost: water evaporates, heat escapes, bacteria enter and pain is unopposed. Beyond roughly a fifth of the body surface, the [inflammatory response](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md) stops being local. Every capillary bed leaks, plasma volume collapses within hours, and the body enters the most prolonged hypermetabolic state in medicine, which lasts for months. The organising idea is **area and depth**: area predicts the systemic illness and the fluid the patient needs; depth predicts whether the skin will heal itself or must be replaced by a graft.

## Core tenets

1. **Depth decides whether the skin can regrow.** The epidermis is renewed from stem cells in its basal layer and in the hair follicles and sweat glands that reach into the dermis. A superficial burn (epidermis only, red and painful) heals in days; a superficial partial-thickness burn (into upper dermis, blistered, blanching, very painful) re-epithelialises from surviving follicles within two weeks with little scar; a deep partial-thickness burn (into deep dermis, mottled, slow to blanch) heals slowly from few remaining appendages and scars badly; a full-thickness burn (through the dermis, white or charred, leathery, insensate because the nerve endings are dead) cannot re-epithelialise except from its edges and needs a graft. Depth is often underestimated on day one because the burn continues to evolve as the zone of stasis around the dead core either reperfuses or dies.

2. **Area decides the systemic response, and it is estimated, not measured.** Burned area is expressed as a percentage of total body surface area, by the rule of nines in adults (head 9%, each arm 9%, each leg 18%, front and back of trunk 18% each) or by charts corrected for a child's proportionally larger head. Above about 15–20% in adults (10% in children) the burn is a systemic illness that needs intravenous fluid, and mortality climbs with area, age and inhalation injury together.

3. **Burn shock is a capillary leak, and the fluid logic follows from it.** Heat-injured and inflamed capillaries leak protein-rich fluid for the first day or so ([capillary exchange](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/microcirculation-and-capillary-exchange.md)); in large burns the leak is everywhere, so a patient loses litres into oedema while losing more by evaporation from the wound. The result is hypovolaemic [shock](../03-critical-care/shock-and-haemodynamic-support.md) that is delayed by hours, not immediate. Resuscitation formulas scale crystalloid volume to body weight and burned area over the first 24 hours, with roughly half given in the first eight hours when the leak is fastest, and are then adjusted against urine output, because the formula is a starting estimate. Too little fluid deepens the burn and injures the kidney; too much ("fluid creep") worsens oedema in the airway, the limbs and the gut. Fluid is a drug with a [dose–response](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#dose-response).

4. **The hypermetabolic response outlasts the wound.** From a few days onward catecholamines, cortisol and glucagon drive a metabolic rate up to double normal in large burns, with muscle protein breakdown, insulin resistance and a core temperature set point raised by about 2 °C ([stress hormones](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md)). It persists for months after the wound has closed. The treatments reverse the mechanism: early enteral feeding at high energy and protein, a warm environment so the body does not spend energy on heat, early wound closure to remove the stimulus, and β-blockade, which blunts the catecholamine drive and reduces muscle loss.

5. **Inhalation injury is three injuries and the first to kill.** Hot gas burns the upper airway, which swells over hours into obstruction, so a burned face, singed nasal hair or stridor means a tube is placed before the swelling closes the larynx ([airway management](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/airway-and-ventilation-management.md)). Smoke products injure the lower airway chemically, causing bronchospasm, sloughed mucosa and later pneumonia and [acute respiratory distress syndrome](../03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md). And the combustion gases poison: carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin with about 200–250 times the affinity of oxygen while the pulse oximeter reads normal, and cyanide from burning plastics blocks mitochondrial respiration ([poisoning](../04-emergency-medicine/poisoning-and-overdose.md)). Inhalation injury roughly doubles the mortality of a burn of any given size.

6. **Dead skin is a culture medium and a tourniquet.** The burn eschar is avascular protein that bacteria colonise within days, and once the barrier is gone the burned patient has no defence against the organisms on their own skin and in the unit; [sepsis](../03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md) is the leading cause of death after the first days. Antimicrobials cannot reach dead tissue, so the treatment is excision of eschar and closure of the wound, not prophylaxis. A circumferential full-thickness burn of a limb or the chest also contracts as it dries, and with oedema rising beneath it the limb loses its perfusion or the chest cannot expand: an escharotomy, a full-thickness incision through the burn, releases the pressure within hours, by the same logic as a fasciotomy for [compartment syndrome](orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md).

7. **The treatment of a deep burn is to replace the skin.** Early excision of dead tissue and cover with a split-thickness autograft (a shaving of the patient's own epidermis and upper dermis, meshed to cover a larger area) shortened hospital stay and reduced mortality from the 1970s onward. The graft survives by diffusion for the first days and then by vessel ingrowth, so it fails on infected or poorly perfused beds ([plastic surgery](surgical-subspecialties.md)). When donor sites are scarce, cadaveric or synthetic dermal substitutes and cultured keratinocytes bridge the gap. Deep partial-thickness burns are the judgement call: wait for healing with a worse scar, or graft early.

8. **The scar is the lifelong disease, and who else is involved.** Healed deep burns produce hypertrophic scars that contract across joints, itch and disfigure ([repair and fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)); pressure garments, splinting, stretching and later release surgery continue for years, and the psychological injury is as lasting. [Emergency medicine](../04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and prehospital teams cool, cover and estimate; [anaesthesia](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) secures the airway and runs the many operations; [critical care](../03-critical-care/README.md) carries the systemic illness; [infectious diseases](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md) manage the resistant flora of a burns unit; dietetics feeds the hypermetabolism; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) sees scalds and must consider [non-accidental injury](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/adolescent-health-and-safeguarding.md); [rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md) and [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) own the years after. Burns are concentrated in regional centres because this team cannot exist everywhere.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Burn depth | Superficial, superficial partial, deep partial or full thickness; decides healing versus grafting |
| Total body surface area | The percentage of skin burned, estimated by the rule of nines or a chart; drives fluid and prognosis |
| Zone of stasis | The ischaemic tissue around the dead core that may survive or die over 48 hours |
| Burn shock | Delayed hypovolaemia from generalised capillary leak and evaporative loss |
| Fluid creep | Over-resuscitation that worsens oedema, airway swelling and compartment pressure |
| Hypermetabolic response | Months of catecholamine-driven raised energy expenditure and muscle catabolism |
| Inhalation injury | Upper airway thermal injury, lower airway chemical injury and carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning |
| Eschar and escharotomy | The dead burned tissue; the releasing incision through it when it constricts |
| Split-thickness skin graft | Epidermis and upper dermis shaved from a donor site and laid on the excised wound |
| Hypertrophic scar and contracture | Raised, thickened scar that shortens across joints after deep burns |

## Common misconceptions

- **"The most painful burn is the worst burn."** A full-thickness burn is painless because the nerve endings are dead; the exquisitely painful burn is superficial.
- **"A burned patient in shock on arrival is in burn shock."** Burn shock takes hours to develop; shock in the first hour means another injury, usually haemorrhage, and is treated as [trauma](trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md).
- **"Normal oxygen saturation excludes carbon monoxide poisoning."** The oximeter cannot distinguish carboxyhaemoglobin from oxyhaemoglobin; a blood gas with co-oximetry is needed.
