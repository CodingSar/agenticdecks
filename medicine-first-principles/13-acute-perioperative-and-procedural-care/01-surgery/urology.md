## Essence

Urology is the surgery of a plumbing system: two filters, two tubes, a muscular reservoir and an outflow that in men passes through a gland that grows for life. Nearly everything the specialty treats is one of four events in that system. A tube is blocked (stone, prostate, stricture), and pressure backs up to the filter. The reservoir fails to hold or fails to empty (incontinence, retention). The lining bleeds (haematuria), which is cancer until proved otherwise. Or the gonads and their glands fail or turn malignant. The organising idea is **pressure upstream of a block**: because the kidney filters only while the pressure in Bowman's space is lower than in the glomerulus, any obstruction between calyx and urethra threatens filtration, and an obstruction that is also infected is one of the few surgical emergencies measured in hours.

## Core tenets

1. **Obstruction stops filtration by raising the pressure downstream of the glomerulus.** Filtration depends on glomerular capillary pressure exceeding the hydrostatic pressure in the tubule ([glomerular filtration](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/glomerular-filtration-and-clearance.md)); a blocked ureter raises tubular pressure until net filtration falls towards zero, the collecting system dilates (hydronephrosis, visible on [ultrasound](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md)) and, if unrelieved for weeks, the nephrons are lost. One obstructed kidney is silent in the blood tests because the other compensates ([redundancy](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#redundancy-and-reserve)); bilateral obstruction, or obstruction of a single kidney, presents as [acute kidney injury](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/acute-kidney-injury.md). Drainage, by a stent passed up the ureter or a nephrostomy through the flank, restores filtration within hours.

2. **An obstructed, infected kidney is an abscess under pressure.** Bacteria behind a block multiply in stagnant urine, the pressure forces them and their endotoxin into the renal veins, and the patient becomes [septic](../03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md) faster than with almost any other infection. Antimicrobials cannot sterilise an undrained, pressurised space, so fever, loin pain and a dilated system on imaging mean decompression that night; the stone itself can wait.

3. **Stones form when urine is supersaturated, and they declare themselves by colic.** Calcium oxalate (most), uric acid, struvite (infection) and cystine stones precipitate when solute concentration exceeds solubility, promoted by low urine volume, hypercalciuria, acid or alkaline urine and infection ([water and solutions](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/water-solutions-and-concentration.md)). A stone moving down the ureter stretches it and the smooth muscle contracts against the block, producing colic radiating from loin to groin ([referred pain](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)). Most stones under about 5 mm pass; larger ones are fragmented by shock waves focused from outside ([acoustics](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md)), by a laser passed up the ureter, or removed percutaneously. Recurrence is common, so the metabolic cause is sought and water is the main treatment.

4. **The prostate enlarges with age, and the bladder pays.** Benign prostatic enlargement is stromal and glandular growth driven by dihydrotestosterone in a gland that surrounds the urethra; the outflow narrows, the detrusor hypertrophies to push through it ([smooth muscle](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/smooth-muscle.md)), and symptoms follow: hesitancy, poor stream, frequency, nocturia and incomplete emptying. The drugs reverse the mechanism: α1-blockers relax prostatic smooth muscle within days; 5α-reductase inhibitors shrink the gland over months. When the bladder decompensates, acute retention (a painful, palpable bladder, relieved by a catheter) or chronic retention with a large painless residual and back-pressure on the kidneys follows; resection or ablation of the obstructing tissue is the surgical remedy.

5. **Incontinence has distinct mechanisms, each with a different treatment.** Stress incontinence is leakage when abdominal pressure exceeds a weak urethral closure (pelvic floor damage after childbirth, after prostatectomy in men); urge incontinence is detrusor overactivity; overflow is a full, failing bladder; and functional incontinence is a working bladder in a person who cannot reach the toilet ([geriatric syndromes](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md)). Neurological disease (spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, diabetes) disconnects the bladder from the pontine centre that coordinates it, producing a bladder that is overactive, underactive or both ([autonomic nervous system](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/the-autonomic-nervous-system.md)). History, a residual volume scan and sometimes pressure-flow studies separate them.

6. **Blood in the urine is a cancer until proved otherwise.** Painless visible haematuria in an adult is investigated by imaging the upper tract and looking inside the bladder with a cystoscope, because urothelial carcinoma of the bladder, driven by tobacco and industrial amines excreted in urine ([causes of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md)), presents this way in most cases. Most bladder cancers are superficial and are resected through the urethra, then kept under surveillance with intravesical chemotherapy or BCG immunotherapy because they recur; invasion into muscle changes the disease to one needing removal of the bladder or radiotherapy ([staging](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/tumour-classification-grading-and-staging.md)).

7. **Prostate and testicular cancers sit at opposite ends of the natural-history spectrum.** Prostate cancer is the commonest male cancer in high-income countries, is present at autopsy in most old men, and ranges from indolent disease that is watched to lethal disease that spreads to bone; prostate-specific antigen screening finds many of the former, which is why it is the textbook case of [overdiagnosis](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md), and androgen deprivation controls advanced disease by removing the hormone the tumour grew on. Testicular germ-cell tumours are the commonest cancer of young men, present as a painless lump, and are cured in over 95% of cases even when metastatic, by orchidectomy and platinum chemotherapy ([common solid tumours](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md)). Renal cell carcinoma, often found incidentally on imaging, is treated by resection because it responds poorly to chemotherapy.

8. **Male infertility and who else is involved.** A male factor contributes in about 40–50% of infertile couples; semen analysis is the first test, and the causes are failed production (varicocele, undescended testes, chemotherapy, hypogonadism), obstruction or ejaculatory failure ([fertility](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md)). Urology shares the kidney with [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md) (obstruction versus intrinsic disease, transplant surgery), its cancers with [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md) and radiotherapy, the pelvic floor and incontinence with [gynaecology](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md), the incontinent and retaining older patient with [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md), the septic obstructed kidney with [critical care](../03-critical-care/README.md), the neuropathic bladder with [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md), and the haematuria and prostate question with [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hydronephrosis | Dilatation of the renal collecting system from obstruction below it |
| Obstructive uropathy | Loss of filtration from raised pressure downstream of the glomerulus |
| Nephrostomy and ureteric stent | Drainage of an obstructed kidney through the flank or along the ureter |
| Renal colic | Waves of loin-to-groin pain from ureteric smooth muscle contracting against a stone |
| Lithotripsy | Fragmentation of stones by focused shock waves or laser |
| Benign prostatic enlargement | Age-related androgen-driven growth narrowing the prostatic urethra |
| Urinary retention | Failure to empty the bladder; acute (painful) or chronic (painless, with back-pressure) |
| Urothelial carcinoma | Cancer of the lining of the urinary tract, presenting as painless haematuria |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A normal creatinine means the kidneys are not obstructed."** One obstructed kidney leaves the creatinine normal while it is being destroyed; imaging, not blood, detects it.
- **"Antibiotics treat an infected obstructed kidney."** They cannot sterilise a pressurised undrained space; drainage is the treatment and the antibiotic is the adjunct.
- **"Painless haematuria that stops can be ignored."** Bladder cancer bleeds intermittently; the first episode is the one to investigate.
