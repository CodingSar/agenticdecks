## Essence

An operation is a deliberate injury accepted because the disease it removes, repairs or bypasses is worse. The body cannot tell a scalpel from a knife: both provoke the same [acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md), the same neuroendocrine stress response and the same programme of [repair](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md). The surgical decision is therefore an explicit comparison of two natural histories: the disease left alone, and the disease plus a wound in a patient whose reserve may or may not carry the load. Everything else in surgery (technique, asepsis, timing, the team around the table) exists to shrink the cost side of that comparison.

## Core tenets

1. **Surgery is controlled injury, and the body responds to it as to any injury.** Tissue damage releases damage-associated molecular patterns that trigger [pattern recognition](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md) and the inflammatory cascade, while afferent nerves from the wound drive the hypothalamus. The result, described by Cuthbertson in 1932 as an "ebb" then a "flow" phase, is a surge of catecholamines, cortisol, glucagon and growth hormone with relative insulin resistance ([stress hormones](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md)): hyperglycaemia, protein catabolism, lipolysis, salt and water retention through antidiuretic hormone and aldosterone, and a raised metabolic rate for days. Its magnitude scales with the tissue injured, which is why a laparoscopic operation is physiologically smaller than the same operation done open.

2. **The wound heals by one programme whose failure modes are predictable.** Haemostasis, inflammation, proliferation (fibroblasts, angiogenesis, epithelial migration) and remodelling follow in order over weeks to months. A healed scar regains at most about 70–80% of the original tensile strength, never 100%. Anything that starves the programme of oxygen, substrate or cells impairs it: hypoperfusion, hypoxia, infection, diabetes, malnutrition, smoking, corticosteroids, irradiated tissue. An anastomotic leak, a burst abdomen and a non-union are all the same event, failure of repair, in different tissues.

3. **Infection is the price of breaching the barrier, and asepsis buys it down.** Skin and gut epithelium are [barriers](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md); an incision opens one and bowel surgery opens two. Operations are classed clean, clean-contaminated, contaminated and dirty, and surgical site infection rates rise across the classes from roughly 1–2% to well over 20%. Sterile technique reduces the inoculum, haemostasis removes the dead tissue and blood that bacteria feed on, and a single pre-incision dose of an antimicrobial puts tissue concentrations above the inhibitory level at the moment contamination happens ([antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md)). Prophylaxis cannot rescue a technique that leaves ischaemic tissue and haematoma behind.

4. **The decision is natural history against natural history, weighted by reserve.** The operation is indicated when the expected course without it (perforation, obstruction, metastasis, pain, loss of function) is worse than the expected course with it, including the wound, the anaesthetic and the complications. The patient's side of the equation is functional reserve: cardiac, respiratory, renal and cognitive capacity to meet the stress response. A hernia repair is low risk in a fit forty-year-old and may be the final insult in a frail ninety-year-old with heart failure. [Perioperative risk](../02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/perioperative-risk-and-complications.md) is how that reserve is measured.

5. **What can be removed is set by what remains.** [Redundancy](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#redundancy-and-reserve) is the surgeon's licence. A kidney, a lung, a spleen, most of the stomach and, because the liver regenerates, up to roughly two thirds of a healthy liver can be removed, because the remainder can meet demand. The limit is physiological, not anatomical: before a lung resection the predicted postoperative function of the remaining lung is estimated with [spirometry and exercise testing](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/functional-testing-spirometry-exercise-and-sleep.md), and a cirrhotic liver cannot spare what a healthy one can.

6. **Timing is set by the disease's clock, not the surgeon's.** Elective surgery allows weeks to optimise anaemia, glycaemia, nutrition and smoking. Urgent surgery allows hours of resuscitation. Emergency surgery allows none, because the disease (ischaemic bowel, a bleeding vessel, rising intracranial pressure) is consuming reserve faster than resuscitation restores it. The judgement is when preparation stops improving the odds and starts costing tissue.

7. **The intention of an operation changes what counts as success.** Curative surgery is judged by whether the disease is gone (resection margins, [staging](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/tumour-classification-grading-and-staging.md)); palliative surgery by symptom relief against operative cost; diagnostic surgery by whether it changed management; prophylactic surgery (a colectomy for familial polyposis) by risk avoided over a lifetime. A technically perfect operation that does not serve its intention has failed.

8. **The decision belongs to a team and to the patient.** The anaesthetist assesses and modifies the cost side; the intensivist decides whether the aftermath can be supported; the radiologist increasingly offers a percutaneous alternative; in cancer the multidisciplinary tumour board sequences surgery against [systemic therapy](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/systemic-anticancer-therapy.md); in the older patient the geriatrician weighs [frailty](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/frailty-multimorbidity-and-polypharmacy.md). The patient weighs all of it against their own values in a decision that, unlike a drug, cannot be stopped once begun ([consent](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stress response | The neuroendocrine and metabolic reaction to injury: catecholamines, cortisol, insulin resistance, catabolism, fluid retention |
| Ebb and flow | Cuthbertson's two phases: early hypometabolic shock-like state, then days of hypermetabolism |
| Primary intention | Healing of an apposed, closed wound by minimal granulation |
| Secondary intention | Healing of an open wound by granulation, contraction and epithelialisation |
| Surgical site infection | Infection of the incision or operated space within about 30 days (longer with implants) |
| Wound class | Clean, clean-contaminated, contaminated, dirty: the expected bacterial load at operation |
| Functional reserve | The capacity of an organ system to increase output under stress beyond resting need |
| Elective, urgent, emergency | Operations timed by choice, within hours, or immediately because the disease sets the clock |
| Intention of surgery | Curative, palliative, diagnostic, prophylactic or reconstructive: what success means |
| Multidisciplinary team | Joint decision by surgeon, anaesthetist, oncologist, radiologist and others |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A small operation carries a small risk."** Risk is the product of the procedure and the patient. The same inguinal hernia repair is trivial in one body and lethal in another with no reserve.
- **"Antibiotics make asepsis less important."** Prophylaxis lowers infection from a given inoculum; it does not sterilise dead tissue or a haematoma, and it selects resistant flora when prolonged.
- **"A healed wound is as good as new."** Scar is fibrous tissue without the architecture of the original: weaker, inelastic, and in the gut, liver or heart not functional parenchyma.
- **"The operation went well, so the patient did well."** Most postoperative deaths are medical (myocardial infarction, pneumonia, thromboembolism, renal failure) and follow a technically successful operation.
