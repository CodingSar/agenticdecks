# Social Determinants and Health Inequity

## Essence

In every society with data, health follows social position step by step: each rung down in income, education or occupation brings shorter life and more disease, with no threshold below which the effect stops and no ceiling above which it ends. In England in 2018–2020 men in the most deprived tenth of areas lived about 9.7 years less than men in the least deprived tenth; in the United States, Chetty and colleagues (2016) found a 14.6-year gap between the richest and poorest 1% of men. Social determinants are the conditions in which people are born, grow, work and age; health inequity is the part of the resulting difference that is avoidable and unfair. The idea that makes the subject tractable is Rose's: **the causes of cases differ from the causes of incidence**, and most of the gradient is explained not by who is in the clinic but by what happens before anyone arrives.

## Core tenets

1. **It is a gradient, not a poverty line.** The Whitehall studies of British civil servants (Marmot and colleagues, from 1978) found that coronary mortality rose with each step down the grade, among men who all had secure jobs, housing and healthcare, and the gradient persisted after adjusting for smoking, blood pressure and cholesterol. A threshold model would predict a flat line above poverty; the data show a slope all the way up, which rules out explanations that work only for the destitute.

2. **The gradient is carried through ordinary biology.** Social position reaches the body by three routes. *Material*: damp housing, air pollution, food quality, hazardous work, cold. *Behavioural*: smoking, diet, alcohol and inactivity are patterned by class, and account for perhaps a third to a half of the gradient. *Psychosocial*: low control, insecurity and chronic threat sustain [cortisol and sympathetic activation](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md), raising blood pressure, abdominal fat, insulin resistance and inflammation (allostatic load; McEwen, 1998). Each route ends in [multifactorial disease](../../08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/multifactorial-disease-and-risk.md).

3. **Exposure accumulates over a life course and starts before birth.** Low birth weight predicts adult cardiovascular disease and diabetes (Barker, from 1986); adverse childhood experiences predict adult mental illness, addiction and chronic disease in a dose-dependent way (Felitti and colleagues, 1998). Disadvantage at each stage raises the probability of disadvantage at the next, so the gradient in old age is the integral of a lifetime.

4. **Medical care reaches the gradient last and least.** McKeown (1976) showed that mortality from tuberculosis and most infections in England fell by most of its total before any effective treatment or vaccine existed, through nutrition, sanitation and housing. Healthcare matters more now, but estimates still attribute only a minority of variation in population health to clinical care, and the inverse care law (Hart, 1971) holds that good care is least available where need is greatest. Clinicians see the gradient's results and seldom reach its causes.

5. **Fundamental causes persist because they are flexible.** Link and Phelan (1995) explained why the gradient survives every change in what kills people: those with more money, knowledge, power and connections use whatever protective resource is newest, whether a safer neighbourhood, a statin or a vaccine, so each new intervention is adopted first by the already advantaged and widens the gap before narrowing it.

6. **Race is a social exposure, and medicine has mistaken it for a biological one.** Racial differences in health in the United States and elsewhere track discrimination, segregation and income, not ancestry-specific biology, and treating race as physiology has caused harm: a race coefficient in kidney-function estimates, removed in 2021, had systematically understated disease in Black patients; pulse oximeters miss hypoxaemia about three times as often in Black as in white patients (Sjoding and colleagues, 2020) because they were calibrated on light skin. [Human variation](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/human-evolution-and-variation.md) is real and continuous; racial categories are a poor proxy for it and a good proxy for exposure.

7. **The gradient is modifiable, which is what makes it inequity rather than fate.** Gaps differ several-fold between countries of similar wealth and have widened or narrowed within countries over decades, tracking policy. Tobacco taxation, early-years programmes, income support, housing standards and universal [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/family-medicine-and-the-generalist.md) all shift the gradient. Marmot's principle of proportionate universalism (2010): act across the whole gradient, with intensity proportional to need, rather than only at the bottom, because a gradient needs a gradient of response.

8. **Measuring inequity needs care about cause.** The gradient is an association, and the obvious confounder runs backwards: illness causes poverty (lost work, costs) as well as poverty causing illness. Cohorts that measure position before disease, natural experiments and the Whitehall finding among the securely employed all support causation from position to health, but the [causal inference](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/02-inference-and-causation/causal-inference.md) is always partly reverse, and honest attribution says so.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Social determinants | The conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age |
| Health inequity | The avoidable, unfair part of differences in health between groups |
| Social gradient | The stepwise relation between social position and health across the whole range |
| Allostatic load | Cumulative physiological wear from sustained stress responses |
| Inverse care law | Availability of good care varies inversely with the need for it |
| Fundamental cause | A resource (money, knowledge, power) that protects through whatever the current mechanisms are |
| Proportionate universalism | Universal action scaled in intensity to the level of disadvantage |

## How it fits

- **Below:** disease is [multifactorial](../../08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/multifactorial-disease-and-risk.md), so social exposures enter the same causal web as genes and microbes; the body translates them through [stress physiology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md); the arguments are [causal inference](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/02-inference-and-causation/causal-inference.md) on observational data.
- **Above:** nothing; the gradient is the context of every consultation, and the explicit concern of [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/family-medicine-and-the-generalist.md) and public health.
- **Siblings:** [global health](global-health-and-pandemics.md) is the same gradient between countries; [planetary health](environmental-and-planetary-health.md) adds exposures that fall first on the poor; [history](history-of-medicine.md) contains McKeown's evidence that living conditions did most of the work.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Health inequalities are mostly about healthcare access."** Access matters, but countries with universal free care still show the full gradient. Most of it arises before and outside the clinic.
- **"It is a matter of individual choices."** Choices are patterned by circumstance, and the gradient persists after adjusting for smoking, diet and exercise.
- **"Race-based medicine is precision medicine."** Ancestry-informative genetics is precision; self-identified race mostly measures exposure, and using it as biology has caused documented harm.
