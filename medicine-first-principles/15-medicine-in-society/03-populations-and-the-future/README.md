## What this area is about

The rest of the tree explains disease in one body and its treatment in one consultation. This area steps back to ask **why health is distributed as it is across people, places and time**: why a poor man dies a decade before a rich one in the same city, why a child born in one country faces a tenfold higher risk of dying before five than a child born in another, why a warming climate is a medical fact, and why medicine believes what it now believes and did not a century ago. The unifying idea, from Geoffrey Rose (1985), is that **the causes of cases are not the causes of incidence**: what makes one person ill (a genotype, an exposure, a microbe) is a different question from what makes a population's rate high or low (income, housing, air, policy, what is known), and the second question usually has the bigger answer.

Read the first three as one argument at widening scale: inequity within a society, inequity between societies, and the planetary conditions under which all societies live. Each uses the same tools (rates, gradients, attributable fractions, [surveillance](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/01-epidemiology/surveillance-and-outbreak-investigation.md)) and the same conclusion, that the largest levers on population health lie outside the clinic. History then shows that this has always been so: mortality in Europe fell mainly before effective treatment existed, through food, water and housing, and the ideas that now organise the tree arrived in a sequence that each time required the previous one to fail visibly. The future leaf applies the lesson: the next displacements will come from where the current frame is failing, and the task is to tell signal from hype.
