## What this area is about

A cell is sealed inside a membrane that nothing useful can cross on its own, yet it must take in fuel, hold an electrical charge, and respond to hormones, transmitters, growth factors and antigens arriving from outside. This area answers how matter and information cross the membrane. Matter crosses through channels, carriers and pumps, powered ultimately by Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) spent on the sodium–potassium pump, whose gradients then drive almost everything else. Information crosses when a ligand binds a receptor and changes its shape, and the change is relayed inward by second messengers and kinase cascades that amplify it, combine it with other signals, carry it to the nucleus and then switch it off.

The unifying idea is the controlled release of stored free energy. A gradient is energy stored across a barrier; opening a channel spends it to make an electrical signal, coupling a carrier to it spends it to move glucose uphill. Likewise a receptor is a protein poised between conformations, a messenger is a molecule held far below its possible concentration, and a kinase cascade runs on ATP kept far from equilibrium; a signal is each of these released in turn. Because every step is an enzyme or a transporter, every step is a drug target, and roughly half of all drugs act on the proteins described here.

```
   outside   ligand ---> receptor (channel | GPCR | kinase-linked | nuclear)
   ========= membrane: channels, carriers, pumps (Na+/K+ ATPase builds the gradients) =====
   inside    G protein / kinase --> second messenger (cAMP, Ca2+, IP3/DAG, PIP3, NO)
                 |  amplify x10^3-10^6     |  integrate with other inputs
                 v                          v
             kinase cascade --> effector enzymes, channels, transcription factors
                 ^
             termination: esterases, reuptake, phosphatases, PDEs, GTP hydrolysis, arrestin
```

Read transport first; the gradients it describes are what channels spend and what calcium signalling draws on, and its epithelial section is the bridge to kidney and gut physiology. Receptors come next and give the vocabulary of pharmacology. Second messengers follow the signal inward. The last leaf steps back to ask what the whole circuit computes, and is where the feedback mathematics of layer 01 becomes biology.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Macromolecules](../01-macromolecules/README.md):** channels, receptors and kinases are proteins whose conformational changes are the signal; the bilayer's impermeability is what makes transport and gradients necessary; several messengers are lipids.
- **[Metabolism](../02-metabolism/README.md):** the sodium pump is metabolism's largest customer; insulin and glucagon signalling is the switch that integrates metabolism; Adenosine Monophosphate (AMP) and reduced Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide (NADH) are metabolic signals read by kinases and transcription factors.
- **[Genetic information flow](../03-genetic-information-flow/README.md):** most cascades end at a transcription factor, nuclear receptors are transcription factors, and the slow time course of steroid action is the time course of transcription and protein turnover.
