## Essence

A receptor at the surface has changed shape; now the inside of the cell must hear about it. The message is relayed by small molecules made or released in bulk (cyclic Adenosine Monophosphate (cAMP), calcium, inositol trisphosphate, diacylglycerol, nitric oxide) and by chains of protein kinases that each phosphorylate the next. These relays amplify one bound receptor into thousands of altered proteins and carry the signal to the nucleus. Only a few dozen relays exist, shared by hundreds of receptors; the specificity of a hormone's effect lies in which receptors and target enzymes a cell owns, not in the messenger. Kinases are mutated in most cancers and are the target of some eighty approved drugs.

## Core tenets

1. **Second messengers are small, abundant, quickly made and quickly destroyed.** cAMP is made by adenylyl cyclase under a stimulatory G protein (Gs), restrained by an inhibitory one (Gi), and destroyed by phosphodiesterases. Cyclic GMP is made when nitric oxide from endothelium diffuses into smooth muscle and activates guanylyl cyclase; nitrates supply the gas and sildenafil blocks the phosphodiesterase that removes cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate (cGMP). Phospholipase C, activated by Gq, cleaves a membrane lipid into inositol trisphosphate, which releases calcium from the endoplasmic reticulum, and diacylglycerol, which activates protein kinase C. Each can rise ten- to a hundredfold within seconds.

2. **Phosphorylation is the universal reversible switch.** About 500 human kinases and 200 phosphatases add and remove phosphate on serine, threonine or tyrosine. The two negative charges reshape the protein, activating or inhibiting it or creating a docking site. Because Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) is kept far from equilibrium, phosphorylation is one-way and only a phosphatase reverses it, so phosphatases set a signal's duration.

3. **Calcium is the fastest messenger because its gradient is the steepest.** Cytosolic calcium rests near 100 nmol/L against 1–2 mmol/L outside and in the reticulum, so opening a few channels raises it tenfold in milliseconds. It couples electrical events to chemical ones: contraction, transmitter release, insulin secretion. Calmodulin and its kinases, and the phosphatase calcineurin (inhibited by ciclosporin and tacrolimus, which is how they suppress T cells), read it. Uncontrolled calcium is lethal: in ischaemia it activates proteases and opens the mitochondrial permeability pore, the final step of necrosis.

4. **A few canonical cascades carry most signals, and each has a pharmacology.** The cAMP–protein kinase A pathway mediates adrenaline's and glucagon's effects on glycogen and fat and beta-agonists' relaxation of airways; cholera toxin locks Gs on, pertussis toxin disables Gi. The Ras–Raf–Mitogen-activated protein kinase Kinase (MEK)–Extracellular signal-Regulated Kinase (ERK) cascade carries growth-factor signals to the nucleus and is activated by mutation in about a third of cancers (Kirsten Rat Sarcoma viral oncogene homologue (KRAS), v-Raf murine sarcoma viral oncogene homologue B (BRAF)); BRAF and MEK inhibitors treat melanoma. The Phosphoinositide 3-Kinase (PI3K)–Akt–mechanistic Target Of Rapamycin (mTOR) pathway governs growth and survival and is insulin's metabolic arm; loss of its brake Phosphatase and Tensin homologue (PTEN) is common in cancer. Janus Kinase (JAK)–Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription (STAT) carries cytokine signals; a JAK2 mutation drives polycythaemia and JAK inhibitors treat inflammatory arthritis. Nuclear Factor kappa B (NF-kappaB) carries inflammatory signals and is repressed by glucocorticoids. Wnt, Notch, Hedgehog and Transforming Growth Factor beta (TGF-beta) govern development and are hijacked in colorectal, skin and fibrotic disease.

5. **The same messenger produces different responses in different cells.** Adrenaline raises cAMP in liver, heart, fat and airway, and the result is glycogenolysis, a stronger beat, lipolysis and bronchodilation respectively, because each cell's protein kinase A finds different substrates. Specificity lives in the cell's receptor and effector repertoires and in scaffolds that hold a kinase next to one set of substrates, which is why a drug acting on one pathway has effects all over the body.

6. **Insulin signalling is the worked example that links biochemistry to the clinic.** Insulin binding makes its receptor phosphorylate itself and then the insulin receptor substrate proteins; these recruit PI3K, which makes the lipid Phosphatidylinositol Trisphosphate (PIP3), which activates Akt. Akt moves the Glucose Transporter 4 (GLUT4) glucose transporter to the surface, switches on glycogen synthase, shuts off gluconeogenic genes and, via mTOR, drives protein synthesis. Excess lipid intermediates (diacylglycerol, ceramide) and inflammatory kinases phosphorylate the substrate proteins on serines instead, which blocks the relay: that is insulin resistance, and it is how ectopic fat and inflammation produce type 2 diabetes.

7. **Kinases are drug targets because the ATP pocket is druggable, and the same fact limits selectivity.** Imatinib (2001) inhibits the Breakpoint Cluster Region–Abelson (BCR-ABL) fusion kinase and turned chronic myeloid leukaemia from a fatal disease into a managed one, proving that a cancer can be addicted to one signal. Resistance arises by pocket mutations, answered by later inhibitors. Because all kinases bind ATP, inhibitors hit several, giving unexpected uses and toxicities. KRAS, long thought undruggable, yielded in 2021 to inhibitors that lock the G12C mutant in its off state.

8. **GTPases and proteases are switches too.** Ras and its relatives are on when bound to Guanosine Triphosphate (GTP) and off when they hydrolyse it; activators load GTP, GTPase-Activating Protein (GAP) proteins accelerate hydrolysis, and oncogenic Ras is a mutant that GAPs cannot switch off. Cleavage is irreversible signalling: caspases execute apoptosis, and the [coagulation cascade](../../06-physiology/08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md) is an extracellular protease cascade that amplifies a few activated molecules into a clot.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Second messenger | Small intracellular molecule whose concentration carries a signal |
| G protein | GTP-binding switch coupling a receptor to effectors; Gs, Gi and Gq families |
| Protein kinase, phosphatase | Enzymes that add and remove phosphate; the on and off of most signalling |
| Ras–MAPK pathway | Growth cascade from Ras through Raf, MEK and ERK to transcription |
| PI3K–Akt–mTOR pathway | Survival, growth and insulin's metabolic signalling |
| Oncogene addiction | A tumour's dependence on one activated signalling protein |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Each hormone has its own second messenger."** cAMP serves dozens of hormones; specificity comes from the cell, not the messenger.
- **"Phosphorylation activates proteins."** It changes them; glycogen synthase is inhibited by it and glycogen phosphorylase activated, which is how one kinase flips a pathway.
- **"Kinase inhibitors are chemotherapy."** They block a signal rather than damaging Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA), which is why they are selective for addicted tumours and why resistance is a pocket mutation rather than a repair change.
