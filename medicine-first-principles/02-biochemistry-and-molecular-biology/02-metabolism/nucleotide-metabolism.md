## Essence

Nucleotides are the monomers of Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) and Ribonucleic Acid (RNA), the energy currency (Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)) and the core of the coenzymes; a dividing cell needs a large supply. Cells make them de novo, building a ring from amino acids, carbon dioxide and folate-carried one-carbon units on a ribose phosphate from the pentose phosphate pathway, or by salvage, recycling bases cheaply. Purine breakdown ends in poorly soluble uric acid. It is the bottleneck of S phase, so antimetabolite drugs strike here; its inborn errors each expose one rule.

## Core tenets

1. **The pentose phosphate pathway supplies the ribose and the reducing power.** Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) starts an irreversible oxidative branch yielding ribose-5-phosphate and two reduced Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide Phosphate (NADPH); a reversible, thiamine-dependent branch (transketolase) lets a cell make ribose without NADPH, or the reverse. NADPH is the reducing currency for lipid synthesis, the phagocyte's oxidative burst and, above all, glutathione. The red cell has no other NADPH source, so in G6PD deficiency (X-linked, ~400 million people, 2020s, commonest where malaria is or was endemic because it protects against it) an oxidant drug, fava beans or infection oxidises haemoglobin and it haemolyses.

2. **Purines are built on the sugar; pyrimidines are built first and then attached.** 5-Phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate (PRPP), made from ribose-5-phosphate and ATP, is the activated sugar for every route. The purine ring is built on it from glycine, glutamine, aspartate, carbon dioxide and two folate-carried formyl groups, giving inosine monophosphate (IMP), the branch point to Adenosine Monophosphate (AMP) and Guanosine Monophosphate (GMP). The pyrimidine ring is made free from carbamoyl phosphate and aspartate, then joined to PRPP. Its carbamoyl phosphate comes from a cytosolic synthetase distinct from the mitochondrial one of the [urea cycle](amino-acid-and-nitrogen-metabolism.md); when that cycle is blocked, carbamoyl phosphate spills over and orotic acid appears in urine.

3. **Control sits at the committed steps and keeps the four bases balanced.** The first purine-specific enzyme and PRPP synthetase are inhibited by AMP, GMP and IMP, and each branch needs the other's triphosphate, so they stay in step. One enzyme, ribonucleotide reductase, makes all four deoxyribonucleotides, with allosteric sites that read the four triphosphates so the pools match DNA; an unbalanced pool is mutagenic.

4. **Thymidylate synthesis is the step that consumes folate, which is why folate deficiency stops DNA synthesis first.** Thymidylate synthase methylates deoxyuridine monophosphate using methylene-tetrahydrofolate and leaves dihydrofolate, which dihydrofolate reductase must reduce before one-carbon chemistry can continue. Folate or vitamin B12 deficiency therefore slows replication while RNA and protein synthesis continue: megaloblastic anaemia ([vitamins](vitamins-minerals-and-cofactors.md)).

5. **Salvage is cheaper than synthesis and dominant in most tissues.** A de novo purine costs about five ATP, salvage one. Hypoxanthine–guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT) reattaches free bases to PRPP and kinases rephosphorylate nucleosides; the brain relies on salvage. In Lesch–Nyhan syndrome (X-linked HGPRT deficiency) bases are degraded to urate instead of recycled and unused PRPP drives de novo synthesis unchecked: gout, stones, dystonia and compulsive self-injury, showing that dopaminergic neurons depend on purine pools in ways not yet understood. The same kinases activate nucleoside drugs such as aciclovir and cytarabine.

6. **Purine degradation ends at urate because humans lost uricase.** AMP and GMP are broken to hypoxanthine and xanthine, which xanthine oxidase converts to uric acid. Urate is a plasma antioxidant but circulates near its solubility limit (~0.4 mmol/L at 37 °C), less still in cool joints; two thirds leaves by the kidney, which reabsorbs most of the filtered load. When production outruns excretion, urate crystals in a joint activate the inflammasome ([gout](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/osteoarthritis-gout-and-regional-pain.md)); tumour lysis floods the kidney with urate. Allopurinol inhibits xanthine oxidase; recombinant uricase does what the human enzyme would have. Pyrimidines degrade to soluble products and never form stones.

7. **Antimetabolites exploit the bottleneck, and their selectivity is the cell's division rate.** Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase; fluorouracil, as fluorodeoxyuridylate, locks thymidylate synthase in a dead-end complex; mercaptopurine (from azathioprine) is salvaged by HGPRT into thio-nucleotides that block synthesis and corrupt DNA; mycophenolate blocks IMP dehydrogenase, on which lymphocytes depend for guanine nucleotides; hydroxycarbamide inhibits ribonucleotide reductase. All kill cells in proportion to their division, so marrow and gut share the toxicity, the logic of [anticancer](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anticancer-drugs.md) and immunosuppressive therapy. The map also predicts that allopurinol makes mercaptopurine toxic by blocking its clearance, and that trimethoprim is selective because bacteria make their own folate.

8. **A block in purine degradation destroys lymphocytes selectively.** Adenosine deaminase (ADA) converts adenosine and deoxyadenosine to their inosine forms. Without it deoxyadenosine is phosphorylated to deoxy-ATP, which accumulates in lymphocytes and shuts down ribonucleotide reductase, so neither T nor B cells can divide: severe combined immunodeficiency, roughly 10–15% of cases ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)). Enzyme replacement, stem cell transplantation and gene therapy (first attempted in this disease, 1990) all restore one enzyme.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pentose phosphate pathway | Glucose-6-phosphate to ribose-5-phosphate plus NADPH; oxidative branch irreversible |
| PRPP | Activated ribose phosphate on which purines are built and to which pyrimidines are attached |
| Salvage | Recycling a base or nucleoside for one ATP instead of building a ring for five |
| Ribonucleotide reductase | Single enzyme converting all four ribonucleotides to deoxy forms; keeps pools balanced |
| Thymidylate synthase | Folate-consuming step making dTMP; blocked by antifolates and fluorouracil |
| HGPRT | Purine salvage enzyme; missing in Lesch–Nyhan syndrome |
| Uric acid (urate) | Human end product of purines; antioxidant, poorly soluble, cause of gout |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Gout is caused by eating purines."** Diet shifts urate modestly; about nine in ten cases are renal under-excretion, and most urate comes from cell turnover.
- **"Antimetabolites are selective for cancer cells."** They are selective for dividing cells; marrow and gut toxicity is the same mechanism as the benefit.
