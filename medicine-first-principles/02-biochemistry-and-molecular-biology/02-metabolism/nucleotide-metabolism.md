## Essence

Nucleotides are the monomers of Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) and Ribonucleic Acid (RNA), the energy currency (Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP), Guanosine Triphosphate (GTP)) and the core of the main coenzymes, so every cell needs a supply and a dividing cell needs a large one. Cells obtain them by de novo synthesis, which builds a purine or pyrimidine ring from amino acids, carbon dioxide and folate-carried one-carbon units on a ribose phosphate made by the pentose phosphate pathway, and by salvage, which recycles bases at a fraction of the cost. Purine breakdown ends in poorly soluble uric acid. Because the pathway is the bottleneck of S phase, antimetabolite drugs strike dividing cells here, and its inborn errors (gout, Lesch–Nyhan syndrome, immunodeficiency from adenosine deaminase deficiency) each expose one rule of the pathway.

## Core tenets

1. **The pentose phosphate pathway supplies the ribose and the reducing power.** Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) starts an irreversible oxidative branch that yields ribose-5-phosphate and two reduced Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide Phosphate (NADPH); a reversible, thiamine-dependent branch (transketolase) lets a cell make ribose without NADPH or the reverse. NADPH is the reducing currency for fatty-acid and cholesterol synthesis, cytochrome P450, the phagocyte's oxidative burst and, above all, regenerating glutathione. The red cell has no other NADPH source, so in G6PD deficiency (X-linked, ~400 million people, 2020s, commonest where malaria is or was endemic because it protects against it) an oxidant drug such as primaquine, fava beans or an infection oxidises haemoglobin and the cell haemolyses.

2. **Purines are built on the sugar; pyrimidines are built first and then attached.** 5-Phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate (PRPP), made from ribose-5-phosphate and ATP, is the activated sugar for every route. The purine ring is assembled on it from glycine, two glutamine nitrogens, aspartate, carbon dioxide and two folate-carried formyl groups, giving inosine monophosphate (IMP), which branches to Adenosine Monophosphate (AMP) and Guanosine Monophosphate (GMP). The pyrimidine ring is made free from carbamoyl phosphate and aspartate, then joined to PRPP to give uridine monophosphate. Its carbamoyl phosphate comes from a cytosolic synthetase distinct from the mitochondrial one of the [urea cycle](amino-acid-and-nitrogen-metabolism.md); when that cycle is blocked downstream, carbamoyl phosphate spills into pyrimidine synthesis and orotic acid appears in urine, a diagnostic clue.

3. **Control sits at the committed steps and keeps the four bases balanced.** The first purine-specific enzyme and PRPP synthetase are inhibited by AMP, GMP and IMP, and each branch needs the other's triphosphate, so the two stay in step; a rare overactive PRPP synthetase causes gout by overproduction. Deoxyribonucleotides are made by a single enzyme, ribonucleotide reductase, whose allosteric sites read all four triphosphates so that the pools match DNA's composition; an unbalanced pool is mutagenic.

4. **Thymidylate synthesis is the step that consumes folate, which is why folate deficiency stops DNA synthesis first.** Thymidylate synthase methylates deoxyuridine monophosphate using methylene-tetrahydrofolate and leaves dihydrofolate, which dihydrofolate reductase must reduce again before one-carbon chemistry can continue. Folate or vitamin B12 deficiency therefore slows replication while RNA and protein synthesis continue, so marrow cells grow large and die: megaloblastic anaemia ([vitamins](vitamins-minerals-and-cofactors.md)).

5. **Salvage is cheaper than synthesis and dominant in most tissues.** A de novo purine costs about five ATP; salvage costs one. Hypoxanthine–guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT) and adenine phosphoribosyltransferase reattach free bases to PRPP, and nucleoside kinases rephosphorylate nucleosides; the brain relies heavily on salvage. In Lesch–Nyhan syndrome (X-linked HGPRT deficiency) hypoxanthine and guanine are degraded to urate instead of recycled, and unused PRPP drives de novo synthesis unchecked: gout, urate stones, dystonia and compulsive self-injury, a neurological picture showing that dopaminergic neurons depend on purine pools in ways still not fully understood. The same salvage kinases activate nucleoside drugs: aciclovir by a viral thymidine kinase, cytarabine and gemcitabine by cellular ones.

6. **Purine degradation ends at urate because humans lost uricase.** AMP and GMP are broken to hypoxanthine and xanthine, which xanthine oxidase converts to uric acid. Urate is a plasma antioxidant but circulates near its solubility limit (~0.4 mmol/L at 37 °C) and is less soluble in cool joints; about two thirds leaves by the kidney, which reabsorbs most of the filtered load, and one third by the gut. When production outruns excretion, monosodium urate crystals in a joint activate the inflammasome and interleukin-1, one of the most intense acute inflammations in medicine ([gout](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/osteoarthritis-gout-and-regional-pain.md)); massive cell death (tumour lysis) floods the kidney with urate. Allopurinol and febuxostat inhibit xanthine oxidase; recombinant uricase (rasburicase) does what the human enzyme would have. Pyrimidines degrade to soluble beta-amino acids and never form stones.

7. **Antimetabolites exploit the bottleneck, and their selectivity is the cell's division rate.** Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase; fluorouracil, converted to fluorodeoxyuridylate, locks thymidylate synthase in a dead-end complex; mercaptopurine and its prodrug azathioprine are salvaged by HGPRT into thio-nucleotides that inhibit de novo synthesis and corrupt DNA; mycophenolate blocks IMP dehydrogenase, to which lymphocytes are unusually sensitive because they lack an alternative GTP supply; hydroxycarbamide inhibits ribonucleotide reductase. All kill cells in proportion to their division, so marrow, gut lining and hair follicles share the toxicity, the logic of [anticancer](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anticancer-drugs.md) and immunosuppressive therapy. The map predicts two more facts: allopurinol blocks the xanthine oxidase that clears mercaptopurine, so the combination is hazardous; and sulfonamides and trimethoprim are selective antibacterials because bacteria make their own folate and humans cannot.

8. **A block in purine degradation destroys lymphocytes selectively.** Adenosine deaminase (ADA) converts adenosine and deoxyadenosine to their inosine forms. Without it deoxyadenosine is phosphorylated to deoxy-ATP, which accumulates in lymphocytes (rich in the kinase, poor in the reverse enzyme) and shuts down ribonucleotide reductase, so neither T nor B cells can divide: severe combined immunodeficiency, roughly 10–15% of cases ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)). Enzyme replacement, stem cell transplantation and gene therapy, first attempted in this disease (1990), all work by restoring one enzyme. Adenosine is also a signal, acting through receptors that caffeine blocks and that slow cardiac conduction.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pentose phosphate pathway | Glucose-6-phosphate to ribose-5-phosphate plus NADPH; oxidative branch irreversible |
| PRPP | Activated ribose phosphate on which purines are built and to which pyrimidine rings are attached |
| De novo synthesis vs salvage | Building a ring from small precursors (costly) versus recycling a base or nucleoside (cheap) |
| Ribonucleotide reductase | Single enzyme converting all four ribonucleotides to deoxy forms; keeps pools balanced |
| Thymidylate synthase | Folate-consuming methylation of dUMP to dTMP; blocked by antifolates and fluorouracil |
| HGPRT | Purine salvage enzyme; missing in Lesch–Nyhan syndrome |
| Xanthine oxidase | Final enzyme of purine degradation; target of allopurinol |
| Uric acid (urate) | Human end product of purines; antioxidant, poorly soluble, cause of gout |
| Antimetabolite | Drug mimicking a metabolite to block nucleotide synthesis in dividing cells |
| Adenosine deaminase | Purine degradation enzyme whose absence causes severe combined immunodeficiency |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Gout is caused by eating purines."** Diet shifts urate modestly; about nine in ten cases are renal under-excretion, and most urate comes from the body's own cell turnover.
- **"Antimetabolites are selective for cancer cells."** They are selective for dividing cells; the toxicity to marrow and gut is the same mechanism as the benefit.
- **"Uric acid is simply waste."** It is a major plasma antioxidant, and the loss of uricase in primates may have been selected for; the cost is a molecule held near its solubility limit.
