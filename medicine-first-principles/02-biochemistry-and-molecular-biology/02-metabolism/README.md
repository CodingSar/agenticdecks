## What this area is about

Metabolism is how a cell extracts free energy from food, converts one kind of molecule into another, and keeps both processes matched to demand. The question it answers is how a body made of molecules that are thermodynamically unstable in air, and a brain that needs a continuous supply of one particular sugar, survives on meals eaten a few times a day. The answer has three parts: electrons stripped from fuel fall to oxygen and the drop is captured as Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) and reduced carriers; a small set of shared intermediates (pyruvate, acetyl-CoA, the Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) cycle acids) lets carbohydrate, fat and protein be interconverted within thermodynamic limits; and a few hormones switch the direction of the pathways so that fuel is stored when plentiful and released when scarce.

The unifying idea is that every pathway is a sequence of enzyme-catalysed reactions that is controlled at the few steps held far from equilibrium, and that the controlling signals are the cell's own energy and redox ratios and the body's hormones. Disease enters wherever a step is missing (inborn errors), a signal fails (diabetes), a supply fails (hypoxia, vitamin deficiency) or a store is misplaced (fatty liver, atheroma).

```
   carbohydrate        fat               protein
       |                |                   |
   glycolysis      beta-oxidation      transamination --> NH3 --> urea
       \                |                  /
        +----------> acetyl-CoA <---------+       (glucose <-- lactate, glycerol,
                        |                           amino acids; NOT acetyl-CoA)
                    TCA cycle --> NADH, FADH2
                        |
                 respiratory chain --> O2 ;  H+ gradient --> ATP
   insulin  <--- fed / fasted switch ---> glucagon, adrenaline, cortisol
```

Read bioenergetics first; it supplies the units everything else is measured in. The central route comes next, because fat and amino acids are explained as tributaries into acetyl-CoA and the TCA cycle. Lipid and amino acid metabolism can be read in either order. Integration assumes all four and is where the pathways become physiology. Vitamins and cofactors can be read at any point; it is placed last because it collects the cofactors the earlier leaves name in passing.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Macromolecules](../01-macromolecules/README.md):** metabolism is enzymes acting on carbohydrates, lipids and amino acids; the macromolecules leaves describe the substrates and catalysts, this area the flows.
- **[Genetic information flow](../03-genetic-information-flow/README.md):** metabolism supplies the nucleotides, amino acids and ATP that replication, transcription and translation consume, and nutrient state regulates gene expression through transcription factors such as Sterol Regulatory Element-Binding Protein (SREBP) and the sirtuins, which read reduced Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide (NADH).
- **[Cell signalling](../04-cell-signalling/README.md):** insulin and glucagon act through receptors, second messengers and kinase cascades; the fed–fasted switch is a signalling circuit whose effectors are metabolic enzymes. Ion gradients are metabolism's largest customer.
