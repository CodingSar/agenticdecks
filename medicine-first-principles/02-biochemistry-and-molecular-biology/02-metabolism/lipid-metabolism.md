## Essence

Fat is the body's long-term energy store, and lipid metabolism is the set of pathways that deposit it after a meal, mobilise it during fasting, burn it for Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP), and convert surplus carbohydrate into it. Because fatty acids and cholesterol are insoluble, every step is also a transport problem: albumin for free fatty acids, lipoproteins for triglyceride and cholesterol, carnitine for entry into mitochondria, ketone bodies as the soluble form in which the liver exports fat energy to the brain. Insulin decides the direction of flow: present, fat is stored; absent, fat is released and burned. The common metabolic diseases of affluent societies, and some of the rarest inherited ones, are failures somewhere in this traffic.

## Core tenets

1. **Insulin is the switch between storage and mobilisation.** In adipocytes, insulin stimulates lipoprotein lipase to unload dietary triglyceride and suppresses the hormone-sensitive and adipose triglyceride lipases that release fatty acids. Glucagon and adrenaline, through cyclic Adenosine Monophosphate (AMP) and protein kinase A, do the reverse. With insulin absent (untreated type 1 diabetes), lipolysis runs unchecked, the liver is flooded with fatty acids and ketoacidosis follows; the hyperglycaemia is the visible sign, the fat flux the dangerous one.

2. **Lipoproteins solve the solubility problem and create the disease.** Chylomicrons carry dietary fat from the gut and very-low-density lipoprotein carries liver-made triglyceride; both are unloaded by lipoprotein lipase on capillary walls. The Very-Low-Density Lipoprotein (VLDL) remnant becomes low-density lipoprotein (LDL), the main cholesterol carrier, cleared by the LDL receptor; high-density lipoprotein returns cholesterol from tissues to the liver. LDL concentration is set largely by receptor number, which the liver adjusts to its own cholesterol content, so blocking cholesterol synthesis (statins) or receptor degradation (PCSK9 inhibitors) raises receptors and lowers plasma LDL.

3. **Beta-oxidation disassembles fatty acids two carbons at a time.** A fatty acid is activated to acyl-CoA, carried into the mitochondrion as acyl-carnitine by carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT1), and cycled through four reactions that each yield reduced Flavin Adenine Dinucleotide (FADH2), reduced Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide (NADH) and acetyl-CoA; palmitate gives about 106 ATP. CPT1 is the gate: malonyl-CoA, the first intermediate of fatty-acid synthesis, inhibits it, so a cell cannot burn and build fat at once. Inherited defects of the shuttle or of the medium-chain dehydrogenase cause hypoglycaemia without ketones after fasting, because the liver cannot burn fat to fuel gluconeogenesis; newborn screening by tandem mass spectrometry finds them, and never fasting treats them.

4. **Ketone bodies export fat energy to the brain.** Fatty acids cannot cross the blood–brain barrier in quantity, and acetyl-CoA cannot become glucose. In fasting the liver, with oxaloacetate drained into gluconeogenesis, condenses surplus acetyl-CoA into acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate, which reach the brain; after a few days they supply about two thirds of its energy, sparing glucose and so muscle protein. The liver lacks the enzyme to use them. Both are acids: ketoacidosis is production unrestrained by insulin.

5. **Fatty-acid synthesis runs in the cytosol, in the opposite direction, with different enzymes.** Acetyl-CoA leaves the mitochondrion as citrate, is carboxylated by acetyl-CoA carboxylase (the committed step, activated by insulin, inhibited by glucagon and AMP-activated protein kinase) and assembled into palmitate by fatty-acid synthase using reduced Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide Phosphate (NADPH) from the pentose phosphate pathway. Separate compartments and reciprocal control prevent a futile cycle. Surplus carbohydrate, fructose especially, becomes fat by this route; deposited in the liver, it is fatty liver disease.

6. **Cholesterol is made from acetyl-CoA, regulated by its own level, and excreted only as bile.** 3-Hydroxy-3-Methylglutaryl-Coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) reductase is the rate-limiting enzyme, controlled by sterol-sensing transcription factors (SREBP) that cut synthesis when cholesterol is plentiful. An adult synthesises roughly 1 g a day and absorbs a few hundred milligrams, so synthesis matters more than diet. There is no degradative pathway: cholesterol leaves as bile acids and biliary cholesterol, and when bile holds more than it can dissolve, gallstones form.

7. **Essential fatty acids become signalling lipids.** Humans cannot insert double bonds beyond carbon 9, so linoleic (omega-6) and alpha-linolenic (omega-3) acids are dietary. Arachidonic acid, made from linoleate, is freed from membranes by phospholipase A2 and converted by cyclo-oxygenases into prostaglandins and thromboxane and by lipoxygenases into leukotrienes; aspirin acetylates platelet cyclo-oxygenase irreversibly, its antithrombotic mechanism.

8. **Lipid in the wrong place is the common mechanism of metabolic disease.** Triglyceride and its intermediates (diacylglycerol, ceramide) accumulating in liver and muscle interfere with insulin signalling, linking obesity to type 2 diabetes. LDL retained in the artery wall becomes [atherosclerosis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md). Sphingolipids that cannot be degraded because a lysosomal enzyme is missing (Gaucher, Fabry, Tay–Sachs) fill cells of brain, liver and spleen; some are now treated by infusing the missing enzyme.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lipolysis | Hydrolysis of stored triglyceride to fatty acids and glycerol |
| Lipoprotein lipase | Capillary enzyme that unloads triglyceride from chylomicrons and VLDL |
| LDL receptor | Hepatic and peripheral receptor that clears LDL; its number sets plasma LDL |
| Carnitine shuttle | CPT1/CPT2 system carrying long-chain acyl groups into mitochondria |
| Beta-oxidation | Four-step cycle removing acetyl-CoA from the carboxyl end of a fatty acid |
| Ketone bodies | Acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate; soluble fat-derived fuel |
| Acetyl-CoA carboxylase | Committed enzyme of fatty-acid synthesis; its product inhibits CPT1 |
| HMG-CoA reductase | Rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis; target of statins |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Fat can be converted to glucose."** Only the glycerol backbone (about 5% of a triglyceride's mass) can; the fatty-acid carbons leave the Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) cycle as CO2.
- **"Ketones are toxic by-products."** They are the normal fasting fuel of brain and heart; they are harmful only when produced without insulin's restraint, as acids in large excess.
- **"Dietary cholesterol sets blood cholesterol."** Endogenous synthesis is several times larger and is feedback-regulated; plasma LDL depends mainly on receptor-mediated clearance.
