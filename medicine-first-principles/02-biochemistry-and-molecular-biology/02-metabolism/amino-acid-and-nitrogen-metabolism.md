## Essence

Amino acids are the only major fuel with nitrogen, and the body has no way to store either. Protein is turned over at about 300 g a day, surplus amino acids are burned or converted to glucose and fat, and the nitrogen is stripped off, carried safely to the liver, and excreted as urea. The organising problem is that the simplest nitrogen product, ammonia, is a neurotoxin, so nitrogen moves between tissues as glutamine and alanine and is detoxified by one organ. Amino acids are also the raw material for neurotransmitters, haem, nucleotides and methyl groups, so a block in their metabolism shows up as neurological disease or a cancer-drug target as often as a nutritional one. The inborn errors of amino acid metabolism were the first genetic diseases understood as missing enzymes and the first treated by diet.

## Core tenets

1. **There is no nitrogen store, so protein balance is a daily account.** Amino acids are not laid down as reserve; the body's protein is all functional, mostly muscle. Nine amino acids are essential, because humans lost the pathways to make their carbon skeletons. A healthy adult excretes nitrogen equal to intake; in fasting, injury or sepsis the balance is negative and the loss is muscle, which is why critical illness wastes patients.

2. **Transamination funnels every amino group to glutamate.** Aminotransferases, using pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B6), swap the amino group of an amino acid onto alpha-ketoglutarate, making glutamate and leaving a carbon skeleton that enters glycolysis or the TCA cycle. Skeletons that reach pyruvate or a cycle intermediate are glucogenic; leucine and lysine, which reach only acetyl-CoA, are ketogenic. The two most abundant aminotransferases (alanine and aspartate aminotransferase) leak from injured liver and muscle into plasma, which is why they are measured as liver tests.

3. **Ammonia is toxic and travels in disguise.** Free ammonia at even 0.1 mmol/L disturbs the brain, in part by driving glutamine synthesis in astrocytes, which swell. Peripheral tissues therefore load ammonia onto glutamate to make glutamine, and muscle loads it onto pyruvate to make alanine; both carry it to the liver (and glutamine to the kidney, which excretes ammonium to dispose of acid, linking nitrogen metabolism to [acid–base chemistry](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/acids-bases-and-buffers.md)).

4. **The urea cycle converts two nitrogens into one harmless, soluble molecule at a cost of four ATP.** Five enzymes, split between mitochondrion and cytosol, join ammonia and aspartate nitrogen onto ornithine and release urea, 20–30 g a day. Plasma urea rises with protein load, tissue breakdown or kidney failure and falls with liver failure. A child born lacking one enzyme becomes comatose with hyperammonaemia within days of feeding; an adult whose cirrhotic liver cannot keep up develops hepatic encephalopathy, which lactulose and protein restriction treat by reducing gut ammonia.

5. **Amino acids are precursors of the molecules that carry signals and oxygen.** Tyrosine becomes dopamine, noradrenaline, adrenaline, thyroxine and melanin; tryptophan becomes serotonin, melatonin and niacin; glutamate becomes gamma-aminobutyric acid; arginine becomes nitric oxide and creatine; histidine becomes histamine. Glycine and succinyl-CoA start haem synthesis, whose blocked intermediates accumulate in the porphyrias and in lead poisoning. Methionine, via S-adenosylmethionine, donates the methyl groups that modify DNA, histones, neurotransmitters and drugs.

6. **Inborn errors reveal the pathway logic: substrate piles up, product runs out, side paths open.** In phenylketonuria (about 1 in 10,000 births in Europe) phenylalanine hydroxylase is missing, so phenylalanine accumulates and damages the developing brain, tyrosine and melanin fall, and phenylketones appear in urine. A newborn blood spot (Guthrie, 1963) detects it, and a low-phenylalanine diet prevents the intellectual disability: a genetic disease that is environmentally determined.

7. **Creatinine is a metabolic clock that reads kidney function.** Creatine buffers ATP in muscle as phosphocreatine, and about 1–2% of the pool cyclises spontaneously each day to creatinine, at a rate proportional to muscle mass. Creatinine is filtered freely and barely reabsorbed, so its plasma level rises as glomerular filtration falls, which is why it estimates [kidney function](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/glomerular-filtration-and-clearance.md), and why the estimate is wrong in the very muscular and the frail.

8. **Nucleotide synthesis from amino acids is where chemotherapy strikes.** Purines are built from glutamine, glycine, aspartate and one-carbon units carried by folate; pyrimidines from aspartate and glutamine. Dividing cells need the flux, so antifolates (methotrexate), purine analogues (mercaptopurine, azathioprine) and pyrimidine analogues (fluorouracil) are [anticancer](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anticancer-drugs.md) and immunosuppressive drugs. Purine breakdown ends in uric acid, which is sparingly soluble: gout, kidney stones and the tumour lysis syndrome follow when production outruns excretion, and allopurinol blocks the last enzyme, xanthine oxidase.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Essential amino acid | One whose carbon skeleton humans cannot make; nine in adults |
| Nitrogen balance | Intake minus excretion of nitrogen; negative in catabolic states |
| Transamination | Transfer of an amino group to a keto acid, via pyridoxal phosphate |
| Glucogenic, ketogenic | Carbon skeleton can yield glucose; can yield only acetyl-CoA |
| Urea cycle | Hepatic pathway converting ammonia and aspartate nitrogen to urea |
| S-adenosylmethionine | Universal methyl donor derived from methionine |
| Creatinine | Spontaneous breakdown product of creatine; marker of glomerular filtration |
| Uric acid | End product of purine degradation in humans; poorly soluble |

## How it fits

- **Below:** amino acid chemistry is the chemistry of [protein](../01-macromolecules/proteins-structure-and-folding.md) building blocks; the carbon skeletons are burned in the [TCA cycle](glycolysis-tca-cycle-and-oxidative-phosphorylation.md); ammonium handling is [acid–base](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/acids-bases-and-buffers.md) chemistry.
- **Above:** the [liver](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/the-liver-as-metabolic-hub.md) owns the urea cycle and most amino acid catabolism, so [liver disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md) presents with encephalopathy and low urea; creatinine and urea are the working measures of [filtration](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/glomerular-filtration-and-clearance.md); the [inborn errors](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/inborn-errors-of-metabolism.md) are the paradigm of treatable genetic disease.
- **Siblings:** carbon skeletons join [glucose](glycolysis-tca-cycle-and-oxidative-phosphorylation.md) and [fat](lipid-metabolism.md) metabolism; the [fed–fasted cycle](metabolic-integration-and-the-fed-fasted-cycle.md) decides when muscle protein is sacrificed for glucose; B6, folate and B12 are the [cofactors](vitamins-minerals-and-cofactors.md) of this leaf.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Urea is the toxin in kidney failure."** Urea is nearly harmless; it is a marker. Ammonia is the toxin, and it is a liver problem.
- **"Eating more protein builds more muscle."** Beyond requirement the surplus is deaminated and burned; muscle protein accrues only with the anabolic signal of loading and insulin.
- **"A normal creatinine means normal kidneys."** Creatinine rises only after about half of filtration is lost, and a frail patient with little muscle can have a normal value with poor kidneys.
