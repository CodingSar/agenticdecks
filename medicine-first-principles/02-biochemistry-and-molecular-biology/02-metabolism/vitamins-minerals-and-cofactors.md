## Essence

A vitamin is a small molecule the body needs in milligram or microgram amounts because it cannot make it, and almost every one is needed because an enzyme uses it to do chemistry that amino-acid side chains cannot: carry electrons, stabilise a carbanion, move a one-carbon unit, activate an acyl group. Minerals are the metal ions that do the same at the active site, plus the bulk electrolytes. Because cofactors are recycled, the amounts are tiny; because they serve many enzymes, a deficiency presents as the failure of several pathways at once, in the tissues that depend on them most. The clinical picture of each deficiency is therefore a readout of the biochemistry: fast-dividing marrow and gut fail first when DNA synthesis needs folate, nerves fail when energy metabolism needs thiamine, collagen fails when hydroxylation needs ascorbate.

## Core tenets

1. **A vitamin is an evolutionary loss, not a nutritional bonus.** Most animals make ascorbate; primates carry a broken gene for the last enzyme, so for humans it is vitamin C. Diets rich in a compound removed the selection pressure to synthesise it, and the pathway decayed. Requirements are small because cofactors are catalytic, turning over thousands of times before they are degraded, but they are absolute.

2. **Each B vitamin is a coenzyme with one chemical trick.** Thiamine pyrophosphate cleaves bonds next to a carbonyl (pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase); its lack stalls carbohydrate oxidation, giving lactic acidosis, heart failure (beriberi) and the brain lesions of Wernicke's encephalopathy, classically in alcohol dependence. Riboflavin and niacin become FAD and NAD, the electron carriers. Pyridoxal phosphate (B6) forms a Schiff base with amino acids and catalyses transamination and decarboxylation. Biotin carries carbon dioxide for carboxylases. Pantothenate is the core of coenzyme A.

3. **Folate and B12 are one system, and it feeds DNA synthesis.** Folate carries one-carbon units for thymidylate and purine synthesis, so deficiency starves dividing cells: megaloblastic anaemia, and neural tube defects when the embryo is closing its neural tube, which is why flour is fortified. Cobalamin (B12) regenerates the folate form that methylates homocysteine to methionine; without it folate is trapped in an unusable form and the same anaemia results, plus a demyelinating neuropathy that folate alone will not prevent. B12 absorption needs intrinsic factor from gastric parietal cells, so autoimmune gastritis (pernicious anaemia) and gastric surgery cause deficiency in the face of adequate intake.

4. **Fat-soluble vitamins follow fat, accumulate, and act like hormones.** A, D, E and K need bile and intact fat absorption, so cholestasis, pancreatic insufficiency and cystic fibrosis cause their deficiency, and A and D can be stored to toxicity. Retinal is the light-absorbing group of rhodopsin; retinoic acid is a transcription-factor ligand that patterns the embryo, making it both a teratogen and a drug. Vitamin D is a prohormone made in skin by ultraviolet light and activated in liver and kidney to govern calcium and phosphate absorption; rickets and osteomalacia are its lack. Vitamin E breaks the chain reaction of lipid peroxidation. Vitamin K is the cofactor for carboxylating glutamates on clotting factors II, VII, IX and X so they can bind calcium; warfarin blocks its recycling, and newborns, whose gut bacteria have not yet made any, are given it at birth.

5. **Vitamin C is the reducing agent of collagen synthesis.** Prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases need ascorbate to keep their iron reduced; without it collagen cannot form its stabilising cross-links, and scurvy follows: bleeding gums, poor wound healing, old scars reopening. Lind's 1747 trial of citrus on scorbutic sailors is counted as the first controlled clinical trial.

6. **Iron is essential, toxic and irreplaceable, so the body hoards it.** Iron carries oxygen in haem and electrons in cytochromes and iron–sulfur clusters, but free iron catalyses the formation of hydroxyl radicals, so it is always protein-bound (transferrin in plasma, ferritin in stores). There is no excretory route; balance is set at absorption by the hepatic hormone hepcidin, which blocks the exporter ferroportin when stores or inflammation are high. Iron deficiency is the commonest nutritional deficiency worldwide, affecting on the order of a billion people, and causes microcytic anaemia; hereditary haemochromatosis is hepcidin failure, with iron loading of liver, pancreas and heart.

7. **Trace metals are active-site chemistry.** Zinc stabilises structure (zinc-finger transcription factors) and polarises water (carbonic anhydrase, several hundred enzymes). Copper carries electrons in cytochrome oxidase and cross-links collagen via lysyl oxidase; Wilson's disease is copper that cannot be excreted into bile. Selenium forms selenocysteine in glutathione peroxidases and the deiodinases that activate thyroid hormone. Iodine is built into thyroid hormone itself, and its deficiency was the commonest preventable cause of intellectual disability until salt iodisation. Magnesium binds every ATP molecule in the cell and is needed by hundreds of enzymes, and hypomagnesaemia drags potassium and calcium down with it.

8. **Dose–response for nutrients is U-shaped, and plausibility is not benefit.** Below requirement, function fails; above it, nothing improves; far above, toxicity appears (vitamin A, D, iron, selenium). Fortification of staples with iodine, folate and vitamin D has been among the most effective public health measures, yet trials of antioxidant supplements in well-fed adults have shown no benefit and, for beta-carotene in smokers, more lung cancer. A few inborn errors are treated with megadoses because the mutant enzyme has a raised Km for its cofactor and can be saturated.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Coenzyme | Organic cofactor, usually a vitamin derivative, that supplies chemistry to an enzyme |
| Prosthetic group | Cofactor permanently bound to its enzyme (haem, FAD, biotin) |
| One-carbon metabolism | Folate- and B12-dependent transfer of methyl, methylene and formyl groups |
| Intrinsic factor | Gastric protein required for B12 absorption in the terminal ileum |
| Fat-soluble vitamins | A, D, E, K: absorbed with fat, stored, potentially toxic |
| Gamma-carboxylation | Vitamin K-dependent modification that lets clotting factors bind calcium |
| Hepcidin | Hepatic hormone that lowers iron absorption by blocking ferroportin |
| Fortification | Adding a nutrient to a staple food for a whole population |

## Common misconceptions

- **"More vitamins are better."** Above requirement there is no gain and, for the fat-soluble vitamins and iron, real harm.
- **"A good diet makes B12 deficiency impossible."** Most deficiency is failure of absorption (intrinsic factor, ileal disease, metformin, acid suppression), not of intake.
- **"Vitamin D is a vitamin."** It is a hormone made in skin; it is a vitamin only for people who do not see the sun.
