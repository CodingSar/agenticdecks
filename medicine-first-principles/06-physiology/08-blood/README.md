## What this area is about

Blood is the one tissue that is a fluid, ~5 L of it in an adult, and the physiology of blood is the physiology of what that fluid must carry and how it must behave. It must carry oxygen in quantities water cannot dissolve, so it is packed with a protein built for cooperative binding inside cells built for deformability. It must stay liquid in a closed system yet seal any breach in minutes, so it carries an enzyme cascade held one step from firing. And because it can be moved from one person to another, its cells carry the antigens that decide whether that transfer is a rescue or a lethal reaction. The white cells, which also travel in blood, are the subject of the [immunology layer](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/README.md); here blood is the oxygen carrier and the self-sealing fluid.

The unifying idea is **a tissue regulated by hormones and cascades rather than by nerves**. Red cell mass is set by erythropoietin closing a loop on renal oxygen tension; iron is set by hepcidin closing a loop on stores and inflammation; clotting is an amplifier with brakes rather than a controller with a set point; and compatibility is set by inherited antigens and the antibodies the immune system makes against the ones it lacks. All of it is measured in the full blood count and the clotting screen, the two most ordered tests in medicine.

```
 marrow --(erythropoietin from kidney; iron via hepcidin/ferroportin)--> red cells (120 d) --> spleen/liver recycle iron, make bilirubin
 marrow --> platelets (7-10 d) --+
 liver  --> clotting factors ----+--> injury: platelet plug -> fibrin clot -> fibrinolysis
 inherited antigens on red cells + acquired antibodies in plasma --> compatibility rules
```

Read red cells first: it introduces the marrow, the hormone loop and the laboratory measures the other two assume. Haemostasis is independent in mechanism but shares the liver (factors) and marrow (platelets) as sources. Blood groups builds on the red cell membrane and on the idea, developed in haemostasis, that plasma carries proteins with their own consequences; it is also the handover to transfusion and to immunology.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Integrative principles](../01-integrative-principles/README.md):** plasma is the sampled compartment of body fluid; haemoglobin is a major buffer in acid–base physiology; exercise and altitude raise red cell mass through the same erythropoietin loop.
- **[Nervous system](../02-nervous-system/README.md):** the brain is the organ least tolerant of reduced oxygen delivery and the one where thrombosis and haemorrhage do the most irreversible harm; it has no direct control over blood composition.
- **[Cardiovascular system](../03-cardiovascular-system/README.md):** oxygen delivery is cardiac output times content, so the heart compensates for anaemia and the blood's viscosity sets part of vascular resistance; endothelium is the anticoagulant surface and shear is what activates von Willebrand factor.
- **[Respiratory system](../04-respiratory-system/README.md):** the lung loads what haemoglobin carries; the dissociation curve is the interface, and hypoxaemia drives erythropoietin.
- **[Renal system](../05-renal-system/README.md):** the kidney is the oxygen sensor that makes erythropoietin and the organ most damaged by the free haemoglobin of intravascular haemolysis; renal failure causes anaemia and platelet dysfunction.
- **[Gastrointestinal system](../06-gastrointestinal-system/README.md):** iron, B12 and folate are absorbed in specific segments; the liver makes the clotting factors, hepcidin, transferrin and haptoglobin and clears bilirubin; occult gut bleeding is the commonest cause of iron deficiency in men.
- **[Endocrine system](../07-endocrine-system/README.md):** erythropoietin and hepcidin are hormones; thyroid hormone, androgens and cortisol modulate red cell production; oestrogens raise clotting factors and thrombosis risk.
- **[Reproduction and the life course](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/README.md):** menstruation and pregnancy are the physiological iron losses; fetal haemoglobin has a left-shifted curve; pregnancy is a hypercoagulable state; Rhesus disease is a conflict between maternal antibody and fetal antigen.
- **[Musculoskeletal system and skin](../10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/README.md):** bone marrow is housed in bone and shares the skeleton's blood supply; muscle holds myoglobin, the monomeric cousin of haemoglobin; bruising and petechiae in skin are the visible sign of haemostatic failure.
