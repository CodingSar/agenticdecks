## Essence

The blood circulation is a closed loop with a leak: every day a few litres of plasma water and about 100 g of protein filter out of the capillaries into the interstitium and do not come back the way they left. The lymphatic system is the return path. Blind-ended vessels in almost every tissue collect the filtrate, propel it by their own contractions and by the squeeze of surrounding muscle through one-way valves, pass it through chains of lymph nodes that sample it for antigen and filter out particles and cells, and empty it into the great veins at the root of the neck. Because this is the only route by which interstitial protein can leave a tissue, and the route by which absorbed fat, migrating immune cells and escaping tumour cells travel, the lymphatics explain a protein-rich oedema that no diuretic clears, the reason a lymph node is biopsied to stage a cancer, and why fat reaches the blood without passing through the liver.

## Core tenets

1. **Lymph is net capillary filtrate, so lymph flow is set by the Starling balance.** Across most capillary beds the hydrostatic push slightly exceeds the oncotic pull along the whole vessel, so fluid filters continuously and little is reabsorbed at the venous end ([microcirculation](microcirculation-and-capillary-exchange.md)). The filtrate carries the plasma proteins that leak through the wall, which would otherwise accumulate and abolish the oncotic gradient within hours. Roughly 2–4 L/day reaches the central veins as lymph (up to ~8 L/day enters the initial lymphatics, with part reabsorbed into blood within lymph nodes); its protein content, ~20–40 g/L on average and highest in liver lymph, is the interstitium's protein being returned.

2. **Initial lymphatics fill passively, and their structure is the valve.** The smallest lymphatics are tubes of overlapping endothelial cells with no continuous basement membrane, held open by anchoring filaments to the surrounding matrix. When interstitial pressure rises or the tissue is stretched, the overlapping flaps part and fluid, protein, particles and cells enter; when the vessel fills, the flaps close. The entry is therefore open to everything in the interstitium, which is how bacteria, dyes, vaccine antigens and tumour cells get in, and why lymph flow rises steeply (tenfold or more) as soon as a tissue starts to swell.

3. **Collecting lymphatics pump: lymph is propelled by intrinsic contraction and extrinsic compression through intraluminal valves.** Downstream vessels acquire smooth muscle and bicuspid valves every few millimetres; each segment between valves (a lymphangion) contracts rhythmically when stretched, several times a minute, pushing lymph forward because the valves stop it going back. Skeletal muscle movement, arterial pulsation, respiration and the negative intrathoracic pressure add to the pumping. This is why the legs of someone standing still swell, why exercise and elevation reduce swelling, and why paralysis or immobility worsens oedema.

4. **Lymph nodes are filters and sampling stations in series on every route to the blood.** Lymph enters a node through afferent vessels into a subcapsular sinus, percolates past macrophages that remove particles, microbes and debris, and leaves by one efferent vessel. Dendritic cells arriving in the lymph present antigen to the lymphocytes that enter the node from blood through high endothelial venules; antibody and activated cells leave in the efferent lymph. A tender, enlarged node is the immune response to what the lymph has brought; the anatomy of drainage, with each region feeding a predictable first node, is what makes the examination of nodes informative ([vascular and lymphatic architecture](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/vascular-and-lymphatic-architecture.md); the immunology follows in [adaptive immunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/README.md)).

5. **Everything returns to the veins through two ducts, and the thoracic duct carries most of it.** Lymph from the legs, abdomen, left arm and left side of the head and chest collects in the thoracic duct, which runs up beside the aorta and empties at the junction of the left internal jugular and subclavian veins; the right lymphatic duct serves only the right upper quadrant. Raised central venous pressure impedes this emptying, which is one reason right heart failure produces oedema and, when the duct is torn by trauma or surgery, lymph fills the pleural space as chylothorax.

6. **Lymphatic obstruction produces a protein-rich oedema that behaves differently from any other.** In venous or hypoalbuminaemic oedema the interstitial fluid is low in protein and still drains; in lymphoedema protein accumulates, the oncotic gradient that favours reabsorption is lost, and over months the swelling becomes firm, non-pitting and fibrotic, with fat deposition, skin thickening and recurrent cellulitis as bacteria enter a tissue the immune system can no longer patrol. Causes are inherited lymphatic malformation (mutations in the lymphatic growth factor receptor VEGFR-3 in Milroy disease), filarial infection (the commonest cause worldwide), and surgery or radiotherapy to axillary or groin nodes. Diuretics remove water but not protein, so they do not help; compression and movement, which drive the remaining pump, do ([oedema](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/edema-and-fluid-imbalance.md)).

7. **The lymphatics are the route for absorbed fat, because chylomicrons are too large for blood capillaries.** Each intestinal villus holds a central lymphatic (a lacteal). Enterocytes package absorbed triglyceride into chylomicrons hundreds of nanometres across, which cannot cross the gut's fenestrated capillaries but enter the open lacteals freely, turning post-prandial intestinal lymph milky (chyle). Chyle reaches the blood through the thoracic duct and bypasses the portal vein, so dietary fat and fat-soluble vitamins reach the systemic circulation before the liver ([digestion and absorption](../06-gastrointestinal-system/secretion-digestion-and-absorption.md)). A leaking or blocked thoracic duct loses fat, protein and lymphocytes.

8. **Tumour cells spread along lymphatics, so the first draining node reports whether a cancer has left its site.** Carcinoma cells that invade the interstitium enter initial lymphatics as easily as any particle, travel to the first node, and may lodge there before entering the blood through the efferent route. The sentinel node, identified by injecting dye or tracer at the tumour, is the first node a tumour drains to; if it is clear, the rest of the basin almost always is, which spares most patients the lymphoedema that follows removal of the whole chain ([invasion and metastasis](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/invasion-metastasis-and-the-tumour-microenvironment.md), [common solid tumours](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md)). Sarcomas, by contrast, spread mainly through the blood.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lymph | Interstitial fluid, protein, cells and particles collected by lymphatics; ~2–4 L/day reaches the veins |
| Initial lymphatic | Blind-ended vessel of overlapping endothelial flaps that open when interstitial pressure rises |
| Lymphangion | Valved, muscular segment of a collecting lymphatic that contracts when stretched; the intrinsic pump |
| Thoracic duct | Main return vessel, draining all but the right upper quadrant into the left subclavian–jugular junction |
| Lymphoedema | Protein-rich oedema from lymphatic obstruction or malformation; firm, fibrotic, prone to cellulitis |
| Chyle / lacteal | Fat-laden intestinal lymph / the lymphatic at the core of each villus that carries chylomicrons |
| Sentinel node | First node draining a tumour; its status predicts the rest of the basin |
| Chylothorax | Lymph in the pleural space from a damaged thoracic duct |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Capillaries reabsorb at their venous end, so lymph is a minor overflow."** Most beds filter along their whole length; lymph is the main return route for filtered fluid and the only one for protein.
- **"Lymphoedema is treated like other oedema."** The excess is protein, not sodium and water; diuretics shrink the plasma volume and leave the limb swollen.
- **"The brain has no lymphatic drainage."** It lacks conventional lymphatics in its parenchyma, but cerebrospinal and interstitial fluid drain through perivascular channels and meningeal lymphatics to cervical nodes.
