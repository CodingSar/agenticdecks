## What this area is about

The circulation exists because diffusion works only over micrometres. Every cell in a 70 kg body must sit within a few tens of micrometres of a capillary, and the capillaries must be fed by a pressure head high enough to drive blood through them and bring it back. This area answers how a pump of ~300 g moves ~5 L/min at rest and 20–25 L/min in exercise, how it times itself, how pressure and flow are distributed among organs with very different needs, and how exchange finally happens at the capillary wall.

The unifying idea is **flow down a pressure gradient against a resistance**, Ohm's law for fluids: cardiac output × total peripheral resistance = arterial pressure. The heart sets the output (with the Frank–Starling mechanism matching it to venous return), the arterioles set the resistance (with local metabolism and the sympathetic system competing for control), and the body regulates the product, arterial pressure, because that is what the baroreceptors can sense. The capillaries are where the whole apparatus pays off, and the Starling balance there decides whether fluid stays in the vessel or leaves it.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Integrative principles](../01-integrative-principles/README.md):** blood pressure is the regulated variable the body defends above almost all others; plasma is the compartment that is pumped; exercise is the system's stress test.
- **[Nervous system](../02-nervous-system/README.md):** the [autonomic nervous system](../02-nervous-system/the-autonomic-nervous-system.md) sets heart rate, contractility and vascular tone; the cardiac action potential is a variant of the neuronal one; the brain is the organ least tolerant of a pause in flow.
- **[Respiratory system](../04-respiratory-system/README.md):** the right heart drives the pulmonary circulation, a low-pressure circuit whose resistance rises with hypoxia; [ventilation–perfusion matching](../04-respiratory-system/ventilation-perfusion-matching.md) is the meeting point.
- **[Renal system](../05-renal-system/README.md):** the kidney takes ~20% of cardiac output, senses pressure and sodium, and sets the long-term level of arterial pressure through [sodium and volume regulation](../05-renal-system/sodium-water-and-volume-regulation.md).
- **[Gastrointestinal system](../06-gastrointestinal-system/README.md):** the splanchnic bed is the largest reservoir of blood that can be mobilised in haemorrhage and the first sacrificed in shock.
- **[Endocrine system](../07-endocrine-system/README.md):** angiotensin II, aldosterone, vasopressin, natriuretic peptides and catecholamines are the hormonal arm of pressure and volume control.
- **[Blood](../08-blood/README.md):** haematocrit sets viscosity and oxygen content; [haemostasis](../08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md) is the circulation's leak repair, and its excess, thrombosis, is its commonest failure.
- **[Reproduction and the life course](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/README.md):** pregnancy raises cardiac output ~40% and lowers resistance; the [fetal circulation](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/fetal-and-neonatal-physiology.md) runs the two sides of the heart in parallel until birth.
- **[Musculoskeletal system and skin](../10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/README.md):** the muscle pump returns venous blood; skin blood flow is the thermoregulatory effector.
