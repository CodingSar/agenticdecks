## Essence

Arterial blood gases are decided not by how much air and blood the lung receives but by how well they are matched, alveolus by alveolus. The whole lung has a ventilation-to-perfusion ratio (V̇/Q̇) of ~0.8, but gravity, disease and posture give individual units ratios from zero (shunt) to infinity (dead space), and each unit's gas tensions follow its own ratio. Because the oxygen–haemoglobin curve is flat at the top, well-ventilated units cannot make up for poorly ventilated ones, so mismatch always lowers arterial oxygen; because the carbon dioxide curve is linear, mismatch raises carbon dioxide only when ventilation cannot increase. The lung's one defence is hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction, which steers blood away from alveoli without air; it is the lung's best adaptation and, when the whole lung is hypoxic, the cause of right heart failure.

## Core tenets

1. **Each lung unit's gas tensions are set by its own ventilation–perfusion ratio.** A unit with V̇/Q̇ near 1 reaches a partial pressure of oxygen (PO2) of ~100 and of carbon dioxide (PCO2) of ~40 mmHg. As its ratio falls its gas approaches mixed venous blood (PO2 40, PCO2 46); at zero it *is* venous blood (shunt). As the ratio rises its gas approaches inspired air (PO2 150, PCO2 0); at infinity it is dead space. Arterial blood is the flow-weighted sum of the units, so what matters is the *distribution* of ratios.

2. **Gravity makes both ventilation and perfusion greatest at the base, but perfusion more so, so the ratio rises toward the apex.** The pulmonary circulation runs at ~25/10 mmHg (mean ~15), and a 30 cm upright lung spans ~23 mmHg of hydrostatic pressure, so apical vessels are barely perfused and basal ones distended. West's zones: at the apex alveolar pressure can exceed arterial (zone 1, no flow; absent in health, produced by haemorrhage or positive-pressure ventilation); in the middle flow depends on the arterial–alveolar difference (zone 2); at the base both vascular pressures exceed alveolar (zone 3). Ventilation also falls toward the apex, but less steeply, so V̇/Q̇ is ~0.6 at the base and ~3 at the apex (apical PO2 ~130, basal ~90 mmHg); tuberculosis favours the oxygen-rich apex.

3. **Mismatch lowers arterial oxygen more than it raises carbon dioxide, and the shape of the two carriage curves explains why.** Blood from a low-ratio unit leaves undersaturated; blood from a high-ratio unit cannot carry much extra oxygen because its haemoglobin is already ~97% saturated. Mixing them gives an arterial PO2 lower than normal. Carbon dioxide content is linear in PCO2, so the high-ratio unit's deficit offsets the low-ratio unit's excess, and any residual rise in arterial PCO2 is sensed by the chemoreceptors, which raise total ventilation until it is normal. Hence mismatch presents as hypoxaemia with a normal or low PCO2, and hypercapnia appears only when ventilation can no longer rise (exhaustion, severe obstruction, depressed drive).

4. **Shunt is the one cause of hypoxaemia that oxygen does not correct.** Blood that passes no ventilated alveolus (consolidated or collapsed lung in pneumonia, atelectasis and acute respiratory distress syndrome; intracardiac right-to-left defects; the ~2–5% normal shunt of bronchial and coronary venous blood) never meets inspired gas, however rich. Breathing 100% oxygen adds little content to the already saturated blood from ventilated units, and the shunted fraction still mixes in. A low-ratio unit, by contrast, is corrected by oxygen because its alveoli receive some gas; the response to oxygen separates the two.

5. **Dead space wastes ventilation, and physiological dead space rises in disease.** Anatomical dead space (~150 mL) is fixed; alveolar dead space is ventilated units without flow: pulmonary embolism, low cardiac output, overdistension by a ventilator. Physiological dead space is their sum, measured by the Bohr equation V_D/V_T = (P_aCO2 − P_ECO2)/P_aCO2 (~0.3 in health). A patient with a large embolus must raise total ventilation to keep PCO2 normal; the hypoxaemia comes mostly from flow redistributed to the remaining units, which then have low ratios.

6. **Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction steers blood toward air, uniquely in this circulation.** Systemic arterioles dilate to hypoxia; pulmonary arterioles constrict when alveolar PO2 falls below ~70 mmHg, through inhibition of oxygen-sensitive potassium channels in their smooth muscle. A consolidated region thus loses its blood flow to better-ventilated lung, which limits the shunt. The same reflex closes the fetal pulmonary circulation and opens it at the first breath. When the whole lung is hypoxic (altitude, chronic lung disease) every vessel constricts, pulmonary pressure rises and the right ventricle hypertrophies and fails (cor pulmonale). Anaesthetics, vasodilators and sepsis blunt the reflex and worsen oxygenation.

7. **The pulmonary circulation is low-pressure and recruitable, so its resistance falls as flow rises.** Pulmonary vascular resistance is about a tenth of the systemic; as cardiac output triples in exercise, closed capillaries open and open ones distend, so pressure rises little. Resistance rises at high lung volumes (alveolar vessels compressed), one reason high-pressure ventilation loads the right heart. Pulmonary capillary pressure is ~7–10 mmHg against an oncotic pressure of ~25, so the Starling balance favours a dry alveolus ([capillary exchange](../03-cardiovascular-system/microcirculation-and-capillary-exchange.md)); oedema forms when left atrial pressure exceeds ~20–25 mmHg or the barrier leaks.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ventilation–perfusion ratio | Alveolar ventilation / blood flow for a unit; ~0.8 for the whole lung |
| Shunt | Perfused units with no ventilation (ratio 0); hypoxaemia not corrected by oxygen |
| Dead space | Ventilated units with no perfusion (ratio ∞); anatomical plus alveolar = physiological |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Hypoxaemia with a normal PCO2 means the lung is fine and the patient is just hypoventilating."** Hypoventilation raises PCO2; a normal or low PCO2 with hypoxaemia means mismatch or shunt.
