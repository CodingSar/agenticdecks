## Essence

The body is a heat engine that converts chemical energy from food into work, storage and, mostly, heat, and it holds its core at ~37 °C whether the air is 0 °C or 40 °C. Heat is produced by metabolism everywhere, carried by blood to the skin and lost by radiation, convection, conduction and evaporation down a temperature gradient from core to skin to environment. The hypothalamus compares core and skin temperature with a set point and adjusts skin blood flow, sweating, shivering and behaviour. Energy balance is the same bookkeeping over weeks instead of minutes: intake minus expenditure equals change in stores, and the stores (mostly fat) are large enough that the balance is regulated loosely, by appetite and expenditure signals, rather than tightly like temperature.

## Core tenets

1. **Heat production is metabolism, and at rest it is ~80 W, spread unevenly.** Basal metabolic rate is ~1,500–1,800 kcal/day in adults (~1 kcal/kg/h), scaling with mass to roughly the 3/4 power across species ([allometry](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/scale-and-allometry.md)). Brain, liver, heart and kidneys, ~6% of mass, produce ~60% of resting heat; resting muscle ~20%. Exercise can raise production tenfold or more, and nearly all of it is heat, since muscle is ~20–25% efficient.

2. **Heat leaves down a gradient through the skin, and skin blood flow is the valve.** Core → skin → environment: radiation (~60% at rest in a cool room), convection and conduction, and evaporation (~0.58 kcal per gram of water). Skin blood flow can vary from well under 0.5 L/min to several litres per minute, which changes the skin temperature and so the gradient to the air; countercurrent arrangement of limb arteries and veins conserves core heat in cold. Within the thermoneutral zone (~25–30 °C for a naked adult) vasomotor adjustment alone keeps balance; outside it, sweating or shivering must be added.

3. **Above skin temperature only evaporation works, so humidity, not air temperature, sets the limit of survival.** Sweat can reach 1–2 L/h, removing ~600–1,200 kcal/h if it evaporates; in saturated air it cannot, and core temperature rises regardless of fitness. Evaporation also costs water and salt, which links thermoregulation to [fluid balance](body-fluid-compartments-and-electrolytes.md): a sweating body loses hypotonic fluid and concentrates its plasma.

4. **The hypothalamus is a thermostat with a set point, and fever is the set point raised, not the thermostat broken.** Preoptic warm-sensitive neurons integrate core temperature with anticipatory input from skin thermoreceptors. Pyrogenic cytokines (interleukin-1, interleukin-6, tumour necrosis factor) from immune cells induce prostaglandin E2 in the hypothalamus, which raises the set point: the patient feels cold, shivers and vasoconstricts until the new value is reached, then sweats when the set point falls. Antipyretics block prostaglandin synthesis. Hyperthermia (heat stroke, malignant hyperthermia, some drugs) is different: the set point is normal but heat loss or production is out of control, and antipyretics do nothing.

5. **Cold is answered by insulation first, then by burning fuel to make heat.** Vasoconstriction and behaviour come first because they cost nothing. Shivering, rhythmic involuntary contraction, raises production up to four- or fivefold. Non-shivering thermogenesis uses brown adipose tissue, whose uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1) lets protons bypass adenosine triphosphate (ATP) synthase so that oxidation releases heat directly; it matters most in the newborn, who has a large surface-to-volume ratio, no shivering and little insulation. Hypothermia below ~32 °C depresses the hypothalamus itself, so the loop fails and cooling accelerates.

6. **Energy balance is a mass balance with a large buffer.** Intake = expenditure + storage. Expenditure is basal metabolism (~60–70%), the thermic effect of food (~10%) and activity (the rest, highly variable). Stores: glycogen ~400–500 g (~2,000 kcal, under a day), fat ~10–20 kg in a lean adult (~100,000 kcal, weeks), and protein, which is functional tissue and is spent last. Because fat is ~9 kcal/g and the daily flux ~2,000–2,500 kcal, a persistent 1% error in balance changes body mass by about a kilogram a year.

7. **Body weight is regulated, loosely, by signals from the stores to the hypothalamus.** Leptin from adipose tissue and insulin report long-term stores; ghrelin from the stomach, and gut peptides such as glucagon-like peptide 1 and peptide YY, report the meal. The arcuate nucleus integrates them through appetite-stimulating (neuropeptide Y, agouti-related peptide) and appetite-suppressing (pro-opiomelanocortin) neurons. The loop defends against loss harder than against gain: when weight falls, leptin falls, appetite rises and resting expenditure drops below what the new mass predicts (adaptive thermogenesis), which is why lost weight tends to return.

8. **Fuel choice follows the fed–fasted switch, and temperature and fuel are linked through thyroid hormone and catecholamines.** Thyroid hormone sets basal heat production (hyperthyroidism: heat intolerance, weight loss; hypothyroidism: cold intolerance, weight gain). Catecholamines raise both lipolysis and thermogenesis. Starvation lowers thyroid hormone and resting metabolism to spare protein, at the cost of a lower core temperature and tolerance of cold ([fed–fasted cycle](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/metabolic-integration-and-the-fed-fasted-cycle.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Core temperature | Temperature of the deep tissues, ~36.5–37.5 °C, with a ~0.5–1 °C daily rhythm |
| Thermoneutral zone | Ambient range in which vasomotor control alone maintains balance |
| Evaporative heat loss | ~0.58 kcal per gram of water; the only route when air is warmer than skin |
| Set point | The hypothalamic target; raised by prostaglandin E2 in fever |
| Fever vs hyperthermia | Raised set point (regulated) vs heat gain exceeding loss (unregulated) |
| Shivering thermogenesis | Involuntary muscle contraction raising heat production several-fold |
| Non-shivering thermogenesis | Heat from uncoupled oxidation in brown fat (UCP1) |
| Basal metabolic rate | Resting energy expenditure, ~1 kcal/kg/h; ~60–70% of the total |
| Adaptive thermogenesis | Fall in expenditure below prediction after weight loss |
| Leptin | Adipose hormone proportional to fat mass; low leptin drives appetite and conserves energy |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Fever is dangerous because of the temperature."** Fever is regulated and rarely exceeds ~41 °C; the danger is the cause. Hyperthermia is unregulated and can exceed that, and it is the heat that kills.
- **"Obesity is simply eating more than you burn."** The arithmetic is true but the two terms are coupled by a loop that defends stored fat, which is why sustained loss is hard and why the loop, not willpower, is the target of effective treatment.
- **"Alcohol warms you."** It dilates skin vessels, which feels warm and loses core heat faster.
