## What this area is about

Before any organ system can be understood, the reader needs to know what the organ systems are *for*: which whole-body quantities they defend, how tightly, with what reserves, and what happens when the whole body is pushed, by exertion, heat, cold, injury or the clock. This area answers that question. It names the regulated variables of the internal environment (the volume and composition of the fluid around every cell, its hydrogen ion concentration, its temperature, the energy available to it) and the one mechanism, negative feedback with a set point, that defends all of them.

The unifying idea is Claude Bernard's *milieu intérieur*, made quantitative by Walter Cannon (homeostasis, 1929) and by control theory: a cell in a 70 kg body lives in a bath whose composition is held steady by organs acting on sensed error. Everything from the kidney's handling of sodium to the brainstem's handling of carbon dioxide is an instance of the loops drawn here.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Nervous system](../02-nervous-system/README.md):** the hypothalamus is the comparator for temperature, osmolality, energy and the clock; the [autonomic nervous system](../02-nervous-system/the-autonomic-nervous-system.md) is the fast effector for nearly every loop here.
- **[Cardiovascular system](../03-cardiovascular-system/README.md):** plasma is the smallest fluid compartment but the one that is pumped; exercise is the cardiovascular system's stress test, and blood pressure is the loop the body defends above volume.
- **[Respiratory system](../04-respiratory-system/README.md):** the lung disposes of some 15,000 mmol of acid a day as carbon dioxide; [control of breathing](../04-respiratory-system/control-of-breathing.md) is the fast half of acid–base regulation.
- **[Renal system](../05-renal-system/README.md):** the kidney sets the volume and composition of the extracellular fluid in the long run and excretes the non-volatile acid; [sodium, water and volume regulation](../05-renal-system/sodium-water-and-volume-regulation.md) completes the loop opened in fluid compartments.
- **[Gastrointestinal system](../06-gastrointestinal-system/README.md):** intake side of energy balance and of water and salt; the [liver](../06-gastrointestinal-system/the-liver-as-metabolic-hub.md) switches fuels between fed and fasted states.
- **[Endocrine system](../07-endocrine-system/README.md):** the slow effectors: aldosterone and vasopressin for volume and osmolality, thyroid hormone for basal heat, cortisol and catecholamines for stress, leptin and insulin for energy stores.
- **[Blood](../08-blood/README.md):** haemoglobin is the main non-bicarbonate buffer and the oxygen reserve that exercise draws on.
- **[Reproduction and the life course](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/README.md):** pregnancy and the newborn run on shifted set points for osmolality, temperature and carbon dioxide.
- **[Musculoskeletal system and skin](../10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/README.md):** muscle is the main heat source and the main consumer in exercise; skin is the thermoregulatory effector.
