## Essence

Pain is a sensory system with a threat detector at the periphery and a volume control at every relay. Nociceptors, free nerve endings tuned to tissue damage, signal through thin fibres to the dorsal horn, where the signal is amplified or suppressed before ascending to a network that produces both the location of the pain and its unpleasantness. Inflammation and repeated firing turn the gain up, so a healing wound hurts to touch; descending pathways driven by expectation, context and endogenous opioids turn it down. Pain is a perception built from nociception, not a readout of damage, and chronic pain is often a disorder of the gain rather than the tissue. Nearly every analgesic acts at one of these control points.

## Core tenets

1. **Nociceptors are specialised, high-threshold, and come in two speeds.** Free nerve endings carry channels opened by noxious heat and acid (TRPV1, also opened by capsaicin), by mechanical injury and by adenosine triphosphate from broken cells. Thinly myelinated Aδ fibres (~5–30 m/s) carry sharp, localised first pain; unmyelinated C fibres (<2 m/s) carry the dull, burning second pain. Nociceptors adapt little and are silent in health. People born without functional Nav1.7 sodium channels feel no pain and accumulate injuries, which shows the system's purpose.

2. **The dorsal horn is the first gate, and its output can be turned down by other sensation.** Nociceptive afferents release glutamate and substance P onto second-order neurons in the superficial laminae. Melzack and Wall's gate control theory (1965) proposed, and later work confirmed, that large-diameter touch afferents excite inhibitory interneurons that dampen these neurons, which is why rubbing a bruise helps. The same interneurons are the target of descending inhibition.

3. **Two ascending systems carry two aspects of pain: where it is, and how much it matters.** The lateral spinothalamic pathway, through the ventroposterior thalamus to somatosensory cortex, gives location and intensity. Medial pathways, through the parabrachial nucleus and medial thalamus to the anterior cingulate, insula and amygdala, give the unpleasantness and the drive to escape. Drugs can separate them: a patient on morphine may report the pain is still there but no longer bothers them.

4. **Descending modulation from the brainstem sets the gain, and the endogenous opioids run it.** The periaqueductal grey, driven by cortex, hypothalamus and amygdala, excites the rostral ventromedial medulla, whose serotonergic and noradrenergic projections inhibit dorsal horn transmission. Enkephalins and β-endorphin act on μ-opioid receptors at every level, reducing transmitter release and hyperpolarising neurons. Morphine mimics them; naloxone blocks them and also blocks much of placebo analgesia, which is how expectation becomes chemistry. Amitriptyline and duloxetine relieve neuropathic pain by prolonging noradrenaline and serotonin action in these pathways.

5. **Peripheral sensitisation lowers the threshold at the injury; central sensitisation spreads it beyond.** Prostaglandin E2, bradykinin, nerve growth factor and protons from inflamed tissue sensitise nociceptor channels so that innocuous warmth and pressure hurt (primary hyperalgesia); non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs act here by blocking prostaglandin synthesis. Sustained C-fibre input makes dorsal horn neurons progressively more responsive ("wind-up", through NMDA receptors), recruits microglia and weakens inhibition, so touch is felt as pain (allodynia) and pain spreads to uninjured skin. When this outlasts the injury it is a disease of the nervous system itself, with no peripheral lesion left to treat.

6. **Neuropathic pain arises from damaged nerves firing on their own.** Injured axons accumulate sodium channels and fire ectopically; the pain is burning, shooting, present at rest and in the territory of the nerve or root. Carbamazepine quiets the ectopic firing; gabapentin and pregabalin bind the α2δ subunit of presynaptic calcium channels and reduce release. Phantom limb pain shows that the map persists and can hurt when its input is gone.

7. **Visceral pain is poorly localised and referred, because viscera share spinal neurons with skin.** The viscera have few nociceptors, respond to stretch, ischaemia and inflammation but not to cutting, and their afferents travel with autonomic nerves to converge on dorsal horn neurons that also receive input from a dermatome ([segmental innervation](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)). The brain attributes the signal to the skin it usually comes from: cardiac ischaemia to the left arm, diaphragmatic irritation to the shoulder tip, early appendicitis to the umbilicus. Visceral pain is dull, midline, colicky when a hollow organ contracts against obstruction, and accompanied by sweating and nausea; it becomes sharp and localised only when the inflamed organ touches the somatically innervated parietal peritoneum.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nociception vs pain | The neural encoding of noxious stimuli vs the unpleasant experience built from it |
| Aδ / C fibres | Fast, sharp, localised first pain / slow, dull, diffuse second pain |
| Gate control | Inhibition of dorsal horn pain transmission by large-fibre touch input |
| Periaqueductal grey | Midbrain origin of descending pain inhibition; rich in opioid receptors |
| Hyperalgesia / allodynia | Increased pain from a painful stimulus / pain from a normally painless one |
| Peripheral / central sensitisation | Lowered nociceptor threshold at the injury / amplified spinal and brain processing |
| Neuropathic pain | Pain from a lesion of the somatosensory system itself |
| Referred pain | Visceral pain felt in the dermatome that shares its spinal neurons |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Pain is proportional to tissue damage."** Soldiers with major wounds often report little pain; people with no lesion can have severe pain; the gain, not the input, decides.
- **"Chronic pain means something is still wrong in the tissue."** Often the tissue has healed and the nervous system has stayed sensitised; imaging the tissue then finds nothing to fix.
