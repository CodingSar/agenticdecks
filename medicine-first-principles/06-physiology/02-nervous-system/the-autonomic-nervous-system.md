## Essence

The autonomic nervous system is the motor system of the viscera: it runs the heart, vessels, airways, gut, bladder, pupils, glands and metabolism without conscious command, and it is the fast effector of nearly every homeostatic loop. The sympathetic division mobilises the body and is organised for mass action; the parasympathetic conserves and acts organ by organ. Most organs receive both, tonically active, so the output is a balance that can be shifted either way. Because the transmitters act on a small set of receptor subtypes with known second messengers, the autonomic nervous system is the most predictable pharmacological target in medicine.

## Core tenets

1. **Both divisions are two-neuron chains with acetylcholine at the ganglion, and they differ in where the ganglion sits.** A preganglionic neuron synapses on nicotinic receptors of a postganglionic neuron, which innervates the target. Sympathetic preganglionic cells lie in T1–L2 and synapse near the cord in the paravertebral chain or prevertebral ganglia, so a short fibre fans out to long postganglionic fibres reaching many targets (mass action). Parasympathetic preganglionic cells lie in cranial nerves III, VII, IX and X and in S2–S4, and synapse in ganglia within or beside the organ, so each output is local. The vagus carries about three-quarters of parasympathetic outflow.

2. **The postganglionic transmitter is noradrenaline for the sympathetic and acetylcholine for the parasympathetic, with instructive exceptions.** Sympathetic fibres to sweat glands release acetylcholine onto muscarinic receptors (so anticholinergic drugs cause dry skin); the adrenal medulla is a sympathetic ganglion whose cells secrete adrenaline (~80%) and noradrenaline into blood, turning a neural discharge into a hormone that reaches every tissue. Co-transmitters (adenosine triphosphate, neuropeptide Y, nitric oxide) add slower components; erection is parasympathetic nitric oxide, not acetylcholine.

3. **The receptor subtype, with its G-protein, predicts the response and the drug effect.** α1 (Gq → inositol trisphosphate, calcium): vascular and sphincter smooth muscle contraction, pupil dilation. α2 (Gi): presynaptic inhibition of noradrenaline release; central sympathetic inhibition. β1 (Gs → cyclic adenosine monophosphate, cAMP): faster, stronger heart; renin release. β2 (Gs): relaxation of bronchial, uterine and skeletal-muscle-vessel smooth muscle; glycogenolysis; potassium uptake into cells. Muscarinic M2 (Gi): slows the sinus node and atrioventricular conduction. M3 (Gq): contracts bronchial, gut and bladder smooth muscle, drives secretion, releases endothelial nitric oxide. The effects of a β-blocker, an α1-blocker or a muscarinic antagonist follow from this table ([autonomic drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/autonomic-and-cardiovascular-drugs.md)).

4. **Most organs are dually innervated with tonic activity in both, so control is a balance and removing one arm reveals the other.** The sinus node fires at ~100/min when denervated; resting rate is ~70 because vagal tone outweighs sympathetic. Atropine raises the rate, a β-blocker lowers it, and a transplanted heart runs at ~100 and speeds up in exercise only as adrenaline arrives. The two arms are not always antagonists: salivation is driven by both; erection is parasympathetic and emission sympathetic.

5. **Blood vessels are the exception: mostly sympathetic only, so vascular tone is set by the level of one signal.** Arterioles and veins receive tonic sympathetic vasoconstrictor fibres (α1) and almost no parasympathetic supply, so vasodilation comes from *withdrawing* sympathetic tone, from local metabolites, or from adrenaline on β2 receptors in muscle vessels. This is why the baroreflex can adjust resistance with a single variable, and why high spinal cord injury or spinal anaesthesia drops blood pressure: nothing is telling the vessels to constrict.

6. **The system is run from the hypothalamus and brainstem, and the baroreflex is its model loop.** Baroreceptor afferents (glossopharyngeal, vagus) reach the nucleus of the solitary tract, which inhibits the rostral ventrolateral medulla (sympathetic drive) and excites the nucleus ambiguus (vagal drive), lowering pressure within a heartbeat or two ([hemodynamics](../03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md)). The hypothalamus layers temperature, volume, hunger, circadian and emotional inputs on top, and can command the whole sympathetic outflow at once (fight or flight) or a single channel.

7. **Autonomic failure and autonomic excess are clinical syndromes, and denervated targets become supersensitive.** Loss of sympathetic vasoconstriction (diabetic neuropathy, multiple system atrophy, drugs) gives orthostatic hypotension without compensatory tachycardia; loss of parasympathetic tone gives a fixed heart rate, constipation, urinary retention and dry mouth. Excess discharge (phaeochromocytoma, autonomic dysreflexia after high cord injury) produces hypertensive crisis. A target deprived of its transmitter increases its receptors and over-responds to circulating agonists.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Preganglionic / postganglionic | First (cholinergic, nicotinic synapse) and second neuron of the autonomic chain |
| Thoracolumbar / craniosacral outflow | Origin of the sympathetic (T1–L2) and parasympathetic (III, VII, IX, X; S2–S4) divisions |
| Adrenal medulla | Modified sympathetic ganglion releasing adrenaline and noradrenaline into blood |
| Adrenoceptors (α1, α2, β1, β2, β3) | Noradrenaline and adrenaline receptors; each with a characteristic G-protein and tissue |
| Muscarinic receptors (M1–M3) | Acetylcholine receptors on targets; M2 heart, M3 smooth muscle and glands |
| Vasomotor tone | Resting sympathetic constriction of vessels; its withdrawal is the main route to vasodilation |
| Baroreflex | Pressure-sensing loop through the nucleus of the solitary tract that adjusts both divisions |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Sympathetic means excitation, parasympathetic means inhibition."** The sympathetic system inhibits gut motility and the parasympathetic excites it; the receptor and tissue decide.
- **"The two divisions always oppose each other."** Many targets have one supply (vessels, sweat glands, adrenal medulla), and some functions use both in sequence.
