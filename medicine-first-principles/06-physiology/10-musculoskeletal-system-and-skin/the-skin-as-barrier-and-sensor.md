## Essence

The skin is the largest organ, ~1.8 m² and ~15% of body weight, and it is the boundary condition for the rest of physiology. It stops the body's 60% water from evaporating into air, keeps out microbes, chemicals and ultraviolet light, dissipates or conserves the heat the body produces, and reports touch, temperature and injury to the brain. It does this with a dead outer layer a few hundredths of a millimetre thick, continuously renewed from below; a vascular dermis whose blood flow can vary a hundred-fold; and a dense innervation. Loss of the barrier (burns, blistering disease) kills by fluid loss and infection within days, and nearly every skin disease is a failure of renewal, barrier, pigment or immune surveillance.

## Core tenets

1. **The barrier is the stratum corneum: dead cells in a lipid mortar.** Keratinocytes are born in the basal layer, differentiate as they move up over ~2 weeks, then die, flatten into corneocytes packed with cross-linked keratin, and are cemented by ceramides, cholesterol and fatty acids extruded from lamellar bodies into a "bricks and mortar" wall 10–20 cells thick. This layer, with the tight junctions beneath it, limits transepidermal water loss to ~300–400 mL/day and blocks most water-soluble molecules. Filaggrin, the protein that aggregates keratin and whose breakdown products hold water in the corneocyte, is defective in many people with atopic eczema: a leaky barrier that lets allergens in and water out ([inflammatory skin disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/11-dermatology/inflammatory-skin-disease.md)). The whole stratum corneum is shed and replaced every ~2 weeks; the full epidermis turns over in ~4–6 weeks, and in psoriasis in ~4 days.

2. **The same lipid barrier decides what drugs cross it.** Lipid-soluble, small, unionised molecules diffuse through the intercellular lipid; water-soluble and large ones do not. Transdermal delivery (nitrates, oestrogen, nicotine, fentanyl) works for potent lipophilic drugs and gives slow, steady absorption; occlusion, hydration, heat and a damaged barrier increase it greatly, which is why topical steroids are more potent under a dressing and in infants, whose surface-to-volume ratio is three times an adult's ([absorption and bioavailability](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/absorption-and-bioavailability.md)). Burns remove the barrier entirely and the patient loses water, protein and heat through the wound in proportion to its area.

3. **Skin blood flow is the main effector of thermoregulation, and it is controlled centrally.** The dermis holds a superficial and a deep plexus with arteriovenous anastomoses in the hands, feet, ears and face. Sympathetic noradrenergic tone constricts them when core temperature falls, reducing skin flow to ~0.2 L/min and making the skin an insulating shell; when core rises, active vasodilatation (sympathetic cholinergic fibres, with sweat gland co-transmitters) raises flow to as much as 7–8 L/min, a third of cardiac output, turning the skin into a radiator. Core and skin temperature are sensed separately; the hypothalamus integrates them ([thermoregulation](../01-integrative-principles/thermoregulation-and-energy-balance.md)). The pale, cold skin of shock and the flushed skin of fever are this loop seen from outside.

4. **Sweating is the only way to lose heat when the air is warmer than the skin.** Two to four million eccrine glands secrete a hypotonic fluid under sympathetic cholinergic control; evaporating one litre removes ~2,400 kJ, and a heat-acclimatised adult can sweat 2–3 L/hour. The gland reabsorbs sodium from the primary secretion, more efficiently after acclimatisation and under aldosterone, so sweat is saltier in the unacclimatised and in cystic fibrosis, where the chloride channel defect leaves sodium chloride unabsorbed (the basis of the sweat test). Sweating fails in high humidity, in dehydration and under anticholinergic drugs, and heatstroke follows.

5. **The skin is a sense organ with receptors tuned to different physical quantities.** Mechanoreceptors divide labour by adaptation and field: Merkel discs (fine texture, slowly adapting), Meissner corpuscles (light touch, flutter), Pacinian corpuscles (vibration, deep, very rapidly adapting) and Ruffini endings (stretch). Free nerve endings carry temperature (through TRP channels that open at specific temperature ranges; menthol and capsaicin hijack them) and nociception, with fast myelinated A-delta fibres for sharp pain and slow unmyelinated C fibres for burning pain and itch. Receptor density sets acuity: two-point discrimination is ~2 mm on the fingertip and ~40 mm on the back, mapped onto proportionally sized cortical areas ([sensory systems](../02-nervous-system/sensory-systems.md)). Loss of protective sensation (diabetic neuropathy, leprosy) is why painless injuries become ulcers.

6. **Melanin is a tunable ultraviolet shield, and the shield has a cost.** Melanocytes in the basal layer (about one per ten keratinocytes, similar in number in all people) package melanin into melanosomes and hand them to keratinocytes, where they cap the nucleus. Darker skin has more and larger eumelanin-rich melanosomes, not more melanocytes, and absorbs ultraviolet before it reaches Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA). Ultraviolet B causes direct DNA damage (pyrimidine dimers) and ultraviolet A generates oxidative damage; both drive the mutations of [skin cancer](../../12-organ-system-specialties/11-dermatology/skin-cancer-and-skin-infections.md), which is 10–50 times commoner in light than dark skin at the same latitude. But the same ultraviolet B is what makes vitamin D from 7-dehydrocholesterol, so pigmentation is a trade-off between photoprotection and vitamin D synthesis set by ancestral latitude ([human evolution and variation](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/human-evolution-and-variation.md), [waves and radiation](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md)). Tanning is melanin induction by damaged DNA: a repair response, not a sign of health.

7. **The skin is an immune organ and a microbial habitat.** Keratinocytes express pattern-recognition receptors and secrete antimicrobial peptides and cytokines; Langerhans cells in the epidermis and dendritic cells and resident memory T cells in the dermis sample antigen and mount or recall responses locally ([barriers and pattern recognition](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md)). The surface is acidic (pH ~5), dry and salty, and carries a resident community (staphylococci, corynebacteria, *Cutibacterium*, *Malassezia*) that varies by site and excludes pathogens. Contact dermatitis, psoriasis and eczema are this immune apparatus misdirected; impetigo and cellulitis are the microbial community breaching a damaged barrier.

8. **Wound healing is the skin's version of the universal repair sequence, and it leaves a scar.** Haemostasis, then inflammation (neutrophils, then macrophages clearing debris and releasing growth factors), then proliferation (keratinocytes migrate across the wound within hours to days, fibroblasts lay down collagen, new capillaries form granulation tissue, myofibroblasts contract the wound), then months of remodelling as type III collagen is replaced by type I and the scar gains ~70–80% of normal strength but never regains hair, glands or full elasticity. Epidermis regenerates perfectly; dermis does not. Oxygen, perfusion, nutrition, glucose control and the absence of infection and pressure decide whether a wound closes; the chronic ulcers of diabetes, venous disease and immobility are each a failure of one of those ([tissue repair](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stratum corneum | Outer layer of dead, keratin-filled corneocytes in a lipid matrix; the water and chemical barrier |
| Filaggrin | Keratin-aggregating protein whose deficiency weakens the barrier, predisposing to eczema |
| Transepidermal water loss | Passive water diffusion through intact skin, ~300–400 mL/day |
| Arteriovenous anastomoses | Dermal shunts in extremities whose sympathetic control sets skin blood flow for heat exchange |
| Eccrine sweat gland | Cholinergic sympathetic gland producing hypotonic sweat for evaporative cooling |
| Mechanoreceptor classes | Merkel, Meissner, Pacinian and Ruffini endings, distinguished by stimulus, field size and adaptation |
| TRP channels | Temperature-gated ion channels in free nerve endings; targets of capsaicin and menthol |
| Melanosome | Melanin-containing organelle transferred from melanocyte to keratinocyte to shield the nucleus |
| Langerhans cell | Epidermal dendritic cell that captures antigen and presents it in lymph nodes |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Skin is a passive wrapping."** It is a thermoregulatory effector handling up to a third of cardiac output, an immune organ, an endocrine gland (vitamin D) and the largest sense organ.
- **"Dark skin has more melanocytes."** Melanocyte numbers are similar; the difference is the amount, type and distribution of melanin within keratinocytes.
- **"A tan protects."** A tan is evidence of DNA damage and gives a sun protection factor of only ~2–4; it marks exposure rather than preventing harm.
