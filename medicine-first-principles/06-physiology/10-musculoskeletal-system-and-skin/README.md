## What this area is about

This area is the body's interface with the physical world: the muscle that moves it, the skeleton and joints that give it shape and leverage and bear its loads, and the skin that separates it from the environment while sensing that environment and exchanging heat with it. Together they are ~60% of body mass and most of what a patient can see and feel of themselves. They are often taught as structure, but each is a dynamic physiological system: muscle converts Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) into force and heat and is the body's largest metabolic sink; bone is remodelled continuously and tunes its mass to the loads muscle applies; skin is renewed every few weeks and varies its blood flow a hundred-fold to hold core temperature.

The unifying idea is **tissues that remodel themselves to match the demands placed on them**. Muscle hypertrophies with load and wastes without it; bone adds mass along lines of strain and loses it in disuse; skin thickens where it is rubbed and heals where it is cut. Each therefore carries a record of how the body has been used, each can be trained, and each fails in two ways: acute injury that exceeds what it can bear, and the slow decline of disuse, ageing and hormonal change that reduces reserve until an ordinary load breaks it.

Read muscle first: it introduces the force–energy relationship and the loads that the bone leaf treats as the stimulus for remodelling. Bone and joints follow, with the endocrine side cross-referenced to [calcium and bone mineral metabolism](../07-endocrine-system/calcium-and-bone-mineral-metabolism.md). Skin stands slightly apart in structure but closes two loops: the heat that muscle generates must leave through it, and the vitamin D that bone needs is made in it.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Integrative principles](../01-integrative-principles/README.md):** skin is the main effector of thermoregulation; muscle is the main heat producer and the main consumer of oxygen in exercise; exercise physiology is largely muscle energetics scaled to the whole body; posture and movement are lost in sleep.
- **[Nervous system](../02-nervous-system/README.md):** the motor system ends at the neuromuscular junction; proprioceptors in muscle, tendon, joint and skin feed motor control; the skin is the somatosensory periphery; pain begins in skin, muscle and joint nociceptors.
- **[Cardiovascular system](../03-cardiovascular-system/README.md):** skeletal muscle blood flow rises twenty-fold in exercise and its pump returns venous blood; skin flow can take a third of cardiac output in heat; both compete with vital organs for a fixed output.
- **[Respiratory system](../04-respiratory-system/README.md):** the respiratory muscles are skeletal muscle and fatigue like any other; the chest wall is a bony-cartilaginous bellows whose compliance is part of lung mechanics; muscle carbon dioxide output drives ventilation.
- **[Renal system](../05-renal-system/README.md):** muscle is the potassium reservoir and the source of creatinine, so Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) estimates depend on muscle mass; rhabdomyolysis injures the kidney with myoglobin and potassium; bone and kidney share calcium and phosphate balance; skin is the other route of water and salt loss.
- **[Gastrointestinal system](../06-gastrointestinal-system/README.md):** calcium, phosphate and vitamin D from the gut supply bone; muscle takes up most post-prandial glucose; the gut and skin are the body's two barrier epithelia with layered defences and rapid turnover.
- **[Endocrine system](../07-endocrine-system/README.md):** bone is the target of Parathyroid Hormone (PTH), vitamin D, oestrogen, cortisol and growth hormone and the source of Fibroblast Growth Factor 23 (FGF23); skin makes vitamin D; muscle is the main insulin-sensitive tissue; cortisol and thyroid hormone excess waste both muscle and bone.
- **[Blood](../08-blood/README.md):** the marrow lives inside bone; myoglobin in muscle is haemoglobin's monomeric relative; bruising, petechiae and pallor in skin are the visible signs of blood disorders.
- **[Reproduction and the life course](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/README.md):** oestrogen closes the growth plates in both sexes and its loss at menopause starts bone loss; pregnancy and lactation draw on maternal calcium; the newborn's skin is a poor barrier and its surface-to-volume ratio makes heat loss rapid.
