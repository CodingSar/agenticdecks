## Essence

Ionised calcium in extracellular fluid (~1.2 mmol/L) must be held within a few percent because it sets the threshold of every excitable membrane, triggers every muscle contraction and secretion, and is a ubiquitous second messenger. The skeleton holds 99% of the body's calcium (~1 kg) and acts as the reservoir, and three hormones manage the exchange between it, the gut and the kidney: Parathyroid Hormone (PTH), the minute-to-minute defender of plasma calcium; active vitamin D (calcitriol), the day-to-week supplier through absorption; and Fibroblast Growth Factor 23 (FGF23), bone's own signal for disposing of phosphate. The parathyroid senses calcium directly with a membrane receptor, so this loop has no pituitary tier and reacts in minutes. Disorders of calcium are read as this loop: which hormone, which effector organ, and whether the skeleton is being sacrificed to keep the plasma right.

## Core tenets

1. **The parathyroid gland is the sensor and PTH the fast controller.** The calcium-sensing receptor on chief cells is a G-protein-coupled receptor whose ligand is ionised calcium itself; a fall of ~0.05 mmol/L raises PTH secretion within seconds to minutes, with a steep sigmoid relationship. PTH, a peptide with a half-life of a few minutes, raises plasma calcium through three effectors: it releases calcium (and phosphate) from bone, it increases calcium reabsorption and decreases phosphate reabsorption in the kidney, and it switches on renal 1α-hydroxylase to make calcitriol, which raises intestinal absorption. Rising calcium then suppresses PTH; calcitriol suppresses PTH gene transcription as a second brake.

   ```
   low ionised Ca2+ --> parathyroid (CaSR) --> PTH
                                   |-> bone: osteoclast resorption -> Ca2+ and PO4 released
                                   |-> kidney: Ca2+ reabsorbed, PO4 excreted, 1a-hydroxylase on
                                   |-> calcitriol -> gut: Ca2+ and PO4 absorbed
                                   v
                        ionised Ca2+ restored --> PTH suppressed
   ```

2. **Vitamin D is a hormone the body makes, not a vitamin it must eat, and it is activated in two organs.** Ultraviolet B converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin to cholecalciferol; the liver hydroxylates it to 25-hydroxyvitamin D, the storage form measured in blood (half-life ~2–3 weeks); the kidney, under PTH and low phosphate, makes 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol), the active hormone, and FGF23 inhibits this step. Calcitriol acts through a nuclear receptor to increase intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption from ~15% to ~30–40% of intake. Without it, dietary calcium is poorly absorbed, PTH rises, and new bone cannot mineralise: rickets in children, osteomalacia in adults. Skin pigmentation, latitude, clothing and indoor life all reduce synthesis ([vitamins and cofactors](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/vitamins-minerals-and-cofactors.md)).

3. **Phosphate is managed by bone's own hormone, and the two loops conflict.** Osteocytes secrete FGF23 when phosphate and calcitriol are high; it removes sodium–phosphate cotransporters from the proximal tubule and suppresses 1α-hydroxylase. PTH is also phosphaturic, so it can raise calcium from bone without raising the calcium–phosphate product to the point of precipitation. In [chronic kidney disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md), falling GFR retains phosphate, FGF23 and PTH rise, calcitriol falls, calcium falls, PTH rises further: secondary hyperparathyroidism that strips bone and calcifies vessels, derived step by step from the loop.

4. **Bone is a mineral bank and PTH's dose pattern decides whether it withdraws or deposits.** Continuous high PTH drives osteoblasts to express RANK ligand, recruiting osteoclasts that dissolve matrix and release calcium: net resorption, as in hyperparathyroidism. Intermittent brief PTH exposure stimulates osteoblasts more than osteoclasts and builds bone, which is why daily injections of a PTH analogue are an anabolic osteoporosis treatment while the same hormone secreted continuously by an adenoma causes bone loss. The remodelling cell biology is in [bone remodelling and joints](../10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/bone-remodelling-and-joints.md).

5. **Plasma calcium is defended at the skeleton's expense.** The loop regulates extracellular calcium, not bone mass. In chronic calcium or vitamin D deficiency, PTH keeps plasma calcium normal by resorbing bone for years, and the patient presents with fractures, not hypocalcaemia. Oestrogen restrains osteoclasts, so its loss at menopause accelerates withdrawal; cortisol excess, immobility and ageing do the same. Peak bone mass is reached by ~30 and declines after, so the skeleton at 70 is set by what was banked and what was spent.

6. **Calcitonin and the other calcium hormones are minor in adults.** Calcitonin from thyroid C cells lowers calcium by inhibiting osteoclasts, but its removal (thyroidectomy) or excess (medullary thyroid cancer) leaves calcium normal; it is a marker, not a regulator, in humans. Oestrogen, androgens, growth hormone, thyroid hormone and cortisol all modify bone turnover but are not sensing calcium. The loop has one fast controller (PTH) and one slow supplier (vitamin D), which keeps diagnosis tractable.

7. **Symptoms of calcium disorder are symptoms of altered excitability.** Low ionised calcium lowers the threshold of sodium channels, so nerves fire spontaneously: tingling, carpopedal spasm, laryngospasm, seizures. Alkalosis causes the same by shifting calcium onto albumin, the mechanism of tetany in hyperventilation. High calcium raises thresholds and impairs renal concentrating ability: lethargy, constipation, polyuria and dehydration, with the kidney stones and bone pain that follow prolonged elevation ([bone and calcium disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/bone-and-calcium-disorders.md)). The free ion, not the total, produces all of this.

8. **The paired measurement reads the loop.** High calcium with high or unsuppressed PTH is primary hyperparathyroidism (the gland is autonomous); high calcium with suppressed PTH is from elsewhere: malignancy secreting PTH-related peptide or dissolving bone, excess vitamin D, or granulomatous tissue making calcitriol. Low calcium with high PTH is a working gland facing vitamin D deficiency or kidney failure; low calcium with low PTH is parathyroid failure, most often after neck surgery. Phosphate, moving opposite to PTH, confirms the direction.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ionised calcium | The free ~50% of plasma calcium, ~1.2 mmol/L, the regulated and active fraction |
| Calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) | Parathyroid G-protein-coupled receptor for ionised calcium; mutations shift the set point |
| Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) | Fast peptide controller raising calcium via bone, kidney and calcitriol synthesis; phosphaturic |
| 25-hydroxyvitamin D | Hepatic storage form; the measure of vitamin D status |
| Calcitriol | 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, the active hormone made by renal 1α-hydroxylase; increases gut absorption |
| FGF23 | Osteocyte hormone that excretes phosphate and suppresses calcitriol synthesis |
| RANK ligand | Osteoblast signal that recruits and activates osteoclasts; induced by continuous PTH |
| Secondary hyperparathyroidism | Appropriate PTH rise in response to low calcium (vitamin D deficiency, kidney failure), at the cost of bone |
| Tetany | Spontaneous nerve and muscle firing from low ionised calcium |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Vitamin D is a nutrient like vitamin C."** It is a prohormone made in skin, activated by liver and kidney, acting through a nuclear receptor under PTH control; diet is a minor source for most people.
- **"Calcium supplements build bone."** Bone mass is set by remodelling balance; extra calcium beyond adequacy does little, and the loop will resorb bone whenever plasma calcium needs defending regardless of intake.
- **"PTH destroys bone."** Continuous PTH resorbs; intermittent PTH builds. Pattern, not presence, decides.
