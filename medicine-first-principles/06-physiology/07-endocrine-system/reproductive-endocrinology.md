## Essence

Reproduction is run by one axis in both sexes: hypothalamic Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH), pituitary Luteinising Hormone (LH) and Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), and gonadal steroids made from cholesterol. The axis is unusual in three ways. Its hypothalamic signal is a pulse train whose frequency is the message. It is switched off for a decade of childhood and on again at puberty. And in females its feedback changes sign once a month, so that oestrogen which normally suppresses LH instead triggers the surge that releases an egg. Understanding those three features explains puberty, the menstrual cycle, contraception, the menopause and most of the infertility and hypogonadism seen in clinics.

## Core tenets

1. **GnRH is pulsatile, and frequency encodes which gonadotropin is made.** A hypothalamic pulse generator (kisspeptin neurones driving GnRH neurones) releases GnRH into the pituitary portal blood roughly every 60–120 minutes. Fast pulses favour LH synthesis, slow pulses favour FSH; continuous GnRH down-regulates its receptor and shuts both off. The axis can therefore be suppressed pharmacologically with a long-acting GnRH agonist (used in prostate cancer, endometriosis and before fertility treatment), and a pulse generator that fails or slows (anorexia, intense exercise, stress, prolactin excess) produces hypogonadotropic hypogonadism with the gonads intact ([hormone axes](hormone-classes-axes-and-feedback.md)).

2. **The gonads are two-cell factories that split steroid synthesis between LH and FSH targets.** In the testis, LH drives Leydig cells to make testosterone (~5–7 mg/day in adult men), and FSH with testosterone drives Sertoli cells to support spermatogenesis. In the ovary, LH drives theca cells to make androgens, and FSH drives granulosa cells to aromatise those androgens into oestradiol. All of it begins with cholesterol, as in the adrenal, so the same enzymes appear and the same enzyme defects cross both glands. Androgens are thus obligatory precursors of oestrogens, and aromatase inhibitors lower oestrogen by blocking the last step ([endocrine drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/endocrine-and-metabolic-drugs.md)).

3. **Gonadal feedback has two arms: steroids on LH and inhibin on FSH.** Testosterone and oestradiol inhibit GnRH and LH; inhibin, a peptide from Sertoli and granulosa cells, inhibits FSH selectively. This lets the pituitary read gamete production (inhibin) separately from steroid output. A man with destroyed seminiferous tubules but intact Leydig cells has high FSH and normal LH; primary gonadal failure in either sex gives high LH and FSH with low steroids, whereas pituitary failure gives low values throughout: the paired-measurement logic of every axis.

4. **In females, oestrogen feedback flips from negative to positive, and that flip is ovulation.** Through most of the cycle oestradiol suppresses LH. When a dominant follicle sustains oestradiol above a threshold (~700 pmol/L or more) for about two days, the hypothalamus and pituitary switch to positive feedback and release an LH surge some ten times baseline, which triggers meiotic resumption, follicle rupture ~36 hours later and conversion of the follicle into a corpus luteum. Progesterone from the corpus luteum then restores negative feedback and slows the pulse generator. A bistable switch in a feedback loop is what makes the cycle oscillate rather than settle ([oscillations](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/03-systems-and-models/nonlinear-dynamics-oscillations-and-networks.md)); the cellular events are followed in [gametogenesis and the menstrual cycle](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/gametogenesis-and-the-menstrual-cycle.md).

   ```
   follicular phase: FSH -> follicle grows -> oestradiol rises -> (negative feedback on LH, FSH)
                     oestradiol > threshold for ~2 days -> feedback flips POSITIVE -> LH surge
   ovulation (~36 h after surge onset)
   luteal phase: corpus luteum -> progesterone (+ oestradiol) -> strong negative feedback
                 no pregnancy -> luteolysis at ~14 days -> steroids fall -> menses, FSH rises again
   ```

5. **Contraception exploits the negative arm.** Combined oral contraceptives supply enough oestrogen and progestogen to keep LH and FSH suppressed, so no follicle matures and no surge occurs; progestogen alone also thickens cervical mucus and thins the endometrium. The same logic makes steroid feedback the mechanism of the amenorrhoea of pregnancy (placental steroids and chorionic gonadotropin maintain the corpus luteum and suppress the axis) and of anabolic steroid use in men (testicular shrinkage from suppressed LH) ([fertility, contraception and menopause](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md)).

6. **The axis is active in the fetus and infant, silent in childhood, and reawakened at puberty.** Fetal gonadotropins and testosterone drive male genital differentiation; a 'mini-puberty' of a few months follows birth. The pulse generator is then restrained until, at ~8–13 years in girls and ~9–14 in boys, kisspeptin signalling increases, GnRH pulses first appear during sleep, and gonadal steroids produce the secondary sexual characteristics, the growth spurt (through growth hormone and then epiphyseal closure by oestrogen in both sexes) and fertility. Timing is set by genes, nutrition (leptin signals adequate fat stores) and health; the trigger for the restraint and its release is incompletely understood ([growth, puberty and aging](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/03-development/growth-puberty-and-aging.md)).

7. **Prolactin is the axis's main antagonist, and the one pituitary hormone under tonic inhibition.** Hypothalamic dopamine restrains lactotrophs continuously; anything that cuts that restraint (a pituitary stalk lesion, dopamine antagonists such as antipsychotics, a prolactin-secreting adenoma) raises prolactin, which suppresses GnRH pulses and causes amenorrhoea or hypogonadism. Suckling raises prolactin physiologically, which is why lactation delays fertility. Dopamine agonists treat prolactinomas by restoring the inhibition.

8. **Reproductive ageing is asymmetric between the sexes because the ovary has a fixed stock.** Oocyte number falls from millions in the fetus to ~1 million at birth, ~300,000 at puberty and near zero at ~51 years (median age of menopause in high-income countries), with ~400 ovulated and the rest lost to atresia. As follicles run out, inhibin and oestradiol fall, FSH rises, cycles become irregular and then stop; the hot flushes, bone loss and urogenital atrophy of menopause are oestrogen withdrawal. The testis has renewing stem cells, so men lose testosterone slowly (~1% per year after 40) and remain fertile, though with declining sperm quality. Menopause is the clearest example of a primary gonadal failure that is normal physiology.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| GnRH pulse generator | Hypothalamic kisspeptin–GnRH network whose pulse frequency selects LH versus FSH output |
| Two-cell model | LH-driven androgen synthesis in theca/Leydig cells, FSH-driven aromatisation or gamete support in granulosa/Sertoli cells |
| Inhibin | Gonadal peptide that selectively suppresses FSH; a readout of gamete-producing tissue |
| Positive feedback switch | Sustained high oestradiol converting inhibition of LH to the pre-ovulatory surge |
| Corpus luteum | Post-ovulatory follicle secreting progesterone for ~14 days unless rescued by chorionic gonadotropin |
| Hypogonadotropic vs hypergonadotropic hypogonadism | Low gonadal function from low drive (pituitary or hypothalamus) versus from a failed gonad with high LH and FSH |
| Prolactin | Lactotroph hormone under dopamine inhibition; suppresses the gonadal axis when high |
| Aromatase | Enzyme converting androgens to oestrogens; present in granulosa cells, fat, bone and brain |
| Menopause | Exhaustion of ovarian follicles; low oestradiol with high FSH; median ~51 years |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Oestrogen is the female hormone and testosterone the male one."** Both sexes make both; oestrogen is synthesised from androgen in every case, and oestrogen closes the growth plates and protects bone in men too.
- **"Ovulation is caused by rising oestrogen."** It is caused by the LH surge, which rising oestrogen triggers only after crossing a threshold for long enough to flip feedback; before that, the same oestrogen is suppressing LH.
- **"A GnRH agonist stimulates the axis."** Briefly, then it desensitises the receptor and suppresses the axis; this is the intended effect of the long-acting preparations.
