## What this area is about

The gastrointestinal system takes irregular, bulky, chemically diverse and microbially contaminated input and converts it into a steady, sterile supply of small molecules for the blood. Everything about it follows from that mismatch between what arrives and what the body can use: the tube must move, mix and hold its contents at the pace chemistry allows; secrete the enzymes, acid and detergents to break polymers into monomers; present a huge absorptive surface while keeping microbes and antigens out; and pass the absorbed load through a liver that smooths, stores, transforms and detoxifies it before the rest of the body sees it.

The unifying idea is **local feedback along a staged line, with the liver as the gatekeeper at the end.** Each stage senses its own contents and releases the secretion or motor programme that processes them, through an intrinsic nervous system and a family of gut hormones; no central controller tallies the meal. The brain adjusts gain. The liver, placed in series between gut and systemic circulation, turns the pulse of a meal into the constancy of plasma glucose, protein and lipid, and clears what the barrier lets through.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Integrative principles](../01-integrative-principles/README.md):** the gut is the entry point for every water, electrolyte and energy budget; its secretions and losses are the commonest cause of fluid and acid–base derangement (vomiting loses acid, diarrhoea loses bicarbonate and potassium); diet-induced thermogenesis is part of energy balance.
- **[Nervous system](../02-nervous-system/README.md):** the enteric nervous system is the third division of the autonomic system; vagal afferents carry satiety and nausea; visceral pain is poorly localised and referred along dermatomes.
- **[Cardiovascular system](../03-cardiovascular-system/README.md):** splanchnic blood flow (~25% of cardiac output at rest) is the body's largest redistributable reserve, diverted to muscle in exercise and sacrificed in shock, which opens the gut barrier.
- **[Respiratory system](../04-respiratory-system/README.md):** the two share the pharynx and the swallowing reflex that protects the airway; the liver's bicarbonate and ammonia handling interact with ventilatory acid–base compensation.
- **[Renal system](../05-renal-system/README.md):** the liver makes the urea the kidney excretes and the albumin that sets glomerular oncotic pressure; gut losses are what the kidney most often compensates; both organs activate vitamin D in sequence.
- **[Endocrine system](../07-endocrine-system/README.md):** gut hormones (gastrin, secretin, cholecystokinin, incretins) are the first endocrine signals of a meal; the liver is the main target of insulin and glucagon and the source of insulin-like growth factor 1.
- **[Blood](../08-blood/README.md):** iron, folate and vitamin B12 for red cell production enter through specific gut segments; the liver makes the clotting factors and clears bilirubin from haem; the spleen drains into the portal vein.
- **[Reproduction and the life course](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/README.md):** pregnancy slows gut motility and raises reflux; the neonatal gut is colonised at birth and primed by breast milk; the microbiome matures over the first three years.
- **[Musculoskeletal system and skin](../10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/README.md):** absorbed calcium and vitamin D supply bone; the gut and skin are the two great barrier epithelia and share the logic of layered defence and rapid turnover.
