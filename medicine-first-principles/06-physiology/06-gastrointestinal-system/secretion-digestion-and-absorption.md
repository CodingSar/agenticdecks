## Essence

Food is polymer: starch, protein, triglyceride. The gut cannot absorb polymers, so it secretes about 7 L of fluid a day carrying enzymes, acid, bicarbonate and bile to take them apart, then reabsorbs the monomers and nearly all of the fluid across an epithelium with the surface area of a small apartment. The whole process is a staged assembly line: the stomach denatures and begins, the pancreas and bile do most of the chemistry in the duodenum, the small-intestinal brush border finishes it and transports the products, and the colon salvages water and what bacteria can ferment. Each secretion is released by a feedback signal from the contents it is meant to act on, so the system matches enzyme to substrate and bicarbonate to acid without any central accounting.

## Core tenets

1. **Secretion is triggered by the substance it will process.** Gastric acid is released in three phases: cephalic (sight and taste, via the vagus), gastric (distension and peptides, via gastrin from antral G cells) and intestinal. Acid itself switches gastrin off through somatostatin from D cells once pH falls below ~3. Duodenal acid releases secretin, which makes the pancreas secrete bicarbonate; duodenal fat and protein release cholecystokinin (CCK), which makes it secrete enzymes and contracts the gallbladder. Every secretion thus closes a loop on its own substrate.

2. **Gastric acid is a proton pump working against a million-fold gradient.** The parietal cell H⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps hydrogen ions into the lumen to pH ~1–2 from a cytosol at pH 7.2, a gradient of some 10⁶. Histamine (from enterochromaffin-like cells, acting on H₂ receptors), gastrin and acetylcholine all converge on the pump, which is why blocking the pump itself is more complete than blocking one receptor. Acid denatures protein, activates pepsinogen to pepsin, liberates vitamin B₁₂ and iron from food and kills most ingested microbes; the stomach protects itself with a mucus–bicarbonate layer and rapid epithelial turnover, and the balance between acid and defence is the subject of [acid-peptic disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/acid-peptic-disease-and-gi-bleeding.md).

3. **The pancreas delivers the enzymes as inactive zymogens and the bicarbonate to make them work.** Acinar cells secrete trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, procarboxypeptidases, lipase, colipase, amylase and nucleases. Duodenal brush-border enteropeptidase cleaves trypsinogen to trypsin, which activates the rest: a cascade with a single trigger located outside the gland, protecting the pancreas from digesting itself. Duct cells secrete ~1–2 L/day of bicarbonate-rich fluid under secretin, raising duodenal pH to ~6–7 at which the enzymes work and bile salts stay in solution. Premature activation inside the gland is the mechanism of pancreatitis ([pancreatic and biliary disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/pancreatic-and-biliary-disease.md)).

4. **Fat needs emulsification and a carrier because it is insoluble.** Bile salts, amphipathic steroids made by the liver, coat fat droplets so lipase and colipase can reach the interface, then package the released fatty acids and monoglycerides into micelles small enough to diffuse through the unstirred water layer to the brush border. Inside the enterocyte, triglyceride is re-synthesised and packaged with apolipoproteins into chylomicrons that are too large for capillaries and enter lymph instead, reaching the blood via the thoracic duct. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) ride the same route, which is why bile obstruction or pancreatic failure cause their deficiency along with steatorrhoea. Bile salts are reabsorbed in the terminal ileum and recycled 6–10 times a day (the enterohepatic circulation); ileal disease breaks the cycle.

5. **Carbohydrate and protein are reduced to small units at the membrane and absorbed by sodium-coupled transport.** Amylase cuts starch to oligosaccharides; brush-border disaccharidases (lactase, sucrase, maltase) make monosaccharides. Glucose and galactose enter via the sodium–glucose cotransporter SGLT1, fructose via GLUT5; all exit basolaterally through GLUT2. Peptides are cut to amino acids and di- and tripeptides, taken up by several sodium- or proton-coupled carriers. Because sugar and amino acid uptake are coupled to sodium, their absorption drags water osmotically: this is why a glucose–salt solution rehydrates a patient with cholera even while the toxin is driving chloride secretion, the basis of oral rehydration therapy ([fluids and nutrition](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/fluids-nutrition-and-blood-products.md)). Lactase declines after weaning in most of the world's adults, an evolutionary legacy that makes lactose intolerance the norm rather than a disease.

6. **The epithelium is built for area and for turnover.** Folds, villi and microvilli multiply the small intestine's surface to ~30 m² (older estimates gave ~200). Crypt stem cells replace the entire epithelium every 3–5 days, so anything that stops division (chemotherapy, radiation) strips the mucosa within days. Absorptive capacity is also regional: iron, folate and calcium in the duodenum and jejunum; vitamin B₁₂ (bound to gastric intrinsic factor) and bile salts in the terminal ileum. Which segment is lost or diseased therefore predicts which deficiency follows ([malabsorption](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/inflammatory-bowel-disease-and-malabsorption.md)).

7. **The fluid budget is a large secretion matched by a larger absorption.** About 2 L is drunk and ~7 L secreted (saliva ~1.5, gastric ~2, bile ~0.5, pancreatic ~1.5, intestinal ~1); the small intestine absorbs ~7–8 L and the colon ~1.5 L of the remainder, leaving ~0.1–0.2 L in stool. Diarrhoea results from any imbalance: unabsorbed osmoles holding water in the lumen (osmotic), active chloride secretion driven by cyclic adenosine monophosphate or calcium (secretory, as with cholera toxin), loss of absorptive surface, or rapid transit. The colon's reserve capacity (~4–5 L/day) means small-bowel output must be large before stool volume rises.

8. **Drugs are absorbed by the same physics as nutrients.** A weak acid or base crosses the lipid membrane only in its un-ionised form, so gastric pH, the enormous surface of the small intestine and splanchnic blood flow decide how fast and how completely an oral drug reaches the portal vein, and the liver then takes its first-pass share ([absorption and bioavailability](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/absorption-and-bioavailability.md)). Food, gastric emptying rate and transporter competition explain most of the variability in oral dosing.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gastrin, secretin, cholecystokinin | Gut hormones released by peptides, acid and fat respectively; each recruits the secretion that processes its trigger |
| Parietal cell H⁺/K⁺-ATPase | The gastric proton pump; the final common pathway of acid secretion and the target of proton-pump inhibitors |
| Zymogen activation cascade | Pancreatic proteases secreted inactive and triggered by duodenal enteropeptidase through trypsin |
| Micelle | Bile-salt aggregate carrying lipid digestion products across the unstirred layer to the brush border |
| Chylomicron | Enterocyte lipoprotein that carries absorbed triglyceride into lymph, bypassing the portal vein |
| Enterohepatic circulation | Secretion of bile salts, ileal reabsorption and return to the liver, cycling the pool several times daily |
| Brush-border hydrolases | Membrane-bound disaccharidases and peptidases that finish digestion at the site of absorption |
| Sodium-coupled cotransport | Uptake of glucose and amino acids driven by the sodium gradient; drags water with it |
| Secretory vs osmotic diarrhoea | Active chloride secretion versus retained luminal osmoles; the former persists during fasting, the latter stops |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Most digestion happens in the stomach."** The stomach starts protein digestion and grinds; the duodenum and jejunum, with pancreatic enzymes and bile, do most of the chemistry and nearly all of the absorption.
- **"Diarrhoea is the gut failing to absorb water."** Water follows solute; diarrhoea is a solute problem (unabsorbed osmoles or secreted chloride), which is why glucose–salt solutions, which supply absorbable solute, treat it.
- **"Lactose intolerance is a disorder."** Adult lactase persistence is the derived trait, present in a minority of humans worldwide; its absence is the ancestral state.
