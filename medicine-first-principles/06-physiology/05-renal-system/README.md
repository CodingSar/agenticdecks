## What this area is about

The kidney answers one question continuously: given what the body has eaten, made and lost today, what must leave in the urine so that the volume and composition of the extracellular fluid stay constant? Claude Bernard's "constancy of the internal environment" is mostly the kidney's work. It does the job by filtering a large fraction of the plasma and then reclaiming, selectively, almost all of it, so that the quantity excreted of each substance is a small difference between two large numbers. That design gives enormous dynamic range (water excretion can vary forty-fold, sodium excretion a hundred-fold) at the price of a high and continuous energy cost and a medulla that lives on the edge of hypoxia.

The unifying idea is **filter, then reclaim under hormonal command**. Filtration is unselective and pressure-driven; reabsorption is selective, pump-driven and organised by segment; the final adjustment is made in the distal nephron by hormones that each close a feedback loop on one regulated variable: Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH) on osmolality, aldosterone on volume and potassium, Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) on calcium and phosphate, and the tubule's own acid secretion on pH.

```
 plasma --(pressure)--> glomerulus --> 180 L/day filtrate
                                            |
      proximal tubule: bulk, iso-osmotic reclaim (~65%), secretion of organic acids
                                            |
      loop of Henle: separates salt from water -> medullary gradient (countercurrent)
                                            |
      distal nephron: fine control  <-- ADH (water), aldosterone (Na+/K+), PTH (Ca2+/PO4), H+ secretion
                                            |
                                     1-2 L/day urine
```

Read filtration first: it defines GFR and clearance, the vocabulary the other three use. Tubular transport explains the machinery and the one trick (countercurrent multiplication) that gives the kidney its range. The third leaf is the control layer: it is where most renal reasoning at the bedside happens, because oedema, hypertension and hyponatraemia are all stories about these loops. The fourth leaf extends the same logic to the ions that govern excitability, bone and pH.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Integrative principles](../01-integrative-principles/README.md):** the kidney is the main effector for body fluid compartments and the slow arm of acid–base physiology; renal water loss is part of thermoregulation's water budget and renal blood flow is diverted away during exercise and stress.
- **[Nervous system](../02-nervous-system/README.md):** the sympathetic nervous system releases renin and constricts the afferent arteriole; the hypothalamus senses osmolality and makes ADH; plasma potassium and calcium set the excitability of every neurone.
- **[Cardiovascular system](../03-cardiovascular-system/README.md):** the kidney sets long-term blood pressure through pressure natriuresis and volume, while the heart sets the pressure that drives filtration; the glomerulus is a Starling capillary with the parameters turned up.
- **[Respiratory system](../04-respiratory-system/README.md):** lungs and kidneys share acid–base control; the lung is fast and handles carbon dioxide, the kidney is slow and handles fixed acid and bicarbonate. Angiotensin-converting enzyme sits mostly in pulmonary endothelium.
- **[Gastrointestinal system](../06-gastrointestinal-system/README.md):** intake of water, salt, potassium and acid precursors enters through the gut; the liver makes angiotensinogen and albumin (the oncotic term in filtration); gut losses are the commonest cause of volume depletion the kidney must answer.
- **[Endocrine system](../07-endocrine-system/README.md):** ADH, aldosterone, PTH and natriuretic peptides are the kidney's command signals; the kidney is itself an endocrine organ, making erythropoietin, renin and active vitamin D.
- **[Blood](../08-blood/README.md):** the kidney senses oxygen delivery and makes erythropoietin, so chronic kidney disease causes anaemia; plasma proteins made by the liver and retained by the glomerular sieve set the oncotic pressure of both.
- **[Reproduction and the life course](../09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/README.md):** pregnancy raises GFR by ~50% and resets osmotic and volume set points; the fetal kidney makes amniotic fluid; the neonatal kidney cannot yet concentrate urine fully.
- **[Musculoskeletal system and skin](../10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/README.md):** bone is the calcium and phosphate reservoir the kidney balances against; muscle is the potassium reservoir and the source of creatinine; skin is the other route of water and salt loss.

## Cross-cutting threads

- [Gradients and flow](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#gradients-and-flow): filtration down a pressure gradient; reabsorption down the sodium gradient a pump has paid for; the countercurrent multiplier stacking small gradients into a large one.
- [Feedback and homeostasis](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#feedback-and-homeostasis): osmolality, volume, potassium, calcium and pH each have a sensor, a hormone and a tubular effector.
- [Supply and demand](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#supply-and-demand): the medulla's oxygen supply is sacrificed to preserve the concentration gradient; renal oxygen use tracks sodium reabsorbed.
- [Compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation): the reciprocal creatinine curve hides half the nephrons' loss; remaining nephrons hyperfilter until they too fail.
- [Redundancy and reserve](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#redundancy-and-reserve): two kidneys and a million nephrons each; one kidney suffices for a normal life.
- [Scale and surface-to-volume](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#scale-and-surface-to-volume): a filtering area of ~1 m² of glomerular capillary and ~100 km of tubule packed into two fist-sized organs.
