## Essence

Pregnancy is a 40-week experiment in which one organism builds another inside itself, half of it genetically foreign, and supplies all its oxygen, fuel and waste disposal across a single organ, the placenta, that the fetus grows for the purpose. The placenta is simultaneously an exchange membrane, an endocrine gland that takes over the ovary's job and reprogrammes maternal metabolism, and an immunological barrier. The mother's physiology is remodelled around it: blood volume up by half, cardiac output up by 40%, ventilation up, glomerular filtration up, insulin resistance deliberately induced in the second half so that glucose is spared for the fetus. Almost every complication of pregnancy is one of these adaptations failing or overshooting, and almost every drug question in pregnancy is a question about what crosses the placenta.

## Core tenets

1. **Implantation is an invasion, and the depth of invasion sets the risk of the whole pregnancy.** About six days after fertilisation the blastocyst's outer trophoblast burrows into the secretory endometrium. Extravillous trophoblast cells then invade the maternal spiral arteries over the first 20 weeks, destroying their smooth muscle and converting them into wide, low-resistance, non-reactive channels that flood the intervillous space with maternal blood at ~600–750 mL/min at term. Shallow invasion leaves high-resistance vessels, an underperfused placenta, and the hypertensive, proteinuric syndrome of pre-eclampsia and fetal growth restriction later in pregnancy; the disease is set up months before it shows ([pregnancy and its complications](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md)).

2. **The placenta is a counter-flow exchanger with the mother's blood on the outside and the fetus's in capillaries inside villi.** Maternal blood bathes chorionic villi; fetal blood runs in capillaries within them; a trophoblast layer and fetal endothelium separate the two, thinning to a few micrometres by term across ~12 m² of villous surface. No blood mixes. Oxygen, carbon dioxide, water and small lipid-soluble molecules diffuse; glucose crosses by facilitated transport (fetal glucose tracks maternal glucose, which is why maternal hyperglycaemia produces a large, hyperinsulinaemic fetus); amino acids are actively pumped; immunoglobulin G is carried by receptor-mediated transport from ~20 weeks, giving the newborn passive immunity for months. Most drugs under ~500 Da that are lipid-soluble and unionised cross, which is the first question of prescribing in pregnancy ([distribution](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/distribution-and-protein-binding.md)).

3. **Fetal oxygen uptake works at low tension because of three stacked advantages.** Umbilical venous oxygen tension is only ~30–35 mmHg, saturating adult haemoglobin to ~60%, yet fetal blood is ~80% saturated because fetal haemoglobin (two alpha, two gamma chains) binds 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate poorly and so has a left-shifted dissociation curve; fetal haemoglobin concentration is higher (~170 g/L); and the Bohr effect runs in both directions at the placenta (maternal blood takes up fetal carbon dioxide and acid and releases oxygen more readily, fetal blood gives up acid and binds oxygen more readily). The fetus lives at the oxygen tension of the summit of Everest and does so comfortably ([gas exchange and transport](../04-respiratory-system/gas-exchange-and-transport.md)).

4. **The placenta takes over endocrine control, first rescuing the corpus luteum and then replacing it.** Human chorionic gonadotropin from the trophoblast, detectable in maternal blood within days of implantation (the basis of pregnancy tests), binds the Luteinising Hormone (LH) receptor and keeps the corpus luteum making progesterone for the first ~8–10 weeks. The placenta then makes its own progesterone (from maternal cholesterol) and oestrogens (from fetal adrenal androgen precursors, a feto-placental unit), at rates rising to ~250 mg/day of progesterone by term. Progesterone keeps the myometrium quiescent, suppresses the maternal hypothalamic–pituitary axis (no cycles), relaxes smooth muscle everywhere (reflux, constipation, ureteric dilatation) and drives the ~40% rise in ventilation that lowers maternal carbon dioxide and creates a gradient for fetal carbon dioxide to leave.

5. **Maternal metabolism is reprogrammed to feed the fetus, and this is why gestational diabetes exists.** Placental lactogen, growth hormone variant, progesterone and cortisol make the mother progressively insulin-resistant from mid-pregnancy, so that after meals her glucose stays higher and longer for the placenta to take, while fasting she turns faster to fat and ketones, sparing glucose. The beta cell must roughly double its output to keep up; a woman whose beta cells cannot develops gestational diabetes, which resolves at delivery with the placenta and predicts later type 2 diabetes ([glucose homeostasis](../07-endocrine-system/glucose-homeostasis.md)). Fat is laid down early for the energy demands of late pregnancy and lactation.

6. **The maternal circulation is remodelled into a high-flow, low-resistance state.** Plasma volume rises ~45% and red cell mass ~20%, so haemoglobin falls (physiological dilution) while oxygen delivery rises; cardiac output rises ~40% by mid-pregnancy through both stroke volume and heart rate; systemic resistance falls (progesterone, nitric oxide, the placental shunt) so blood pressure falls in the second trimester and returns by term. Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) rises ~50% (creatinine and urea fall), the osmotic set point for thirst and Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH) drops by ~10 mOsm/kg, and clotting factors rise while fibrinolysis falls, a hypercoagulable state that limits haemorrhage at delivery and raises thrombosis risk five-fold. A heart with no reserve, a kidney that cannot increase filtration, or a vascular bed that stays constricted (pre-eclampsia) each decompensates here ([haemodynamics](../03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md)).

7. **Parturition is progesterone's restraint giving way to a positive-feedback loop.** Over the final weeks, rising oestrogen-to-progesterone action, fetal cortisol, prostaglandins and increasing oxytocin receptor density in the myometrium turn a quiescent muscle into one that contracts in synchrony through gap junctions. Once labour begins, each contraction pushes the fetal head on the cervix, cervical stretch triggers oxytocin release from the maternal posterior pituitary (the Ferguson reflex), and oxytocin strengthens contractions: a positive-feedback loop that runs until the stimulus, the fetus, is delivered. Oxytocin and prostaglandins are used to induce labour, and the same contraction compresses the spiral arteries after the placenta separates, the main defence against post-partum haemorrhage ([labour and delivery](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/labour-delivery-and-the-puerperium.md)).

8. **Lactation is prepared by pregnancy, unlocked by delivery and sustained by demand.** Oestrogen, progesterone, prolactin and placental lactogen grow the ductal and alveolar tissue during pregnancy, but the high steroids block milk secretion. Their withdrawal at delivery releases prolactin's action and copious milk begins within 2–4 days; before that, colostrum delivers concentrated secretory Immunoglobulin A (IgA) and other defences. Thereafter suckling drives both prolactin (synthesis, acting over hours) and oxytocin (ejection by myoepithelial contraction, within a minute, and conditioned to the infant's cry); milk removed is milk made, so supply follows demand. Suckling also suppresses Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH) pulses, delaying ovulation, an imperfect but population-significant contraceptive. Human milk supplies ~70 kcal/100 mL, with lactose, fat, whey proteins, oligosaccharides that feed the infant's bifidobacteria, and little iron or vitamin K.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trophoblast | Outer cell layer of the blastocyst that invades the endometrium and forms the placenta |
| Spiral artery remodelling | Trophoblast conversion of maternal arteries into low-resistance channels; deficient in pre-eclampsia |
| Intervillous space | The maternal blood lake surrounding fetal villi; the exchange interface |
| Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) | Trophoblast hormone that maintains the corpus luteum; the pregnancy test analyte |
| Feto-placental unit | Shared steroidogenesis: placental progesterone from maternal cholesterol, oestrogens from fetal adrenal precursors |
| Placental lactogen | Placental hormone driving maternal insulin resistance and lipolysis |
| Physiological anaemia of pregnancy | Fall in haemoglobin as plasma volume rises more than red cell mass |
| Ferguson reflex | Cervical stretch releasing oxytocin, which strengthens contractions: labour's positive-feedback loop |
| Prolactin and oxytocin in lactation | Milk synthesis over hours versus milk ejection within a minute; both driven by suckling |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Maternal and fetal blood mix in the placenta."** They are separated by trophoblast and endothelium throughout; the small leaks that do occur (at delivery) are what sensitise a Rhesus-negative mother.
- **"The placenta is a barrier that protects the fetus from drugs."** It is an exchange membrane; most small lipid-soluble drugs cross freely, and the few that do not are the exceptions.
- **"Low haemoglobin in pregnancy means iron deficiency."** A modest fall is dilution from plasma expansion; iron deficiency is common too, but the two must be distinguished by iron studies.
