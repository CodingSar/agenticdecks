## Essence

Sexual reproduction needs haploid gametes, made by meiosis, and a way to bring them together at the right time in a prepared place. The two sexes solve this differently. The testis makes sperm continuously, hundreds of millions a day, from stem cells, and ships them with a pump and a nutrient fluid. The ovary holds a fixed, declining stock of oocytes arrested in the first meiotic division since fetal life, and releases one roughly every 28 days by an endocrine cycle that also rebuilds the uterine lining each time, sheds it if no embryo implants, and resets. The menstrual cycle is therefore two coupled cycles, ovarian and uterine, driven by the hormonal switch described in [reproductive endocrinology](../07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md), and most disorders of fertility and menstruation are one of the coupled parts failing.

## Core tenets

1. **Meiosis halves the chromosome number and shuffles the genome, and errors in it are the commonest cause of pregnancy loss.** One round of replication followed by two divisions yields four haploid cells; crossing-over in prophase I and independent assortment make each gamete unique. Non-disjunction, a pair failing to separate, gives aneuploid gametes; most such embryos die early, and trisomy 21 is among the few compatible with life. The error rate rises steeply with maternal age because the oocyte has been held in prophase I for decades, with cohesion between chromatids slowly decaying ([meiosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/mitosis-meiosis-and-chromosome-segregation.md), [cytogenetics](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/chromosomes-and-cytogenetics.md)).

2. **Spermatogenesis is continuous, stem-cell-based and takes about 74 days.** Spermatogonia at the base of the seminiferous tubule divide mitotically, and daughters pass through meiosis and then spermiogenesis, in which Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) is packed with protamines, a flagellum grows and cytoplasm is shed, as they move toward the lumen, nursed by Sertoli cells behind a blood–testis barrier that shields haploid cells from immune attack. Testosterone from Leydig cells, at concentrations in the tubule ~50 times those in blood, and Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) on Sertoli cells sustain the process; the testes hang outside the body because it needs ~2–3 °C below core temperature. Sperm mature and gain motility in the epididymis over ~2 weeks, and at ejaculation are mixed with fructose-rich seminal vesicle fluid and prostatic secretions. Because production is continuous, male fertility declines slowly and any insult (heat, drugs, radiation) shows up in the semen about three months later.

3. **Oogenesis is front-loaded, arrested twice, and finite.** Oogonia multiply only in fetal life, reaching ~6–7 million by mid-gestation, then enter meiosis I and arrest in prophase as primary oocytes within primordial follicles; ~1–2 million remain at birth and ~300,000–400,000 at puberty. Each cycle, a cohort is recruited; one completes meiosis I at the Luteinising Hormone (LH) surge, producing a secondary oocyte and a discarded polar body, then arrests again in metaphase II and finishes only if a sperm enters. Cytoplasm is kept in one cell (the egg must supply the embryo for its first divisions), which is why the divisions are asymmetric. Only ~400 oocytes ovulate in a lifetime; the rest die by atresia, and the stock runs out at menopause.

4. **The ovarian cycle is follicle growth, ovulation, corpus luteum, and luteolysis.** Follicular phase (variable, ~14 days): FSH rescues a cohort of antral follicles; the one with most FSH receptors becomes dominant, makes rising oestradiol and inhibin, which lower FSH and starve the rest. Ovulation: the LH surge triggered by sustained oestradiol resumes meiosis, loosens the cumulus, and proteases open the follicle wall ~36 hours later. Luteal phase (fixed, ~14 days): the collapsed follicle becomes the corpus luteum, secreting progesterone and oestradiol; without chorionic gonadotropin from an implanting embryo it regresses after ~14 days. Cycle length varies almost entirely in the follicular phase; ovulation can be dated by counting back 14 days from the next period.

   ```
   day 1        ~day 7         ~day 14          ~day 21          day 28
   menses       dominant       LH surge ->      peak            luteolysis ->
   FSH rises    follicle       ovulation        progesterone     steroids fall
   |--- follicular / proliferative (variable) ---|--- luteal / secretory (~14 d) ---|
   endometrium: shed -> rebuilt by oestradiol -> made secretory by progesterone -> shed
   ```

5. **The uterine cycle is the endometrium following the ovary's steroids.** Oestradiol in the follicular phase makes the endometrium proliferate (glands lengthen, stroma thickens to several millimetres, spiral arteries grow). Progesterone in the luteal phase stops proliferation and makes the glands secrete glycogen-rich fluid: a surface ready for an embryo in the implantation window ~6–10 days after ovulation. When progesterone is withdrawn at luteolysis, spiral arteries constrict, the upper layer becomes ischaemic and is shed as menstruation (~30–80 mL over 3–7 days), with fibrinolytic enzymes keeping menstrual blood liquid. Menstruation is thus progesterone withdrawal bleeding; the same bleed can be produced pharmacologically by stopping a progestogen ([gynaecological disease](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/gynaecological-disease.md)).

6. **The cervix and tubes gate and transport, and they too follow the steroids.** Oestradiol makes cervical mucus thin, watery and penetrable at mid-cycle; progesterone makes it thick and hostile, the basis of progestogen-only contraception. Sperm reach the tubes within minutes to hours, capacitate (membrane changes that enable the acrosome reaction) in the female tract, and can survive ~3–5 days; the oocyte is fertilisable for ~12–24 hours. The fertile window is therefore the few days before ovulation plus the day itself. Ciliary beat and peristalsis move the fertilised egg to the uterus over ~3–4 days; damaged tubes (infection, endometriosis) are a leading cause of infertility and of ectopic implantation.

7. **Anovulation and its consequences follow from the loop.** If no follicle becomes dominant (as in polycystic ovary syndrome, extreme weight change, prolactin excess or at the extremes of reproductive age), there is no LH surge, no corpus luteum and no progesterone; oestradiol acts unopposed on the endometrium, which proliferates until it outgrows its blood supply and bleeds irregularly and heavily. Years of unopposed oestrogen raise the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and cancer, which is why oestrogen replacement is given with a progestogen to women with a uterus ([fertility, contraception and menopause](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md)).

8. **Fertility is probabilistic and declines with age on the female side.** A healthy couple has roughly a 20–30% chance of conception per cycle in the woman's twenties, ~85% within a year; by 40 the per-cycle chance is well under 10%, because the remaining oocytes are fewer and more often aneuploid, not because the uterus has aged (donor-egg pregnancies succeed at older ages). Semen parameters, tubal patency, ovulation and the endometrium are the four things that must each work, and infertility assessment tests each in turn.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Non-disjunction | Failure of chromosome pairs or chromatids to separate in meiosis, yielding aneuploid gametes |
| Sertoli and Leydig cells | Nurse cells of the seminiferous tubule (FSH target, blood–testis barrier) and testosterone-producing interstitial cells (LH target) |
| Primordial follicle | Oocyte arrested in prophase I with a single layer of granulosa cells; the finite ovarian reserve |
| Dominant follicle | The one follicle per cycle that survives falling FSH and ovulates |
| Corpus luteum | Progesterone-secreting body formed from the ruptured follicle; lifespan ~14 days unless rescued |
| Proliferative and secretory endometrium | Oestradiol-driven growth, then progesterone-driven glandular secretion ready for implantation |
| Progesterone withdrawal bleeding | Menstruation; also any bleed produced by stopping progestogen |
| Capacitation | Changes in sperm within the female tract that enable fertilisation |
| Fertile window | Roughly five days before to the day of ovulation, set by sperm and oocyte survival times |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Ovulation happens on day 14."** It happens ~14 days before the *next* period; in a 35-day cycle that is day 21. The luteal phase is the fixed part.
- **"A woman is fertile for a day a month."** Sperm survive up to five days in the tract, so the window is about six days ending on ovulation day.
- **"Sperm are made at puberty and stored."** They are made continuously from stem cells throughout life; the ovary's stock, not the testis's, is the finite one.
