## Essence

A multicellular body works only if cells agree on what to do, and agreement needs communication. Cells talk over four ranges: by direct contact (a ligand on one membrane binding a receptor on the next, or a channel joining two cytoplasms), over micrometres by secreted paracrine factors, over metres by hormones in the blood, and over the same metres at millisecond speed by nerves releasing transmitter at a synapse. Junctions are the structures that organise the contact end of this: tight junctions seal an epithelium into a barrier, anchoring junctions bind cells into a sheet that bears force, and gap junctions wire cells into an electrical and metabolic unit. The one idea is **range and speed set the mechanism**: diffusion is fast over micrometres and hopeless over centimetres, so the body uses local signals where it can and a circulatory system or a nerve where it must. Layer 02 described the receptor; this leaf describes the conversation.

## Core tenets

1. **Signal range follows from diffusion and destruction.** A molecule released into tissue spreads a distance of roughly the square root of (diffusion coefficient times its lifetime) before it is degraded or bound ([diffusion](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/diffusion-osmosis-and-transport.md)). A short-lived messenger such as nitric oxide (half-life of seconds) acts within tens of micrometres; a cytokine bound by matrix acts on neighbours; a stable hormone must be carried by blood to go further. Paracrine signals are local because the tissue destroys them, which is why a growth factor injected into blood does something very different from the same factor secreted in a wound.

2. **Contact-dependent signalling gives exact spatial control.** Notch on one cell is cleaved and activated by Delta on an adjacent cell, and only that cell; the released intracellular domain enters the nucleus and changes fate. Because a cell that commits to one fate raises its Delta and suppresses its neighbours' Notch, a uniform field of equivalent cells resolves into a precise pattern of alternating fates (lateral inhibition), which is how neurons are spaced among skin cells and how intestinal stem cells alternate with secretory cells. Immune synapses between a T cell and an antigen-presenting cell are the same principle: a decision that must be made about this cell and no other ([receptors](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/receptors-and-ligands.md)).

3. **Tight junctions make an epithelium a barrier with a controllable leak.** Strands of claudins and occludin encircle each cell at its apical border and bind the same proteins on the neighbour, sealing the paracellular path. Different claudins form pores of different charge and size, so the "tightness" of a tissue is tuned: the proximal tubule is leaky, the distal nephron and urinary bladder are tight, the blood–brain barrier is tightest. Tight junctions also fence apical from basolateral membrane proteins, maintaining the polarity on which directional transport depends ([membrane transport](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/membrane-transport-and-ion-gradients.md)). Loss of tight junction integrity (inflammatory cytokines, bacterial toxins such as *Clostridioides difficile* toxins, ischaemia) lets luminal contents into tissue: this is the "leaky gut" of inflammatory bowel disease and the brain oedema of stroke ([gut barrier](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/the-gut-barrier-and-microbiome.md)).

4. **Anchoring junctions distribute force across a sheet.** Adherens junctions (cadherin to actin) and desmosomes (desmosomal cadherins to intermediate filaments) link every cell's cytoskeleton to its neighbours', so a stretch on the epidermis or a beat of the heart is carried by the whole tissue rather than tearing one cell from the next ([adhesion](cell-adhesion-and-the-extracellular-matrix.md)). Desmosomal mutations cause arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, in which cardiomyocytes detach, die and are replaced by fat and fibrosis under the strain of exercise; the disease is a junction failing under load.

5. **Gap junctions couple cells electrically and metabolically.** Six connexins form a hemichannel; two hemichannels dock to make a pore ~1.5 nm across that passes ions, second messengers and metabolites below ~1 kDa directly between cytoplasms. Cardiac muscle is a functional syncytium because of them: the action potential spreads cell to cell without synapses, which gives the heart its coordinated beat and makes conduction velocity depend on how well coupled the cells are ([cardiac electrophysiology](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/cardiac-electrophysiology.md)). Remodelled or closed gap junctions in ischaemic or fibrotic myocardium slow conduction and create the substrate for re-entrant arrhythmias. Connexin 26 mutations are the commonest cause of inherited deafness, because potassium recycling in the cochlea runs through gap junctions.

6. **Endocrine signalling is slow, broadcast and dose-dependent; neural signalling is fast, private and digital.** A hormone reaches every cell with the receptor at a concentration set by secretion against clearance, so its effect is graded and its time course is minutes to days. A neuron releases transmitter into a 20 nm cleft onto one target in under a millisecond and clears it at once; the signal is all-or-none and addressed by wiring. The two systems meet in neuroendocrine cells (hypothalamus, adrenal medulla) and in the autonomic control of glands. The body uses each where its properties fit: a hormone to set blood glucose over hours, a nerve to withdraw a hand from a flame ([hormones](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/hormone-classes-axes-and-feedback.md)).

7. **The same ligand means different things to different cells.** Acetylcholine slows the heart (muscarinic receptor, potassium channel opening) and contracts skeletal muscle (nicotinic receptor, sodium channel opening); adrenaline constricts skin arterioles and dilates those of muscle. The meaning of a signal is set by the receptor and the intracellular wiring of the recipient, not by the molecule. This is why one drug can have opposite effects in two tissues and why receptor subtype selectivity is the central design problem of pharmacology.

8. **Communication fails in characteristic ways, and each is a disease class.** A signal that is sent without a stimulus (a hormone-secreting tumour), a receptor that is constitutively on (activating mutations in growth factor receptors in cancer), a receptor that is missing or blocked (antibodies against the acetylcholine receptor in myasthenia gravis, insulin receptor defects), a junction that leaks or seals (the barrier diseases above), or a cell that stops listening to its neighbours (contact inhibition lost in cancer). The vocabulary of this leaf is the vocabulary of much of [pathology](../../08-pathology/README.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Autocrine / paracrine / endocrine / synaptic | Signalling to self, to neighbours, by blood, or across a synapse |
| Contact-dependent signalling | Ligand and receptor both membrane-bound; Notch–Delta, immune synapse |
| Lateral inhibition | A committed cell suppresses the same fate in its neighbours, creating pattern |
| Tight junction | Claudin-based seal at the apical border; sets paracellular permeability and polarity |
| Adherens junction / desmosome | Cadherin-based anchors to actin / to intermediate filaments |
| Gap junction | Connexin channel joining two cytoplasms; electrical and metabolic coupling |
| Functional syncytium | Tissue behaving as one cell because of gap junctions (heart, smooth muscle) |
| Paracellular / transcellular | Passage between cells through the junction, or through the cell |
| Signal range | Distance a messenger acts, set by diffusion against degradation |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Hormones are powerful because they travel far."** Travelling far is a limitation that forces slowness and broadcast; local signals are more precise and faster.
- **"Junctions are passive structures."** Tight junction permeability is regulated within minutes by cytokines and signalling; gap junctions close in response to calcium, acid and phosphorylation.
- **"A signalling molecule has a fixed effect."** The effect belongs to the receiving cell; the same molecule can excite one tissue and inhibit another.
