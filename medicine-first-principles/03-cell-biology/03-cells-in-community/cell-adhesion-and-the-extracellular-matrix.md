## Essence

Cells become a tissue by sticking to each other and to a scaffold they secrete. The Extracellular Matrix (ECM) is that scaffold: collagen for tensile strength, elastin for recoil, proteoglycans that hold water and resist compression, and adhesive glycoproteins that cells grip. Adhesion molecules (cadherins between cells, integrins to matrix) link the scaffold through the membrane to the cytoskeleton, so forces are transmitted across a whole organ and every cell can feel where it is and what is pulling on it. The one idea is **mechanical continuity with information in it**: adhesion is a signalling receptor, not glue, and the matrix is a reservoir of growth factors and a record of the tissue's history, not filler.

## Core tenets

1. **Collagen gives tensile strength because of its structure, and one substitution can destroy it.** Collagen is a triple helix with glycine at every third position, because only glycine fits at the helix centre. Chains are hydroxylated (needing vitamin C), secreted as procollagen, trimmed and cross-linked by lysyl oxidase (needing copper) into fibrils that bear load like rope ([mechanics](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/mechanics-and-materials.md)). A glycine substitution in type I collagen gives osteogenesis imperfecta by a dominant-negative effect: one bad chain ruins the whole helix. Scurvy is collagen that cannot be hydroxylated.

2. **Elastin and proteoglycans supply the other two mechanical properties.** Elastin is a cross-linked network that stretches and recoils by entropy, giving arteries, lung and skin their elasticity; adults make little new elastin, so its loss in emphysema (neutrophil elastase) and ageing is permanent. Proteoglycans carry long sulfated glycosaminoglycan chains ([glycobiology](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/01-macromolecules/carbohydrates-and-glycobiology.md)) whose fixed negative charges bind water and swell the matrix against compression: cartilage is ~70% water held by aggrecan. Heparan sulfate also binds growth factors, making the matrix a slow-release store.

3. **Integrins connect matrix to cytoskeleton and signal in both directions.** An integrin binds fibronectin, laminin or collagen outside and actin inside. Binding activates kinases and small GTPases ("outside-in"), so an attached cell survives and can proliferate; signals from inside change the integrin's affinity ("inside-out"), which is how a platelet or leukocyte switches from rolling to firm adhesion in seconds. Leukocyte adhesion deficiency, a beta-2 integrin defect, leaves neutrophils unable to leave the blood, so infections lack pus.

4. **Cadherins hold cells to each other and define tissue identity.** Cadherins bind the same cadherin on a neighbour, calcium-dependently, and anchor to actin at adherens junctions and to intermediate filaments at desmosomes. E-cadherin loss is the signature of epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition and inherited E-cadherin mutations cause diffuse gastric cancer; autoantibodies against desmosomal cadherins cause pemphigus, in which the epidermis splits into blisters.

5. **The basement membrane organises epithelia and filters.** Every epithelium, muscle fibre and nerve rests on a sheet of type IV collagen, laminin and perlecan. It polarises the cell, filters the glomerulus (charge and pore size exclude albumin), and is the boundary a carcinoma must breach to become invasive, which is the formal definition of malignancy in epithelial cancer ([invasion and metastasis](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/invasion-metastasis-and-the-tumour-microenvironment.md)). Alport syndrome (type IV collagen) causes kidney failure and deafness; anti-basement-membrane antibodies cause Goodpasture disease.

6. **The matrix is remodelled continuously, and the balance is a disease axis.** Matrix Metalloproteinases (MMPs) from fibroblasts, macrophages and tumour cells degrade it; tissue inhibitors restrain them. Too much is emphysema, aneurysm and tumour invasion; too much synthesis is fibrosis, driven by transforming growth factor beta acting on myofibroblasts, which in liver, lung, kidney and heart replaces working tissue with collagen that cannot be removed ([tissue repair and fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)).

7. **Cells read the stiffness of their matrix.** Through integrins and actin ([cytoskeleton](../01-cell-structure/the-cytoskeleton-and-cell-motility.md)) a cell pulls on its substrate, senses the resistance, and relays it to the nucleus via mechanosensitive transcription factors (YAP/TAZ). Stiff matrix promotes proliferation and fibroblast activation, so fibrosis is self-perpetuating (stiff scar activates more fibroblasts) and stiff tumour stroma favours malignant growth. Mechanical environment is a signal of the same rank as a growth factor.

8. **Connective tissue disorders show the matrix as a system.** Marfan syndrome (fibrillin-1, scaffold for elastin and regulator of transforming growth factor beta) affects aorta, lens and skeleton together; Ehlers–Danlos syndromes (various collagens) give hyperextensible skin, loose joints and fragile vessels.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Extracellular matrix (ECM) | Secreted network of fibres, proteoglycans and adhesive glycoproteins that cells live in |
| Collagen | Triple-helical fibrous protein giving tensile strength; type I in bone and tendon, IV in basement membranes |
| Elastin | Cross-linked network giving recoil; not replaced in adult life |
| Proteoglycan | Protein with sulfated sugar chains; holds water and resists compression |
| Integrin | Cell–matrix receptor linking matrix to actin and signalling both ways |
| Cadherin | Calcium-dependent cell–cell adhesion molecule binding its own type |
| Basement membrane | Thin matrix sheet under epithelia; filter and boundary |
| Matrix metalloproteinase | Zinc protease that remodels matrix |

## Common misconceptions

- **"The matrix is inert packing."** It stores growth factors, sets a stiffness cells read, and turns over continuously.
- **"Fibrosis is just too much healing."** It is a self-sustaining state in which stiff matrix and activated myofibroblasts reinforce each other; it does not resolve when the injury stops.
- **"Adhesion molecules only hold cells in place."** Integrins and cadherins are receptors whose engagement decides survival, proliferation and gene expression.
