## What this area is about

A human is ~3 × 10^13 cells that must act as one organism. This area asks what a cell needs in order to live among others: something to hold on to, a way to talk to its neighbours and to distant tissues, and a way to cope when its environment turns hostile. Adhesion and the extracellular matrix give the body mechanical continuity; junctions and signalling give it coordination; the stress response gives each cell a repertoire for surviving injury and a way to tell the rest of the body that it is in trouble. The unifying idea is **the cell as a social unit**: its survival, division and identity are decided by signals from matrix and neighbours, so that a cell detached, silenced or ignored dies or turns into a tumour. This area is the bridge from the single cell to tissues (layer 05) and to the two great multicellular responses, inflammation and repair (layers 07 and 08).

       |  integrins                              neighbours

       v                                            |  gap: couple

Read adhesion first: it establishes that attachment is a signal and that the matrix is a mechanical and chemical environment. Communication builds on it, since junctions are made of adhesion molecules and the range of a signal is set by the tissue it diffuses through. The stress response completes the picture: it is what a cell does with the signals when they report damage, and it is the layer at which cell biology becomes pathology.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cell structure](../01-cell-structure/README.md):** integrins and cadherins are anchored to the cytoskeleton and transmit tissue-scale force to the nucleus; apical and basolateral trafficking depend on tight junctions; the heat shock and unfolded protein responses are the regulatory layer over protein quality control.
- **[The cell life cycle](../02-the-cell-life-cycle/README.md):** loss of adhesion triggers apoptosis; the stem cell niche is a set of junctions and paracrine signals; the DNA damage response of the stress leaf is what drives a checkpoint, and its output is proliferation, senescence or death.

- [Dose–response](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#dose-response): a few breaks are repaired, many trigger death; low oxidant levels signal, high levels injure.
