## Essence

Every cell lives in conditions that vary: oxygen falls, temperature rises, nutrients run out, toxins and radiation damage molecules, pathogens invade. The cellular stress response is the set of conserved programmes that detect each kind of insult and shift the cell from growth to defence: heat shock proteins against unfolded protein, the unfolded protein response in the endoplasmic reticulum, the antioxidant response against oxidants, the hypoxia response against low oxygen, the DNA damage response against broken genomes, and the integrated stress response that throttles translation. The single idea is **sense the damage, not the cause**: each programme is triggered by the molecular lesion itself (unfolded protein, oxidised thiols, stalled hydroxylation, a broken strand), which makes it general. The response decides between adaptation, reversible injury, permanent arrest and death, and that decision is what a pathologist reads in an injured tissue.

## Core tenets

1. **The sensor is the lesion.** Heat shock factor 1 is held inactive by Heat Shock Protein 70 (Hsp70); when unfolded proteins accumulate they bind Hsp70, freeing the factor to transcribe more chaperones ([protein quality control](../01-cell-structure/protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md)). Keap1 holds the transcription factor Nrf2 for degradation until oxidants modify Keap1's cysteines, releasing Nrf2 to switch on antioxidant and detoxifying enzymes ([redox](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/redox-and-electron-transfer.md)). Hypoxia-Inducible Factor (HIF) is hydroxylated by oxygen-dependent enzymes and destroyed; without oxygen the hydroxylation stops and HIF accumulates. In each case the damaging agent inactivates an inhibitor, so the response is proportional to the damage and self-limiting: a negative feedback loop with the lesion as the measured variable.

2. **Hypoxia reprograms metabolism and recruits blood supply.** Stabilised HIF transcribes glycolytic enzymes and glucose transporters (ATP without oxygen), erythropoietin (more red cells), vascular endothelial growth factor (new vessels) and nitric oxide synthase (vasodilation). This is how the kidney senses anaemia and how altitude raises haematocrit over weeks. Tumours use the same programme: hypoxic regions of a tumour drive angiogenesis and the glycolytic phenotype, and HIF is constitutively on in clear cell renal carcinoma because its destroyer (the von Hippel–Lindau protein) is lost ([hallmarks of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/hallmarks-of-cancer.md)). HIF prolyl hydroxylase inhibitors are now drugs for anaemia of kidney disease.

3. **Oxidative stress is an imbalance, and the cell's defence is enzymatic and renewable.** Mitochondria leak ~0.1–1% of electrons to form superoxide; phagocytes make it deliberately; radiation and redox-cycling drugs make more. Superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase and the glutathione and thioredoxin systems (regenerated by NADPH from the pentose phosphate pathway) keep the cytosol reducing. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, the commonest human enzyme defect (~400 million people, 2020s, mainly malaria-endemic ancestry), means red cells cannot regenerate glutathione and haemolyse under oxidant drugs or infection. Oxidised lipids, proteins and DNA are the common pathway of ischaemia-reperfusion injury, radiation damage and some of ageing.

4. **The integrated stress response throttles protein synthesis to buy time.** Four kinases, each sensing a different stress (unfolded protein in the endoplasmic reticulum, amino acid starvation, double-stranded viral RNA, haem deficiency), converge on phosphorylating one translation initiation factor (eIF2 alpha). Global translation falls, saving energy and reducing the load of new unfolded protein, while a few stress transcripts (ATF4) are translated preferentially. The cell thus has one brake with four pedals. Sustained signalling through this pathway, via the transcription factor CHOP, converts adaptation into apoptosis; this is the mechanism by which chronic endoplasmic reticulum stress kills beta cells in diabetes and neurons in neurodegeneration.

5. **Nutrient sensing sets the balance between growth and maintenance.** The kinase mTOR integrates amino acids, growth factors and energy status and, when all are present, drives protein synthesis, lipid synthesis and growth while suppressing autophagy; AMP-activated protein kinase senses a low ATP:AMP ratio and does the reverse. Starvation, exercise and the drug metformin tip the balance toward catabolism and repair ([autophagy](../02-the-cell-life-cycle/cell-death-apoptosis-necrosis-and-autophagy.md)); reduced mTOR signalling extends lifespan in every model organism tested, and rapamycin is the best-replicated pharmacological life extension in mice. Growth and maintenance compete for the same resources, so the stress response is also a theory of ageing.

6. **The DNA damage response is the stress response for the genome, and its output is the cell cycle decision.** Double-strand breaks activate ATM, single-strand gaps and stalled replication forks activate ATR; both phosphorylate hundreds of substrates, recruit repair machinery, and stabilise p53. The outcome scales with the dose: arrest and repair for a few breaks, senescence or apoptosis for many ([cell cycle](../02-the-cell-life-cycle/the-cell-cycle-and-its-checkpoints.md)). Inherited defects (ataxia telangiectasia, Fanconi anaemia, Lynch syndrome, BRCA mutations) produce cancer predisposition because damaged cells survive, and sensitivity to radiation or chemotherapy because repair fails. The same defect that causes the cancer makes it treatable: BRCA-mutant tumours die when a second repair pathway is inhibited (synthetic lethality with PARP inhibitors).

7. **Adaptation is the usual outcome, and it has recognisable forms.** A cell under sustained stress within its capacity changes rather than dies: hypertrophy (more organelles, as in the heart under pressure load), hyperplasia (more cells, as in the breast in pregnancy), atrophy (fewer, as in a disused muscle), metaplasia (a different cell type better suited, as bronchial epithelium becomes squamous in a smoker). These are the adaptations of [pathology](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/cell-injury-adaptation-and-death.md), and each is the stress programmes above acting over weeks instead of minutes. They are reversible if the stress is removed in time; beyond a point of no return, mitochondrial failure and membrane rupture make injury irreversible.

8. **A stressed cell tells its neighbours and the organism.** Damaged cells release alarmins (ATP, HMGB1, interleukin-1 alpha, interleukin-33) and infected cells release type I interferons, converting a local lesion into inflammation and an immune response ([cytokines](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/cytokines-interferons-and-nk-cells.md)). At organism scale the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis and the sympathetic system mount the systemic stress response that cortisol and adrenaline coordinate ([exercise and stress physiology](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/exercise-and-stress-physiology.md)). Cellular and systemic stress responses are nested feedback loops at two scales.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Heat shock response | Induction of chaperones by unfolded protein via heat shock factor 1 |
| Unfolded protein response | Endoplasmic reticulum stress programme; adaptive first, apoptotic if sustained |
| Nrf2–Keap1 | Oxidant-sensing switch that induces antioxidant and detoxifying enzymes |
| Hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) | Oxygen-regulated transcription factor driving glycolysis, erythropoietin and angiogenesis |
| Reactive oxygen species | Superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, hydroxyl radical; by-products and weapons |
| Integrated stress response | Four kinases converging on eIF2 alpha phosphorylation to throttle translation |
| mTOR / AMPK | Opposing nutrient and energy sensors setting growth against maintenance |
| DNA damage response | ATM/ATR signalling that repairs, arrests or kills a cell with broken DNA |
| Adaptation (hypertrophy, hyperplasia, atrophy, metaplasia) | Reversible structural changes of a cell under sustained stress |
| Alarmin | Molecule released by stressed or dying cells that triggers inflammation |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Stress responses are emergency systems that are off in health."** They run at low level continuously; mitochondria produce oxidants every second and the heat shock system turns over all the time.
- **"Antioxidants protect against disease."** Large trials of antioxidant vitamins have shown no benefit and some harm (beta-carotene raised lung cancer in smokers, 1990s); the enzymatic systems, not dietary radical scavengers, do the work, and some oxidant signalling is necessary.
- **"A cell either survives or dies."** Between them lie adaptation, reversible injury and senescence; the decision depends on dose and duration, and most injured cells recover.
