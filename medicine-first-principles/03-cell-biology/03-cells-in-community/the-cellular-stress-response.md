## Essence

Every cell lives in conditions that vary: oxygen falls, temperature rises, nutrients run out, toxins and radiation damage molecules, pathogens invade. The cellular stress response is the set of conserved programmes that detect each insult and shift the cell from growth to defence: heat shock proteins against unfolded protein, the unfolded protein response in the endoplasmic reticulum, the antioxidant response, the hypoxia response, the DNA damage response and the integrated stress response that throttles translation. The single idea is **sense the damage, not the cause**: each programme is triggered by the lesion itself (unfolded protein, oxidised thiols, stalled hydroxylation, a broken strand), which makes it general. The response decides between adaptation, arrest and death, which is what a pathologist reads in an injured tissue.

## Core tenets

1. **The sensor is the lesion.** Heat shock factor 1 is held inactive by Heat Shock Protein 70 (Hsp70); when unfolded proteins accumulate they occupy Hsp70, freeing the factor to transcribe more chaperones ([protein quality control](../01-cell-structure/protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md)). Keap1 holds Nrf2 for degradation until oxidants modify Keap1's cysteines, releasing Nrf2 to switch on antioxidant enzymes ([redox](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/02-chemistry-of-life/redox-and-electron-transfer.md)). Hypoxia-Inducible Factor (HIF) is hydroxylated by oxygen-dependent enzymes and destroyed; without oxygen it accumulates. In each case the damaging agent inactivates an inhibitor, so the response is proportional to the damage: negative feedback with the lesion as the measured variable.

2. **Hypoxia reprograms metabolism and recruits blood supply.** Stabilised HIF transcribes glycolytic enzymes (ATP without oxygen), erythropoietin (more red cells), vascular endothelial growth factor (new vessels) and nitric oxide synthase (vasodilation). Tumours use the same programme, and HIF is constitutively on in clear cell renal carcinoma because its destroyer, the von Hippel–Lindau protein, is lost ([hallmarks of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/hallmarks-of-cancer.md)).

3. **Oxidative stress is an imbalance, and the defence is enzymatic and renewable.** Mitochondria leak ~0.1–1% of electrons as superoxide; phagocytes make it deliberately. Superoxide dismutase, catalase and the glutathione system, regenerated by NADPH, keep the cytosol reducing. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, the commonest human enzyme defect (~400 million people, 2020s), leaves red cells unable to regenerate glutathione, so they haemolyse under oxidant drugs or infection.

4. **The integrated stress response throttles protein synthesis to buy time.** Four kinases, each sensing a different stress (unfolded protein in the endoplasmic reticulum, amino acid starvation, viral double-stranded RNA, haem deficiency), converge on phosphorylating one translation initiation factor, eIF2 alpha. Translation falls while a few stress transcripts (ATF4) are favoured: one brake with four pedals. Sustained signalling, via the transcription factor CHOP, converts adaptation into apoptosis; this is how chronic endoplasmic reticulum stress kills beta cells in diabetes and neurons in neurodegeneration.

5. **Nutrient sensing sets growth against maintenance.** The kinase mTOR integrates amino acids, growth factors and energy and, when all are present, drives protein and lipid synthesis while suppressing autophagy; AMP-activated protein kinase senses a low ATP:AMP ratio and does the reverse. Starvation, exercise and metformin tip the balance toward catabolism and repair ([autophagy](../02-the-cell-life-cycle/cell-death-apoptosis-necrosis-and-autophagy.md)); reduced mTOR signalling extends lifespan in every model organism tested.

6. **The DNA damage response is the stress response for the genome, and its output is a cell cycle decision.** Double-strand breaks activate ATM, stalled forks activate ATR; both recruit repair and stabilise p53. The outcome scales with dose: arrest and repair for a few breaks, senescence or apoptosis for many ([cell cycle](../02-the-cell-life-cycle/the-cell-cycle-and-its-checkpoints.md)). Inherited repair defects (Lynch syndrome, BRCA mutations) cause cancer because damaged cells survive, and the same defect makes the tumour treatable: BRCA-mutant tumours die when a second repair pathway is inhibited (PARP inhibitors).

7. **Adaptation is the usual outcome, and it has recognisable forms.** A cell under sustained stress within its capacity changes rather than dies: hypertrophy (the heart under pressure load), hyperplasia (the breast in pregnancy), atrophy (a disused muscle), metaplasia (bronchial epithelium becoming squamous in a smoker). These are the adaptations of [pathology](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/cell-injury-adaptation-and-death.md); they reverse if the stress is removed in time, and beyond a point of no return injury becomes irreversible.

8. **A stressed cell tells its neighbours and the organism.** Damaged cells release alarmins (ATP, HMGB1, interleukin-1 alpha) and infected cells release type I interferons, converting a local lesion into inflammation ([cytokines](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/cytokines-interferons-and-nk-cells.md)). At organism scale the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis mounts the systemic response ([stress physiology](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/exercise-and-stress-physiology.md)): nested feedback loops at two scales.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Heat shock response | Induction of chaperones by unfolded protein via heat shock factor 1 |
| Unfolded protein response | Endoplasmic reticulum stress programme; adaptive first, apoptotic if sustained |
| Nrf2–Keap1 | Oxidant-sensing switch that induces antioxidant and detoxifying enzymes |
| Hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) | Oxygen-regulated transcription factor driving glycolysis, erythropoietin and angiogenesis |
| Integrated stress response | Four kinases converging on eIF2 alpha to throttle translation |
| mTOR / AMPK | Opposing nutrient and energy sensors setting growth against maintenance |
| DNA damage response | ATM/ATR signalling that repairs, arrests or kills a cell with broken DNA |
| Adaptation | Hypertrophy, hyperplasia, atrophy, metaplasia: reversible change under sustained stress |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Antioxidants protect against disease."** Large trials of antioxidant vitamins showed no benefit and some harm (beta-carotene raised lung cancer in smokers, 1990s); the enzymatic systems do the work, and some oxidant signalling is necessary.
- **"A cell either survives or dies."** Between them lie adaptation, reversible injury and senescence; most injured cells recover.
