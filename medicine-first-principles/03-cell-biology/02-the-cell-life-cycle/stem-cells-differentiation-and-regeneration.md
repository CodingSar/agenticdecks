## Essence

Every cell carries the same genome, yet a neuron, a hepatocyte and a red cell could not be more different. Differentiation is the stable, heritable choice of which genes a cell expresses, and a stem cell is one that has not yet made the final choice: it renews itself and produces more specialised offspring. Tissues that wear out (blood, gut lining, skin) are maintained by stem cells for life; tissues that do not divide (heart muscle, most of the brain) heal by scar. The single idea is **hierarchy with memory**: a stem cell sits at the top of a lineage tree, held there by signals from a niche, and each step down is locked in by chromatin rather than by changing the Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA).

## Core tenets

1. **Differentiation is epigenetic: the genome is constant, its accessibility is not.** A cell type is a stable pattern of transcription factors, DNA methylation and histone marks that opens some genes and closes others ([gene regulation](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/03-genetic-information-flow/gene-regulation-and-epigenetic-mechanisms.md)), copied through mitosis so a daughter remembers what its mother was. Nothing is lost: nuclear transfer (Gurdon, 1962) and reprogramming (Yamanaka, 2006: four transcription factors return a fibroblast to pluripotency) prove it.

2. **Potency narrows stepwise, and each step is a transcription factor switch.** The zygote and early blastomeres are totipotent (embryo and placenta); inner cell mass cells are pluripotent (any body cell); adult stem cells are multipotent (one tissue's lineages) or unipotent. Each branch is decided by cross-antagonistic factors (GATA1 versus PU.1 in blood) whose mutual repression makes the choice stable and binary.

3. **Stem cells balance symmetric and asymmetric divisions, and the niche sets the balance.** A division can yield two stem cells, two committed cells or one of each; over the population the ratio must average to maintenance. Too much self-renewal is a tumour, too little is exhaustion. The niche (neighbouring cells, matrix and signals such as Wnt and Notch) keeps the cell undifferentiated; a daughter pushed out loses the signal and differentiates ([cell communication](../03-cells-in-community/cell-to-cell-communication-and-junctions.md)). In the intestinal crypt, Wnt from the base maintains stem cells and cells moving up differentiate in the falling gradient.

4. **Quiescence protects the stem pool.** Most adult stem cells spend most of their time in G0 ([cell cycle](the-cell-cycle-and-its-checkpoints.md)), while transit-amplifying progenitors do the bulk of the dividing. This limits [telomere loss](senescence-and-telomeres.md) and mutation in cells that must last a lifetime, and it is why cytotoxic chemotherapy, which kills cycling cells, spares stem cells and lets blood counts recover in two to three weeks.

5. **Regenerative capacity is a tissue's stem cell organisation, and it decides how injury heals.** Labile tissues (marrow, epithelia) regenerate fully. Stable tissues (liver, kidney tubule) are quiescent but their differentiated cells re-enter the cycle on demand: two-thirds of a liver regrows in weeks. Permanent tissues (cardiomyocytes, neurons) have almost no renewal, so an infarct or stroke leaves a scar and a deficit. Even a regenerating tissue heals by fibrosis if its scaffold is destroyed ([tissue repair](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)), which is why cirrhosis follows repeated liver injury.

6. **Cancer is a caricature of the hierarchy.** Many tumours contain a minority of self-renewing cells that regenerate the tumour after treatment while the bulk cannot; in chronic myeloid leukaemia the BCR–ABL mutation sits in the haematopoietic stem cell, so cure requires eliminating that cell ([haematological malignancies](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/haematological-malignancies.md)). The stem cell concept explains relapse after apparent remission.

7. **Stem cells are already medicine.** Haematopoietic stem cell transplantation has treated leukaemia and marrow failure since the 1960s because a few stem cells rebuild an entire blood system. Induced pluripotent stem cells can be made from the patient, and derived therapies (retinal pigment epithelium, dopaminergic neurons, islets) entered trials in the 2010s–2020s. Gene therapy of sickle cell disease and beta-thalassaemia edits a patient's own haematopoietic stem cells ex vivo ([advanced therapies](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)).

8. **Stem cell ageing limits repair in the old.** Aged haematopoietic stem cells are less functional and skewed toward myeloid output. Clonal haematopoiesis, in which one mutant stem cell clone supplies a large fraction of blood, is found in more than 10% of people over 70 and raises the risk of leukaemia and, through inflammatory monocytes, of cardiovascular disease. Stem cell exhaustion is a hallmark of ageing.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stem cell | A cell that can both self-renew and produce differentiated progeny |
| Potency (toti-, pluri-, multi-, uni-) | The range of cell types a cell can still produce |
| Niche | The local cellular and matrix environment that maintains a stem cell |
| Asymmetric division | One daughter remains a stem cell, the other commits |
| Reprogramming | Returning a differentiated cell to pluripotency with defined factors |
| Labile / stable / permanent tissue | Tissues that renew continuously, on demand, or not at all |
| Cancer stem cell | Tumour subpopulation with self-renewal that regenerates the tumour |
| Clonal haematopoiesis | Expansion of one mutant stem cell clone in ageing blood |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Differentiated cells have lost genes."** They have silenced them; reprogramming proves the information is intact.
- **"Adult stem cells can become anything."** They are multipotent within a lineage; claims of broad transdifferentiation have mostly not survived rigorous lineage tracing.
