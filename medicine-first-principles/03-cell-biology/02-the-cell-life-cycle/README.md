## What this area is about

A tissue is a population of cells, and its size and composition are set by four decisions that each cell makes: divide, differentiate, stop permanently, or die. This area describes the machinery of each decision and how they are coupled. The cell cycle is the engine of division and the checkpoints are its brakes; mitosis and meiosis are the mechanics of sharing chromosomes; apoptosis and necrosis are the two ways a cell ends; senescence is the permanent stop; and stem cells are the cells that keep the whole population going by balancing all four. The unifying idea is **regulated fate under surveillance**: every one of these decisions is made by a signalling circuit with a sensor, a threshold and an irreversible step, and every one of them is a point at which disease begins. Cancer is a cell that divides when it should not and refuses to die; neurodegeneration is cells that die and cannot be replaced; ageing is the accumulation of cells that have stopped.

Read the cell cycle first: it defines the phases, the restriction point and the checkpoints that every other child refers to. Mitosis and meiosis describe M phase and its meiotic variant in mechanical detail. Cell death and senescence are the two outcomes of a checkpoint that cannot be satisfied, and they are alternatives to each other. Stem cells put the pieces together at the level of a tissue: a hierarchy in which a few quiescent cells feed a stream of dividing, differentiating and eventually dying cells, and in which the balance of division against death and arrest sets tissue size.
