## Essence

Cells die in two fundamentally different ways. Apoptosis is a programme: the cell dismantles itself from the inside with dedicated proteases, packages its contents and is eaten by neighbours without spilling or inflammation. Necrosis is a catastrophe: energy fails, membranes rupture and the contents leak out, provoking inflammation. The distinction matters because the body uses apoptosis constantly (~10^10 cells a day) to sculpt tissues, remove damaged or surplus cells and end immune responses, while necrosis marks injury: an infarct, a burn, a toxin. Autophagy is a third process that is usually survival rather than death, the recycling of a cell's own components under starvation or damage. The single idea is that **death is regulated**: a cell has the machinery to kill itself, keeps it on a hair trigger, and is kept alive by continuous signals. Too little apoptosis is cancer and autoimmunity; too much is neurodegeneration and the tissue loss of stroke and heart attack.

## Core tenets

1. **Apoptosis is executed by caspases, a cascade of proteases that is irreversible once started.** Initiator caspases (8, 9) are activated by dimerisation on a scaffold; they cleave and activate executioner caspases (3, 7), which cut several hundred substrates: the nuclear lamina, cytoskeletal proteins, the inhibitor of a DNA-cutting nuclease. The amplification of a cascade makes the decision fast and all-or-none, like coagulation ([specificity](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/01-macromolecules/enzymes-and-catalysis.md)). The result is the morphology a pathologist recognises: shrinkage, chromatin condensation, membrane blebbing, fragmentation into apoptotic bodies.

2. **The mitochondrion is the intrinsic switch, and the Bcl-2 family sets its threshold.** Stress signals (DNA damage via p53, growth factor withdrawal, unfolded protein, loss of adhesion) tip the balance between pro-apoptotic (Bax, Bak, and the BH3-only sensors) and anti-apoptotic (Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, Mcl-1) members. When Bax/Bak win, they form pores in the outer mitochondrial membrane, cytochrome c leaks into the cytosol, assembles the apoptosome with Apaf-1, and activates caspase-9. The captured bacterium ([organelles](../01-cell-structure/organelles-and-compartmentalization.md)) is thus the cell's executioner as well as its power plant. Bcl-2 was discovered as the oncogene of follicular lymphoma: a cell that cannot die accumulates even without dividing faster. Drugs mimicking BH3 proteins (venetoclax) kill leukaemia cells that depend on Bcl-2.

3. **The extrinsic pathway lets other cells order a death.** Death receptors (Fas, TNF receptor 1, TRAIL receptors) on the target bind ligands on killer cells or in plasma, recruit adaptor proteins and activate caspase-8. Cytotoxic T cells and natural killer cells use Fas ligand and also inject granzymes through perforin pores to activate caspases directly ([T cells](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/t-cells.md)). This is how virus-infected and tumour cells are eliminated, how activated lymphocytes are culled after an infection, and why Fas mutations cause lymphoproliferation with autoimmunity.

4. **Survival is active: a cell dies unless told not to.** Most cells need continuous trophic signals (growth factors, cytokines, matrix attachment through integrins) that keep anti-apoptotic proteins expressed and the BH3-only sensors silent. Withdraw the signal and apoptosis follows: neurons that fail to reach a target die during development, epithelial cells that detach die (anoikis), and lymphocytes lacking antigen fade. Tumour cells that metastasise must override anoikis, and growth factor pathways such as PI3K–Akt are oncogenic largely because they suppress death.

5. **Apoptotic cells are removed before they leak, which is why apoptosis is silent.** Phosphatidylserine flips to the outer leaflet and is read by macrophage receptors as an "eat me" signal; the cell is engulfed intact and digested. The macrophage responds with anti-inflammatory cytokines. When clearance fails, apoptotic bodies undergo secondary necrosis and release nuclear antigens; defective clearance is one route to systemic lupus erythematosus, where antibodies against DNA and nucleosomes are the hallmark.

6. **Necrosis is energy failure, and its sequence is predictable.** Without Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) ([bioenergetics](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/bioenergetics-atp-and-redox-carriers.md)) the sodium pump stops, the cell swells, calcium floods in and activates phospholipases and proteases, mitochondria open a permeability transition pore, lysosomes leak and the plasma membrane ruptures. Released contents (ATP, DNA, uric acid, HMGB1) are damage-associated molecular patterns that trigger acute inflammation. Infarction is necrosis at tissue scale ([infarction](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md)); enzymes leaking from dying cells (troponin, transaminases) are the blood tests that detect it.

7. **Some necrosis is programmed too, and the body uses it as an alarm.** Necroptosis (via RIPK1, RIPK3 and MLKL) is a regulated membrane rupture triggered when caspase-8 is blocked, which viruses often do; pyroptosis is inflammasome-driven death of infected macrophages through gasdermin pores, releasing interleukin-1; ferroptosis is iron-dependent lipid peroxidation. These forms are inflammatory on purpose: a cell dying loudly recruits the immune system. The pharmacological interest is that regulated necrosis, unlike accidental necrosis, has inhibitable enzymes.

8. **Autophagy is a survival response that can become a death route.** Under starvation, hypoxia or organelle damage the cell digests its own cytoplasm and mitochondria in the lysosome to recover amino acids and remove the source of reactive oxygen species ([protein quality control](../01-cell-structure/protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md)). This usually prolongs life; only when prolonged and extreme does it contribute to death. In the ischaemic heart and brain, and in tumours surviving chemotherapy, autophagy is a resistance mechanism, which is why its modulation is a therapeutic idea.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Apoptosis | Caspase-mediated, non-inflammatory programmed cell death with intact membranes |
| Necrosis | Death by energy failure and membrane rupture, releasing contents and causing inflammation |
| Caspase | Cysteine protease cutting after aspartate; initiators activate executioners |
| Bcl-2 family | Mitochondrial outer membrane gatekeepers; pro- and anti-apoptotic members set the threshold |
| Cytochrome c release | The intrinsic pathway's point of no return; triggers the apoptosome |
| Death receptor | Surface receptor (Fas, TNFR1) that activates caspase-8 when bound by its ligand |
| Anoikis | Apoptosis caused by loss of attachment to matrix |
| Damage-associated molecular pattern | Molecule released by necrotic cells that triggers inflammation |
| Necroptosis / pyroptosis / ferroptosis | Regulated, inflammatory forms of necrosis with defined enzymes |
| Autophagy | Lysosomal self-digestion for recycling and organelle clearance |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Cell death is always bad."** The adult body kills billions of cells a day by apoptosis to stay the same size; failure to die is the problem in cancer and autoimmunity.
- **"Apoptosis and necrosis are distinguished by cause."** They are distinguished by mechanism and consequence; the same insult (ischaemia, toxin) produces apoptosis at low dose and necrosis at high dose, when ATP runs out before the programme can complete.
- **"Autophagy is a way of dying."** It is mainly a way of surviving; cells that cannot perform it die sooner under stress.
