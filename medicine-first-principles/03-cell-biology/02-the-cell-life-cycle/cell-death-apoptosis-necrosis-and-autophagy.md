## Essence

Cells die in two fundamentally different ways. Apoptosis is a programme: the cell dismantles itself with dedicated proteases, packages its contents and is eaten by neighbours without spilling or inflammation. Necrosis is a catastrophe: energy fails, membranes rupture and the contents leak, provoking inflammation. The body uses apoptosis constantly (~10^10 cells a day) to sculpt tissues, remove damaged or surplus cells and end immune responses; necrosis marks injury. Autophagy, self-recycling under starvation or damage, is usually survival rather than death. The single idea is that **death is regulated**: a cell keeps the machinery to kill itself on a hair trigger and is kept alive by continuous signals. Too little apoptosis is cancer and autoimmunity; too much is neurodegeneration and the tissue loss of stroke and heart attack.

## Core tenets

1. **Apoptosis is executed by caspases, a protease cascade that is irreversible once started.** Initiator caspases (8, 9) are activated by dimerisation on a scaffold; they cleave executioner caspases (3, 7), which cut hundreds of substrates: the nuclear lamina, cytoskeletal proteins, the inhibitor of a DNA-cutting nuclease. The cascade makes the decision fast and all-or-none and produces the morphology a pathologist recognises: shrinkage, chromatin condensation, blebbing, apoptotic bodies.

2. **The mitochondrion is the intrinsic switch, and the Bcl-2 family sets its threshold.** Stress (DNA damage via p53, growth factor withdrawal, unfolded protein, loss of adhesion) tips the balance between pro-apoptotic (Bax, Bak, BH3-only sensors) and anti-apoptotic (Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, Mcl-1) members. When Bax and Bak win they form pores in the outer membrane, cytochrome c leaks out, assembles the apoptosome and activates caspase-9. Bcl-2 was discovered as the oncogene of follicular lymphoma: a cell that cannot die accumulates without dividing faster. BH3 mimetics (venetoclax) kill leukaemia cells that depend on Bcl-2.

3. **The extrinsic pathway lets other cells order a death.** Death receptors (Fas, TNF receptor 1) bind ligands on killer cells, recruit adaptors and activate caspase-8. Cytotoxic T cells and natural killer cells use Fas ligand and also inject granzymes through perforin pores ([T cells](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/t-cells.md)). This eliminates infected and tumour cells and culls lymphocytes after an infection; Fas mutations cause lymphoproliferation with autoimmunity.

4. **Survival is active: a cell dies unless told not to.** Most cells need continuous trophic signals (growth factors, cytokines, integrin attachment) that keep anti-apoptotic proteins expressed. Withdraw the signal and apoptosis follows: neurons that fail to reach a target die, detached epithelial cells die (anoikis). Metastasising tumour cells must override anoikis, and growth factor pathways such as PI3K–Akt are oncogenic largely because they suppress death.

5. **Apoptotic cells are removed before they leak, which is why apoptosis is silent.** Phosphatidylserine flips to the outer leaflet as an "eat me" signal; macrophages engulf the cell intact and respond with anti-inflammatory cytokines. When clearance fails, apoptotic bodies undergo secondary necrosis and release nuclear antigens; defective clearance is one route to systemic lupus erythematosus, with its antibodies against DNA and nucleosomes.

6. **Necrosis is energy failure, and its sequence is predictable.** Without Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) ([bioenergetics](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/bioenergetics-atp-and-redox-carriers.md)) the sodium pump stops, the cell swells, calcium floods in and activates phospholipases and proteases, lysosomes leak and the membrane ruptures. Released contents (ATP, DNA, uric acid, HMGB1) are damage-associated molecular patterns that trigger acute inflammation. Infarction is necrosis at tissue scale ([infarction](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md)); enzymes leaking from dying cells (troponin, transaminases) are the blood tests that detect it.

7. **Some necrosis is programmed, and the body uses it as an alarm.** Necroptosis (RIPK1, RIPK3, MLKL) is regulated rupture triggered when caspase-8 is blocked, as viruses often do; pyroptosis is inflammasome-driven death of infected macrophages through gasdermin pores, releasing interleukin-1; ferroptosis is iron-dependent lipid peroxidation. These are inflammatory on purpose and, unlike accidental necrosis, have inhibitable enzymes.

8. **Autophagy is a survival response that can become a death route.** Under starvation, hypoxia or organelle damage the cell digests its own cytoplasm and mitochondria in the lysosome to recover amino acids and remove sources of oxidants ([quality control](../01-cell-structure/protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md)). This usually prolongs life; in tumours surviving chemotherapy it is a resistance mechanism.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Apoptosis | Caspase-mediated, non-inflammatory programmed death with intact membranes |
| Necrosis | Death by energy failure and membrane rupture, releasing contents and causing inflammation |
| Caspase | Cysteine protease cutting after aspartate; initiators activate executioners |
| Bcl-2 family | Mitochondrial gatekeepers whose pro- and anti-apoptotic members set the threshold |
| Death receptor | Surface receptor (Fas, TNFR1) that activates caspase-8 |
| Anoikis | Apoptosis caused by loss of attachment to matrix |
| Damage-associated molecular pattern | Molecule released by necrotic cells that triggers inflammation |
| Autophagy | Lysosomal self-digestion for recycling and organelle clearance |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Cell death is always bad."** The adult body kills billions of cells a day to stay the same size; failure to die is the problem in cancer and autoimmunity.
- **"Apoptosis and necrosis are distinguished by cause."** The same insult produces apoptosis at low dose and necrosis at high dose, when ATP runs out before the programme can finish.
- **"Autophagy is a way of dying."** It is mainly a way of surviving; cells that cannot perform it die sooner under stress.
