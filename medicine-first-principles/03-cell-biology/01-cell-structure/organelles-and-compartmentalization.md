## Essence

A eukaryotic cell is not a bag of enzymes but a set of membrane-bounded compartments, each with its own chemistry. Compartments let incompatible reactions run at once: acid hydrolysis in the lysosome at pH ~4.5, oxidative phosphorylation across the inner mitochondrial membrane, transcription in the nucleus, all a few micrometres apart in a cytosol at pH ~7.2. The price is that every molecule must be addressed, transported and sorted, and every membrane is a gradient that costs energy to maintain. This is where thermodynamics (gradients across membranes), chemistry (enzymes confined to a compartment) and information (a signal sequence that says where a protein belongs) first combine into one self-maintaining unit. Nearly every organelle has a disease named after its failure.

## Core tenets

1. **A barrier is useful only with selective transport.** The bilayer blocks ions and most polar molecules, so the two sides can differ. Pumps and channels (layer 02) then make each lumen distinct: a proton Adenosine Triphosphatase (ATPase) acidifies the lysosome, the endoplasmic reticulum holds ~100-fold more calcium than the cytosol, the mitochondrial matrix is alkaline relative to the intermembrane space. Each difference is stored free energy that costs ATP to hold and can be released as a signal or as work.

2. **The nucleus separates the genome from the machinery that reads it, and the separation is regulatory.** Messenger Ribonucleic Acid (mRNA) is transcribed and spliced inside, exported through nuclear pores (selective gates for cargo above ~40 kDa) and translated outside. The gap permits splicing before translation and lets the cell decide which transcription factors reach the Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA). Nuclear envelope defects (laminopathies) cause premature ageing and muscular dystrophy.

3. **Mitochondria are a captured bacterium, and that history explains their biology and pathology.** They keep their own circular DNA (~16.6 kb, 37 genes), their own ribosomes and a double membrane. The inner membrane carries the respiratory chain, folded into cristae for surface area, and its proton gradient drives ATP synthesis. Because mitochondrial DNA comes only from the egg and exists in hundreds of copies per cell, mitochondrial diseases follow maternal inheritance and vary with the fraction of mutant genomes (heteroplasmy). They are also the switch for intrinsic [apoptosis](../02-the-cell-life-cycle/cell-death-apoptosis-necrosis-and-autophagy.md).

4. **The endomembrane system is one continuous flow of membrane.** Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) to Golgi to plasma membrane or lysosome, and back by endocytosis. The rough ER folds and glycosylates secreted and membrane proteins; the smooth ER makes lipids, stores calcium and, in liver, detoxifies drugs with cytochrome P450 enzymes; the Golgi modifies sugars and sorts cargo; the lysosome digests. Because the ER lumen is topologically outside the cell, a protein's orientation set at insertion is preserved to the surface ([trafficking](membrane-trafficking-and-secretion.md)).

5. **Lysosomes and peroxisomes confine dangerous chemistry.** Lysosomes hold ~60 acid hydrolases that would digest the cytoplasm at neutral pH and are safe only because they work at pH ~4.5. Peroxisomes oxidise very-long-chain fatty acids and handle hydrogen peroxide. When a single hydrolase is missing its substrate accumulates and the cell swells and malfunctions: the ~70 lysosomal storage diseases (Gaucher, Tay–Sachs, Pompe) are each one missing enzyme, and the organs affected are those handling the most substrate.

6. **Compartment size is set by diffusion.** Diffusion time scales with the square of distance ([scale](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/scale-and-allometry.md)): a metabolite crosses a 10 µm cell in ~0.1 s but 1 mm in ~1,000 s. Organelles keep partners within micrometres; neurons with metre-long axons need active transport along the [cytoskeleton](the-cytoskeleton-and-cell-motility.md); mitochondria cluster where ATP is spent.

7. **Every protein carries its address.** A signal sequence sends a nascent chain into the ER; a nuclear localisation signal binds importins; a mannose-6-phosphate tag routes hydrolases to the lysosome; an amphipathic helix targets mitochondria ([protein targeting](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/03-genetic-information-flow/translation-and-protein-targeting.md)). Compartmentalisation is an information problem as much as a structural one: in I-cell disease the mannose-6-phosphate tag cannot be added, lysosomes stay empty and the enzymes appear in blood.

8. **Compartments make injury legible.** Hypoxia stops the mitochondrion first (no ATP), then the sodium pump (the cell swells), then calcium floods in from ER and outside and activates proteases and phospholipases, and finally lysosomes leak. This ordered failure is the subject of [cell injury](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/cell-injury-adaptation-and-death.md), predictable because each organelle has a known job.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Compartment | Membrane-bounded space with its own composition, pH and enzymes |
| Lumen | The inside of an organelle; for ER and Golgi, equivalent to the outside of the cell |
| Cristae | Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane multiplying surface for the respiratory chain |
| Heteroplasmy | Mixture of normal and mutant mitochondrial genomes; sets disease severity |
| Nuclear pore complex | Protein gate controlling traffic of RNA and proteins across the nuclear envelope |
| Lysosomal storage disease | Substrate accumulation because one lysosomal hydrolase is missing |
| Signal sequence | Short peptide directing a protein to a compartment |
| Smooth ER | Lipid synthesis, calcium storage and (in liver) drug metabolism |

## Common misconceptions

- **"The cytoplasm is a dilute solution."** Protein concentration is ~200–300 g/L; the cytosol is crowded and organised, and many reactions run in assemblies or on membranes.
- **"Mitochondria just make ATP."** They set the apoptotic switch, buffer calcium, generate heat and oxidants and make haem and steroid precursors; many mitochondrial diseases present as neurological or muscular disorders.
