## Essence

A cell makes millions of protein molecules an hour, and many misfold, are damaged by heat or oxidation, or are no longer needed. Protein quality control recognises these, tries to refold them and otherwise destroys them: chaperones, the ubiquitin–proteasome system and autophagy to the lysosome. Its logic is that **a protein's concentration is synthesis minus degradation**, so controlled destruction is as much a regulatory tool as transcription. When the system is overwhelmed, misfolded proteins aggregate, and aggregates are the common pathology of Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, Huntington's and prion diseases, of amyloidosis and of much of ageing.

## Core tenets

1. **Folding is marginal, so misfolding is common and must be managed continuously.** The folded state is only ~20–60 kJ/mol more stable than the unfolded ensemble ([protein structure](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/01-macromolecules/proteins-structure-and-folding.md)), and a crowded cytosol favours aggregation of exposed hydrophobic surfaces. Quality control is not an emergency system; it runs all the time.

2. **Chaperones prevent aggregation by binding exposed hydrophobic patches.** Heat Shock Protein 70 (Hsp70) binds unfolded segments in an Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)-dependent cycle and releases them to try again; chaperonin cages (Hsp60, TRiC) enclose a single chain so it can fold alone; Hsp90 matures kinases and steroid receptors. Chaperone expression rises within minutes of heat, oxidative or ischaemic stress because unfolded protein is itself the signal, a negative feedback loop ([stress response](../03-cells-in-community/the-cellular-stress-response.md)).

3. **The ubiquitin–proteasome system destroys chosen proteins fast and specifically.** An E1–E2–E3 enzyme cascade attaches a chain of ubiquitin to the target; the 26S proteasome recognises the chain, unfolds the protein and cuts it to peptides. Specificity lives in the ~600 human E3 ligases, each recognising a motif such as a phosphorylated degron or a misfolded surface. This is how the [cell cycle](../02-the-cell-life-cycle/the-cell-cycle-and-its-checkpoints.md) advances irreversibly (cyclins are destroyed, not inhibited), how p53 is kept low in healthy cells, and how a hypoxic cell stabilises its hypoxia-inducible factor when oxygen-dependent hydroxylation stops marking it for destruction.

4. **The endoplasmic reticulum inspects its products and has a route back out.** Lectin chaperones read glycan trimming as a timer; a chain that fails repeatedly is retrotranslocated to the cytosol for the proteasome (ER-Associated Degradation, ERAD). When misfolded protein accumulates faster than this, the Unfolded Protein Response slows translation, expands the Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) and raises chaperones, and if that fails, triggers apoptosis. The common cystic fibrosis mutation (F508del) is a channel destroyed by ERAD; the alpha-1 antitrypsin Z variant polymerises in hepatocyte ER, causing liver disease by accumulation and lung disease by absence.

5. **Autophagy clears what the proteasome cannot: aggregates, organelles and bulk cytoplasm.** A double membrane engulfs a region of cytoplasm, a damaged mitochondrion (mitophagy) or a tagged aggregate and fuses with the lysosome. It is the only route for turning over whole organelles. Mutations in mitophagy genes (PINK1, Parkin) cause early-onset Parkinson's disease, and lipofuscin in ageing cells is undigested autophagic residue.

6. **Aggregation is a nucleated polymerisation, which explains its tempo and its spread.** Misfolded proteins with exposed beta strands stack into cross-beta amyloid fibrils. Nucleation is slow and rare, growth on an existing fibril fast, so aggregation diseases have a long silent phase and then accelerate; and a seed can template the misfolding of normal protein, which is how prions propagate without nucleic acid and how tau and alpha-synuclein pathology appear to spread along anatomical pathways ([neurodegeneration](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/neurodegenerative-disease.md)). Systemic amyloidosis is the same chemistry with light chains or transthyretin deposited in kidney, heart and nerve.

7. **Proteostasis declines with age, a leading theory of why ageing has a shared phenotype.** Chaperone induction weakens, proteasome activity falls, lysosomes fill with indigestible material. Long-lived post-mitotic cells (neurons, cardiomyocytes) cannot dilute damage by dividing, which is why neurodegeneration and cardiac amyloid are diseases of late life ([diseases of aging](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/diseases-of-aging-and-degeneration.md)).

8. **Degradation is a drug target in both directions.** Proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib) kill myeloma plasma cells because a cell secreting its own weight in antibody every few days depends on ERAD and the proteasome more than any other. Targeted degraders (thalidomide analogues, designed bifunctional molecules) recruit an E3 ligase to a chosen protein and remove it rather than block it ([anticancer drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anticancer-drugs.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proteostasis | The network keeping every protein at the right concentration and conformation |
| Chaperone | Protein that binds unfolded chains to prevent aggregation and assist folding |
| Ubiquitin | 76-residue tag whose chains mark a protein for the proteasome |
| E3 ligase | The enzyme that chooses the substrate; the source of specificity |
| Proteasome | Barrel protease that unfolds and digests ubiquitinated proteins |
| ERAD | Retrotranslocation of misfolded ER proteins for proteasomal destruction |
| Autophagy | Lysosomal degradation of engulfed cytoplasm, aggregates or organelles |
| Amyloid | Cross-beta fibrils formed by nucleated polymerisation of misfolded protein |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Proteins are stable once folded."** Folding is reversible and marginal; a protein samples unfolded states continuously and relies on constant surveillance.
- **"Degradation is waste disposal."** It is regulation with the precision of transcription and the only way to make a process irreversible within minutes.
- **"Aggregates kill because they are big."** Small soluble oligomers are often the most toxic species; large inclusions may be partly protective sequestration.
