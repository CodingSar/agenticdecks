## Essence

The cytoskeleton is the cell's bones, muscles and railways in one system of protein polymers: actin filaments, microtubules and intermediate filaments. It gives the cell shape and stiffness, moves organelles and vesicles along defined tracks, pulls chromosomes apart, pinches the cell in two, and lets white cells crawl and sperm swim. The single idea is **dynamic polymers plus motor proteins**: filaments assemble and disassemble in seconds under control of Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) or Guanosine Triphosphate (GTP) hydrolysis, and motors walk along them converting ATP into directed force. Muscle contraction, mitosis, phagocytosis, axonal transport and cancer invasion are this machinery at different scales.

## Core tenets

1. **Three polymers, three jobs, chosen by mechanics.** Actin filaments (~7 nm, flexible, dense under the membrane) handle shape change and crawling. Microtubules (~25 nm hollow tubes radiating from the centrosome) are long-range tracks and the mitotic spindle. Intermediate filaments (~10 nm ropes: keratins, vimentin, neurofilaments, nuclear lamins) resist tension and hold tissues together. Bending stiffness scales with the fourth power of radius ([mechanics](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/mechanics-and-materials.md)), so a microtubule is ~100 times stiffer than an actin filament: one is a beam, the other a mesh.

2. **Filaments are polar and dynamic, and that is what makes them useful.** Actin and tubulin subunits bind a nucleotide, add faster at the plus end and hydrolyse it after assembly; the hydrolysed polymer is less stable, so a filament can grow at one end while shrinking at the other (treadmilling) or collapse abruptly (dynamic instability). A cell can rebuild its skeleton in minutes and probe its surroundings with microtubule tips. Drugs that freeze the dynamic kill dividing cells: taxanes stabilise microtubules, vinca alkaloids block assembly, and both arrest mitosis ([anticancer drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anticancer-drugs.md)).

3. **Motor proteins couple ATP hydrolysis to a conformational cycle that moves a fixed step.** Myosins walk on actin; kinesins (mostly plus-end directed) and dyneins (minus-end directed) walk on microtubules. Each cycle of bind, stroke, release and recover moves ~8 nm (kinesin) or ~36 nm (myosin V) for one ATP. Direction is built into motor and track, so a neuron sends vesicles outward on kinesin and returns worn components on dynein. Axons a metre long depend on this, and failing axonal transport is an early event in many neurodegenerative diseases.

4. **Muscle is the cytoskeleton specialised to one task.** In striated muscle, actin and myosin II are arranged into sarcomeres so every power stroke pulls the same way, and calcium gates the interaction through troponin and tropomyosin. Sliding filaments, force proportional to overlap and ATP cost per cycle are cytoskeletal facts before they are physiology ([skeletal muscle](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/skeletal-muscle-contraction-and-energetics.md)). Rigor mortis is myosin frozen on actin when ATP runs out.

5. **Crawling is actin polymerisation at the front and myosin contraction at the back.** A neutrophil or fibroblast pushes out a lamellipodium by nucleating branched actin (Arp2/3 complex) against the membrane, anchors it through integrin adhesions, and contracts its rear with myosin II. Direction comes from chemical gradients sensed by receptors that activate small GTPases (Rac at the front, Rho at the rear). Neutrophil chemotaxis, fibroblast migration into a wound and tumour invasion are one programme with different triggers.

6. **Cilia and flagella are microtubule machines, and their failure is a syndrome.** Motile cilia beat with a 9+2 microtubule array driven by axonemal dynein; sperm swim with the same engine. Primary ciliary dyskinesia gives chronic sinusitis and bronchiectasis (mucus not cleared), male infertility and, in about half of patients, situs inversus, because a motile cilium at the embryonic node sets left–right asymmetry ([patterning](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/03-development/morphogens-patterning-and-developmental-genes.md)). Non-motile primary cilia are sensory antennae; their defects cause polycystic kidneys and retinal degeneration.

7. **Intermediate filaments are tissue-specific, hence diagnostic and mechanical markers.** Epithelia express keratins, mesenchymal cells vimentin, muscle desmin, neurons neurofilaments, glia glial fibrillary acidic protein; a pathologist traces an undifferentiated tumour by staining for them. Mechanically they carry force across tissues through desmosomes; mutations in epidermal keratins make skin blister at slight shear (epidermolysis bullosa simplex).

8. **The cytoskeleton senses and signals.** Tension on integrins and cadherins is transmitted through actin to the nucleus and alters gene expression: stem cells on stiff substrates turn bone-like, on soft ones fat-like or neural. The aggregates of neurodegeneration (tau tangles, Lewy bodies) are largely cytoskeleton-associated proteins that [quality control](protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md) failed to clear.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Actin filament | ~7 nm polar polymer; cortex, lamellipodia, contractile rings, sarcomeres |
| Microtubule | ~25 nm hollow tubulin polymer; tracks, spindle, cilia |
| Intermediate filament | ~10 nm tension-bearing rope; keratin, vimentin, desmin, neurofilament, lamin |
| Dynamic instability | Abrupt switching of a microtubule between growth and collapse |
| Motor protein | ATPase walking along a polar filament: myosin, kinesin, dynein |
| Lamellipodium | Sheet of branched actin pushing a crawling cell's leading edge |
| Small GTPase (Rho, Rac, Cdc42) | Switches that pattern the actin cytoskeleton in space |
| Axoneme | The 9+2 microtubule core of a motile cilium or flagellum |

## Common misconceptions

- **"The cytoskeleton is rigid, like a skeleton."** Most of it turns over within minutes; its function depends on disassembly as much as assembly.
- **"Only muscle uses actin and myosin for force."** Every cell uses them for division, crawling and shape; muscle is the abundant special case.
- **"Cilia are a minor feature."** Nearly every cell has a primary cilium, and ciliary defects explain disease in kidney, retina, airway, brain and body asymmetry at once.
