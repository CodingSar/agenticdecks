## Essence

The abdomen and pelvis hold the gut, its glands, the kidneys and the reproductive organs inside one peritoneal sac and a muscular wall that must be both strong and openable. The region's anatomy is the embryo's made permanent: the gut is divided into three territories by the three arteries it was given before it rotated, every organ keeps the nerve supply and lymph drainage of the level it came from, and the holes through which the testis descended and the umbilical vessels ran remain as the weak points of hernias. The most consequential relation is the portal vein: all gut blood passes through the liver before the heart, so the liver sees first what the gut absorbs, and when the liver scars the gut's veins burst where they meet the systemic circulation.

## Core tenets

1. **Three arteries divide the gut into three territories, each with its own nerve, pain and watershed.** The coeliac trunk (T12) supplies the foregut, from lower oesophagus to mid-duodenum with liver, pancreas and spleen; the superior mesenteric artery (L1) the midgut to two thirds along the transverse colon, including the appendix; the inferior mesenteric (L3) the hindgut to the upper rectum. Each sends its pain to one place (epigastrium, periumbilical, suprapubic), and the joins at the splenic flexure and rectosigmoid are the watersheds that infarct when pressure falls. Sudden superior mesenteric occlusion kills the whole midgut with pain out of proportion to signs. The midgut rotated 270 degrees anticlockwise around that artery, placing the caecum in the right iliac fossa; incomplete rotation leaves a narrow root around which bowel twists.

2. **The portal vein makes the liver the gatekeeper, and its pressure problems appear at the borders.** The superior mesenteric and splenic veins join into the portal vein, which carries about three quarters of the liver's blood at 5–10 mmHg before the hepatic veins drain to the cava. Drugs and nutrients pass the liver before the body (first-pass metabolism), and any gut cancer can seed it. When cirrhosis raises sinusoidal resistance, portal blood finds the systemic veins at the lower oesophagus (varices that bleed torrentially), the umbilicus, the rectum and the retroperitoneum; fluid filters out as ascites and gut ammonia bypasses the liver to the brain. The liver's eight segments each have their own portal inflow and bile drainage, so any can be resected; bile and pancreatic ducts open together at the ampulla, so one stone gives jaundice and pancreatitis at once.

3. **The peritoneal spaces decide where fluid goes and how a perforation presents.** Supine, the most dependent upper-abdominal space is between liver and right kidney, so free blood is first seen there on ultrasound; upright, fluid runs down the paracolic gutters into the pelvis. A perforated anterior duodenal ulcer releases gas that rises under the diaphragm on an erect radiograph; a posterior ulcer erodes the gastroduodenal artery and bleeds instead. Perforations of retroperitoneal segments (posterior duodenum, ascending and descending colon) spare the peritoneal signs and are missed for that reason.

4. **The anterior wall fails where things pass through.** The inguinal canal is the tunnel the testis took, from the deep ring lateral to the inferior epigastric artery to the superficial ring above the pubic tubercle: an indirect hernia follows it through the deep ring, often in a congenitally patent sac; a direct hernia pushes through the weakened posterior wall medial to the artery in older men. The femoral canal, below the inguinal ligament and medial to the femoral vein, admits a hernia with a narrow rigid neck that strangulates readily and is commoner in women. The midline linea alba is where surgeons enter and incisional hernias form.

5. **The kidneys and ureters are retroperitoneal, and the ureter crosses the surgeon's path.** The kidneys lie from T12 to L3 and are supplied by end arteries, so an embolus infarcts a wedge. The ureters descend on the psoas, cross the pelvic brim at the common iliac bifurcation and pass under the uterine artery or vas deferens into the bladder; they are narrowest at the renal pelvis, the brim and the bladder wall, where stones lodge, and their pain runs along T10–L1 from loin to groin. The ureter is cut most often in hysterectomy, a centimetre from the uterine artery. The left gonadal vein drains into the left renal vein, so varicoceles are mostly left-sided.

6. **The pelvis is a ring with a muscular floor, and its nerves come from two segmental levels.** A ring breaks in two places and its venous plexus bleeds into the retroperitoneum unopposed. The pelvic floor (levator ani) holds bladder, uterus and rectum against the pressure of standing and coughing; torn in childbirth, it lets them prolapse and the sphincters leak. Parasympathetic fibres from S2–S4 empty bladder and rectum and produce erection; sympathetic fibres from T11–L2 close the bladder neck and drive ejaculation; the pudendal nerve (S2–S4) supplies perineal sensation and the voluntary sphincters, so the level of a spinal injury predicts bladder, bowel and sexual function. A ruptured ectopic pregnancy bleeds into the peritoneum and is felt at the shoulder tip; the prostate encircles the urethra below the bladder and obstructs it when enlarged.

7. **Lymph follows the arteries and the embryo, so node spread is predictable.** Gut lymph follows the three arteries to pre-aortic nodes and the thoracic duct, so a stomach cancer can present as a node at the root of the left neck. The testis drains to para-aortic nodes at L2, the level it descended from, while the scrotum drains to the groin. The rectum above the pectinate line drains to inferior mesenteric and internal iliac nodes; the anal canal below it drains to the groin. The aorta divides at L4; aneurysms form mostly below the renal arteries and rupture into the retroperitoneum, which may tamponade the bleed long enough to reach theatre.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Foregut / midgut / hindgut | The three embryonic gut territories, supplied by the coeliac, superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric arteries |
| Portal vein | Vessel carrying all venous blood from gut and spleen through the liver before the heart |
| Portosystemic anastomosis | Junction of portal and systemic veins (oesophagus, umbilicus, rectum, retroperitoneum) that dilates in portal hypertension |
| Hepatic segment | One of eight units of liver with its own portal inflow and bile drainage |
| Hepatorenal recess | Space between liver and right kidney; the most dependent part of the upper abdomen when supine |
| Inguinal canal | Oblique passage through the lower abdominal wall taken by the spermatic cord or round ligament |
| Indirect / direct / femoral hernia | Through the deep ring, through the posterior canal wall, or under the inguinal ligament |
| Ureteric constrictions | Renal pelvis, pelvic brim and bladder wall: where stones lodge |
| Pelvic floor | Levator ani and associated muscles supporting the pelvic organs against intra-abdominal pressure |
| Pectinate line | Level in the anal canal above which lymph drains inward and below which it drains to the groin |

- **Above:** the surgical [acute abdomen](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/the-acute-abdomen.md) is these spaces, territories and pains read at the bedside; the [liver as metabolic hub](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/the-liver-as-metabolic-hub.md) and the complications of [liver disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md) follow from the portal vein; [labour and delivery](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/labour-delivery-and-the-puerperium.md) is the pelvic ring and floor under load; abdominal [ultrasound](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md) looks first in the dependent spaces named here.
- **Siblings:** the diaphragm's openings and the liver and spleen under the ribs are shared with the [thorax](thorax.md); the femoral vessels and nerve pass into the [lower limb](upper-and-lower-limbs.md), and the groin nodes receive lymph from both.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Abdominal pain is felt where the organ is."** Visceral pain is felt in the midline at the level of the organ's embryonic territory; only when the parietal peritoneum is inflamed does it move to the organ's position.
- **"The liver's blood comes from the hepatic artery."** About three quarters comes from the portal vein, at low pressure and low oxygen; the liver is the one large organ perfused mostly by venous blood.
- **"A small hernia is a safe hernia."** The femoral hernia is small, often unnoticed and the most likely to strangulate, because its neck is narrow and rigid.
