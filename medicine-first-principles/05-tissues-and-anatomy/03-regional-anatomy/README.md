## What this area is about

Regional anatomy is the body described place by place: what lies next to what, and therefore what is injured, compressed, infected or operated on together. An atlas does this exhaustively. These four leaves do not; they are essences of the **load-bearing relations**, the few dozen facts of position that explain why a disease presents as it does and why a procedure is done where it is. The recurrent laryngeal nerve under the aortic arch, the right ventricle behind the sternum, the portal vein through the liver, the median nerve in the carpal tunnel: each is a structural fact from which a whole family of clinical facts follows.

The unifying idea is that **every regional relation is an instance of a general rule** stated in the sibling area on body organization. The skull is a closed compartment; the coronary arteries are end arteries; the splenic flexure is a watershed; the appendix refers its pain to the midgut's segment; the hip is a lever with a short arm. The regional leaves show those rules at work in a particular place, and are written so that a reader who knows the rules can predict most of what they contain.

Read them in the order listed. The head, neck and spine leaf introduces the cord–vertebra mismatch and the pharyngeal-arch logic that the thorax picks up with the left recurrent laryngeal and phrenic nerves. The thorax ends at the diaphragm, whose openings and whose relation to the liver and spleen open the abdomen. The abdomen and pelvis leaf ends with the femoral vessels and lymph nodes that the limbs leaf begins with. Each leaf leans on all three of the body-organization rules (compartments, vessels, segments); the limbs lean also on biomechanics.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Tissues](../01-tissues/README.md):** the behaviour of each region is the behaviour of its tissues in a particular geometry. The inextensible fibrous pericardium and deep fascia are dense connective tissue; the avascular femoral head and scaphoid die because bone lives by its vessels; nerves are compressed in tunnels because axons depend on a blood supply and transport that pressure stops; cardiac muscle's inability to regenerate is why a coronary territory becomes scar.
- **[Body organization](../02-body-organization/README.md):** supplies every rule the regions instantiate. The regional leaves are deliberately written as applications, and a reader who finds a regional fact surprising should look for the rule behind it there.

## Connections across layers

From below, the regions are the end state of [organogenesis](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/03-development/germ-layers-and-organogenesis.md): gut rotation, the descent of the thyroid and testis, the pharyngeal arches and the limb buds explain most of the odd relations (the recurrent laryngeal nerve, the inguinal canal, the position of the appendix, the spiralling dermatomes). Upward, this area is cited by every clinical layer. [06 Physiology](../../06-physiology/README.md) assumes the pleural coupling, the coronary circulation and the portal vein. [08 Pathology](../../08-pathology/README.md) maps [infarction](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md) onto these territories. [11 Clinical Method and Diagnostics](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/README.md) examines and images the body region by region. [12 Organ-system specialties](../../12-organ-system-specialties/README.md) draws on the head for [stroke](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/stroke.md), the thorax for [ischaemic heart disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md) and the abdomen for [liver disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md).
