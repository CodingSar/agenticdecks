## What this area is about

Between the cell and the organ lies the tissue: a population of cells and the matrix they secrete, organised so that the whole does something no single cell can. The body builds every organ from only four basic tissues (epithelium, connective tissue, muscle and nerve), with blood counted as a connective tissue whose matrix is liquid. This area asks what each tissue is *for* and shows that its microscopic structure is the answer: the sealed, polarised sheet of an epithelium exists to separate compartments and move things across; the matrix of connective tissue exists to bear load; muscle exists to turn Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) into force; nervous tissue exists to carry information fast and far.

The idea that unifies the five children is that **a tissue's design fixes both what it can do and how it fails**. An avascular cartilage cannot heal; a post-mitotic neuron cannot be replaced; a constantly dividing epithelium is where cancers start; a muscle that depends on a single nerve wastes when that nerve dies. Reading a tissue's structure therefore tells you its diseases before you have met them.

## How the children fit together

| Child | Its role in the whole | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| [Epithelium](epithelium.md) | Surfaces and glands: barrier, selective transport, secretion; origin of most cancers | Junctions, polarity, membrane transport |
| [Connective tissue, bone and cartilage](connective-tissue-bone-and-cartilage.md) | Matrix that bears load, packages organs, stores calcium and repairs every injury | Extracellular matrix, mechanics of materials |
| [Muscle tissue](muscle-tissue.md) | Force from the actin–myosin motor in three designs: skeletal, cardiac, smooth | Cytoskeleton, excitable membranes, connective-tissue scaffold |
| [Nervous tissue](nervous-tissue.md) | Fast, long-range signalling by neurons; support and insulation by glia | Excitable membranes, axonal transport |
| [Blood and haematopoiesis](blood-and-haematopoiesis.md) | Liquid tissue for transport; a stem-cell factory in bone marrow | Stem-cell hierarchy, fluid physics, bone |

```
          ectoderm            mesoderm              endoderm
             |            /     |      \                |
         nervous      connective  muscle   blood     epithelium
         tissue       tissue              (in marrow   (gut, glands,
         + skin         |                  of bone)     airway)
         epithelium     +-- bone, cartilage, fat, tendon, dermis
                        +-- supports and feeds every epithelium
                        +-- transmits every muscle's pull
```

Read epithelium first: it introduces the basement membrane and polarity that every later organ uses. Connective tissue comes next because it supports every epithelium, scaffolds every muscle and is the material of all repair. Muscle and nervous tissue are the two excitable tissues; muscle is read before nerve because its three types show most clearly how structure is tuned to a task, and nerve closes with the barriers and regeneration rules that neurology rests on. Blood is last because it is made inside bone and distributed through vessels that are themselves made of epithelium, connective tissue and smooth muscle. Together the five are the whole parts list: every organ in the body is some arrangement of them, and the arrangements are the subject of the two sibling areas.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Body organization](../02-body-organization/README.md):** takes the tissues and shows how they are packaged: deep fascia (dense connective tissue) makes the compartments; endothelium, smooth muscle and elastic tissue make the vessel wall; the segmental wiring of nervous tissue produces dermatomes and referred pain; the material properties of bone, cartilage and tendon set the biomechanics of joints.
- **[Regional anatomy](../03-regional-anatomy/README.md):** every relation described there is a consequence of tissue properties: the inextensible fibrous pericardium makes tamponade possible, the avascular femoral head dies after a neck fracture, the long peripheral axons of the limbs are the ones compressed at bony tunnels, and the absence of cartilage healing makes joint injury cumulative.

## Cross-cutting threads

- [Structure determines function](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#structure-determines-function): the whole area is this principle applied to the four tissues.
- [Barriers and compartments](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#barriers-and-compartments): epithelial tight junctions, the basement membrane and the blood–brain barrier decide what is kept apart.
- [Injury and repair](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#injury-and-repair): epithelium regenerates, connective tissue scars, nerve and heart muscle do neither well.
- [Supply and demand](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#supply-and-demand): avascular cartilage and epithelium live by diffusion; nervous tissue dies within minutes without oxygen.
- [Gradients and flow](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#gradients-and-flow): polarised epithelia build gradients; blood flows down the pressure gradient the heart makes.
- [Redundancy and reserve](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#redundancy-and-reserve): the marrow's idle capacity and the neutrophil reserve.
- [Scale and surface-to-volume](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#scale-and-surface-to-volume): the diffusion limit sets epithelial thickness, the osteon's size and the red cell's shape.
