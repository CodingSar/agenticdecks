# Acid-peptic disease and GI bleeding

## Essence

The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid to a pH of 1–2 and the protease pepsin, and does not digest itself only because a mucus–bicarbonate layer, tight epithelial junctions, rapid cell turnover and a rich blood flow defend the mucosa. Acid-peptic disease is that defence failing: reflux of acid into an oesophagus not built for it, erosion and ulceration of the stomach and duodenum, and the bleeding and perforation that follow when an ulcer reaches a vessel or the wall. The discovery that a bacterium, *Helicobacter pylori*, causes most ulcers converted a chronic disease of stress and diet into a curable infection. Gastrointestinal bleeding from any level is the specialty's commonest emergency and is approached as a problem of circulation first and source second; the endoscope then finds the lesion and usually treats it.

## Core tenets

1. **Acid is pumped against a million-fold gradient, and the pump is the drug target.** Parietal cells secrete hydrogen ions through the hydrogen–potassium ATPase, driven by histamine (from enterochromaffin-like cells), gastrin and acetylcholine converging on the same cell ([secretion](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/secretion-digestion-and-absorption.md)). Histamine H2-receptor antagonists block one input; proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole) bind the pump covalently and stop acid output from every input, which is why they heal ulcers and oesophagitis that resisted everything before them ([receptor pharmacology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/receptor-theory-agonists-and-antagonists.md)). Suppressing acid does not remove the cause; it tilts the acid–defence balance long enough for the mucosa to heal.

2. **Ulcers form where aggression exceeds defence, and two causes account for nearly all of them.** *H. pylori*, a spiral bacterium that lives in the mucus layer and neutralises acid around itself with urease, infects about half the world's population and causes chronic gastritis in all of them, peptic ulcer in 10–15% and gastric cancer in a few ([bacteria](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/bacteria.md)). Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and aspirin inhibit the cyclo-oxygenase that makes the prostaglandins maintaining mucus, bicarbonate and blood flow, removing the defence. Together they explain over 90% of ulcers; eradicating the bacterium with antibiotics cures the disease rather than suppressing it, and the fall in ulcer incidence since the 1990s followed. Marshall and Warren's demonstration of this against a century of belief in stress and acid is a lesson in [cause and mechanism](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#cause-and-mechanism).

3. **Reflux is a valve problem, and its danger is in the epithelium that adapts to it.** The lower oesophageal sphincter and the diaphragm's crura keep gastric contents out; transient relaxations, a hiatus hernia, obesity and delayed emptying let acid up. Squamous oesophageal epithelium has no mucus defence: heartburn, oesophagitis, stricture. In some patients the squamous lining is replaced by intestinal-type columnar epithelium (Barrett's oesophagus), a [metaplasia](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/cell-injury-adaptation-and-death.md) that resists acid but is the precursor of oesophageal adenocarcinoma, which is why it is surveyed by endoscopy. Dysphagia, weight loss or vomiting blood in a patient with reflux symptoms are the alarm features that change the question from "how to suppress acid" to "is there a cancer" ([common solid tumours](../07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md)).

4. **Symptoms localise poorly, so the endoscope is the diagnosis.** Epigastric pain, nausea and bloating (dyspepsia) arise from ulcer, reflux, cancer, gallstones and from a gut with no visible lesion at all (functional dyspepsia, the commonest cause) in proportions that history cannot reliably separate. Non-invasive *H. pylori* tests (stool antigen, urea breath test) and a trial of acid suppression manage most younger patients without endoscopy; alarm features and age select the rest for direct inspection and biopsy ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md)).

5. **Bleeding presents by volume and by level, and the first treatment is the circulation.** Vomited blood (haematemesis) or black, tarry stool (melaena, blood digested by acid and bacteria) means a source above the ligament of Treitz; fresh red blood per rectum usually means a colonic or anorectal source, unless the upper bleed is torrential. Loss of a litre or more produces the signs of [haemorrhagic shock](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md): tachycardia, postural hypotension, pallor, confusion. The haemoglobin falls only after plasma volume is restored over hours, so a normal first value is no reassurance. Resuscitation with fluid and blood, and correction of anticoagulation, precede any search for the source; a raised urea with a normal creatinine is the chemical footprint of digested blood protein.

6. **Endoscopy finds the lesion and stops most bleeding; the source dictates the method.** Peptic ulcer, oesophagitis, erosions and varices cause most upper bleeds; diverticular disease, angiodysplasia, colitis, haemorrhoids and cancer most lower bleeds. A visible vessel in an ulcer base is injected, clipped or coagulated; proton pump inhibitors after haemostasis stabilise the clot, because platelets and fibrin do not function at pH below 6. Varices, which are dilated portosystemic collaterals under [portal hypertension](liver-disease.md), are banded, and drugs that lower portal pressure (terlipressin, octreotide) and antibiotics (because cirrhotic bleeders become septic) are given. Bleeding that endoscopy cannot control goes to interventional radiology (embolisation) or [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/the-acute-abdomen.md). Mortality from upper bleeding remains about 5–10% (high-income countries, 2010s), mostly in old patients with comorbidity rather than from exsanguination.

7. **Chronic occult bleeding presents as iron deficiency, and that is a cancer search.** A lesion losing a few millilitres a day produces no visible blood but a slow drain on iron stores ([red cells and iron](../../06-physiology/08-blood/red-cells-haemoglobin-and-iron.md)). Iron deficiency anaemia in a man or a postmenopausal woman is therefore assumed to be gastrointestinal blood loss until the upper tract, the colon and coeliac serology have been examined, because colorectal cancer is a common cause and a curable one when found early ([anaemias](../06-haematology/anaemias.md)). Population screening for colorectal cancer by faecal occult blood testing rests on the same logic ([screening](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** [Surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) takes perforation, uncontrolled bleeding and the cancers. [Emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) resuscitate the shocked bleeder; [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) and [transfusion](../06-haematology/transfusion-medicine.md) supply blood and advise on reversing anticoagulants. [Cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md) negotiates the antiplatelet drugs that cause the bleeding and protect the stents. [Oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) receives the cancers found. [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) manages the vast volume of dyspepsia and reflux, prescribes most of the acid suppression and non-steroidal drugs, and applies the referral criteria that decide who is endoscoped.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Parietal cell | Gastric cell that secretes acid through the hydrogen–potassium ATPase |
| *Helicobacter pylori* | Urease-producing bacterium of the gastric mucus layer; the main cause of ulcer and gastric cancer |
| Peptic ulcer | A breach through the muscularis mucosae of stomach or duodenum from acid and pepsin |
| Gastro-oesophageal reflux | Retrograde flow of gastric contents into an oesophagus without mucus defence |
| Barrett's oesophagus | Columnar metaplasia of the lower oesophagus; precursor of adenocarcinoma |
| Dyspepsia | Upper abdominal pain or discomfort; usually functional, sometimes structural |
| Haematemesis and melaena | Vomited blood and digested black stool; markers of an upper source |
| Varices | Dilated portosystemic venous collaterals that bleed under portal hypertension |
| Alarm features | Dysphagia, weight loss, bleeding, vomiting, anaemia or age that mandate endoscopy |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Ulcers are caused by stress and spicy food."** They are caused by *H. pylori* and anti-inflammatory drugs; eradicating the bacterium cures them.
- **"A normal haemoglobin means the bleed was small."** Haemoglobin concentration falls only after plasma volume is replaced; pulse and blood pressure are the early measures of loss.
- **"Heartburn is harmless."** Usually, but long-standing reflux can produce Barrett's metaplasia and adenocarcinoma; alarm features change the approach.
- **"Black stool always means bleeding."** Iron tablets and bismuth blacken stool; melaena is tarry, sticky and foul, and the urea is raised.
