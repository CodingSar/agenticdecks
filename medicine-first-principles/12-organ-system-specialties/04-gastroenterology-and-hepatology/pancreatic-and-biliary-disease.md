## Essence

The pancreas and the biliary tree share a duct, an opening into the duodenum, and a set of diseases that follow from that anatomy. Bile, made by the liver and concentrated in the gallbladder, is a detergent that precipitates into stones when its cholesterol or pigment exceeds what its salts can hold; stones then block whatever duct they reach. The pancreas makes the enzymes that digest protein and fat and stores them as inactive precursors; anything that activates them inside the gland (a stone in the shared duct, alcohol) sets off autodigestion, which is acute pancreatitis, a chemical burn that can become a systemic inflammatory illness. Chronic injury scars the gland and removes both its digestive and its endocrine function, and the ductal epithelium of both organs gives rise to cancers that present late because the organs lie deep and silent. The single idea is obstruction and activation in a shared duct system.

## Core tenets

1. **Gallstones are a solubility failure, and they matter only when they move.** Bile holds cholesterol in solution as mixed micelles with bile salts and phospholipid ([secretion and digestion](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/secretion-digestion-and-absorption.md)); when cholesterol is supersaturated (obesity, oestrogen, rapid weight loss) or the gallbladder empties poorly, crystals nucleate and grow. Pigment stones form from excess bilirubin in haemolysis. About 10–20% of adults in high-income countries have stones; most never know. Symptoms arise when a stone obstructs: the gallbladder neck (biliary colic, steady epigastric or right upper quadrant pain after meals, as the gallbladder contracts against the block), persistently (acute cholecystitis, with inflammation, fever and a tender gallbladder), the common bile duct (obstructive jaundice, pale stools, dark urine, itch), or the ampulla (pancreatitis). Infection of an obstructed duct (cholangitis) adds rigors and is a sepsis emergency.

2. **Obstructive jaundice has a chemical signature and an anatomical question.** Conjugated bilirubin that cannot reach the gut refluxes into blood and is excreted in urine; alkaline phosphatase and gamma-glutamyl transferase rise more than the aminotransferases ([liver disease](liver-disease.md)). Fat-soluble vitamin K is not absorbed without bile, so the prothrombin time lengthens and corrects with injected vitamin K, which distinguishes obstruction from hepatocellular failure. Ultrasound shows dilated ducts and usually the stone or the mass ([ultrasound](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md)); magnetic resonance cholangiography maps the tree without instruments. The question is then stone or tumour: painful jaundice with a history of colic suggests stone, painless progressive jaundice with weight loss suggests cancer of the pancreatic head or bile duct.

3. **Acute pancreatitis is premature activation of enzymes inside the gland.** Trypsinogen is normally activated only in the duodenum; when a gallstone obstructs the ampulla or alcohol injures the acinar cell, trypsin is activated within the cell, activates the other proteases and lipases, and the gland digests itself and its surrounding fat ([enzymes](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/01-macromolecules/enzymes-and-catalysis.md)). Gallstones and alcohol cause about 70–80%; hypertriglyceridaemia, drugs, endoscopic instrumentation of the duct and genetic defects in trypsin regulation account for most of the rest. The patient has severe epigastric pain radiating to the back, vomiting, and a serum amylase or lipase several times normal, because the enzymes leak into blood.

4. **The danger is systemic inflammation, not the pancreas itself.** Cytokines from the burning gland spill into the circulation and cause the capillary leak, hypovolaemia, acute respiratory distress and acute kidney injury of [systemic inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md); fat necrosis sequesters calcium (hypocalcaemia). About 80% of attacks are mild and resolve with fluid and analgesia in days. The remainder develop necrosis of the gland, which may become infected from gut bacteria in the second week and is the main cause of death, with mortality in severe disease of 15–30%. No drug stops the autodigestion; treatment is support (fluid, oxygen, nutrition), removal of the cause (the stone, by endoscopy if cholangitis coexists, and cholecystectomy to prevent recurrence), and drainage of infected collections, increasingly by endoscope rather than open surgery ([critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md)).

5. **Chronic pancreatitis destroys both functions of the gland and causes constant pain.** Repeated injury, most often from alcohol and smoking, and sometimes from inherited trypsinogen or cystic fibrosis gene mutations, replaces acinar tissue with fibrosis and calcification ([fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)). When enzyme output falls below about 10% of normal, fat is malabsorbed (steatorrhoea, weight loss, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency; [malabsorption](inflammatory-bowel-disease-and-malabsorption.md)), and when the islets are lost, diabetes follows, which is brittle because glucagon is lost with insulin ([diabetes](../05-endocrinology/diabetes-mellitus.md)). Pain, from inflamed nerves and ductal pressure, dominates the illness and is hard to treat. Oral pancreatic enzyme replacement reverses the malabsorption; nothing restores the gland.

6. **Cancers of the pancreas and bile duct present late because the organs are deep and the early symptoms are vague.** Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, about 3% of cancers but the third or fourth leading cause of cancer death in high-income countries (2020s), arises through a defined sequence of mutations (*KRAS* in over 90%, then *CDKN2A*, *TP53*, *SMAD4*) in ductal precursor lesions ([oncogenes and tumour suppressors](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/oncogenes-tumour-suppressors-and-genomic-instability.md)). A tumour in the head obstructs the bile duct and presents as painless jaundice; one in the body or tail presents with back pain and weight loss once it has invaded nerves and vessels. Fewer than 20% are resectable at diagnosis and five-year survival is around 10% overall; new-onset diabetes in a lean older adult is one of the few early clues. Cholangiocarcinoma and gallbladder cancer behave similarly; risk factors are chronic ductal inflammation (primary sclerosing cholangitis, liver flukes, stones).

7. **Imaging and endoscopy both diagnose and treat.** Ultrasound is the first test for stones and duct dilatation; computed tomography stages pancreatitis and cancer; magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography maps ducts non-invasively. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography cannulates the ampulla to remove duct stones and place stents across malignant strictures, and endoscopic ultrasound sees the pancreas through the stomach wall and biopsies it. Each intervention carries its own risk (post-procedure pancreatitis in a few percent), so the non-invasive test is done first and the endoscope is reserved for therapy ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)).

8. **Who else is involved.** [Hepatobiliary surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) owns cholecystectomy, necrosectomy and pancreatic resection, and pancreatitis and cholecystitis are shared [acute abdomen](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/the-acute-abdomen.md) problems; [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) manages severe pancreatitis; interventional [radiology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/README.md) drains collections and ducts. [Oncology](../07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md) treats the cancers; [endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/diabetes-mellitus.md) manages the diabetes of pancreatic destruction and the hypertriglyceridaemia that causes pancreatitis; [palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/palliative-care-and-symptom-control.md) is needed early in pancreatic cancer, and [addiction services](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/addiction-and-substance-use.md) for the alcohol behind much of it.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cholelithiasis | Stones in the gallbladder, formed from supersaturated cholesterol or pigment |
| Biliary colic | Steady pain from the gallbladder contracting against an obstructing stone |
| Cholecystitis | Inflammation of an obstructed gallbladder |
| Cholangitis | Infection of an obstructed bile duct; a sepsis emergency |
| Obstructive jaundice | Jaundice from blocked bile flow with conjugated bilirubin, pale stools and dark urine |
| Autodigestion | Activation of pancreatic proteases inside the gland; the mechanism of acute pancreatitis |
| Pancreatic necrosis | Dead pancreatic tissue in severe pancreatitis, dangerous when infected |
| Exocrine insufficiency | Loss of enzyme output below the threshold for fat digestion |
| Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography | Endoscopic cannulation of the ampulla to clear stones or stent strictures |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Gallstones found on a scan should be removed."** Most are silent for life; cholecystectomy is for stones that have caused symptoms or complications.
- **"The amylase level tells you how bad the pancreatitis is."** It confirms the diagnosis; severity is judged from the systemic response (organ failure) and later from imaging of necrosis.
- **"Antibiotics treat pancreatitis."** Sterile inflammation does not respond to them; they are for proven infected necrosis or coexisting cholangitis.
