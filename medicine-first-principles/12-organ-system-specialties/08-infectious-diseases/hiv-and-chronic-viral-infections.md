# HIV and chronic viral infections

## Essence

Some viruses are never cleared. Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) integrates its genome into the host's helper T cells; hepatitis B persists as a stable circular DNA in hepatocytes; herpesviruses hide latent in neurons and lymphocytes; hepatitis C, which can be cured, outruns antibody by mutating. Chronic viral infection is therefore a problem of a reservoir the immune system cannot reach, and chronic disease comes from two sources: what the virus does directly (HIV destroys the cells that coordinate immunity) and what the host's response does over decades (hepatitis B and C cause cirrhosis and cancer through unending inflammation). The achievement of the last thirty years is that drugs can suppress replication indefinitely, turning HIV into a chronic condition with near-normal life expectancy and cure hepatitis C outright, provided the infection is found.

## Core tenets

1. **HIV causes disease by destroying the conductor of the immune orchestra.** The virus binds CD4 and a chemokine co-receptor, enters helper T cells and macrophages, reverse-transcribes its RNA into DNA and integrates it into the host genome ([viruses](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/viruses.md)). Infected CD4 cells die from viral cytotoxicity, immune killing and chronic activation; the count falls over years from about 500–1,500 cells/microlitre toward zero. Because CD4 cells license both antibody and cytotoxic responses ([T cells](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/t-cells.md)), the result is a global acquired immunodeficiency, and the opportunistic infections appear in a predictable order as the count crosses thresholds: tuberculosis and bacterial pneumonia early, *Pneumocystis* pneumonia and oesophageal candidiasis below about 200, cytomegalovirus and cryptococcal meningitis below about 50 ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)).

2. **The natural history has a long silent middle, which is why testing matters more than symptoms.** A flu-like seroconversion illness follows infection by weeks, then years of clinical latency during which the virus replicates at billions of particles a day and is transmissible, then the Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS) when the count is low. Most transmission occurs from people who do not know they are infected. Antibody and antigen tests are highly accurate; viral load by polymerase chain reaction measures replication and treatment response ([microbiological and serological diagnosis](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/microbiological-and-serological-diagnosis.md)).

3. **Combination antiretroviral therapy works because three drugs defeat an error-prone polymerase.** Reverse transcriptase makes about one error per genome per cycle, so every single-drug-resistant mutant exists within days ([genomic variation and mutation](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/genomic-variation-and-mutation.md)). The probability of a variant resistant to three drugs with different targets (reverse transcriptase, integrase, protease) arising at once is vanishingly small, so suppression is durable as long as adherence is maintained ([antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md)). Suppressed viral load lets CD4 cells recover and makes the person non-infectious ("undetectable equals untransmittable"), which turns treatment into prevention at population scale ([infectious disease dynamics](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/01-epidemiology/infectious-disease-dynamics.md)). The integrated provirus in resting memory cells is the reservoir that prevents cure.

4. **Hepatitis B and C injure the liver through the immune response, not the virus.** Both viruses are barely cytopathic. Cytotoxic T cells attacking infected hepatocytes cause the hepatitis; decades of repeated injury and repair produce fibrosis, cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma ([tissue repair and fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md), [liver disease](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md)). Hepatitis B infection in infancy is almost always chronic because the immature immune system tolerates it, while adult infection usually clears, which is why universal infant vaccination has cut liver cancer in countries that adopted it ([immunological memory and vaccination](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/immunological-memory-and-vaccination.md)). Hepatitis C has no vaccine but direct-acting antivirals cure over 95% of cases in weeks (2020s), the first cure of a chronic viral infection by drugs.

5. **Herpesviruses persist by latency and reactivate when surveillance lapses.** Herpes simplex and varicella-zoster hide in sensory ganglia; Epstein–Barr virus and cytomegalovirus in lymphocytes. The latent genome expresses almost nothing and is invisible to T cells; reactivation, as shingles along a dermatome ([segmental innervation and dermatomes](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)) or as cytomegalovirus disease after transplantation, is a marker of waning cellular immunity. Antiviral nucleoside analogues such as aciclovir act only on replicating virus, so they shorten episodes but cannot clear latency.

6. **Chronic viral infection is a cause of cancer.** Hepatitis B and C, human papillomavirus (cervical, anal and oropharyngeal carcinoma), Epstein–Barr virus (lymphomas, nasopharyngeal carcinoma) and human herpesvirus 8 (Kaposi sarcoma) together account for roughly one in ten cancers worldwide, more in low-income countries ([causes of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md)). The mechanism is either viral oncoproteins disabling tumour suppressors or chronic inflammation, and the prevention is vaccination and antiviral suppression rather than oncology.

7. **Who else is involved.** HIV care is now chronic-disease medicine shared with [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), with [cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md) and [endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/README.md) for the accelerated vascular and metabolic disease of long-term infection and treatment, and with [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) over stigma, depression and substance use that drive non-adherence. Hepatitis belongs jointly to [hepatology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md), which surveys for cirrhosis and cancer. [Obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) prevents mother-to-child transmission of HIV and hepatitis B; [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) and [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) manage the virus-driven cancers; [transplant medicine](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md) and [rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) must screen for hepatitis B before immunosuppression, which can reactivate it fatally. [Public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/global-health-and-pandemics.md) runs testing, needle exchange, pre-exposure prophylaxis and vaccination.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CD4 count | Number of helper T cells per microlitre; the measure of HIV-induced immunodeficiency |
| Viral load | Copies of viral RNA per millilitre of plasma; measures replication and treatment success |
| Opportunistic infection | Infection by an organism that healthy immunity controls, appearing when CD4 falls |
| Antiretroviral therapy | Combination of drugs against different HIV enzymes giving durable suppression |
| Reservoir | Latently infected cells carrying integrated provirus, invisible to drugs and immunity |
| Latency | Persistence of a viral genome with minimal gene expression, allowing reactivation |
| Seroconversion | Appearance of antibody after infection; the window before it is the gap in antibody testing |
| Chronic hepatitis | Persistent immune-mediated liver injury from hepatitis B or C |
| Direct-acting antiviral | Drug targeting a specific viral enzyme; curative in hepatitis C |
| Pre-exposure prophylaxis | Antiretroviral drugs taken by uninfected people to prevent HIV acquisition |

## Common misconceptions

- **"HIV kills by itself."** It kills by removing the defence against other organisms; nearly every death is from an opportunistic infection or a virus-driven cancer, and treatment prevents them by restoring CD4 cells.
- **"A chronic virus is a mild virus."** Hepatitis B and C are quiet for decades and then present as cirrhosis or liver cancer; the silence is the danger.
- **"Antivirals eradicate the virus."** Most suppress replication and leave latency untouched; hepatitis C cure is the exception because it has no latent form.
