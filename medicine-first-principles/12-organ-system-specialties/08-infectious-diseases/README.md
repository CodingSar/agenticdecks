## What this area is about

Infectious diseases is the specialty of the host's encounter with another organism. Like oncology, it is defined by a process rather than an organ, so it reaches into every other specialty: a pneumonia is pulmonology, a meningitis is neurology, an endocarditis is cardiology, and all three are infectious diseases. What the specialty owns is the organism's side of the encounter: which microbe, how it got there, what drug reaches it, how long for, and how to stop it reaching anyone else.

The unifying idea is that **infection is an interaction between a pathogen, a host and an environment, and that each of the three can be the target**. The pathogen is killed with antimicrobials, but it evolves. The host can be made resistant by vaccination or restored by treating its immunodeficiency, but its own inflammatory response does much of the damage. The environment, crowding, sanitation, hospital devices and antibiotic pressure, determines who is exposed, and changing it has prevented more infection than any drug. Every leaf here takes a different balance of the three.

```
   pathogen ------ antimicrobials (stewardship) ------> resistance (evolution)
       |
   host ---------- fever, sepsis (response as disease); HIV (response destroyed)
       |           TB, hepatitis (response as containment and as injury)
       |
```

Read fever and sepsis first: it establishes that the host response is the disease and that treatment starts before certainty. Stewardship is the discipline that must follow such empirical treatment. HIV and tuberculosis are the two great chronic infections and show, respectively, what happens when cellular immunity is lost and when it succeeds only in containment. Infection control closes the loop by returning to the environment, where the hospital itself is the reservoir.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md):** endocarditis is the infection of a damaged valve or device and needs weeks of bactericidal therapy and often surgery; rheumatic fever is an immune sequel of streptococcal infection.
- **[Pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md):** pneumonia, tuberculosis and bronchiectasis are shared; the lung is the commonest source of sepsis.
- **[Nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md):** urinary infection, the kidney as victim of sepsis and of nephrotoxic antimicrobials, and dosing by renal function.
- **[Gastroenterology and hepatology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md):** viral hepatitis, *Helicobacter pylori*, *C. difficile* colitis, infective diarrhoea and the microbiome.
- **[Endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/README.md):** diabetes impairs neutrophil function and perfusion, so infection is more frequent and more severe; adrenal failure complicates severe sepsis.
- **[Haematology](../06-haematology/README.md):** neutropenic sepsis, infection in asplenia, malaria as haemolysis, and the donor screening behind transfusion.
- **[Oncology](../07-oncology/README.md):** the immunosuppressed host, oncogenic viruses, and reactivation of latent infection under checkpoint and cytotoxic therapy.
- **[Rheumatology and clinical immunology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md):** septic arthritis versus inflammatory flare, infection as the complication of immunosuppression, and the primary immunodeficiencies that present as recurrent infection.
- **[Neurology](../10-neurology/README.md):** meningitis, encephalitis, brain abscess and the neurological sequelae of HIV and syphilis.
- **[Dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md):** cellulitis, necrotising fasciitis, skin manifestations of systemic infection and the skin as portal of entry.

## Interfaces with other specialties

Infectious diseases operates as a **consulting specialty**: most of its patients belong to someone else, and its job is to be called early. It is called by [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) for the septic patient whose source is unclear or whose organism is resistant; by [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and [orthopaedics](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md) for prosthetic-joint, vascular-graft and deep-space infections where the decision is whether the implant must come out; by [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) and [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) for neutropenic fever and prophylaxis during transplantation; and by [rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md), [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) and [dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md) before biologic immunosuppression, to screen for latent tuberculosis and hepatitis B.

**Border conditions** are shared by design. Endocarditis is managed by a team of infectious diseases, [cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md) and cardiac surgery, because the question of when to operate is answered jointly. Tuberculosis sits between infectious diseases, [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) and public health. HIV care has become chronic-disease medicine shared with [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) and [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md). [Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) has its own infectious-disease subspecialty because the organisms, the immune system and the vaccine schedule all differ in children; [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) shares the atypical presentation of infection in the old and the care-home reservoir of resistant organisms.

**Emergencies cross the boundary** from the first minute: sepsis is recognised in [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and primary care, meningitis is treated before lumbar puncture, necrotising fasciitis is a surgical emergency with an infectious name, and a returning traveller with fever is malaria until proven otherwise. In an epidemic, the specialty becomes the bridge between the ward and [public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/global-health-and-pandemics.md), running testing, isolation and the hospital's response.

## Connections across layers

The specialty rests on the whole of [microbiology and immunology](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/README.md): the organisms, [transmission](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/transmission-and-the-chain-of-infection.md), innate and adaptive defence, and [immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md). It takes [inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md), [granulomas](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md) and [sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md) from pathology, [antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md) and their pharmacokinetics from pharmacology, [resistance](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/host-pathogen-coevolution-and-resistance.md) from evolution, [infectious disease dynamics](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/01-epidemiology/infectious-disease-dynamics.md) and [surveillance](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/01-epidemiology/surveillance-and-outbreak-investigation.md) from epidemiology, and [microbiological diagnosis](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/microbiological-and-serological-diagnosis.md) from the laboratory. It gives upward the sepsis that [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md) supports, the prophylaxis and asepsis that [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) depends on, and the pandemic response and resistance problem that [global health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/global-health-and-pandemics.md) addresses.
