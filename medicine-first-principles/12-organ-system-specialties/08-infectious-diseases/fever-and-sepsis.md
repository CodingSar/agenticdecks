## Essence

Fever is a regulated rise in the temperature set point, triggered by cytokines, and is a sign of inflammation, not specifically of infection. Sepsis is what happens when the inflammatory response to infection escapes its local confines and injures the host's own organs; it is defined by organ dysfunction, not by the microbe or the fever. Two ideas organise this leaf: the host response, not the pathogen, does most of the damage, and time is the main modifiable variable, so the clinician acts on probability (cultures, antimicrobials, fluids) before the diagnosis is certain and narrows treatment as evidence arrives.

## Core tenets

1. **Fever is a set-point change, which is why it has chills before and sweats after.** Pyrogenic cytokines (interleukin-1, interleukin-6, tumour necrosis factor) from activated macrophages induce prostaglandin E2 in the hypothalamus, which raises the thermostat ([cytokines](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/cytokines-interferons-and-nk-cells.md), [thermoregulation](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/thermoregulation-and-energy-balance.md)). The body shivers and vasoconstricts to reach the new target, and sweats when the set point falls. Antipyretics such as paracetamol block prostaglandin synthesis and lower the set point; they do not treat the cause. Hyperthermia (heat stroke) is an uncontrolled rise with a normal set point and does not respond to them.

2. **Fever's differential is infection, inflammation, malignancy and drugs, and the pattern of host narrows it.** A short fever with localising symptoms is usually infection; a fever persisting beyond weeks without a source (pyrexia of unknown origin) is as often autoimmune disease, lymphoma or a drug reaction as an occult abscess or tuberculosis. The host matters more than the temperature: neutropenia, Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection, a prosthetic valve or recent travel each change the likely organisms and the urgency ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)).

3. **Sepsis is a dysregulated host response causing organ dysfunction; the microbe is the trigger, not the mechanism.** Pattern-recognition receptors detect microbial molecules and launch the inflammatory cascade ([barriers and pattern recognition](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md)). Locally this contains infection; systemically it dilates vessels, leaks capillaries, activates coagulation and depresses the heart ([haemorrhage, shock and sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md)). The result is distributive shock: blood is not where it is needed, tissues are hypoperfused, lactate rises and organs fail. The current definition (Sepsis-3, 2016) is infection plus an acute rise in organ-dysfunction score; septic shock adds vasopressor dependence and raised lactate despite fluid.

4. **The signs are the physiology of maldistribution and compensation.** Tachycardia and tachypnoea are early compensation for falling stroke volume and rising acid ([hemodynamics](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md), [control of breathing](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/control-of-breathing.md)); warm peripheries reflect vasodilatation; confusion, oliguria and rising lactate reflect under-perfused brain, kidney and muscle. Hypotension is late because sympathetic compensation holds pressure until it fails ([compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation)), which is why bedside scores use respiratory rate, blood pressure and mental state.

5. **Tests confirm infection and measure the damage; neither is allowed to delay treatment.** Blood cultures before antibiotics identify the organism and its sensitivities in a day or two and are the only way to narrow treatment later ([microbiological diagnosis](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/microbiological-and-serological-diagnosis.md)). Lactate, creatinine, bilirubin and platelets measure organ dysfunction; C-reactive protein rises with inflammation of any cause; imaging finds the source. Because culture takes longer than the disease, initial treatment is empirical, informed by the likely source and local resistance, and corrected as data arrive.

6. **Treatment removes the trigger and supports the circulation until the response subsides.** Antimicrobials chosen to cover the probable organisms kill the trigger ([antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md)); draining an abscess or removing an infected line is source control, without which antibiotics fail because they cannot penetrate pus or biofilm. Fluid refills the leaking circulation; vasopressors such as noradrenaline restore tone ([autonomic and cardiovascular drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/autonomic-and-cardiovascular-drugs.md)); organ support buys time. None treats the inflammation itself; decades of anti-cytokine trials failed, because by the time shock is present the cascade is too redundant to block at one node ([redundancy and reserve](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#redundancy-and-reserve)).

7. **Who else is involved.** Sepsis is the paradigm cross-boundary emergency: it presents to [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), is managed by [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) once vasopressors are needed, needs [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) or [radiology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/README.md) for source control, and injures kidney ([nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md)), lung ([pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md)) and clotting ([haematology](../06-haematology/README.md)). Neutropenic fever is shared with [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md); neonatal sepsis with [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md); maternal sepsis with [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md). Infectious diseases physicians own the choice and narrowing of the antimicrobial.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pyrogen | Molecule (microbial or cytokine) that raises the hypothalamic set point |
| Sepsis | Life-threatening organ dysfunction from a dysregulated host response to infection |
| Septic shock | Sepsis with vasopressor requirement and raised lactate despite fluid |
| Distributive shock | Hypoperfusion from vasodilatation and capillary leak with a normal or high cardiac output |
| Empirical therapy | Treatment chosen on probability before the organism is known |
| Source control | Physical removal or drainage of the infectious focus |
| Neutropenic fever | Fever with low neutrophils; treated as sepsis because the usual signs are absent |

## Common misconceptions

- **"No fever, no sepsis."** The old, the immunosuppressed and the very sick may be hypothermic or afebrile; sepsis is defined by organ dysfunction.
- **"Sepsis means bacteria in the blood."** Most septic patients have negative blood cultures; the response, not bacteraemia, defines the syndrome, and viral and fungal infections cause it too.
- **"Lowering the fever treats the patient."** Antipyretics improve comfort; they do not change the course of the infection, and the fever itself may aid host defence.
