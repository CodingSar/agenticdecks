## Essence

Tuberculosis, malaria and the helminths are the infections of poverty, and together they kill or disable more people than any other group of pathogens outside pandemics. They share three features that make them different from the acute bacterial infections of the rich world: they persist for months or years inside a host whose immune response contains but cannot clear them, their burden tracks crowding, nutrition and sanitation as closely as any microbe, and their treatment requires drug combinations over long periods, which fail through non-adherence and breed resistance. Tuberculosis is the model: a bacterium walled off in a granuloma, waiting for immunity to lapse, infecting about a quarter of humanity and killing over a million a year (2020s), nearly all of them preventable.

## Core tenets

1. **Tuberculosis is a disease of the granuloma, which is the host's compromise with a bacterium it cannot kill.** *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* has a waxy mycolic-acid wall that resists phagocyte killing and divides once a day rather than every twenty minutes ([bacteria](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/bacteria.md)). Macrophages ingest it and fail; T-helper cells then wall the infected macrophages into a granuloma with central caseous necrosis ([chronic inflammation and granulomas](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md)). In most people this contains the infection for life (latent tuberculosis); in about 5–10% it reactivates when cellular immunity wanes with age, malnutrition, HIV, diabetes or anti-tumour-necrosis-factor drugs. Disease is therefore a failure of containment, not a new exposure.

2. **The clinical picture is slow destruction plus the systemic cost of chronic inflammation.** Reactivation favours the lung apices, where oxygen tension is highest; cavities form as caseous material erodes into airways, producing the chronic cough and haemoptysis that also make the patient infectious ([ventilation-perfusion matching](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/ventilation-perfusion-matching.md)). Fever, night sweats and weight loss are the cytokine burden of months of granulomatous inflammation. Blood-borne spread seeds bone, kidney, meninges and, in the immunosuppressed, everywhere at once (miliary disease). Diagnosis is by seeing or growing the organism (microscopy, culture over weeks, or rapid nucleic-acid amplification that also detects rifampicin resistance), while interferon-gamma release assays and skin tests detect only the immune memory of latent infection ([microbiological diagnosis](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/microbiological-and-serological-diagnosis.md)).

3. **Treatment is long and combined because the bacterium is slow and hides in three niches.** Actively dividing bacilli in cavities, intermittently dividing bacilli inside macrophages and dormant bacilli in caseum each respond to different drugs, so combinations of four agents over months are needed to sterilise all three ([antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md)). Monotherapy or interrupted treatment selects resistant mutants, and multidrug-resistant tuberculosis is the consequence of poorly supervised programmes rather than of the organism. Directly observed treatment exists because adherence over months is the limiting step, not drug efficacy ([antimicrobial stewardship](antimicrobial-stewardship.md)).

4. **Malaria is a red-cell parasite whose life cycle dictates both its clinical course and its control.** *Plasmodium* sporozoites injected by an *Anopheles* mosquito multiply in the liver, then invade red cells, where cycles of replication and rupture produce the periodic fevers; *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* leave dormant liver forms that relapse ([fungi, protozoa and helminths](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/fungi-protozoa-and-helminths.md)). *P. falciparum* makes infected cells sticky, so they sequester in small vessels of brain, kidney and placenta: cerebral malaria, renal failure, severe anaemia and acidosis are the mechanisms of death, mostly in African children under five. Sickle-cell trait and other red-cell variants persist in the population because they protect against severe malaria, the clearest case of balancing selection in humans ([natural selection](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/natural-selection-and-population-genetics.md)). Control attacks every stage: nets and insecticide against the vector, artemisinin combinations against the blood stage, and now partially effective vaccines.

5. **Helminths and other neglected tropical diseases cause disability more than death, through chronic burden.** Hookworm causes iron-deficiency anaemia by feeding on gut blood; schistosomiasis causes liver fibrosis and bladder cancer through the granulomatous response to eggs; filariasis blocks lymphatics; onchocerciasis blinds. The immune response to large multicellular parasites is the immunoglobulin E and eosinophil arm ([hypersensitivity and allergy](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)), so eosinophilia in a traveller or migrant points to helminths. Mass drug administration of cheap anthelmintics to whole communities, combined with sanitation, is the effective intervention; the diseases persist where sanitation does not.

6. **These are diseases of place and poverty, so geography and social history are diagnostic tools.** Fever in a traveller returning from sub-Saharan Africa is malaria until a blood film or antigen test says otherwise, because the disease kills within days. The incubation period narrows the differential: dengue and chikungunya within two weeks, typhoid and malaria within weeks to months, tuberculosis and some helminths years later ([transmission and the chain of infection](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/transmission-and-the-chain-of-infection.md), [time-courses](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#time-courses)). Crowding transmits tuberculosis; standing water breeds mosquitoes; absent latrines transmit worms. Where incomes rise and housing improves, tuberculosis incidence fell long before effective drugs existed, which is why these are counted among the [social determinants of health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/social-determinants-and-health-inequity.md).

7. **Who else is involved.** Pulmonary tuberculosis is shared with [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md); tuberculous meningitis with [neurology](../10-neurology/README.md); spinal and joint disease with [orthopaedics](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md). [Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md), [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) and [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) must screen for latent infection before immunosuppression. [HIV](hiv-and-chronic-viral-infections.md) co-infection is the strongest risk factor for reactivation and is managed jointly. Severe malaria is a [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) and [paediatric](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) emergency; malaria and tuberculosis in pregnancy belong with [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md). [Public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/global-health-and-pandemics.md) owns contact tracing, vector control, vaccination and mass drug administration, which matter more than any clinic.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Granuloma | Organised collection of macrophages and lymphocytes walling off a persistent pathogen |
| Caseous necrosis | Cheese-like dead tissue at the centre of a tuberculous granuloma |
| Latent tuberculosis | Contained infection with immune memory but no disease or infectivity |
| Reactivation | Breakdown of containment when cellular immunity wanes |
| Multidrug-resistant tuberculosis | Resistance to at least rifampicin and isoniazid, from inadequate treatment |
| Sequestration | Adhesion of *P. falciparum*-infected red cells to small-vessel endothelium |
| Hypnozoite | Dormant liver stage of *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* that causes relapse |
| Eosinophilia | Raised eosinophil count; the signature of helminth infection and allergy |
| Neglected tropical diseases | Chronic parasitic and bacterial infections of poverty with little research investment |
| Incubation period | Interval from exposure to symptoms; narrows the differential in a traveller |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A positive tuberculin or interferon test means tuberculosis."** It means immune memory of the organism; most positives are latent infection and will never develop disease.
- **"Tropical infections stay in the tropics."** Migration, travel and a warming climate bring them everywhere, and a fever within a year of travel is a tropical fever until excluded.
- **"Tuberculosis was conquered by antibiotics."** Mortality in Europe fell by most of its total before streptomycin (1944), through housing, nutrition and isolation; drugs finished what living conditions began.
