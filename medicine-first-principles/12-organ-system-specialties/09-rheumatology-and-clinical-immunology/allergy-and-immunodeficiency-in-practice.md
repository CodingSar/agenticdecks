## Essence

Clinical immunology sees the immune system fail in two opposite directions: too much response to something harmless (allergy) and too little to something dangerous (immunodeficiency). Both are increasingly common, allergy because something in the modern environment biases the developing immune system toward immunoglobulin E (IgE) responses, and immunodeficiency because medicine now keeps alive patients with inherited defects and deliberately suppresses immunity in millions more. The practical skill in allergy is to separate true IgE-mediated reactions, which can kill within minutes and respond to adrenaline, from the far larger number of intolerances and non-allergic reactions that get called allergy. The practical skill in immunodeficiency is to recognise the pattern of infection that points to a missing component, because each arm of the immune system defends against a characteristic set of organisms.

## Core tenets

1. **Allergy is an IgE response to a harmless protein, and its speed is the clue.** Sensitisation produces IgE bound to mast cells; re-exposure cross-links it and releases histamine, tryptase and leukotrienes within minutes, causing urticaria, angio-oedema, bronchospasm, vomiting and, when systemic, vasodilatation and capillary leak ([hypersensitivity and allergy](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md), [B cells and antibodies](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/b-cells-and-antibodies.md)). A reaction beginning more than an hor two after exposure, or lasting days, is almost never IgE-mediated. The history of timing, reproducibility and the organ affected is the main diagnostic tool; skin-prick and specific-IgE tests confirm sensitisation but, since many sensitised people never react, a positive test without a matching history is not allergy ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md)).

2. **Anaphylaxis is distributive shock plus airway obstruction, and adrenaline reverses both by mechanism.** Massive mediator release dilates vessels and leaks plasma (hypotension), constricts bronchi and swells the upper airway. Adrenaline's alpha-1 action constricts vessels and reduces oedema, its beta-2 action dilates bronchi, and its beta-2 action on mast cells reduces further release ([autonomic and cardiovascular drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/autonomic-and-cardiovascular-drugs.md)). Antihistamines and glucocorticoids act too slowly to treat the shock; they treat the skin and may blunt a late phase. Death comes from asphyxia or cardiovascular collapse within minutes to an hour, which is why patients carry adrenaline and why delay is the commonest fatal error.

3. **The allergic diseases are one tendency expressed in different organs across the life course.** Atopy, the inherited tendency to make IgE to environmental antigens, appears as eczema in infancy, food allergy in early childhood, asthma in later childhood and rhinitis thereafter, the "atopic march". A defective skin barrier (filaggrin mutations) lets antigens sensitise through inflamed skin, while early oral exposure induces tolerance; this is why early introduction of peanut reduced peanut allergy in trials and reversed decades of avoidance advice ([tolerance](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/tolerance-and-self-recognition.md), [the skin as barrier](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/the-skin-as-barrier-and-sensor.md)). The rise of allergy with urban living and reduced microbial exposure in early life, the hygiene or "old friends" hypothesis, places allergy among the diseases of [the host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment).

4. **Most "drug allergies" are not, and the label does harm.** Under one in ten patients labelled penicillin-allergic have IgE-mediated allergy on testing; most had a viral rash, a side effect or a reaction that has waned. The label leads to broader, more toxic and more expensive antibiotics and more resistant infection ([adverse drug reactions](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md), [antimicrobial stewardship](../08-infectious-diseases/antimicrobial-stewardship.md)). Delabelling by history and supervised challenge is one of the highest-value acts in clinical immunology. True severe drug reactions include not only anaphylaxis but T-cell-mediated syndromes days to weeks later (Stevens–Johnson syndrome, drug reaction with eosinophilia), which are not IgE and never rechallenged ([skin lesion morphology](../11-dermatology/skin-lesion-morphology-and-diagnosis.md)).

5. **The pattern of infection names the missing component.** Antibody deficiency (the commonest primary immunodeficiency, such as common variable immunodeficiency) gives recurrent bacterial sinus, ear and lung infection with encapsulated organisms, and bronchiectasis if missed. T-cell deficiency gives viral, fungal and opportunistic infection, as in HIV. Neutrophil defects give abscesses and invasive fungal disease; complement deficiencies give recurrent meningococcal infection; splenectomy gives overwhelming sepsis with encapsulated bacteria ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md), [complement and phagocytes](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/complement-phagocytes-and-the-inflammatory-cascade.md)). The clue is infections that are unusually frequent, severe, persistent or caused by unusual organisms; immunoglobulin levels, vaccine responses and lymphocyte subsets then localise the defect.

6. **Secondary immunodeficiency is now the common kind, and it is prescribed.** Glucocorticoids, cytotoxics, biologics against tumour necrosis factor, interleukins or B cells, transplant drugs and chemotherapy each remove a known component, so the infections they invite are predictable: tuberculosis reactivation with anti-tumour-necrosis-factor drugs, hepatitis B reactivation and poor vaccine response with B-cell depletion, *Pneumocystis* with prolonged steroids ([anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anti-inflammatory-and-immunomodulatory-drugs.md)). Screening before starting, prophylaxis during, and vaccination before immunosuppression (live vaccines are contraindicated after) follow from knowing which component is lost ([immunological memory and vaccination](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/immunological-memory-and-vaccination.md)).

7. **Treatment replaces what is missing or re-educates what over-reacts.** Immunoglobulin replacement from pooled donors prevents the infections and lung damage of antibody deficiency; stem-cell transplantation and gene therapy cure some severe combined immunodeficiencies in infancy. Allergen immunotherapy, repeated controlled exposure, shifts the response from IgE toward blocking antibodies and regulatory T cells, and is the only treatment that changes the course of allergic rhinitis and venom allergy; biologics against IgE or the interleukins 4, 5 and 13 now treat severe asthma, eczema and urticaria by removing one mediator ([biologics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** Anaphylaxis is managed by [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md), where neuromuscular blockers and antibiotics are frequent causes; asthma with [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md); eczema and urticaria with [dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md); food allergy and primary immunodeficiency with [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md), where they usually present; rhinitis with [ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md). Immunodeficiency is shared with [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md), which treats the infections, and with every specialty that prescribes immunosuppression: [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md), [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md), [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md), [nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md) and dermatology.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atopy | Inherited tendency to produce IgE against common environmental antigens |
| Sensitisation | Presence of specific IgE without necessarily any clinical reaction |
| Anaphylaxis | Rapid systemic IgE-mediated reaction with airway, breathing or circulatory compromise |
| Mast cell | Tissue cell carrying IgE whose degranulation releases histamine and other mediators |
| Tryptase | Mast-cell enzyme measured in blood to confirm anaphylaxis after the event |
| Atopic march | Progression from eczema to food allergy, asthma and rhinitis through childhood |
| Drug allergy delabelling | Removing an unfounded allergy label by history and supervised challenge |
| Primary immunodeficiency | Inherited defect in an immune component |
| Secondary immunodeficiency | Acquired loss of immunity from disease or treatment |
| Allergen immunotherapy | Graded repeated exposure that induces tolerance to an allergen |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A positive allergy test proves allergy."** It proves sensitisation; many sensitised people eat the food or tolerate the drug without reaction, and the history decides.
- **"Antihistamines treat anaphylaxis."** They treat itch and urticaria; only adrenaline reverses the shock and airway swelling, and delay in giving it is the usual cause of death.
- **"Recurrent colds mean immunodeficiency."** Children normally have many viral infections a year; immunodeficiency is suggested by bacterial infections that are severe, deep, persistent or caused by unusual organisms.
