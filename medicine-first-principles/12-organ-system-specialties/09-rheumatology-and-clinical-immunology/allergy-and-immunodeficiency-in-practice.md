## Essence

Clinical immunology sees the immune system fail in two opposite directions: too much response to the harmless (allergy) and too little to the dangerous (immunodeficiency). Both are increasingly common: allergy because the modern environment biases the developing immune system toward immunoglobulin E (IgE) responses, immunodeficiency because medicine deliberately suppresses immunity in millions. The skill in allergy is to separate true IgE-mediated reactions, which can kill within minutes and respond to adrenaline, from the far larger number of reactions that get called allergy. The skill in immunodeficiency is to recognise the pattern of infection that points to a missing component, because each arm of immunity defends against a characteristic set of organisms.

## Core tenets

1. **Allergy is an IgE response to a harmless protein, and its speed is the clue.** Sensitisation produces IgE bound to mast cells; re-exposure cross-links it and releases histamine, tryptase and leukotrienes within minutes, causing urticaria, angio-oedema, bronchospasm, vomiting and, when systemic, vasodilatation and capillary leak ([hypersensitivity and allergy](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md), [B cells and antibodies](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/b-cells-and-antibodies.md)). A reaction beginning more than an hour or two after exposure is almost never IgE-mediated. The history is the main diagnostic tool; skin-prick and specific-IgE tests confirm sensitisation, but since many sensitised people never react, a positive test without a matching history is not allergy ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md)).

2. **Anaphylaxis is distributive shock plus airway obstruction, and adrenaline reverses both by mechanism.** Massive mediator release dilates vessels and leaks plasma (hypotension), constricts bronchi and swells the upper airway. Adrenaline's alpha-1 action constricts vessels and reduces oedema, its beta-2 action dilates bronchi, and its beta-2 action on mast cells reduces further release ([autonomic and cardiovascular drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/autonomic-and-cardiovascular-drugs.md)). Antihistamines and glucocorticoids act too slowly to treat the shock. Death comes from asphyxia or collapse within minutes to an hour, which is why patients carry adrenaline and why delay is the commonest fatal error.

3. **The allergic diseases are one tendency expressed in different organs across the life course.** Atopy, the inherited tendency to make IgE to environmental antigens, appears as eczema in infancy, then food allergy, asthma and rhinitis, the "atopic march". A defective skin barrier lets antigens sensitise through inflamed skin, while early oral exposure induces tolerance; this is why early introduction of peanut reduced peanut allergy in trials and reversed decades of avoidance advice ([tolerance](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/tolerance-and-self-recognition.md), [the skin as barrier](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/the-skin-as-barrier-and-sensor.md)). The rise of allergy with urban living and reduced early microbial exposure (the "old friends" hypothesis) places it among the diseases of [the host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment).

4. **Most "drug allergies" are not, and the label does harm.** Under one in ten patients labelled penicillin-allergic have IgE-mediated allergy on testing; most had a viral rash, a side effect or a reaction that has waned. The label leads to broader, more toxic and more expensive antibiotics and more resistant infection ([adverse drug reactions](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md), [antimicrobial stewardship](../08-infectious-diseases/antimicrobial-stewardship.md)). Delabelling by history and supervised challenge is one of the highest-value acts in clinical immunology. True severe reactions also include T-cell-mediated syndromes days to weeks later (Stevens–Johnson syndrome), which are not IgE and are never rechallenged ([skin lesion morphology](../11-dermatology/skin-lesion-morphology-and-diagnosis.md)).

5. **The pattern of infection names the missing component.** Antibody deficiency, the commonest primary form, gives recurrent sinus, ear and lung infection with encapsulated bacteria, and bronchiectasis if missed. T-cell deficiency gives viral, fungal and opportunistic infection, as in HIV. Neutrophil defects give abscesses and invasive fungal disease; complement deficiencies, meningococcal infection; splenectomy, overwhelming sepsis with encapsulated bacteria ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md), [complement and phagocytes](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/complement-phagocytes-and-the-inflammatory-cascade.md)). Immunoglobulin levels, vaccine responses and lymphocyte subsets then localise the defect.

6. **Secondary immunodeficiency is now the common kind, and it is prescribed.** Glucocorticoids, cytotoxics, biologics and transplant drugs each remove a known component, so the infections they invite are predictable: tuberculosis reactivation with anti-tumour-necrosis-factor drugs, hepatitis B reactivation with B-cell depletion, *Pneumocystis* with prolonged steroids ([anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anti-inflammatory-and-immunomodulatory-drugs.md)). Screening before starting, prophylaxis during, and vaccination before immunosuppression (live vaccines are contraindicated after) follow from knowing which component is lost ([immunological memory and vaccination](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/immunological-memory-and-vaccination.md)).

7. **Treatment replaces what is missing or re-educates what over-reacts.** Pooled immunoglobulin prevents the infections of antibody deficiency; stem-cell transplantation and gene therapy cure some severe combined immunodeficiencies. Allergen immunotherapy, repeated controlled exposure, shifts the response from IgE toward blocking antibodies and regulatory T cells, and is the only treatment that changes the course of rhinitis and venom allergy; biologics against IgE or interleukins 4, 5 and 13 treat severe asthma, eczema and urticaria by removing one mediator ([biologics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** Anaphylaxis is managed by [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md), where relaxants and antibiotics are frequent causes; asthma with [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md); eczema and urticaria with [dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md); food allergy and primary immunodeficiency with [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md); rhinitis with [ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md). Immunodeficiency is shared with [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) and with every specialty that prescribes immunosuppression: [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md), [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md), [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md), [nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atopy | Inherited tendency to produce IgE against common environmental antigens |
| Sensitisation | Presence of specific IgE without necessarily any clinical reaction |
| Anaphylaxis | Rapid systemic IgE-mediated reaction with airway, breathing or circulatory compromise |
| Mast cell | Tissue cell carrying IgE whose degranulation releases histamine and other mediators |
| Atopic march | Progression from eczema to food allergy, asthma and rhinitis through childhood |
| Drug allergy delabelling | Removing an unfounded allergy label by history and supervised challenge |
| Primary immunodeficiency | Inherited defect in an immune component |
| Allergen immunotherapy | Graded repeated exposure that induces tolerance to an allergen |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A positive allergy test proves allergy."** It proves sensitisation; many sensitised people eat the food or tolerate the drug without reaction, and the history decides.
- **"Antihistamines treat anaphylaxis."** They treat itch and urticaria; only adrenaline reverses the shock and airway swelling, and delay in giving it is the usual cause of death.
