# Connective tissue disease and vasculitis

## Essence

The connective tissue diseases and the vasculitides are systemic autoimmunity aimed at targets present in every organ: nuclear components, the endothelium, the vessel wall, the fibroblast. Because the target is everywhere, the disease is everywhere, and these conditions present as fever, rash, arthritis, kidney failure, lung fibrosis, stroke or blindness in any combination. Systemic lupus erythematosus is the archetype of antibody- and immune-complex-mediated injury; systemic sclerosis of uncontrolled fibrosis; the vasculitides of inflammation of vessel walls, classified by the calibre of vessel attacked. The unifying clinical skill is pattern recognition across organs, and the unifying therapeutic logic is that immunosuppression must be matched to the organ at risk, because the drugs that save a kidney or an eye also kill through infection.

## Core tenets

1. **Lupus is a disease of failed clearance and misdirected antibody.** Apoptotic cells that are not cleared promptly expose nuclear antigens (DNA, histones, ribonucleoproteins); in a susceptible host, these are taken up by dendritic cells, drive interferon production and break B-cell tolerance, yielding antinuclear antibodies ([tolerance and self-recognition](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/tolerance-and-self-recognition.md), [cell death](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/cell-death-apoptosis-necrosis-and-autophagy.md)). Antibody–antigen complexes deposit in glomeruli, skin and vessels and activate complement, a type III hypersensitivity reaction ([hypersensitivity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)). Low complement and high anti-double-stranded-DNA antibody therefore track disease activity. Ultraviolet light, which increases keratinocyte apoptosis, triggers flares and explains the photosensitive rash.

2. **The organs attacked are the ones that filter immune complexes or express the antigen.** Lupus nephritis is complex deposition in the glomerulus ([glomerular diseases](../03-nephrology/glomerular-and-tubular-diseases.md)); it is often silent, so urine is examined at every visit. Antiphospholipid antibodies bind phospholipid-binding proteins on endothelium and platelets and cause thrombosis and recurrent miscarriage. Cytopenias arise from antibodies against blood cells ([anaemias](../06-haematology/anaemias.md)). Nine in ten patients are women, mostly of reproductive age, pointing to oestrogen and X-chromosome gene dosage as susceptibility factors.

3. **Systemic sclerosis is vascular injury that heals by fibrosis and never stops.** Endothelial damage to small arteries produces Raynaud's phenomenon and digital ulcers; the response is an exaggerated fibroblast activation driven by transforming growth factor beta, depositing collagen in skin, lung, gut and kidney ([tissue repair and fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md), [injury and repair](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#injury-and-repair)). Death comes from interstitial lung disease and pulmonary arterial hypertension ([interstitial lung disease](../02-pulmonology/interstitial-and-restrictive-lung-disease.md), [pulmonary vascular disease](../02-pulmonology/pulmonary-vascular-disease-and-respiratory-failure.md)). Immunosuppression helps the inflammation but not the established fibrosis, which is why early lung screening by spirometry and high-resolution computed tomography matters.

4. **Vasculitis is classified by the calibre of vessel, and calibre predicts the clinical picture.** Large-vessel vasculitis (giant cell arteritis, Takayasu) inflames the aorta and its branches, causing headache, jaw claudication and sudden blindness from ophthalmic artery occlusion in the old. Medium-vessel disease (polyarteritis nodosa, Kawasaki) infarcts organs and forms aneurysms. Small-vessel vasculitis, associated with anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA), destroys glomeruli (crescentic glomerulonephritis) and alveolar capillaries (pulmonary haemorrhage), and produces palpable purpura in the skin ([atherosclerosis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md) is the contrasting non-inflammatory vessel disease). The pulmonary–renal syndrome of breathlessness, haemoptysis and rising creatinine is a medical emergency because untreated it destroys both organs within days.

5. **ANCA causes disease by arming the neutrophil against the vessel wall.** The antibodies bind myeloperoxidase or proteinase 3 on the surface of primed neutrophils, activating them to degranulate against endothelium ([complement, phagocytes and the inflammatory cascade](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/complement-phagocytes-and-the-inflammatory-cascade.md)). ANCA is both a marker and a mechanism, and B-cell depletion with rituximab, which removes the source, induces remission as well as cyclophosphamide with less toxicity.

6. **Investigation is autoantibodies plus biopsy, and biopsy is what the drugs are chosen on.** Antinuclear antibody is sensitive for lupus but positive in many healthy people, so it is a screening test interpreted against pre-test probability ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md)); specific antibodies (anti-double-stranded DNA, anti-Sm, anti-centromere, anti-Scl-70, ANCA) narrow the diagnosis. Tissue (kidney, temporal artery, skin) shows the pattern and the activity of injury and whether it is reversible inflammation or fixed scarring ([histopathology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/histopathology-cytology-and-molecular-diagnostics.md)), which determines how hard to treat.

7. **Treatment is matched to the organ at risk: induce remission, then maintain it with the least poison.** Glucocorticoids act within hours and save sight and kidneys, but their chronic cost (infection, osteoporosis, diabetes, cataract) is now the main cause of long-term harm in these diseases. Induction uses cyclophosphamide or rituximab for organ-threatening disease; maintenance uses antimetabolites such as azathioprine or mycophenolate; hydroxychloroquine reduces lupus flares with little toxicity ([anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anti-inflammatory-and-immunomodulatory-drugs.md)). Every escalation is a trade of organ damage against infection ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)).

8. **Who else is involved.** These are the most multidisciplinary diseases in medicine. [Nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md) co-manages lupus nephritis and ANCA glomerulonephritis and performs the biopsy; [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) the lung fibrosis and pulmonary hypertension; [dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md) sees the rash and purpura first and biopsies the skin; [ophthalmology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) treats uveitis, scleritis and the sight-threatening ischaemia of giant cell arteritis; [neurology](../10-neurology/README.md) the strokes and neuropathies; [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) the high-risk pregnancies of lupus and antiphospholipid syndrome; [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) the cytopenias and anticoagulation; [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) the complications of immunosuppression; and [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) the pulmonary–renal syndrome.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Antinuclear antibody | Antibody against nuclear components; sensitive screen for lupus and related diseases |
| Immune complex | Antibody bound to antigen; deposits in tissue and activates complement (type III hypersensitivity) |
| Lupus nephritis | Immune-complex glomerulonephritis of systemic lupus erythematosus |
| Antiphospholipid syndrome | Autoantibodies causing thrombosis and pregnancy loss |
| Raynaud's phenomenon | Episodic digital vasospasm; the first sign of systemic sclerosis |
| ANCA | Anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody; marker and mechanism of small-vessel vasculitis |
| Giant cell arteritis | Large-vessel vasculitis of the old threatening sight; treated before biopsy |
| Pulmonary–renal syndrome | Simultaneous alveolar haemorrhage and glomerulonephritis from small-vessel vasculitis |
| Induction and maintenance | Intense therapy to achieve remission, then lower-toxicity therapy to hold it |
| Damage versus activity | Fixed scarring that drugs cannot reverse versus active inflammation that they can |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A positive antinuclear antibody means lupus."** It is found in 5–15% of healthy people, more with age; without clinical features it means little.
- **"Vasculitis is rare, so think of it last."** Giant cell arteritis is the commonest vasculitis of the old and blinds within hours of the first visual symptom; it is treated on suspicion.
- **"Steroids are the treatment."** They are the bridge; the aim of modern therapy is to minimise their cumulative dose because they now cause more long-term harm than the disease.
