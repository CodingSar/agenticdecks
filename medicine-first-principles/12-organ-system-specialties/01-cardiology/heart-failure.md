## Essence

Heart failure is not a disease but a syndrome: the heart cannot deliver the output the body needs at normal filling pressures, and the body's response to that shortfall becomes the main driver of the illness. The kidney and the sympathetic system read low output as haemorrhage and respond as evolution taught them, by retaining salt and water and constricting arteries. That response is life-saving after blood loss and lethal when the problem is a weak pump: it raises filling pressures (congestion, breathlessness, oedema), raises the load the pump must work against, and drives remodelling that weakens the muscle further. Nearly every treatment that prolongs life in heart failure works by *blocking a compensatory reflex*, which is the single idea that makes the pharmacology intelligible.

## Core tenets

1. **Failure begins as an inability to maintain output at normal filling pressure, from any cause.** The [Frank–Starling relation](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/the-cardiac-cycle-and-pump-function.md) lets a healthy ventricle raise stroke volume by filling more. A damaged ventricle (after [infarction](ischemic-heart-disease.md), from long-standing [hypertension](hypertension-and-vascular-disease.md), a [leaking valve](valvular-and-structural-heart-disease.md), toxins, or a stiff wall that cannot relax) sits on a flattened curve: it needs a higher filling pressure for the same output. That raised pressure is transmitted back into the lungs (left side) or the systemic veins (right side).

2. **Reduced and preserved ejection fraction are two mechanisms with the same symptoms.** When the muscle is weak, the fraction of end-diastolic volume ejected falls below about 40% and the chamber dilates; this is heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. When the muscle is stiff and thick (hypertension, ageing, diabetes), it empties normally but fills only at high pressure; this is heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. The patient is equally breathless, but the drugs that prolong life in the first group mostly do not in the second, because they act on the dilated, over-stimulated ventricle rather than on stiffness. Roughly half of heart failure in older populations is the preserved-fraction kind (high-income countries, 2020s).

3. **The neurohormonal response converts a pump problem into a volume problem.** Low output and low arterial pressure unload the baroreceptors; sympathetic drive rises, and reduced renal perfusion releases renin. Angiotensin II constricts arterioles and stimulates aldosterone, which [retains sodium](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/sodium-water-and-volume-regulation.md); antidiuretic hormone retains water. The expanded blood volume raises venous and capillary hydrostatic pressure, and fluid leaves the capillaries by the [Starling mechanism](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/edema-and-fluid-imbalance.md). The reflex has positive feedback built in: the retained fluid raises the load, which lowers output, which drives more retention.

4. **Symptoms and signs are congestion behind the failing side and low output in front of it.** Left-sided congestion fills the lung interstitium and alveoli: breathlessness on exertion, then on lying flat (orthopnoea, because venous return rises), then at night (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea), with crackles at the lung bases. Right-sided congestion raises the jugular venous pressure, enlarges the liver and produces pitting ankle oedema. Low output gives fatigue, cool peripheries, and a narrow pulse pressure; the sympathetic surge gives tachycardia. A third heart sound is the sound of rapid filling into a stiff, overloaded ventricle.

5. **Tests measure the stretch and the structure.** Myocytes stretched by raised filling pressure secrete natriuretic peptides; a plasma B-type natriuretic peptide level is therefore a direct chemical readout of wall stress, high in failure and low enough in health to exclude it in a breathless patient ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md)). [Echocardiography](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md) measures ejection fraction, chamber size, wall thickness and valve function, and so distinguishes the mechanisms in tenet 2. The chest radiograph shows the enlarged heart and the fluid in the lungs; the Electrocardiogram (ECG) shows the cause (old infarct, hypertrophy) or the complication (atrial fibrillation).

6. **Life-prolonging drugs block the compensation; diuretics relieve the congestion.** Angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers, beta-blockers (bisoprolol, carvedilol) and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (spironolactone) each cut one limb of the neurohormonal loop and each lowers mortality in reduced-fraction failure in [randomised trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md). Sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, developed for diabetes, lower hospitalisation and death in both fraction groups by mechanisms still debated. Loop diuretics make the patient feel better by removing retained fluid but do not change the course of the disease. A beta-blocker in a failing heart seems paradoxical, since it lowers contractility; it works because chronic sympathetic drive is toxic to myocytes and drives remodelling, and because it slows the heart and lengthens filling.

7. **Devices and surgery treat what drugs cannot.** A ventricle with a wide QRS complex contracts unevenly; pacing both ventricles resynchronises it. An implantable defibrillator pre-empts the [arrhythmic death](arrhythmias.md) that accounts for about half of deaths in failure. Mechanical assist devices and transplantation are the end of the line for a small number of patients.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md) is involved because the kidney is both victim and culprit: low output and venous congestion lower filtration, the resulting retention worsens the failure, and the drugs that block the loop lower filtration further (the cardiorenal syndrome). [Pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) shares the breathless patient, and the two coexist in smokers. [Critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) takes over at cardiogenic shock. [Geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) manages the frail majority, [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) titrates the drugs, and [palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/palliative-care-and-symptom-control.md) is needed because advanced heart failure has a prognosis comparable with many cancers (~50% five-year mortality after diagnosis, high-income countries, 2010s–2020s).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ejection fraction | Stroke volume divided by end-diastolic volume; normally above ~55% |
| Preload | End-diastolic stretch of the ventricle; set by filling pressure and volume |
| Afterload | Pressure the ventricle must generate to eject; set largely by arterial pressure and resistance |
| Congestion | Raised venous and capillary pressure behind a failing ventricle, with oedema |
| Neurohormonal activation | Sympathetic, renin–angiotensin–aldosterone and antidiuretic hormone responses to low output |
| Remodelling | Change in ventricular size, shape and myocyte biology under chronic load and neurohormonal drive |
| Natriuretic peptides | Hormones released by stretched myocardium; a blood test for wall stress |
| Cardiorenal syndrome | Mutual worsening of heart and kidney failure through congestion, low output and shared drugs |
| Decompensation | An acute episode of congestion or low output after a stable period; usually has a trigger |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Heart failure means the heart has stopped or is about to."** It is a chronic syndrome, often stable for years; cardiac arrest is a different event, though failure raises its risk.
- **"A normal ejection fraction excludes heart failure."** Half of patients have preserved ejection fraction; their problem is filling, not emptying.
- **"Diuretics treat heart failure."** They treat congestion and relieve symptoms; the drugs that prolong life are the ones that block neurohormonal compensation.
- **"Fluid overload means too much fluid was given."** Most congestion is fluid the kidney retained under hormonal instruction, not fluid administered.
