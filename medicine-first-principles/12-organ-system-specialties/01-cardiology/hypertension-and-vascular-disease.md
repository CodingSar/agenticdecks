## Essence

Hypertension is a regulated variable set too high. Arterial pressure is cardiac output times peripheral resistance, and the kidney, the sympathetic nervous system and the renin–angiotensin system hold it at a set point over hours to years. In most patients no single fault is found; a mixture of genes, salt, weight, age and vascular stiffening shifts the set point upward, and the body then defends the raised pressure as vigorously as it would a normal one. The raised pressure is symptomless for decades while it damages the arterial wall, the heart that pumps against it, the kidney that filters under it, the retina and the brain. Hypertension is therefore a risk factor treated as a disease, and the case for treating it rests entirely on trials showing fewer strokes, infarcts and deaths. The same arterial wall, under pressure and atherosclerosis, also tears (dissection) and balloons (aneurysm), which is where cardiology meets vascular surgery.

## Core tenets

1. **Pressure is flow times resistance, and the kidney sets the long-term level.** Short-term control is baroreceptor reflex adjustment of heart rate, contractility and arteriolar tone ([blood pressure regulation](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md)). Long-term control is renal: the kidney excretes sodium at a rate that depends on perfusion pressure (pressure natriuresis), so any sustained pressure is one at which sodium intake and output balance ([volume regulation](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/sodium-water-and-volume-regulation.md)). Guyton's argument follows: pressure cannot stay high for years unless the kidney's pressure–natriuresis curve has shifted right, which is why every form of chronic hypertension involves the kidney, and why diuretics and salt restriction lower pressure in almost everyone.

2. **Primary hypertension is multifactorial; secondary hypertension is a single lever pulled.** About 90–95% of cases have no single cause: polygenic salt sensitivity, obesity-driven sympathetic and insulin signalling, age-related arterial stiffening and low nephron number combine ([multifactorial disease](../../08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/multifactorial-disease-and-risk.md)). The remainder have one identifiable mechanism: aldosterone excess (sodium retention), renal artery stenosis (renin release), [chronic kidney disease](../03-nephrology/chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md), [phaeochromocytoma](../05-endocrinology/adrenal-pituitary-and-gonadal-disorders.md) (catecholamines), coarctation. Secondary causes are sought when hypertension is severe, early, resistant, or accompanied by a clue such as low potassium, because they are curable.

3. **Stiff arteries raise systolic pressure in the old by a different mechanism from the young.** A compliant aorta stores part of each stroke volume and releases it in diastole, smoothing the pulse. With age, elastin fragments and collagen and calcium accumulate ([mechanics and materials](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/mechanics-and-materials.md)); the stiff aorta cannot store, so systolic pressure rises and diastolic falls. The widened pulse pressure is itself damaging, and isolated systolic hypertension is the dominant form after 60.

4. **Damage follows pressure into the organs with the most exposed small arteries.** Chronic high pressure thickens arteriolar walls (hyaline and hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis), narrowing lumens and accelerating [atherosclerosis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md) in larger vessels. The left ventricle hypertrophies against the load and becomes stiff, the path to [heart failure with preserved ejection fraction](heart-failure.md). Glomerular arterioles sclerose and nephrons are lost. Cerebral small vessels occlude or rupture, giving lacunar and haemorrhagic [stroke](../10-neurology/stroke.md). Retinal arterioles narrow and leak, visible directly with an ophthalmoscope, the one place end-organ damage can be seen rather than inferred.

5. **The diagnosis is a measurement problem.** Blood pressure varies minute to minute with posture, emotion and the measurer's presence, so a single clinic reading misclassifies many people ([measurement error](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/01-probability-and-measurement/measurement-error-and-biological-variation.md)). Ambulatory or home monitoring averages out the noise and reveals both white-coat hypertension (clinic only) and masked hypertension (home only); the average, and the loss of the normal nocturnal dip, predict outcomes better than any clinic value. Tests then look for cause (potassium, renin, aldosterone, renal imaging) and for damage (urine albumin, creatinine, Electrocardiogram (ECG) or echocardiographic hypertrophy).

6. **Treatment lowers pressure by any of four levers and the benefit is proportional to the fall.** Diuretics reduce volume; angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors and receptor blockers remove angiotensin II's constriction and aldosterone's retention; calcium-channel blockers (amlodipine) relax arteriolar smooth muscle; beta-blockers lower output and renin ([cardiovascular drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/autonomic-and-cardiovascular-drugs.md)). Meta-analyses of trials show that each 10 mmHg fall in systolic pressure lowers major cardiovascular events by roughly 20% and stroke by about a quarter, largely regardless of which class achieved it, with benefit continuing down to levels once considered normal ([systematic reviews](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/systematic-reviews-and-meta-analysis.md)). Because the absolute benefit depends on baseline risk, treatment thresholds are risk-based, not pressure-based alone ([risk prediction](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/risk-prediction-and-stratification.md)).

7. **The aortic wall fails by tearing or stretching, and both follow Laplace.** Wall tension rises with pressure and radius (Laplace's law), so a dilated segment stretches further: an aneurysm grows faster the larger it is, and ruptures when wall stress exceeds strength. An intimal tear lets pressurised blood split the media along its length (dissection), occluding branches and threatening rupture into the pericardium; it presents as tearing chest or back pain with unequal pulses, and its mortality rises by the hour. Peripheral arterial disease is atherosclerosis in the legs: exertional calf pain (claudication) that is the limb's angina, and critical ischaemia when resting flow fails.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md) is involved in both directions: kidney disease causes hypertension and hypertension destroys kidneys, and resistant hypertension is often referred there. [Endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/adrenal-pituitary-and-gonadal-disorders.md) works up aldosterone and catecholamine excess. [Vascular surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) repairs aneurysms and dissections and revascularises limbs. [Neurology](../10-neurology/stroke.md) and [ophthalmology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md) see the end organs. [Obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md) owns pre-eclampsia, a hypertension of placental origin. Most of the work is done in [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), where hypertension is the commonest chronic condition managed.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mean arterial pressure | Cardiac output times total peripheral resistance; roughly diastolic plus a third of pulse pressure |
| Pressure natriuresis | The kidney excretes more sodium as perfusion pressure rises; the long-term controller of pressure |
| Primary (essential) hypertension | Raised pressure with no single identifiable cause; ~90–95% of cases |
| Secondary hypertension | Raised pressure from one identifiable mechanism, often curable |
| Pulse pressure | Systolic minus diastolic; widens as the aorta stiffens |
| Arteriolosclerosis | Hypertensive thickening of small artery walls |
| End-organ damage | Structural injury to heart, kidney, brain, retina and arteries from sustained pressure |
| Aortic dissection | Blood entering and splitting the aortic media through an intimal tear |
| Aneurysm | Localised arterial dilatation that grows and ruptures by Laplace's law |

## Common misconceptions

- **"High blood pressure causes headaches and you can feel it."** Hypertension is almost always symptomless until it damages an organ; that is why it is screened for.
- **"Normal blood pressure is a fixed threshold."** Risk rises continuously from about 115/75 mmHg; treatment thresholds are decisions about harm and benefit, not biological boundaries.
- **"The drug class matters more than the pressure achieved."** For most patients the benefit tracks the fall in pressure; class choice matters mainly for coexisting conditions and side effects.
