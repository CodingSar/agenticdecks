# Interstitial and restrictive lung disease

## Essence

Restrictive lung disease is the mirror of obstruction: the lung can empty but cannot fill. Either the lung is stiff and small because its interstitium, the thin scaffold between alveolus and capillary, is thickened by inflammation and scar, or the lung is normal but the bellows around it (chest wall, pleura, muscles, nerves) cannot expand it. The interstitial diseases are many (over 200 named causes), their histology overlaps, and their common endpoint, fibrosis, is repair turned against the organ. The single idea is that gas exchange needs a membrane under a micrometre thick and a lung compliant enough to be inflated by a few centimetres of water; thickening the one and stiffening the other produce exertional breathlessness, hypoxaemia and a small lung on every test.

## Core tenets

1. **Diffusion is fast only because the membrane is thin, so thickening it limits oxygen before carbon dioxide.** Fick's law makes gas transfer proportional to surface area and inversely proportional to thickness ([diffusion](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/diffusion-osmosis-and-transport.md)). The normal barrier is about 0.3 micrometres; a red cell crosses the capillary in ~0.75 s and equilibrates in about a third of that, a reserve that a thickened membrane erodes ([gas exchange](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/gas-exchange-and-transport.md)). On exertion transit time shortens and the reserve is gone: desaturation on walking is the early sign. Carbon dioxide diffuses about twenty times faster than oxygen and is rarely retained until very late.

2. **A stiff lung is a small lung that is expensive to inflate.** Fibrosis and cellular infiltration lower compliance, so the same inspiratory effort produces a smaller volume ([mechanics](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/ventilation-and-lung-mechanics.md)); the patient takes small, fast breaths to minimise elastic work. Spirometry shows reduced volumes with a normal or high FEV1/FVC ratio, since the airways are open and recoil increased. The small, stiff lung also raises pulmonary vascular resistance, so [pulmonary hypertension](pulmonary-vascular-disease-and-respiratory-failure.md) and right heart strain are late consequences.

3. **Fibrosis is repair that never stops.** Repeated alveolar epithelial injury, from dust, drugs, radiation, autoimmune attack or an unknown trigger in a susceptible, ageing epithelium, activates fibroblasts through transforming growth factor beta and lays down collagen ([repair and fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)). In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis the process is relentless and inflammation scanty, so corticosteroids do not help and the antifibrotic drugs that slow it (pirfenidone; nintedanib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor) act on fibroblast signalling instead. Median survival from diagnosis is of the order of three to five years untreated.

4. **The causes group by what the lung was exposed to, and the history finds most of them.** Dusts (asbestos, silica, coal; bird and mould antigens in hypersensitivity pneumonitis), drugs (amiodarone, methotrexate, bleomycin), radiation and connective tissue diseases (systemic sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, myositis) account for a large share; sarcoidosis, a [granulomatous](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md) disease of unknown cause, is another; the rest are idiopathic and classified by pattern. Occupational causes carry legal consequences and latencies of decades, which is why the history asks about jobs forty years ago ([the host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment)).

5. **Examination and imaging read the stiff, scarred lung directly.** Fine end-inspiratory basal crackles are collapsed small airways snapping open; finger clubbing accompanies fibrosis for reasons not understood. High-resolution computed tomography is the decisive test: the distribution (basal and subpleural in idiopathic fibrosis, upper-zone in sarcoidosis and most dust diseases) and the pattern (reticulation, ground-glass, honeycombing) often identify the disease without biopsy ([computed tomography](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/radiography-and-computed-tomography.md)). Gas transfer measured with carbon monoxide quantifies the membrane and is the most sensitive functional test. When these disagree, a multidisciplinary meeting decides, and surgical biopsy is reserved for cases where the answer would change treatment.

6. **Treatment removes the trigger, suppresses inflammation when there is some, and slows fibrosis when there is not.** Hypersensitivity pneumonitis and drug reactions improve when the exposure stops; sarcoidosis and the connective-tissue diseases, which have a real inflammatory component, respond to corticosteroids and immunosuppressants ([anti-inflammatory drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anti-inflammatory-and-immunomodulatory-drugs.md)); established fibrosis responds only to antifibrotics, which roughly halve the rate of loss of vital capacity without reversing it, and to transplantation. Oxygen corrects the hypoxaemia and relieves the right heart. Distinguishing inflammation from scar is the point of the diagnostic effort.

7. **Extrapulmonary restriction is a bellows problem with a normal lung.** Kyphoscoliosis, obesity, a large pleural effusion and neuromuscular diseases such as motor neurone disease and Guillain–Barré syndrome reduce lung volumes with normal gas transfer per unit of lung. The danger is ventilatory failure rather than hypoxaemia: weak muscles cannot sustain minute ventilation and carbon dioxide rises, first during sleep. Nocturnal non-invasive ventilation treats it, and supine vital capacity predicts it ([control of breathing](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/control-of-breathing.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** [Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/connective-tissue-disease-and-vasculitis.md) shares every patient whose fibrosis is part of a connective tissue disease, and often prescribed the methotrexate that may be the cause. [Occupational medicine](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/sports-occupational-and-lifestyle-medicine.md) and the law are involved in dust diseases. [Thoracic surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) performs biopsy and transplantation. [Neurology](../10-neurology/demyelinating-and-neuromuscular-disease.md) shares the neuromuscular bellows failures. [Oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) causes some (radiation, bleomycin, checkpoint-inhibitor pneumonitis). [Palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/palliative-care-and-symptom-control.md) is needed early in progressive fibrosis.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Interstitium | The connective tissue scaffold between alveolar epithelium and capillary endothelium |
| Restrictive pattern | Reduced lung volumes with a preserved FEV1/FVC ratio |
| Gas transfer (DLCO) | Carbon monoxide uptake as a measure of membrane area and thickness |
| Compliance | Volume change per unit pressure; low in fibrosis |
| Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis | Progressive fibrosis of unknown cause with a basal, subpleural, honeycombing pattern |
| Hypersensitivity pneumonitis | Immune-mediated interstitial inflammation from inhaled organic antigen |
| Sarcoidosis | Multisystem granulomatous disease of unknown cause, usually affecting lungs and lymph nodes |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Fibrosis is inflammation, so steroids treat it."** In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis inflammation is minimal and steroid-based immunosuppression increased mortality in a trial; antifibrotics act on fibroblast signalling instead.
- **"Restriction means the airways are blocked."** The airways are open; the lung or chest wall cannot expand, and the FEV1/FVC ratio is normal or high.
