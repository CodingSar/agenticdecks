## What this area is about

Pulmonology is the medicine of gas exchange, and the lung can fail at exactly four places: air cannot get in and out (airways), the gas-exchanging tissue is thickened or destroyed (interstitium and alveoli), the alveoli are filled with something other than air (infection, fluid, blood), or blood does not reach ventilated alveoli (the pulmonary circulation). Each child owns one of these. The specialty's question is: when a patient is breathless, coughing or hypoxic, which of the four has failed, and does the failure limit flow, diffusion, matching or the pump that drives them?

The unifying idea is **ventilation–perfusion matching as the thing every lung disease breaks**. The lung's efficiency depends on air and blood arriving at the same alveoli in the right proportion. Airway disease sends air to the wrong places, interstitial disease stiffens and thickens the exchange surface, pneumonia fills alveoli that are still perfused (shunt), and vascular disease perfuses alveoli that are empty (dead space). Arterial blood gases, spirometry and imaging are the three instruments that tell these apart, and oxygen, bronchodilators, antimicrobials, anticoagulants and ventilators are the levers that reverse each.

Read asthma and COPD first: they introduce spirometry, the obstructive pattern and the idea of airflow limitation. Interstitial disease is the contrasting restrictive pattern and introduces diffusion. Pneumonia applies the inflammation of layer 08 to the alveolus and shows shunt. The vascular leaf closes the set with dead space and ends with respiratory failure, the common endpoint of all four, which is where the specialty hands over to critical care.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md):** the heart and lungs share a circulation and a symptom. Left heart failure causes pulmonary oedema that mimics lung disease; lung disease and emboli overload the right heart. Breathlessness is the territory both must share, and B-type natriuretic peptide and echocardiography are the tie-breakers.
- **[Nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md):** the lung and kidney jointly control acid–base; chronic carbon dioxide retention is compensated by renal bicarbonate retention, and both organs are attacked together by some vasculitides (pulmonary–renal syndromes).
- **[Infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md):** pneumonia, tuberculosis and the opportunistic infections of the immunosuppressed are shared; the lung is the organ most often infected in a hospital.
- **[Oncology](../07-oncology/README.md):** lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide; pulmonology finds and biopsies it, oncology treats it.
- **[Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md):** connective tissue diseases and vasculitis cause interstitial lung disease, pleural effusion and pulmonary hypertension.
- **[Haematology](../06-haematology/README.md):** pulmonary embolism is a thrombosis problem and anticoagulation a shared decision; sickle cell disease causes acute chest syndrome.

## Interfaces with other specialties

The hand-over that defines pulmonology is to [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md): when oxygen by mask and non-invasive ventilation can no longer hold the blood gases, the patient needs [mechanical ventilation](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md), and the decision about whether that is appropriate for a patient with end-stage lung disease is made jointly and in advance where possible. [Emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) treats acute asthma, exacerbations of COPD and pulmonary embolism before the specialty is involved, and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) asks pulmonology to assess fitness for surgery, where the honest question is whether the lungs can tolerate a general anaesthetic and the loss of lung volume after an abdominal or thoracic operation. [Thoracic surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) resects lung cancer, drains empyema and transplants lungs, and the lung cancer multidisciplinary team (pulmonology, thoracic surgery, oncology, radiology, pathology) is the model for shared decisions. [Ear, nose and throat surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) shares the upper airway: obstruction above the larynx presents as stridor and is their emergency, not pulmonology's.

In layer 14, [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) diagnoses and manages most asthma and COPD, delivers smoking cessation (the single most effective intervention in the specialty) and refers for diagnostic uncertainty or frequent exacerbations. [Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) owns childhood asthma, bronchiolitis and cystic fibrosis, with a structured transition to adult care in adolescence. [Geriatrics and palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) manage breathlessness at the end of life in COPD and fibrosis, where opioids relieve the sensation without worsening the gas exchange. [Sleep and occupational medicine](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/sports-occupational-and-lifestyle-medicine.md) overlap on sleep apnoea and on dust diseases (asbestosis, silicosis), where the diagnosis has legal as well as medical consequences.
