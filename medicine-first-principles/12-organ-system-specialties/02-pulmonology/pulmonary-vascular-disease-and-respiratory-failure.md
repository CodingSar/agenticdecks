# Pulmonary vascular disease and respiratory failure

## Essence

This leaf covers the lung's blood supply and the end state of every lung disease. The pulmonary circulation is a low-pressure, high-flow, highly distensible bed that accepts the whole cardiac output at a mean pressure of about 15 mmHg; it fails when clots block it (pulmonary embolism), when its vessels remodel and narrow (pulmonary hypertension), or when hypoxia constricts it everywhere at once. Each of these loads the thin-walled right ventricle, which was not built to generate pressure. Respiratory failure is the common endpoint of airway, interstitial, infective and vascular disease: arterial blood that is too low in oxygen (type 1) or also too high in carbon dioxide (type 2). The one idea that unifies the leaf is that oxygenation and ventilation are different jobs, fail for different reasons, and are supported by different means.

## Core tenets

1. **The pulmonary circulation is built for flow, not pressure, and the right ventricle reflects that.** Pulmonary vascular resistance is about one-tenth of the systemic, and the bed distends and recruits vessels as flow rises, so pressure barely changes with exercise ([matching](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/ventilation-perfusion-matching.md)). The right ventricle is a thin crescent that ejects well against low pressure and fails quickly against high. An acute rise in afterload (a large embolus) dilates it within minutes and can stop it; a chronic rise (pulmonary hypertension) hypertrophies it over years until it fails as cor pulmonale, with raised venous pressure, liver congestion and ankle oedema.

2. **Hypoxic vasoconstriction is the lung's matching mechanism and, when global, its enemy.** Unlike every other bed, pulmonary arterioles constrict to low oxygen, diverting blood away from poorly ventilated alveoli; it is what keeps pneumonia's shunt small. When the whole lung is hypoxic, in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, fibrosis, sleep apnoea or altitude, every arteriole constricts, resistance rises, and the vessels remodel and the rise becomes fixed. This is the commonest mechanism of pulmonary hypertension and the reason long-term oxygen, which removes the stimulus, prolongs life in [hypoxaemic COPD](asthma-and-copd.md).

3. **Pulmonary embolism is venous thrombosis arriving in the lung, and it creates dead space, not shunt.** A clot formed in a deep leg or pelvic vein under [Virchow's conditions](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md) (stasis from immobility, injury to the vessel wall, hypercoagulability from cancer, oestrogen, pregnancy or inherited defects) breaks off and lodges in a pulmonary artery. The alveoli beyond it are ventilated but not perfused: wasted ventilation. Hypoxaemia arises from redistribution of flow and from the right ventricle's falling output, and the ventilatory response produces a low carbon dioxide. A large embolus obstructing much of the bed causes obstructive shock and sudden death; infarction of lung is uncommon because the bronchial circulation supplies the tissue.

4. **Embolism is a diagnosis of probability, and the tests are chosen by pre-test likelihood.** The symptoms (sudden breathlessness, pleuritic pain, haemoptysis, syncope) and signs (tachycardia, a swollen leg) are non-specific, so structured scores first estimate probability ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md)). A negative D-dimer, a fibrin degradation product, excludes embolism when probability is low because its sensitivity is high; it cannot confirm, because anything that forms fibrin (surgery, infection, pregnancy, cancer) raises it. Computed tomography pulmonary angiography shows the clot directly; echocardiography shows the right ventricle's strain and grades the danger.

5. **Anticoagulation does not dissolve the clot; it lets the body do so without adding more.** Heparins and the direct oral anticoagulants (rivaroxaban, apixaban) inhibit thrombin or factor Xa and stop propagation ([haemostasis](../../06-physiology/08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md)); endogenous fibrinolysis clears the embolus over weeks. Fibrinolytic drugs that activate plasminogen dissolve it in hours and are reserved for shock, because their bleeding risk is high. The duration of anticoagulation is set by whether the provoking cause has gone: three months for a transient provocation, indefinitely for cancer or a second unprovoked event, a decision balanced against bleeding ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)). A minority develop chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension from clot that organises instead of dissolving, which is surgically curable.

6. **Respiratory failure has two types because oxygenation and ventilation fail separately.** Type 1 (hypoxaemia with normal or low carbon dioxide) comes from ventilation–perfusion mismatch, shunt or diffusion limitation: pneumonia, oedema, embolism, fibrosis. Type 2 (hypoxaemia with raised carbon dioxide) comes from inadequate alveolar ventilation: the pump has failed, from airway obstruction too severe to sustain, weak or fatigued muscles, a chest wall that will not move, or a brain that will not drive breathing (opioids, [control of breathing](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/control-of-breathing.md)). The arterial blood gas tells which: a high carbon dioxide with a pH that has already been buffered by renal bicarbonate retention means the failure is chronic ([acid–base](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/acid-base-physiology.md)); a high carbon dioxide with a low pH means it is acute and the patient is tiring now.

7. **Support matches the failure: oxygen for type 1, ventilation for type 2.** Added oxygen raises the alveolar partial pressure and corrects mismatch, partly corrects shunt, and does nothing for ventilation. Positive airway pressure recruits collapsed alveoli and reduces shunt. Non-invasive ventilation through a mask augments each breath and rests the muscles, and in acute hypercapnic COPD exacerbations it reduces intubation and death; invasive [mechanical ventilation](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md) takes over the pump entirely. Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation replaces the lung as a last resort. In every case the support buys time for the underlying cause to be treated; it reverses nothing itself.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Haematology](../06-haematology/bleeding-and-clotting-disorders.md) shares the thrombophilia work-up and the anticoagulation decisions. [Cardiology](../01-cardiology/heart-failure.md) shares the right ventricle and the pulmonary hypertension of left heart disease, the commonest kind. [Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/connective-tissue-disease-and-vasculitis.md) refers systemic sclerosis patients, in whom pulmonary arterial hypertension is a leading cause of death. [Critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) owns invasive ventilation and massive embolism; [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md) share the patients at highest risk of embolism, and thromboprophylaxis after surgery is among the most effective preventive measures in hospital medicine. [Oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) sends the cancer-associated clots.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pulmonary vascular resistance | Pressure drop across the lung divided by flow; normally about one-tenth of systemic |
| Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction | Arteriolar constriction in response to low alveolar oxygen; diverts blood from poorly ventilated lung |
| Pulmonary hypertension | Mean pulmonary artery pressure above ~20 mmHg at rest; several causes with different treatments |
| Cor pulmonale | Right heart failure caused by lung disease |
| Pulmonary embolism | Obstruction of a pulmonary artery by thrombus from a systemic vein |
| Dead space | Ventilated but unperfused lung; raises the ventilation needed for a given carbon dioxide |
| D-dimer | Fibrin degradation product; sensitive but non-specific marker of thrombosis |
| Type 1 and type 2 respiratory failure | Hypoxaemia alone (gas-exchange failure) versus hypoxaemia with hypercapnia (ventilatory pump failure) |
| Non-invasive ventilation | Pressure support through a mask without intubation |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A normal oxygen saturation excludes pulmonary embolism."** Many patients with embolism have normal saturation; the diagnosis rests on probability and imaging.
- **"Anticoagulants dissolve clots."** They stop new fibrin forming; the body's own fibrinolysis removes the clot over weeks. Only fibrinolytic drugs dissolve it directly.
- **"High carbon dioxide means the patient needs more oxygen."** It means the patient needs more ventilation; oxygen alone will not remove carbon dioxide and may blunt the drive in a chronic retainer.
- **"Pulmonary hypertension is one disease."** Left heart disease, lung disease, chronic emboli and primary arteriopathy cause it by different mechanisms, and the vasodilator drugs that help the last can harm the first.
