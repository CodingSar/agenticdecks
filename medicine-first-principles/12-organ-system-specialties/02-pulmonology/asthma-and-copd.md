## Essence

Asthma and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) are the two diseases of expiratory airflow limitation. Both show the same obstructive pattern on spirometry; they differ in what narrows the airway and whether it opens again. Asthma is reversible narrowing by smooth-muscle contraction, mucosal swelling and mucus, driven by chronic allergic-type inflammation in airways that over-react to triggers. COPD is largely fixed narrowing from small-airway scarring plus loss of the elastic tissue that holds airways open (emphysema), caused in most patients by decades of inhaled smoke. Underneath both: airways are collapsible tubes held open by the lung's elastic recoil, and anything that narrows them or weakens that recoil traps air.

## Core tenets

1. **Expiration is passive and depends on recoil, so narrowed or unsupported airways trap air.** Inspiration is muscular; expiration relies on elastic recoil of the stretched lung ([lung mechanics](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/ventilation-and-lung-mechanics.md)). Small airways have no cartilage and are held open only by the surrounding tissue's tension. When they narrow (asthma) or lose their tethering (emphysema), they close early in expiration; trapped air raises the resting lung volume (hyperinflation), flattens the diaphragm and puts it at a mechanical disadvantage. Breathlessness in both diseases is largely the work of breathing at high lung volume.

2. **Asthma is airway hyper-responsiveness on a base of type 2 inflammation.** In most asthma, allergens, viruses or irritants drive a T-helper-2 and innate lymphoid response with interleukins 4, 5 and 13, immunoglobulin E and eosinophils ([hypersensitivity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)). The inflamed airway has thickened smooth muscle, hyperplastic mucous glands and a stripped epithelium, and contracts to stimuli (cold, exercise, histamine) that a normal airway ignores. The result is episodic wheeze, cough and tightness that vary over hours and days, worse at night, and reverse spontaneously or with a bronchodilator. The variability is the diagnosis.

3. **COPD is the lung's response to decades of inhaled injury, and it is two lesions.** Cigarette and biomass smoke recruit neutrophils and macrophages whose proteases, once antiprotease defences are overwhelmed (or absent, in alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency), digest alveolar walls: emphysema, which destroys both the exchange surface and the tethering of small airways ([chronic inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md)). The same injury scars and narrows the small airways and causes mucus hypersecretion (chronic bronchitis). The result is slowly progressive breathlessness, a reduced exchange surface that eventually lowers arterial oxygen and, late, retained carbon dioxide. Only a minority of smokers (roughly 15–30%, depending on definition) develop clinically significant COPD; it was the third leading cause of death worldwide in 2019.

4. **Spirometry measures obstruction; reversibility separates the two.** The volume exhaled in the first second (FEV1) as a fraction of the forced vital capacity falls below ~0.7 in both, because flow is limited ([spirometry](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/functional-testing-spirometry-exercise-and-sleep.md)). A substantial rise after a bronchodilator, or variability of peak flow across days, supports asthma; a persistent ratio despite treatment supports COPD. Gas transfer is low in emphysema (surface lost) but normal in asthma (surface intact). In an acute asthma attack the signs that matter are of exhaustion: a silent chest, inability to speak, and a *normal* or rising carbon dioxide, meaning the patient can no longer ventilate enough to blow it off.

5. **Hypoxaemia and hypercapnia are matching and pump failures respectively.** Narrowed airways ventilate some alveoli poorly while blood still flows to them: ventilation–perfusion mismatch, which lowers arterial oxygen and is corrected by added oxygen ([matching](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/ventilation-perfusion-matching.md)). Carbon dioxide retention occurs only when total alveolar ventilation falls, which happens in severe COPD when the work of breathing exceeds what the muscles can sustain; the kidney then retains bicarbonate to compensate the chronic respiratory acidosis ([acid–base](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/acid-base-physiology.md)). Exacerbations, usually infective, tip a compensated patient into acute-on-chronic failure, the specialty's commonest admission.

6. **Bronchodilators open the tube; corticosteroids treat the inflammation that narrows it.** Beta-2 agonists relax airway smooth muscle through cyclic adenosine monophosphate; muscarinic antagonists remove the vagal constrictor tone that is the main reversible component in COPD ([autonomic drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/autonomic-and-cardiovascular-drugs.md)). Inhaled corticosteroids suppress type 2 inflammation, so in asthma they reduce attacks and deaths; in COPD, where the inflammation is neutrophilic, they help only the subgroup with eosinophils and frequent exacerbations. Monoclonal antibodies against immunoglobulin E, interleukin 5 and the interleukin 4 receptor treat severe type 2 asthma by removing the signal rather than its effect ([biologics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)). Inhalation puts the drug on the airway and limits systemic dose, which makes the whole strategy possible.

7. **In COPD, few treatments clearly prolong life, and none is a bronchodilator alone.** Stopping smoking slows the decline in FEV1 toward the rate of normal ageing; long-term oxygen in chronic hypoxaemia reduces mortality, probably by unloading the right ventricle ([pulmonary hypertension](pulmonary-vascular-disease-and-respiratory-failure.md)); non-invasive ventilation in an acute hypercapnic exacerbation rests the muscles and roughly halves the need for intubation. Pulmonary rehabilitation improves breathlessness and exercise capacity more than any drug. The rest improves symptoms and cuts exacerbations, which matters, but feeling better and living longer are different outcomes.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) manages most asthma and COPD and delivers smoking cessation; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/common-childhood-illness.md) owns childhood asthma. [Emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md) take the life-threatening attack and the hypercapnic exacerbation. [Cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md) shares the breathless smoker; [allergy services](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/allergy-and-immunodeficiency-in-practice.md) identify triggers; [palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/palliative-care-and-symptom-control.md) manages terminal breathlessness in end-stage COPD.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Airflow limitation | Reduced maximal expiratory flow from narrowed or collapsing airways |
| FEV1/FVC ratio | Fraction of forced vital capacity exhaled in one second; below ~0.7 defines obstruction |
| Type 2 inflammation | Eosinophilic, interleukin-4/5/13-driven airway inflammation typical of allergic asthma |
| Emphysema | Destruction of alveolar walls with loss of surface and airway tethering |
| Hyperinflation | Raised resting lung volume from air trapping; flattens the diaphragm |
| Hypercapnic respiratory failure | Raised arterial carbon dioxide from inadequate alveolar ventilation |

## Common misconceptions

- **"All that wheezes is asthma."** Heart failure, foreign bodies and tumours wheeze; the diagnosis rests on variable, reversible obstruction, not the sound.
- **"A quiet chest in an asthma attack is reassuring."** It means too little air is moving to make a wheeze, and is a sign of imminent arrest.
- **"Oxygen is dangerous in COPD."** High-flow oxygen can raise carbon dioxide in some chronic retainers, but hypoxaemia kills faster; the answer is titrated oxygen, not none.
