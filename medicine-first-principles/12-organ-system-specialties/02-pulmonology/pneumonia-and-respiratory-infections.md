# Pneumonia and respiratory infections

## Essence

The lung inhales about 10,000 litres of unsterile air a day across a surface the size of a tennis court, so respiratory infection is the commonest infection of all and pneumonia the commonest infectious cause of death. Pneumonia is acute inflammation of the alveoli: a pathogen that has got past the airway's defences multiplies in the air spaces, and the exudate the body pours in to fight it fills alveoli that are still perfused, producing a shunt that no amount of inhaled oxygen fully corrects. The symptoms are the inflammation (fever, cough, pleuritic pain), the signs are the fluid-filled lung (crackles, dullness, bronchial breathing), the radiograph shows the consolidation, and the treatment is an antimicrobial chosen against the likely organism before the organism is known. The specialty's recurrent judgement is severity: who can go home, who needs a bed, and who needs intensive care.

## Core tenets

1. **Infection means the defences were breached, and the breach predicts the organism.** Mucociliary clearance, cough, alveolar macrophages and immunoglobulin A keep the lower airway nearly sterile ([barriers](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md)). Each defence has a characteristic failure: smoking and viral infection paralyse cilia (so bacterial pneumonia follows influenza); a depressed conscious level or a weak swallow lets oral and gastric contents be aspirated (anaerobes, Gram-negatives); a tube through the larynx bypasses everything (ventilator-associated pneumonia); and loss of T cells, neutrophils or antibody opens the door to organisms a normal host clears ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)). The setting of acquisition (community, hospital, immunocompromised) is the first branch in choosing treatment, because it changes the organisms.

2. **The pathology is acute inflammation in an air space, and it produces shunt.** Pattern-recognition receptors on macrophages detect the organism, cytokines recruit neutrophils, and capillaries leak protein-rich fluid into the alveoli ([acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md)). The consolidated lobe is perfused but not ventilated: blood passes through it unoxygenated, mixes with blood from healthy lung, and lowers the arterial oxygen in a way that extra inspired oxygen only partly corrects, because the shunted blood never sees the gas ([matching](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/ventilation-perfusion-matching.md)). Hypoxic vasoconstriction limits the shunt by diverting blood away, which is why pneumonia is less hypoxaemic than its appearance suggests, and why sepsis-induced vasodilatation makes it worse.

3. **The symptoms are the inflammation and the signs are the solid lung.** Cytokines reset the hypothalamic set point (fever, rigors) and drive the systemic response that becomes [sepsis](../08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md) if it spreads; inflamed pleura causes sharp pain on breathing; exudate causes productive cough. Consolidated lung transmits sound like a solid: breath sounds become bronchial, the voice is heard clearly through the chest, and percussion is dull ([physical examination](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/physical-examination-and-signs.md)). The chest radiograph shows the opacity with air bronchograms, and its distribution (lobar, patchy bronchopneumonia, diffuse interstitial) hints at the organism without proving it.

4. **The organism is usually treated before it is identified, so epidemiology guides the first choice.** Sputum and blood cultures, urinary antigens, and polymerase chain reaction panels for viruses and atypical bacteria identify a pathogen in well under half of community cases ([microbiological diagnosis](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/microbiological-and-serological-diagnosis.md)). *Streptococcus pneumoniae* remains the leading bacterial cause; influenza, respiratory syncytial virus and SARS-CoV-2 the leading viral ones; *Mycoplasma*, *Legionella* and *Chlamydophila* the "atypicals" with no cell wall or an intracellular niche that beta-lactams miss. Initial therapy is therefore empirical against the likely set, and narrowed when a result arrives, which is the daily practice of [antimicrobial stewardship](../08-infectious-diseases/antimicrobial-stewardship.md).

5. **Antimicrobials work by hitting what the pathogen has and the host lacks, so viral pneumonia gets no antibiotic.** Beta-lactams such as amoxicillin inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis; macrolides such as clarithromycin inhibit the bacterial ribosome and cover the cell-wall-less atypicals ([antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md)). Viruses have neither target; neuraminidase inhibitors shorten influenza modestly and antivirals exist for a few others, but most viral pneumonia is treated with oxygen, time, and in severe COVID-19 corticosteroids, which treat the host's inflammation rather than the virus. The fever and inflammation take days to settle after effective treatment; the radiograph lags by weeks and is not a measure of response.

6. **Severity is a prognosis, and it is scored from physiology, not from the radiograph.** Confusion, raised respiratory rate, raised urea, low blood pressure and age over 65 (the CURB-65 score) stratify mortality from under 1% to over 25%, and decide the setting of care ([risk prediction](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/risk-prediction-and-stratification.md)). Death comes from respiratory failure, from the systemic consequences of sepsis, or from the empyema, lung abscess or decompensated heart failure that follow. The worst outcome, acute respiratory distress syndrome, is diffuse inflammatory oedema across both lungs and is the subject of [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md).

7. **Chronic and recurrent infection means a structural or immune defect, and tuberculosis is its own disease.** Bronchiectasis, permanent dilatation of airways after repeated infection, creates a reservoir that infects itself; cystic fibrosis is the genetic archetype. Recurrent pneumonia in one lobe suggests an obstructing [tumour](../07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md). [Tuberculosis](../08-infectious-diseases/tuberculosis-and-tropical-infections.md), the leading infectious killer worldwide before COVID-19 (~1.3 million deaths, 2022), is a chronic [granulomatous](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md) infection that walls itself off rather than consolidating, reactivates decades later, and needs months of combination therapy; it is the pulmonologist's disease and the infectious diseases physician's at once.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) co-manages resistant, unusual and immunocompromised infections and runs stewardship and [infection control](../08-infectious-diseases/infection-control-and-healthcare-associated-infection.md), because respiratory pathogens spread between patients. [Critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) takes severe pneumonia and acute respiratory distress syndrome. [Thoracic surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) drains empyema. [Geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) sees the aspiration pneumonias of frailty and dementia, where the decision whether to treat is as important as how. [Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/common-childhood-illness.md) manages bronchiolitis and childhood pneumonia, which remains the leading infectious cause of death in children under five globally. [Public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/global-health-and-pandemics.md) runs the vaccination (pneumococcal, influenza, COVID-19) that prevents a large fraction of it.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pneumonia | Infection of the alveoli with inflammatory exudate filling the air spaces |
| Consolidation | Lung made solid by exudate; dull, bronchial breathing, opaque on radiograph |
| Shunt | Perfusion of unventilated alveoli; hypoxaemia poorly corrected by oxygen |
| Community-acquired versus hospital-acquired | Setting of infection; predicts organisms and resistance |
| Aspiration | Entry of oropharyngeal or gastric contents into the lung |
| Atypical pneumonia | Pneumonia from organisms without a cell wall or with an intracellular niche |
| Empyema | Pus in the pleural space, needing drainage |
| Bronchiectasis | Permanent airway dilatation that harbours recurrent infection |
| CURB-65 | A severity score from confusion, urea, respiratory rate, blood pressure and age |

## How it fits

- **Below:** takes the exudative response from [acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md), the pathogens from [bacteria](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/bacteria.md) and [viruses](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/viruses.md), the defences from [barriers](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md), and the drugs from [antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md). It assumes the likely organism can be predicted from setting and host.
- **Above:** [sepsis](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/sepsis-and-multi-organ-failure.md) is its systemic extension; [stewardship](../08-infectious-diseases/antimicrobial-stewardship.md) and [infection control](../08-infectious-diseases/infection-control-and-healthcare-associated-infection.md) are the system-level responses to treating it.
- **Siblings:** the commonest trigger of exacerbations in [asthma and COPD](asthma-and-copd.md); contrasts with [interstitial disease](interstitial-and-restrictive-lung-disease.md) in tempo (days versus years) and reversibility; its worst form becomes the respiratory failure of the [vascular leaf](pulmonary-vascular-disease-and-respiratory-failure.md).

## Common misconceptions

- **"A chest infection needs an antibiotic."** Most acute bronchitis and much pneumonia in children is viral; antibiotics add resistance and side effects without benefit.
- **"The radiograph should clear before the patient is better."** Clinical recovery precedes radiographic clearing by weeks; a persistent opacity at six weeks, not at one, raises concern about tumour.
- **"More oxygen fixes the hypoxaemia of pneumonia."** Shunted blood never meets the oxygen; beyond a point only recruiting the alveoli (positive pressure) or time helps.
- **"Pneumonia is a disease of the old and frail."** It is commonest and deadliest there, but it kills more children under five worldwide than any other infection.
