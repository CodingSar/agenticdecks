# Pneumonia and respiratory infections

## Essence

The lung inhales about 10,000 litres of unsterile air a day across a surface the size of a tennis court, so respiratory infection is the commonest infection and pneumonia the commonest infectious cause of death. Pneumonia is acute inflammation of the alveoli: a pathogen past the airway's defences multiplies in the air spaces, and the exudate poured in to fight it fills alveoli that are still perfused, producing a shunt that inhaled oxygen cannot fully correct. The symptoms are the inflammation, the signs are the fluid-filled lung, the radiograph shows the consolidation, and the treatment is an antimicrobial chosen against the likely organism before it is known. The recurrent judgement is severity: who goes home, who needs a bed, who needs intensive care.

## Core tenets

1. **Infection means the defences were breached, and the breach predicts the organism.** Mucociliary clearance, cough, alveolar macrophages and immunoglobulin A keep the lower airway nearly sterile ([barriers](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md)). Each defence has a characteristic failure: smoking and viral infection paralyse cilia (so bacterial pneumonia follows influenza); a depressed conscious level or weak swallow lets oral and gastric contents be aspirated; a tube through the larynx bypasses everything (ventilator-associated pneumonia); and loss of T cells, neutrophils or antibody admits organisms a normal host clears ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)). The setting (community, hospital, immunocompromised) is the first branch in choosing treatment, because it changes the organisms.

2. **The pathology is acute inflammation in an air space, and it produces shunt.** Pattern-recognition receptors on macrophages detect the organism, cytokines recruit neutrophils, and capillaries leak protein-rich fluid into the alveoli ([acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md)). The consolidated lobe is perfused but not ventilated: blood passes through it unoxygenated, mixes with blood from healthy lung, and lowers the arterial oxygen in a way that extra inspired oxygen only partly corrects, because the shunted blood never sees the gas ([matching](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/ventilation-perfusion-matching.md)). Hypoxic vasoconstriction limits the shunt by diverting blood away; sepsis-induced vasodilatation undoes this and worsens it.

3. **The symptoms are the inflammation and the signs are the solid lung.** Cytokines reset the hypothalamic set point (fever, rigors) and drive the systemic response that becomes [sepsis](../08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md) if it spreads; inflamed pleura causes sharp pain on breathing; exudate causes productive cough. Consolidated lung transmits sound like a solid: bronchial breath sounds, clearly transmitted voice, dull percussion ([physical examination](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/physical-examination-and-signs.md)). The radiograph shows the opacity, and its distribution (lobar, patchy, diffuse) hints at the organism without proving it.

4. **The organism is usually treated before it is identified, so epidemiology guides the first choice.** Cultures, urinary antigens and polymerase chain reaction panels identify a pathogen in well under half of community cases ([microbiological diagnosis](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/microbiological-and-serological-diagnosis.md)). *Streptococcus pneumoniae* remains the leading bacterial cause; influenza, respiratory syncytial virus and SARS-CoV-2 the leading viral ones; *Mycoplasma*, *Legionella* and *Chlamydophila* the "atypicals" with no cell wall or an intracellular niche that beta-lactams miss. Initial therapy is empirical against the likely set and narrowed when a result arrives: the daily practice of [antimicrobial stewardship](../08-infectious-diseases/antimicrobial-stewardship.md).

5. **Antimicrobials work by hitting what the pathogen has and the host lacks, so viral pneumonia gets no antibiotic.** Beta-lactams inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis; macrolides inhibit the bacterial ribosome and cover the cell-wall-less atypicals ([antimicrobials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/antimicrobials.md)). Viruses have neither target; neuraminidase inhibitors shorten influenza modestly, but most viral pneumonia is treated with oxygen, time and, in severe COVID-19, corticosteroids, which treat the host's inflammation rather than the virus. Fever settles over days after effective treatment; the radiograph lags by weeks and is not a measure of response.

6. **Severity is a prognosis, and it is scored from physiology, not from the radiograph.** Confusion, raised respiratory rate, raised urea, low blood pressure and age over 65 (the CURB-65 score) stratify mortality from under 1% to over 25%, and decide the setting of care ([risk prediction](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/risk-prediction-and-stratification.md)). Death comes from respiratory failure, from sepsis, or from the empyema, abscess or decompensated heart failure that follow. The worst outcome, acute respiratory distress syndrome, is diffuse inflammatory oedema across both lungs and belongs to [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/respiratory-failure-and-mechanical-ventilation.md).

7. **Chronic and recurrent infection means a structural or immune defect, and tuberculosis is its own disease.** Bronchiectasis, permanent airway dilatation after repeated infection, is a reservoir that infects itself; cystic fibrosis is the genetic archetype. Recurrent pneumonia in one lobe suggests an obstructing [tumour](../07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md). [Tuberculosis](../08-infectious-diseases/tuberculosis-and-tropical-infections.md), the leading infectious killer worldwide before COVID-19 (~1.3 million deaths, 2022), is a chronic [granulomatous](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md) infection that walls itself off rather than consolidating, reactivates decades later and needs months of combination therapy; it belongs to pulmonology and infectious diseases at once.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) co-manages resistant, unusual and immunocompromised infections, owns the general logic of [infection by site](../08-infectious-diseases/common-bacterial-infections-by-site.md), and runs stewardship and [infection control](../08-infectious-diseases/infection-control-and-healthcare-associated-infection.md), because respiratory pathogens spread between patients. [Critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) takes severe pneumonia; [thoracic surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) drains empyema. [Geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) sees the aspiration pneumonias of frailty and dementia, where whether to treat matters as much as how. [Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/common-childhood-illness.md) manages childhood pneumonia, still the leading infectious cause of death under five worldwide. [Public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/global-health-and-pandemics.md) runs the vaccination that prevents much of it.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pneumonia | Infection of the alveoli with inflammatory exudate filling the air spaces |
| Consolidation | Lung made solid by exudate; dull, bronchial breathing, opaque on radiograph |
| Shunt | Perfusion of unventilated alveoli; hypoxaemia poorly corrected by oxygen |
| Community-acquired versus hospital-acquired | Setting of infection; predicts organisms and resistance |
| Atypical pneumonia | Pneumonia from organisms without a cell wall or with an intracellular niche |
| Bronchiectasis | Permanent airway dilatation that harbours recurrent infection |
| CURB-65 | A severity score from confusion, urea, respiratory rate, blood pressure and age |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A chest infection needs an antibiotic."** Most acute bronchitis and much childhood pneumonia is viral; antibiotics add resistance and side effects without benefit.
- **"The radiograph should clear before the patient is better."** Recovery precedes radiographic clearing by weeks; an opacity persisting at six weeks, not one, raises concern about tumour.
- **"More oxygen fixes the hypoxaemia of pneumonia."** Shunted blood never meets the oxygen; beyond a point only recruiting the alveoli (positive pressure) or time helps.
