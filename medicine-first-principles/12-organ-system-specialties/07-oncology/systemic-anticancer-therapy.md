## Essence

Systemic therapy is the only treatment that reaches cancer cells wherever they are, and its history is a search for ever-smaller differences between tumour and host to exploit. Cytotoxic chemotherapy exploits proliferation: it damages DNA or the mitotic machinery of any dividing cell, and works because tumours divide more and repair less than most normal tissues. Hormonal therapy exploits a tumour's retained dependence on its tissue of origin. Targeted therapy exploits a driver mutation that the tumour cannot live without and normal cells do not carry. Immunotherapy exploits the fact that the tumour is foreign and has had to switch off the immune response that would otherwise kill it. Each class has a characteristic benefit, a characteristic toxicity that follows from its mechanism, and a characteristic route to resistance, because a tumour is an evolving population under selection.

## Core tenets

1. **Cytotoxics kill by proliferation, so their toxicity is the body's proliferating tissues.** Alkylating agents cross-link DNA, antimetabolites starve DNA synthesis, topoisomerase inhibitors and anthracyclines break strands, and taxanes and vinca alkaloids freeze the mitotic spindle ([anticancer drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anticancer-drugs.md)). The tissues that divide fastest in the host are marrow, gut epithelium and hair follicle, which is why neutropenia, mucositis, nausea and alopecia are the shared toxicities. The therapeutic index is narrow ([dose-response and therapeutic index](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/dose-response-and-therapeutic-index.md)), and marrow recovery sets the interval between cycles.

2. **Chemotherapy kills a fraction, not a number, so cycles and combinations are a matter of arithmetic.** A dose that kills 99% of cells leaves 1% whether the tumour began at a billion cells or a thousand (log-kill). Repeated cycles are needed to bring the population below the level the immune system or chance can clear. Combining drugs with different mechanisms and non-overlapping toxicities kills more per cycle at tolerable total toxicity and, crucially, makes it less likely that a single pre-existing resistant cell survives all of them ([probability](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/01-probability-and-measurement/probability.md)).

3. **Targeted therapy works where a tumour is addicted to one signal.** When a single mutated kinase drives growth (BCR-ABL in chronic myeloid leukaemia, EGFR or ALK in subsets of lung cancer, BRAF in melanoma), blocking it causes rapid regression with far less marrow toxicity than cytotoxics, because normal cells do not depend on that signal ([oncogenes](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/oncogenes-tumour-suppressors-and-genomic-instability.md), [receptors and ligands](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/receptors-and-ligands.md)). The toxicity that remains is on-target in normal tissue: EGFR inhibitors cause rash and diarrhoea because skin and gut use the same receptor. Companion diagnostics are therefore inseparable from the drug: no mutation, no benefit.

4. **Resistance is evolution, and it is the rule rather than the exception in advanced disease.** A tumour of a billion cells with a high mutation rate contains variants before the first dose ([natural selection](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/natural-selection-and-population-genetics.md), [genomic instability](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/oncogenes-tumour-suppressors-and-genomic-instability.md)). Treatment removes the sensitive majority and selects the resistant minority: a second mutation in the drug's binding site, amplification of the target, a bypass pathway, or increased drug efflux. Sequencing the relapsed tumour reveals the escape route and sometimes suggests the next drug. This is why cure of advanced solid cancer by targeted therapy alone is rare and why early, combined treatment matters.

5. **Hormonal therapy starves tumours of the signal their tissue of origin needed.** Many breast cancers depend on oestrogen and most prostate cancers on androgen, retained from the normal cell they came from ([reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md)). Blocking the receptor (tamoxifen, enzalutamide), stopping synthesis (aromatase inhibitors, gonadotrophin-releasing hormone analogues) or removing the gland produces durable control with the toxicity of a menopause or androgen deprivation: bone loss, vascular and metabolic effects. Resistance here is also evolutionary: receptor mutations and ligand-independent activation.

6. **Immune checkpoint inhibitors release a brake rather than attacking the tumour, so their toxicity is autoimmunity.** Tumours survive by expressing ligands such as PD-L1 that engage inhibitory receptors on T cells ([tumour immunology](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md), [tolerance](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/tolerance-and-self-recognition.md)). Antibodies against PD-1, PD-L1 or CTLA-4 remove the brake and can produce responses that last years in melanoma, lung, kidney and other cancers, something cytotoxics almost never do. The same release of tolerance causes colitis, hepatitis, thyroiditis, pneumonitis and diabetes, treated with immunosuppression, and the response is strongest in tumours with many mutations and therefore many foreign antigens.

7. **Supportive care is what makes the therapy deliverable.** Antiemetics acting on serotonin and neurokinin receptors, growth factors that shorten neutropenia, prophylactic antimicrobials and bisphosphonates for bone are not adjuncts but the reason modern regimens can be given at effective intensity ([central nervous system drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/central-nervous-system-drugs.md)). Neutropenic fever remains the main treatment-related killer and is treated as sepsis before culture results ([fever and sepsis](../08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** Systemic therapy is given by medical oncologists and haematologists ([haematological malignancies](../06-haematology/haematological-malignancies.md)) but its complications belong to everyone: [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) and [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) for neutropenic sepsis, [cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md) for anthracycline and trastuzumab cardiotoxicity, [endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/README.md) for checkpoint-inhibitor endocrinopathies, [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) for immune colitis, [nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md) for platinum nephrotoxicity, [dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md) for drug rashes, and [pharmacy and health systems](../../15-medicine-in-society/01-health-systems/README.md) because these are the most expensive drugs in medicine and their funding is a priority-setting problem.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cytotoxic chemotherapy | Drugs that damage DNA or mitosis in dividing cells regardless of lineage |
| Log-kill hypothesis | Each dose kills a constant fraction of cells, not a constant number |
| Therapeutic index | Ratio of toxic to effective dose; narrow for cytotoxics |
| Targeted therapy | Drug directed at a specific mutated or overexpressed protein driving the tumour |
| Oncogene addiction | Dependence of a tumour on one driver pathway, the condition for targeted therapy to work |
| Companion diagnostic | Test for the target that must be positive before the drug is given |
| Checkpoint inhibitor | Antibody blocking inhibitory T-cell receptors (PD-1, CTLA-4) or their ligands |
| Immune-related adverse event | Autoimmune toxicity from released tolerance |
| Acquired resistance | Selection of pre-existing or new variants that survive the drug |
| Neutropenic fever | Fever with low neutrophils after chemotherapy, treated as sepsis |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Chemotherapy targets cancer cells."** Cytotoxics target dividing cells; the selectivity is kinetic, which is why hair, gut and marrow suffer.
- **"Targeted therapy has no side effects."** It has the side effects of blocking its target wherever the target is used, and of the drug's off-target kinase inhibition; they are different, not absent.
- **"Immunotherapy is gentle."** Checkpoint inhibitors can cause fatal colitis, myocarditis or pneumonitis; because the mechanism is loss of tolerance, the toxicity can affect any organ and may be permanent (type 1 diabetes, hypophysitis).
