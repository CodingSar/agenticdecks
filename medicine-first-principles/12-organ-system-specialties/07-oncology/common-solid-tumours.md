## Essence

Five sites, lung, breast, colorectum, prostate and skin, account for roughly half of all cancers in high-income countries (2020s), and together they illustrate every principle of oncology. Most are carcinomas, cancers of epithelium, because epithelia divide constantly and face the outside world's carcinogens ([epithelium](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/01-tissues/epithelium.md), [causes of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md)). Each has a dominant cause that sets its prevention, a precursor that sets its screening, a pattern of spread that sets its staging, and a biology that sets its drugs. Learning them is learning five questions asked five times: what causes it, can it be caught early, where does it go, what does it depend on, who treats it.

## Core tenets

1. **Lung cancer is a smoking disease with a molecular minority that changed therapy.** Tobacco causes about 80–90% of cases, through direct DNA adducts that give these tumours one of the highest mutation burdens of any cancer ([environmental and toxic injury](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/environmental-and-toxic-injury.md)). It presents late because the lung has no pain fibres and large reserve, so most patients are inoperable at diagnosis. Small-cell cancer is neuroendocrine, spreads early and responds to chemotherapy; non-small-cell cancers are staged for surgery, and the minority with EGFR or ALK driver mutations, commoner in never-smokers, respond to targeted drugs, while high-mutation smokers' tumours respond best to checkpoint inhibitors. Low-dose computed tomography screening of heavy smokers reduces mortality ([screening](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md)).

2. **Breast cancer is three diseases defined by what the tumour depends on.** Oestrogen-receptor-positive tumours (most) depend on hormonal signalling and respond to endocrine therapy for years; HER2-amplified tumours depend on a growth-factor receptor and are transformed by antibodies such as trastuzumab; triple-negative tumours lack both and rely on chemotherapy and, increasingly, immunotherapy ([systemic anticancer therapy](systemic-anticancer-therapy.md)). Lifetime risk is about one in eight women in high-income countries; risk tracks lifetime oestrogen exposure and inherited BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutations that disable DNA repair ([DNA replication and repair](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/03-genetic-information-flow/dna-replication-and-repair.md)). Spread is to axillary nodes first, so the sentinel node is sampled, then to bone, liver, lung and brain. Mammographic screening reduces mortality modestly and overdiagnoses some indolent lesions.

3. **Colorectal cancer follows a visible sequence from polyp to carcinoma, which makes it preventable by endoscopy.** Most arise from adenomatous polyps over about a decade through an ordered accumulation of mutations (APC, KRAS, TP53), the adenoma–carcinoma sequence that first demonstrated multistep carcinogenesis ([oncogenes and tumour suppressors](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/oncogenes-tumour-suppressors-and-genomic-instability.md)). Removing polyps at colonoscopy removes the precursor, so screening prevents cancer rather than merely detecting it. Presentation is altered bowel habit, bleeding, iron-deficiency anaemia ([anaemias](../06-haematology/anaemias.md)) or obstruction; spread is via the portal vein to the liver, where isolated metastases are sometimes resectable with curative intent. Lynch syndrome and familial polyposis are the inherited forms.

4. **Prostate cancer is common, often indolent, and the paradigm of overdiagnosis.** Autopsy studies find prostate cancer in a large proportion of older men who died of something else; prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing finds many of these, and treatment causes incontinence and impotence, so active surveillance of low-grade disease is standard and screening remains contested ([screening principles and biases](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md)). The tumour depends on androgen; castration by gonadotrophin-releasing hormone analogue or receptor blockade controls advanced disease for years until castration-resistant clones emerge ([reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md)). Spread is to bone, and cord compression is the characteristic emergency.

5. **Skin cancers are ultraviolet-light diseases and the commonest cancers of all.** Basal cell carcinoma almost never metastasises; squamous cell carcinoma can, especially in the immunosuppressed; melanoma metastasises early relative to its size, and its depth of invasion (Breslow thickness) is the dominant prognostic variable ([skin cancer and skin infections](../11-dermatology/skin-cancer-and-skin-infections.md)). Melanoma carries BRAF mutations in about half of cases and a very high ultraviolet mutation burden, which is why both targeted therapy and checkpoint inhibition transformed its survival first.

6. **Metastatic pattern follows anatomy and soil.** Portal drainage sends gut cancers to the liver; systemic venous drainage sends most others to the lung; prostate and breast seek bone; lung and melanoma seek brain ([vascular architecture](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/vascular-and-lymphatic-architecture.md), [invasion and metastasis](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/invasion-metastasis-and-the-tumour-microenvironment.md)). The pattern determines staging investigations and the symptoms of relapse.

7. **Who else is involved.** Each tumour is diagnosed and often first treated by its organ specialty: [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) bronchoscopes, [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) scopes, [dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md) excises, and the [surgical subspecialties](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) (breast, colorectal, thoracic, urological) resect. [Gynaecology](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) manages the reproductive-tract cancers; neurosurgery and [neurology](../10-neurology/README.md) brain metastases and cord compression. [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) is where almost every cancer first presents, and [public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/README.md) owns tobacco control, sun protection, papillomavirus vaccination and screening, which prevent more deaths than any drug.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carcinoma | Cancer of epithelium; the majority of adult solid tumours |
| Precursor lesion | Pre-malignant change (adenoma, dysplasia, in situ carcinoma) that screening can detect and remove |
| Adenoma–carcinoma sequence | Stepwise mutational progression from polyp to colorectal cancer |
| Hormone receptor status | Whether a breast or prostate tumour depends on oestrogen or androgen |
| HER2 | Growth-factor receptor amplified in a subset of breast cancers, targetable by antibodies |
| Driver mutation | Mutation on which the tumour depends; a target and a companion-diagnostic marker |
| Breslow thickness | Depth of melanoma invasion, its main prognostic measure |
| Overdiagnosis | Detection of cancers that would never have caused symptoms or death |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Cancer is mostly inherited."** Fewer than one in ten of the common cancers follow a high-penetrance inherited mutation; most risk is exposure (tobacco, ultraviolet light, diet, infection) acting on common variation and chance.
- **"Finding a cancer early is always good."** Prostate and some breast and thyroid cancers show that detecting indolent disease produces treatment harm without benefit.
- **"The primary site decides treatment."** Increasingly the molecular target decides: a lung cancer with an ALK fusion and a lymphoma with the same fusion get the same drug.
