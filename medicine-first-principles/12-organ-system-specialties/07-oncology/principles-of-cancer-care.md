## Essence

Cancer care answers one question in sequence: what is this tumour, where has it reached, what does the patient want, and which combination of cutting, burning and poisoning best serves that goal at acceptable cost? The biology comes from general pathology: a clone that has acquired the hallmarks of self-sufficient growth, immortality, invasion and immune evasion ([hallmarks of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/hallmarks-of-cancer.md)). The clinical method adds two ideas on top. First, **stage** (how far the clone has spread) is the dominant predictor of outcome and the main determinant of whether cure is possible, because local treatments cure only local disease. Second, the goal of treatment, cure, prolongation or comfort, must be stated explicitly, because the same drug is a good decision under one goal and a harm under another. Oncology is therefore as much a discipline of decision-making under uncertainty as of pharmacology.

## Core tenets

1. **Diagnosis is tissue, and the tissue is now read genetically.** Imaging suggests; only histology confirms that a mass is malignant, names its lineage and grade, and increasingly its driver mutations and immune markers ([histopathology and molecular diagnostics](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/histopathology-cytology-and-molecular-diagnostics.md)). Grade describes how abnormal the cells look and how fast they divide; molecular profile describes which pathway is driving them and which drug might block it. Treating without tissue risks treating the wrong disease, and treating a lung cancer without knowing its mutation status now means withholding the most effective drug.

2. **Stage determines what treatment can achieve.** The TNM system records tumour size and local invasion (T), regional node involvement (N) and distant metastasis (M) ([classification, grading and staging](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/tumour-classification-grading-and-staging.md)). Surgery and radiotherapy are local: they cure disease that is still confined. Once cells have seeded distant organs ([invasion and metastasis](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/invasion-metastasis-and-the-tumour-microenvironment.md)), only systemic therapy reaches them, and for most solid tumours metastatic disease is treatable but not curable. Staging investigations therefore come before any treatment decision, and their purpose is to avoid both futile surgery and missed cures.

3. **Micrometastasis explains why local treatment is often followed by systemic treatment.** At diagnosis, many apparently localised cancers have already shed cells that imaging cannot see. Adjuvant chemotherapy, hormonal therapy or immunotherapy after surgery treats this invisible burden and raises cure rates in breast, colon and lung cancer by an absolute few to tens of percent, shown only by randomised trials because no individual patient can know whether they needed it ([randomised controlled trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md)). Neoadjuvant therapy before surgery shrinks the tumour and tests the drug's effect in vivo.

4. **The goal of treatment must be explicit because it changes the arithmetic.** With curative intent, severe toxicity is accepted for a chance of decades. With palliative intent, the aim is length and quality of life, and a drug whose benefit is weeks of survival at the cost of months of side effects fails on its own terms. Performance status, a simple scale of how much the patient can do, predicts tolerance of treatment better than age and is the first gate in every decision ([prognosis and shared decision-making](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/prognosis-and-shared-decision-making.md)). Patients and clinicians systematically overestimate the benefit of palliative chemotherapy; stating the goal is the corrective.

5. **Response is measured, and response is not the same as benefit.** Tumour shrinkage on imaging (response rate) is quick to measure and is what drugs are often licensed on, but a tumour can shrink without the patient living longer, and immunotherapy can enlarge a lesion with infiltrating lymphocytes before it regresses. Overall survival is the endpoint that matters; progression-free survival and response rate are surrogates whose validity must be shown separately for each disease ([grading evidence](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/grading-evidence-and-guidelines.md)).

6. **Oncological emergencies are the tumour or its treatment compressing, obstructing or dissolving.** Spinal cord compression from vertebral metastasis, superior vena cava obstruction, malignant hypercalcaemia, neutropenic sepsis and tumour lysis syndrome each need recognition within hours because the damage becomes irreversible. Each has a mechanism in physiology: cord ischaemia from compression ([motor control](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/motor-control.md)), venous hypertension, osteoclast-driven calcium release ([calcium and bone mineral metabolism](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/calcium-and-bone-mineral-metabolism.md)), and the loss of neutrophil defence ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)).

7. **Survivorship and late effects are now part of the disease.** Half or more of patients diagnosed with cancer in high-income countries survive ten years (2020s), so the late costs of cure, second malignancies from radiotherapy and alkylating agents ([radiation biology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/radiotherapy-and-radiation-biology.md)), cardiomyopathy from anthracyclines, infertility, and fear of recurrence, are a growing part of oncology and of primary care.

8. **Who else is involved.** No cancer is treated by one specialty. The multidisciplinary team brings together [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) (resection), clinical oncology (radiotherapy), medical oncology (drugs), [radiology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/README.md) and [pathology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/README.md) (diagnosis and staging), and [palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) from early in the course, not only at the end. Organ specialists own the site: [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) for gut cancers, [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) for lung, [dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md) for skin, [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) for blood. [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) finds the cancer and inherits the survivor; [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) judges fitness in the old, who are most of the patients.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stage (TNM) | Extent of spread: tumour, nodes, metastasis; the main prognostic variable |
| Grade | Degree of cellular abnormality and proliferation under the microscope |
| Adjuvant therapy | Systemic treatment after local therapy to eliminate micrometastases |
| Neoadjuvant therapy | Systemic treatment before local therapy to shrink the tumour and test response |
| Performance status | Scale of functional capacity (for example ECOG 0–4) predicting treatment tolerance |
| Curative versus palliative intent | Whether treatment aims at eradication or at length and quality of life |
| Response rate | Proportion of patients whose tumour shrinks by a defined amount on imaging |
| Overall survival | Time from randomisation or diagnosis to death; the endpoint that cannot mislead |
| Multidisciplinary team | Joint meeting of the specialties that decides each patient's plan |
| Oncological emergency | Complication of tumour or treatment needing action within hours |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Earlier diagnosis always saves lives."** Screening finds slow tumours preferentially and starts the clock earlier, so survival from diagnosis lengthens even when the date of death does not move; only mortality in a randomised screened population proves benefit.
- **"A tumour that shrinks is a treatment that works."** Response is a surrogate; several licensed drugs shrink tumours without extending life or improving its quality.
- **"Palliative means the end."** Palliative intent can mean years of life; palliative care alongside active treatment improves quality of life and, in some trials, survival.
