# Bleeding and clotting disorders

## Essence

Haemostasis must do two opposite things: seal a hole within minutes and never clot inside an intact vessel. It achieves this with three interlocking systems, the platelet plug, the coagulation cascade that stabilises it with fibrin, and the anticoagulant and fibrinolytic systems that confine it ([haemostasis and coagulation](../../06-physiology/08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md)). Bleeding disorders are too little of the first two or too much of the third; thrombotic disorders are the reverse. Clinically, the pattern of bleeding tells you which system has failed, two laboratory clotting times localise the defect within the cascade, and every treatment either replaces a missing component, removes an inhibitor, or deliberately tips the balance one way with a drug whose target is known.

## Core tenets

1. **The pattern of bleeding localises the defect.** Platelets seal small vessels, so platelet and von Willebrand factor problems cause mucocutaneous bleeding: petechiae, bruising, nosebleeds, heavy menstrual loss, bleeding immediately after a cut. Fibrin stabilises large plugs, so coagulation-factor deficiencies (haemophilia A and B) cause deep bleeding into joints and muscles, often delayed by hours because the initial platelet plug forms but then falls apart. This distinction is made at the bedside before any test.

2. **Two clotting times map the cascade.** The prothrombin time tests the tissue-factor (extrinsic) pathway and the common pathway; the activated partial thromboplastin time tests the contact (intrinsic) pathway and the common pathway ([haematology and coagulation testing](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/haematology-and-coagulation-testing.md)). Prolongation of one, the other or both points to which factors are missing; a mixing study with normal plasma then distinguishes deficiency (corrects) from an inhibitor such as an antibody (does not correct). Liver disease and vitamin K deficiency prolong both because the liver makes most factors and factors II, VII, IX and X need vitamin K for their carboxylation.

3. **Thrombocytopenia is low production, high destruction or sequestration, and the marrow decides which.** Platelets live about 7–10 days. Immune thrombocytopenia destroys them with autoantibodies and the marrow responds with abundant megakaryocytes; marrow failure or chemotherapy gives too few megakaryocytes; an enlarged spleen pools them. Bleeding risk rises steeply only when counts fall very low, because platelet function has large reserve ([redundancy and reserve](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#redundancy-and-reserve)). Microangiopathies such as thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura consume platelets in small-vessel thrombi, so a low count with fragmented red cells is a thrombotic emergency rather than a bleeding one.

4. **Disseminated intravascular coagulation is the system firing everywhere at once.** Sepsis, trauma, obstetric catastrophe and some cancers flood the circulation with tissue factor. Thrombin is generated throughout the vasculature, consuming fibrinogen, platelets and factors, and fibrinolysis is activated in response. The result is simultaneous microvascular thrombosis and bleeding from every puncture site, with low fibrinogen, raised fibrin degradation products (D-dimer) and prolonged times. It is treated by removing the trigger, because replacing factors into an active fire only feeds it ([sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md)).

5. **Venous thrombosis is Virchow's triad: stasis, hypercoagulability and endothelial injury.** Immobility, surgery, cancer, pregnancy, oestrogen, inherited thrombophilias (factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation, deficiencies of antithrombin, protein C or S) and antiphospholipid antibodies each tilt one term ([thrombosis, embolism and infarction](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md)). A deep vein thrombus produces a swollen painful leg; a fragment carried to the lung is a pulmonary embolus that raises dead space and strains the right ventricle ([pulmonary vascular disease](../02-pulmonology/pulmonary-vascular-disease-and-respiratory-failure.md)). D-dimer is sensitive but not specific, so it is useful to exclude thrombosis when pre-test probability is low ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md)); ultrasound or computed tomography angiography confirms it.

6. **Anticoagulants and antiplatelets each remove one step, and their benefit is bought with bleeding.** Heparins accelerate antithrombin; vitamin K antagonists such as warfarin starve the liver of carboxylated factors; direct oral anticoagulants inhibit thrombin or factor Xa; aspirin blocks platelet thromboxane; P2Y12 inhibitors block adenosine diphosphate (ADP) signalling. Each drug's bleeding risk is the mirror image of its efficacy, and a reversal agent exists only where a specific target can be neutralised (protamine for heparin, vitamin K or factor concentrate for warfarin, antibody fragments for some direct agents). The decision to anticoagulate is always a number-needed-to-treat against a number-needed-to-harm ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)).

7. **Replacement therapy treats deficiency; immunosuppression treats destruction.** Haemophilia is treated by giving the missing factor or, now, by gene therapy that restores its synthesis, or by antibodies that mimic factor VIII's bridging role ([biologics and advanced therapies](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)). Immune thrombocytopenia responds to glucocorticoids and to blocking the splenic macrophages that clear antibody-coated platelets. The logic is the same as in anaemia: identify whether the problem is input or destruction and treat that side.

8. **Who else is involved.** Bleeding disorders surface in [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) as unexpected haemorrhage, in [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) as heavy menstruation and postpartum bleeding, and in [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) as disseminated intravascular coagulation. Thrombosis is shared with [cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md) (atrial fibrillation, coronary stents), [neurology](../10-neurology/README.md) (stroke) and [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) (embolism). Every anticoagulated patient is a joint responsibility with [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), where monitoring and drug interactions ([drug metabolism and interactions](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/drug-metabolism-and-interactions.md)) are managed.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Prothrombin time (PT) | Clotting time testing the extrinsic and common pathways; basis of the International Normalised Ratio (INR) |
| Activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) | Clotting time testing the intrinsic and common pathways |
| Mixing study | Repeating a prolonged time with added normal plasma to separate deficiency from inhibitor |
| von Willebrand factor | Protein that anchors platelets to damaged vessel wall and carries factor VIII |
| Haemophilia | X-linked deficiency of factor VIII (A) or IX (B) causing deep bleeding |
| Immune thrombocytopenia | Autoantibody-mediated platelet destruction with a responding marrow |
| Disseminated intravascular coagulation | Systemic activation of clotting with consumption of factors and platelets |
| Virchow's triad | Stasis, hypercoagulability, endothelial injury: the causes of thrombosis |
| D-dimer | Fibrin breakdown product; sensitive marker of recent clot formation and lysis |
| Thrombophilia | Inherited or acquired tendency to thrombosis |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A normal platelet count means platelets are working."** Aspirin, uraemia and inherited function defects leave the count normal and the bleeding real; function is tested separately.
- **"Prolonged clotting times always mean bleeding risk."** The lupus anticoagulant prolongs the APTT in vitro yet causes thrombosis in vivo, the clearest example that a test measures a reaction in a tube, not a tendency in a patient.
- **"Anticoagulants dissolve clots."** They stop clots growing and let the body's fibrinolysis clear them; only thrombolytic drugs (plasminogen activators) actively dissolve fibrin, at a much higher bleeding cost.
