## Essence

Diabetes mellitus is chronic hyperglycaemia from a failure of insulin: absent (type 1, autoimmune destruction of the beta cells), or insufficient for a body that has become resistant to it (type 2, which is over 90% of cases). It affected about 540 million adults worldwide in 2021 and is rising with obesity. The acute danger is metabolic: without insulin the body behaves as if starving while glucose floods the blood, and ketoacidosis or extreme dehydration follows. The chronic danger is vascular: years of raised glucose damage small vessels in the retina, glomerulus and nerve, and accelerate atherosclerosis in large ones, so diabetes is a leading cause of blindness, kidney failure, amputation, heart attack and stroke. The one idea that organises treatment is that glucose control prevents the microvascular damage, while blood pressure, lipids and specific newer drugs prevent the macrovascular death, and both must be done at once for decades.

## Core tenets

1. **Insulin is the signal of the fed state, and its absence is a starvation programme running in the presence of food.** Insulin tells liver, muscle and fat to take up glucose, store glycogen and fat, and stop making glucose and ketones ([glucose homeostasis](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/glucose-homeostasis.md), [fed–fasted cycle](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/metabolic-integration-and-the-fed-fasted-cycle.md)). Without it, and with glucagon unopposed, the liver pours out glucose, fat breaks down to fatty acids and ketones, and muscle is catabolised. Glucose above the renal threshold (~10 mmol/L) spills into urine, dragging water and electrolytes: polyuria, thirst, weight loss despite hunger. These are the presenting symptoms of untreated type 1 and of severe type 2.

2. **Type 1 is autoimmune; type 2 is resistance that the beta cell eventually cannot overcome.** In type 1, T cells destroy the beta cells over months to years in genetically susceptible people (Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) class II), with autoantibodies to insulin and islet antigens as the marker ([autoimmunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md)); presentation is usually young, lean and acute, and insulin is needed from the start. In type 2, [obesity](obesity-lipids-and-metabolic-syndrome.md), inactivity and genes make liver, muscle and fat respond less to insulin; the beta cells compensate with hyperinsulinaemia for years, then fail, from exhaustion, lipotoxicity and amyloid deposition. Hyperglycaemia appears only when compensation runs out, so type 2 is usually silent for years before diagnosis and half of its microvascular damage may already be present ([compensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation)). Gestational diabetes is the same resistance unmasked by placental hormones, and monogenic forms show that single transcription factor or channel defects suffice.

3. **The acute emergencies are the starvation programme at full speed.** Diabetic ketoacidosis, mostly in type 1, is the triad of hyperglycaemia, ketonaemia and metabolic acidosis with a raised anion gap: ketoacids consume bicarbonate, osmotic diuresis depletes several litres of water and sodium, and total body potassium is lost although the plasma value is high because acidosis and insulin lack shift it out of cells ([electrolyte and acid–base disorders](../03-nephrology/electrolyte-and-acid-base-disorders.md)). Insulin stops ketogenesis and drives potassium back into cells, so potassium must be replaced as insulin is given or the heart stops. The hyperosmolar state of type 2 is the same dehydration without ketosis, because a little insulin suppresses ketogenesis but not glucose output. Hypoglycaemia, the mirror emergency, is a side effect of treatment: too much insulin or sulfonylurea for the food eaten, producing sympathetic warning signs (sweating, tremor) and then a brain deprived of its only fuel.

4. **Chronic hyperglycaemia damages tissues that cannot regulate their glucose uptake.** Retina, glomerulus, nerve and endothelium take up glucose in proportion to its concentration. Inside them, excess glucose glycates proteins non-enzymatically (advanced glycation end-products stiffen matrix and activate inflammatory receptors), is diverted into the polyol and hexosamine pathways, and activates protein kinase C, all converging on oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction ([microcirculation](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/microcirculation-and-capillary-exchange.md)). Capillary basement membranes thicken, pericytes die, and vessels leak and occlude. Glycated haemoglobin is the same chemistry turned into a test: the fraction of haemoglobin glycated reflects the mean glucose over the red cell's ~120-day life, and it is both the diagnostic and the monitoring measure.

5. **The microvascular complications follow the capillary bed, and screening finds them while they are reversible.** In the retina, microaneurysms, haemorrhages and ischaemia drive new vessel growth that bleeds and detaches the retina; retinal photography finds it before sight is lost and laser or anti-vascular endothelial growth factor injections treat it ([the eye](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md)). In the kidney, glomerular hyperfiltration, then albuminuria, then declining filtration lead to [chronic kidney disease](../03-nephrology/chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md); urine albumin is the screen. In nerves, a length-dependent sensory neuropathy numbs the feet, and autonomic neuropathy disturbs heart rate, gastric emptying and erection. The diabetic foot ulcer combines numb skin, poor arterial supply and infection, and leads to most non-traumatic amputations. Trials in the 1990s (the Diabetes Control and Complications Trial, the United Kingdom Prospective Diabetes Study) showed that lowering glycated haemoglobin reduces each of these in proportion ([randomised trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md)).

6. **Macrovascular disease is the main cause of death, and glucose control alone does little to prevent it.** Diabetes roughly doubles the risk of myocardial infarction and stroke, through [atherosclerosis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md) accelerated by dyslipidaemia, hypertension, inflammation and a prothrombotic state. Intensive glucose lowering in the trials produced only a small, delayed fall in cardiovascular events, while blood pressure control and statins produced large ones. Two newer drug classes changed this: sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (empagliflozin), which make the kidney excrete glucose and reduce heart failure and kidney progression, and glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonists (semaglutide), which amplify meal-stimulated insulin, suppress appetite and reduce cardiovascular events, each by mechanisms partly independent of glucose ([endocrine and metabolic drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/endocrine-and-metabolic-drugs.md)).

7. **Treatment replaces insulin or restores sensitivity, and the right choice follows the mechanism.** Type 1 needs insulin for life, given to mimic basal and meal secretion by injection or pump, with glucose sensors closing the loop in modern systems. In type 2, weight loss and exercise restore sensitivity and can put early disease into remission; metformin lowers hepatic glucose output; the newer classes above add benefit beyond glucose; sulfonylureas stimulate insulin release and cause hypoglycaemia and weight gain; insulin is added when the beta cell has failed. The goal is a glycated haemoglobin low enough to prevent complications but not so low that hypoglycaemia harms, and that target is individual: tight in a young patient with decades to protect, relaxed in a frail one for whom a hypoglycaemic fall is the greater danger ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)).

8. **Who else is involved.** Diabetes pulls in more specialties than any other disease. [Ophthalmology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md) screens and treats the retina; [nephrology](../03-nephrology/chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md) takes over when filtration falls; [cardiology](../01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md) and [neurology](../10-neurology/stroke.md) see the arterial events; [vascular surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md), podiatry and [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) share the foot. [Obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md) manages pregnancy; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) owns childhood type 1; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) matters because depression worsens control and antipsychotics cause diabetes. [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/chronic-disease-and-self-management.md) manages most type 2 and delivers the education without which none of this works, because the patient makes the treatment decisions many times a day.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Insulin resistance | Reduced response of liver, muscle and fat to insulin, demanding more for the same effect |
| Beta-cell failure | Loss of insulin secretory capacity; absolute in type 1, relative and progressive in type 2 |
| Glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) | Fraction of haemoglobin non-enzymatically glycated; integrates glucose over ~3 months |
| Diabetic ketoacidosis | Hyperglycaemia, ketonaemia and acidosis from absolute insulin deficiency |
| Hypoglycaemia | Low blood glucose, usually from treatment; the brain's fuel failure |
| Microvascular complications | Retinopathy, nephropathy and neuropathy from capillary damage |
| Macrovascular complications | Myocardial infarction, stroke and peripheral arterial disease from accelerated atherosclerosis |
| Advanced glycation end-products | Irreversibly glycated proteins that stiffen tissues and signal inflammation |
| Diabetic foot | Ulceration from neuropathy, ischaemia and infection; the main cause of amputation |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Type 2 diabetes is mild diabetes."** It causes most diabetic blindness, kidney failure and amputation, because of its numbers and its silent years.
- **"Good glucose control prevents heart attacks."** It prevents microvascular disease; macrovascular protection comes mainly from blood pressure, statins and the newer drug classes.
- **"High potassium in ketoacidosis means too much potassium."** Total body potassium is depleted; the plasma value is high because of shift, and it will fall dangerously once insulin is given.
