## Essence

Thyroid hormone sets the metabolic rate of nearly every cell, and the thyroid is the gland that shows the logic of endocrinology most clearly: a single hormone, a single controller (Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH) from the pituitary), a tight negative feedback loop, and diseases that are too little, too much, a lump, or a cancer. Most thyroid disease is autoimmune: antibodies and T cells either destroy the gland (hypothyroidism) or, uniquely, stimulate it by mimicking TSH (Graves' disease). The diagnosis rests on measuring the loop at two points, hormone and controller, and the treatments are the two archetypes of the specialty: replace the hormone, or block, destroy or remove its source. The gland is also accessible to the hand, the ultrasound probe and the needle, so its nodules are among the commonest incidental findings in medicine and its cancers among the most curable.

## Core tenets

1. **The axis is a negative feedback loop with a log-linear gain, so TSH is the most sensitive measure of thyroid status.** The hypothalamus releases thyrotropin-releasing hormone, the pituitary releases TSH, the thyroid releases thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3), and T4 and T3 suppress both upstream glands ([hormone axes](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/hormone-classes-axes-and-feedback.md), [feedback](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/03-systems-and-models/feedback-and-control.md)). The relation is logarithmic: a halving of free T4 roughly multiplies TSH by a hundred. A small fall in hormone the T4 assay cannot distinguish from normal therefore produces a large, unmistakable rise in TSH, which is why TSH is the screening test and why "subclinical" disease (abnormal TSH, normal T4) exists as a category.

2. **Reading the pair localises the fault.** Low T4 with high TSH means the gland has failed and the pituitary is responding correctly (primary hypothyroidism); low T4 with low or normal TSH means the pituitary or hypothalamus has failed (secondary, and the adrenal axis must then be checked before replacing thyroid hormone, because thyroxine accelerates cortisol clearance and can precipitate adrenal crisis). High T4 with suppressed TSH means the gland is autonomous or stimulated (primary hyperthyroidism); high T4 with high TSH means a TSH-secreting tumour or resistance. Severe non-thyroidal illness, pregnancy, and drugs such as amiodarone and biotin distort the pair, so it is read in context ([clinical chemistry](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/clinical-chemistry-and-reference-ranges.md)).

3. **Hypothyroidism is a slowed metabolism, and the symptoms are the slowing.** Autoimmune destruction (Hashimoto's thyroiditis, with antibodies to thyroid peroxidase, the commonest cause where iodine is sufficient), iodine deficiency (still the commonest worldwide), surgery, radioiodine and drugs remove the hormone. Everything runs slower: fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, bradycardia, slowed thought and reflexes, dry skin and hair; glycosaminoglycans accumulate in the skin (myxoedema) and the voice deepens; cholesterol rises as its clearance falls ([thyroid hormones](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md)). In the fetus and infant, hormone deficiency stops brain development, which is why every newborn is screened for a raised TSH and treated within days ([the newborn](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/the-newborn-and-neonatology.md)); this is among the most cost-effective screening programmes in medicine. Treatment is replacement with levothyroxine, a once-daily dose of a hormone with a seven-day half-life, titrated to a normal TSH, which is the loop reporting that the dose is right.

4. **Hyperthyroidism is a metabolism driven too fast, and its commonest cause is an antibody that mimics the controller.** In Graves' disease, antibodies against the TSH receptor activate it continuously, outside the loop, so T4 rises and TSH is suppressed but the gland does not switch off ([autoimmunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md)). The same antibodies and T cells inflame the orbital tissue (thyroid eye disease, with proptosis) and, rarely, the shins. Autonomous nodules and a transiently leaking inflamed gland (thyroiditis) are the other causes. The symptoms are sympathetic and metabolic: weight loss with appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, anxiety, palpitations, and in the old, [atrial fibrillation](../01-cardiology/arrhythmias.md) and heart failure. Radioiodine uptake scanning separates the causes: diffuse high uptake in Graves', a hot nodule, or no uptake in thyroiditis, which is treated with time rather than antithyroid drugs ([nuclear medicine](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/nuclear-medicine-and-molecular-imaging.md)).

5. **Treatment of excess blocks synthesis, destroys the gland or removes it; each buys control at a different price.** Thionamides (carbimazole, propylthiouracil) inhibit thyroid peroxidase and so hormone synthesis; they control Graves' disease within weeks and about half of patients remit after a course, but they can cause agranulocytosis. Beta-blockers relieve the sympathetic symptoms at once without touching the hormone. Radioactive iodine, concentrated by the gland through the same transporter it uses for dietary iodine, destroys it from within over months, with hypothyroidism as the usual and intended outcome ([radiation biology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/radiotherapy-and-radiation-biology.md)). Surgery removes it in a day, with risks to the parathyroids and the recurrent laryngeal nerves. In each case the end state is often hypothyroidism, which is easier to replace than excess is to control, a trade the patient should understand ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)). Thyroid storm, uncontrolled thyrotoxicosis with fever, delirium and heart failure, is the emergency, often triggered by infection or surgery in an untreated patient.

6. **Nodules are common and mostly benign; the task is to find the few cancers without operating on the many.** Palpable nodules are found in about 5% of adults and ultrasound nodules in up to half of older people; fewer than 5–10% are malignant. Ultrasound features (solid, hypoechoic, irregular, microcalcified) stratify risk and select nodules for fine-needle aspiration, whose cytology is reported on a standard scale from benign to malignant ([cytology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/histopathology-cytology-and-molecular-diagnostics.md)). A suppressed TSH points to an autonomous nodule, which is almost never cancer, and redirects to the scan. Rising detection of small papillary cancers that would never have caused harm is a textbook case of [overdiagnosis](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md).

7. **Thyroid cancer is usually curable because it keeps its parent's biology.** Papillary and follicular carcinomas, over 90% of thyroid cancers, arise from follicular cells, often with *BRAF* or *RAS* mutations, grow slowly, and still take up iodine and still depend on TSH. Treatment exploits both: after surgery, radioiodine seeks out and destroys residual or metastatic tissue, and levothyroxine at a dose that suppresses TSH removes the growth signal; serum thyroglobulin, made only by thyroid tissue, then serves as a tumour marker. Ten-year survival exceeds 90%. Medullary carcinoma arises from the calcitonin-secreting C cells, is often inherited (*RET* mutations, in the multiple endocrine neoplasia syndromes) and does not take up iodine; anaplastic carcinoma is rare, dedifferentiated and rapidly fatal ([tumour classification](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/tumour-classification-grading-and-staging.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** [Endocrine surgeons](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) remove the gland and its cancers after endocrinology has rendered the patient euthyroid; [ophthalmology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md) manages thyroid eye disease, which can threaten sight. [Cardiology](../01-cardiology/arrhythmias.md) sees the atrial fibrillation of the thyrotoxic elderly, and its iodine-rich drug amiodarone causes both hypo- and hyperthyroidism. [Obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) shares pregnancy, in which thyroxine needs rise and Graves' antibodies cross the placenta; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/mood-and-anxiety-disorders.md) is involved because hypothyroidism mimics depression, thyrotoxicosis mimics anxiety, and lithium causes hypothyroidism. [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) manages most hypothyroidism.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) | Pituitary hormone driving the thyroid; suppressed by thyroid hormone in a log-linear relation |
| Free thyroxine (T4) | The unbound circulating prohormone, converted in tissues to active T3 |
| Primary versus secondary | Failure of the gland (TSH moves opposite to T4) versus failure of its controller (TSH moves with T4) |
| Hashimoto's thyroiditis | Autoimmune lymphocytic destruction of the thyroid; the commonest cause of hypothyroidism |
| Graves' disease | Hyperthyroidism from antibodies that activate the TSH receptor |
| Thyroid storm | Life-threatening decompensated thyrotoxicosis |
| Radioiodine | Iodine-131, concentrated by thyroid tissue, used to image, destroy or treat it |
| Thyroid nodule | A discrete lump in the gland; common, mostly benign, triaged by ultrasound and cytology |
| Thyroglobulin | Thyroid-specific protein used as a tumour marker after total thyroidectomy |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A normal T4 means a normal thyroid."** TSH moves a hundredfold before T4 leaves its reference range; TSH is the sensitive test.
- **"Hypothyroidism explains obesity."** It causes modest weight gain, mostly fluid; thyroxine is not a treatment for obesity in people with a normal thyroid.
- **"Every thyroid nodule needs a biopsy."** Most are benign; ultrasound features and TSH select the minority that do, and over-investigation finds cancers that would never have mattered.
