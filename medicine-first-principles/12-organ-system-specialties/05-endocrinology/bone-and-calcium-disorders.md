## Essence

The skeleton is two things at once: a structure that must bear load, and the body's store of calcium and phosphate, from which the plasma calcium is defended minute by minute. The two roles conflict. Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) and vitamin D keep ionised calcium within a few percent of its set point by withdrawing mineral from bone, so a persistent calcium problem is paid for by the skeleton, and a skeleton that is remodelled faster than it is rebuilt loses strength. The diseases divide accordingly: disorders of the plasma calcium (parathyroid excess and deficiency, vitamin D deficiency, malignancy), and disorders of bone quantity and quality (osteoporosis, osteomalacia, Paget's disease, renal bone disease). The single idea is that bone is living tissue in constant turnover, and every disorder here is a turnover that has been uncoupled from the bone's needs.

## Core tenets

1. **Plasma calcium is defended by PTH acting on bone, kidney and gut, and the defence costs bone.** The parathyroid cell senses ionised calcium through a membrane receptor and releases PTH within seconds of a fall; PTH mobilises calcium from bone through osteoclasts, makes the kidney reabsorb calcium and excrete phosphate, and stimulates renal conversion of vitamin D to its active form, which drives gut absorption ([calcium metabolism](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/calcium-and-bone-mineral-metabolism.md)). Because the loop sacrifices the skeleton to the plasma, any chronic stimulus to PTH (vitamin D deficiency, kidney failure, calcium malabsorption) demineralises bone before the plasma calcium falls ([compensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation)).

2. **Hypercalcaemia is read by its PTH, and the two common causes are opposite.** A high calcium with a high or unsuppressed PTH means a parathyroid adenoma (or, rarely, hyperplasia) is secreting autonomously: primary hyperparathyroidism, found mostly on routine chemistry in otherwise well older women, with stones, bone loss and fatigue when symptomatic. A high calcium with a suppressed PTH means the stimulus is elsewhere: malignancy, either through a PTH-related peptide secreted by the tumour or through osteolytic metastases and myeloma, is the usual cause in hospital patients, and the calcium rises faster and higher. Symptoms are those of a raised excitation threshold and a kidney that cannot concentrate: weakness, confusion, constipation, polyuria, thirst, and a short QT interval ([electrolyte disorders](../03-nephrology/electrolyte-and-acid-base-disorders.md)). Treatment of the parathyroid cause is surgical removal of the adenoma; of malignant hypercalcaemia, fluid and a bisphosphonate to switch off the osteoclasts.

3. **Hypocalcaemia is loss of PTH or loss of vitamin D, and the signs are in excitable membranes.** Removal or damage of the parathyroids at thyroid surgery is the commonest acute cause; autoimmune destruction and genetic defects the chronic ones; the plasma phosphate is high because PTH no longer excretes it. Vitamin D deficiency, from little sunlight, dark skin at high latitude, malabsorption or kidney failure, causes a secondary rise in PTH, which keeps the calcium near normal at the cost of bone, so the calcium falls only when deficiency is severe. Low ionised calcium lowers the threshold of nerve and muscle: tingling, cramps, tetany (carpopedal spasm), laryngospasm, seizures and a long QT interval. Replacement with calcium and active vitamin D (or vitamin D itself when the kidney can activate it) reverses all of it.

4. **Bone strength is mass times quality, and osteoporosis is a loss of both through uncoupled remodelling.** Bone is removed by osteoclasts and replaced by osteoblasts in coupled cycles throughout life ([bone remodelling](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/bone-remodelling-and-joints.md)); peak mass is reached around 30 and then declines, steeply in women after the menopause because oestrogen restrains osteoclasts. When resorption exceeds formation, trabeculae thin and perforate and the cortex becomes porous, and the material fails under loads it once bore ([mechanics and materials](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/mechanics-and-materials.md)). Corticosteroids, hypogonadism, hyperthyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, alcohol, smoking, immobility and low body weight accelerate it. Osteoporosis has no symptoms until it fractures: wrist, vertebra (with height loss and kyphosis) and hip, which carries ~20% one-year mortality in older people and is the fracture that the whole field is organised to prevent ([geriatric syndromes](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md)).

5. **Bone density is measured but fracture risk is what is treated.** Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry measures areal bone mineral density and reports it as a T-score, standard deviations below the young adult mean; below −2.5 defines osteoporosis. Density predicts fracture but incompletely, because quality and falls matter too, so risk calculators add age, prior fracture, steroids, parental hip fracture and other factors to produce a ten-year probability, and treatment is offered on the probability, not the density ([risk prediction](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/risk-prediction-and-stratification.md)). A fragility fracture (a fall from standing height) is itself diagnostic, and the person who has had one is the person most likely to have another and most often left untreated.

6. **Antiresorptive and anabolic drugs act on the two halves of the remodelling cycle.** Bisphosphonates (alendronate) bind to bone mineral and are taken up by osteoclasts, which they inactivate, halving vertebral and reducing hip fractures in trials; because they stay in bone for years, treatment can be paused. Denosumab, an antibody against the osteoclast-activating ligand RANKL, does the same reversibly, and rebound loss follows if it is stopped without cover. Anabolic agents, PTH analogues (teriparatide) given intermittently and the sclerostin antibody romosozumab, stimulate osteoblasts and build bone, and are used in the highest-risk patients ([endocrine and metabolic drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/endocrine-and-metabolic-drugs.md)). Calcium and vitamin D sufficiency are the base, and the fall-prevention and exercise programmes of geriatrics prevent as many fractures as the drugs. Oestrogen prevents postmenopausal loss but is used for bone alone only when its other effects are also wanted.

7. **The other bone diseases are each a different fault in the same tissue.** Osteomalacia (rickets in children) is bone that is formed but not mineralised, from vitamin D or phosphate deficiency: soft, painful, bowing bones and proximal weakness, fully reversible with replacement. Paget's disease is focal, disorganised, excessive remodelling of unknown cause, producing enlarged, deformed, painful bones with a very high alkaline phosphatase, suppressed by bisphosphonates. Renal bone disease is the skeleton paying for [chronic kidney disease](../03-nephrology/chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md): phosphate retention, loss of active vitamin D and secondary hyperparathyroidism produce high-turnover bone and vascular calcification together. Osteogenesis imperfecta is a collagen defect; bone metastases and myeloma destroy bone directly and are the border with oncology.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Orthopaedics](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md) fixes the fracture and should hand the patient to a fracture liaison service for the question of why the bone broke; [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md) owns falls, which are half of the fracture problem. [Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) shares the bone, especially in patients on corticosteroids; [nephrology](../03-nephrology/chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md) co-manages renal bone disease; [endocrine surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) removes parathyroid adenomas; [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) and [haematology](../06-haematology/haematological-malignancies.md) share hypercalcaemia of malignancy and myeloma bone disease. [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) prescribes most bone protection and vitamin D.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Parathyroid hormone (PTH) | Hormone that raises plasma calcium by acting on bone, kidney and vitamin D activation |
| Calcium-sensing receptor | Parathyroid membrane receptor that detects ionised calcium and sets PTH release |
| Primary hyperparathyroidism | Autonomous PTH excess, usually from an adenoma; high calcium with unsuppressed PTH |
| Hypercalcaemia of malignancy | High calcium with suppressed PTH from tumour peptides or bone destruction |
| Tetany | Involuntary muscle contraction from low ionised calcium |
| Remodelling | Coupled osteoclast resorption and osteoblast formation that renews bone throughout life |
| Osteoporosis | Low bone mass and disrupted microarchitecture with fracture under minimal trauma |
| T-score | Bone density in standard deviations below the young adult mean; −2.5 defines osteoporosis |
| Osteomalacia | Defective mineralisation of formed bone from vitamin D or phosphate deficiency |
| Fragility fracture | A fracture from a fall at standing height or less; diagnostic of osteoporosis |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Low calcium intake causes osteoporosis."** Calcium sufficiency matters, but oestrogen loss, ageing, steroids and immobility drive most bone loss; calcium supplements alone barely reduce fractures.
- **"A normal bone density means no fracture risk."** Most fragility fractures occur in people whose density is above the osteoporosis threshold; age, falls and prior fracture matter as much.
- **"High calcium means too much calcium in the diet or supplements."** It almost always means a parathyroid adenoma or a malignancy; the PTH level tells which.
