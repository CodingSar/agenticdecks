# Obesity, lipids and metabolic syndrome

## Essence

Obesity is the storage of more energy as fat than the body can hold without harm, and it is now the upstream cause of much of the disease in this layer. Body weight is regulated by a hypothalamic loop that defends fat stores against loss far more vigorously than against gain, a bias that made sense across most of human history and fails in an environment of abundant energy-dense food and little required movement. Excess fat, especially around and inside the abdominal organs, makes tissues resistant to insulin and inflames them, producing the cluster of raised glucose, triglycerides, blood pressure and low high-density lipoprotein cholesterol called the metabolic syndrome, and sending fat to the liver. Lipid disorders are the related problem of too much cholesterol-carrying lipoprotein in the plasma, where it drives atherosclerosis in proportion to its concentration and to the years of exposure. Both are treated at the level of the mechanism: energy balance, appetite signalling and lipoprotein clearance.

## Core tenets

1. **Body weight is regulated, and the regulator is asymmetric.** Leptin from fat and gut hormones such as glucagon-like peptide 1 report the body's energy state to the hypothalamus, which adjusts hunger and expenditure ([energy balance](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/thermoregulation-and-energy-balance.md)). Loss of fat lowers leptin and triggers a powerful hunger and energy-sparing response; gain raises leptin but the response to it is weak, so the loop defends against famine, not feast ([evolutionary medicine](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/evolutionary-medicine.md)). This is why weight lost by dieting is regained in most people within a few years, and why obesity is best understood as a regulated set point moved upward, not a failure of will. Heritability is high (~40–70%), acting mostly through appetite genes; rare single-gene defects in leptin and melanocortin signalling prove the pathway.

2. **The harm of fat depends on where it is and what it does to insulin signalling.** Subcutaneous fat stores energy safely; visceral and ectopic fat (in liver, muscle, pancreas) does not. Enlarged adipocytes release free fatty acids and inflammatory cytokines and less adiponectin, and the fatty acids impair insulin signalling in liver and muscle ([insulin resistance](diabetes-mellitus.md)). The liver, resistant to insulin's suppression of glucose output yet still driven to make fat, exports triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and accumulates fat ([fatty liver disease](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md)). Waist circumference therefore predicts metabolic risk better than weight, and some people of normal weight carry metabolically harmful fat while some with high body mass index are metabolically healthy ([nutritional disease](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/nutritional-disease-obesity-and-malnutrition.md)).

3. **The metabolic syndrome is one mechanism with five faces.** Insulin resistance with compensatory hyperinsulinaemia produces raised fasting glucose, raised triglycerides, low high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol, raised blood pressure (through renal sodium retention and sympathetic activation) and central obesity; the definitions require three of the five. The cluster roughly doubles cardiovascular risk and multiplies the risk of type 2 diabetes several-fold, and it also underlies fatty liver, polycystic ovary syndrome, obstructive sleep apnoea, gout and several cancers. Treating the cluster means treating insulin resistance, which means weight loss and physical activity, which improve every component at once.

4. **Lipoproteins are the transport problem of an insoluble molecule, and the apolipoprotein B particles are the ones that cause disease.** Cholesterol and triglyceride travel in particles whose protein coats direct them ([lipid metabolism](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/lipid-metabolism.md)). Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL) and the other apolipoprotein B-containing particles below ~70 nm cross the endothelium, are retained in the arterial intima and oxidised, and start the [atherosclerotic](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/atherosclerosis.md) plaque. The evidence that LDL causes atherosclerosis is as strong as any in medicine: it is dose-dependent and cumulative over decades, people born with low LDL from loss-of-function variants are protected, those with familial hypercholesterolaemia (one in ~250, from LDL receptor and related gene defects) have premature disease, and lowering LDL by any means, in randomised trials, lowers events by about a fifth per 1 mmol/L ([cause and mechanism](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#cause-and-mechanism), [randomised trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md)). HDL cholesterol predicts low risk but raising it with drugs has not helped, which shows that a marker is not a cause.

5. **Lipid-lowering drugs work by making the liver clear more LDL.** Statins (atorvastatin) inhibit the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis, so hepatocytes express more LDL receptors and pull LDL from the plasma; ezetimibe blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption with the same downstream effect; antibodies and small interfering RNA against PCSK9, a protein that degrades the LDL receptor, prolong receptor life and lower LDL by a further half ([endocrine and metabolic drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/endocrine-and-metabolic-drugs.md)). The benefit is proportional to the absolute LDL fall and to baseline risk, so treatment is decided by estimating ten-year risk rather than by a cholesterol threshold ([risk prediction](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/risk-prediction-and-stratification.md)). Very high triglycerides are a separate problem: above ~10 mmol/L they cause pancreatitis, and are treated by removing alcohol and sugar, controlling diabetes and with fibrates.

6. **Obesity treatment now reaches the regulator, and the results scale with how deeply it does.** Diet and exercise produce 3–5% sustained loss on average and remain the base of everything, because they improve fitness and metabolic health beyond weight ([behaviour change](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/behaviour-change-and-the-placebo-effect.md)). Glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonists (semaglutide) and the dual agonist tirzepatide act on hypothalamic appetite circuits and slow gastric emptying, producing 15–20% loss in trials and reducing cardiovascular events, the first drugs to change the set point itself; weight returns when they stop. Bariatric surgery (gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy) produces 25–30% loss that lasts, puts most type 2 diabetes into remission within days, before the weight is lost, through changed gut hormone signalling, and lowers mortality. Each step down this list treats the mechanism more directly and costs more.

7. **Obesity is an environmental disease at population scale, so its prevention is not clinical.** Prevalence has roughly tripled worldwide since 1975 to about 16% of adults (2022), with faster rises in children and in low- and middle-income countries, and it follows socioeconomic gradients, more common in poorer groups in rich countries and richer groups in poor ones. Genes did not change over that period; the food environment, portion size, sugar-sweetened drinks, car-dependent cities and sedentary work did ([the host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment)). Taxation of sugary drinks, food labelling, reformulation and urban design are the interventions that act on the cause, and they belong to [public health and policy](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/social-determinants-and-health-inequity.md) rather than to the clinic.

8. **Who else is involved.** Nearly everyone. [Cardiology](../01-cardiology/ischemic-heart-disease.md) manages the atherosclerosis the lipids cause; [hepatology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md) the fatty liver; [bariatric surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) the operations; [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) the sleep apnoea; [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) the polycystic ovary syndrome and the pregnancy complicated by obesity; [orthopaedics](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md) and [rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/osteoarthritis-gout-and-regional-pain.md) the osteoarthritis and gout; [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) the thirteen cancers linked to obesity; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) the eating disorders and antipsychotic weight gain; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) childhood obesity; and [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), where almost all lipid and weight management happens, with dietitians and [lifestyle medicine](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/sports-occupational-and-lifestyle-medicine.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Body mass index | Weight divided by height squared; a crude screen for adiposity, not a measure of its distribution |
| Leptin | Adipocyte hormone signalling fat stores to the hypothalamus; its fall drives hunger |
| Visceral and ectopic fat | Fat within the abdomen and inside organs; metabolically harmful |
| Insulin resistance | Reduced tissue response to insulin; the link between obesity and the syndrome |
| Metabolic syndrome | Cluster of central obesity, raised glucose, raised triglycerides, low HDL and hypertension |
| Low-density lipoprotein (LDL) | Apolipoprotein B particle that carries cholesterol into the arterial wall |
| Familial hypercholesterolaemia | Inherited LDL receptor pathway defect with lifelong high LDL and early atherosclerosis |
| PCSK9 | Protein that degrades the LDL receptor; its inhibition lowers LDL |
| Bariatric surgery | Operations that reduce intake and alter gut hormones, producing large sustained weight loss |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Obesity is a failure of willpower."** Weight is regulated by a hypothalamic loop with strong heritability that defends fat stores; the environment, not character, changed since 1975.
- **"Dietary cholesterol is what raises blood cholesterol."** Saturated fat intake and, above all, hepatic LDL receptor activity set LDL; dietary cholesterol has a small effect in most people.
- **"Raising good cholesterol protects the heart."** HDL is a marker of low risk, but drugs that raise it have not reduced events; only lowering apolipoprotein B particles has.
- **"Bariatric surgery works by restricting food."** Its metabolic effects on gut hormones put diabetes into remission within days, before meaningful weight loss.
