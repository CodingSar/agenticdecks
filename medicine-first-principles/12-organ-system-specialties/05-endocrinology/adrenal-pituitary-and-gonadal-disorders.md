## Essence

The pituitary is a pea-sized gland that controls the thyroid, the adrenal cortex, the gonads, growth and lactation, and sits in a bony pocket beneath the optic chiasm. Its diseases and those of its target glands are the purest exercise in reading feedback loops: for each axis, the question is whether the fault is in the controller (pituitary or hypothalamus) or the gland, and whether it is deficiency or autonomous excess. Cortisol and aldosterone from the adrenal cortex, catecholamines from the adrenal medulla, and the sex steroids from the gonads each have a deficiency syndrome and an excess syndrome with a recognisable face. Two facts organise the clinical danger: cortisol deficiency is rapidly fatal under stress and is easily missed, and a pituitary tumour threatens by mass (pressing on the chiasm and the other hormone cells) as much as by secretion.

## Core tenets

1. **Each axis is read at two points, and the direction of the controller localises the fault.** For every target gland, a low end-hormone with a high pituitary hormone means primary gland failure; low with low means pituitary failure. Autonomous excess suppresses the controller. Because many hormones are pulsatile or circadian (cortisol peaks on waking, growth hormone at night), single random values mislead and dynamic tests are used: suppress the axis and see whether the hormone falls (dexamethasone for cortisol, glucose for growth hormone), or stimulate it and see whether it rises (synthetic corticotropin for the adrenal, insulin-induced hypoglycaemia for the whole pituitary) ([hormone axes](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/hormone-classes-axes-and-feedback.md)). Only after the biochemistry localises the lesion is it imaged, because pituitary and adrenal incidentalomas are common and mostly inert ([probability and uncertainty](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#probability-and-uncertainty)).

2. **Cortisol deficiency is the endocrine emergency that hides.** Cortisol maintains vascular tone, gluconeogenesis and the response to stress ([stress hormones](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/thyroid-adrenal-and-stress-hormones.md)). Primary adrenal failure (Addison's disease, mostly [autoimmune](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md) in high-income countries, tuberculous elsewhere) also loses aldosterone, so sodium is wasted and potassium retained, and the high corticotropin darkens the skin. The commonest cause of all, however, is suppression of the axis by therapeutic corticosteroids: weeks of treatment render the adrenals unable to respond, and stopping the drug or meeting an infection or operation without extra cover causes adrenal crisis, with hypotension, hyponatraemia, hypoglycaemia and collapse that looks like sepsis. The treatment is hydrocortisone, given before the results are back when the diagnosis is suspected, and the patient is taught to double replacement during illness ([perioperative risk](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/perioperative-risk-and-complications.md)).

3. **Cortisol excess reshapes the body, and the first question is whether the patient is taking it.** Cushing's syndrome produces central obesity with thin limbs, a round plethoric face, purple striae, thin bruising skin, proximal muscle weakness, hypertension, diabetes, osteoporosis and depression, by cortisol's catabolic, gluconeogenic and mineralocorticoid actions. Exogenous corticosteroids are far the commonest cause; of the endogenous causes, a pituitary adenoma secreting corticotropin (Cushing's disease) accounts for most, with adrenal adenomas and ectopic corticotropin from lung tumours the rest. Diagnosis shows loss of the circadian rhythm (a midnight cortisol that is not low) and failure to suppress with dexamethasone; corticotropin level then separates pituitary and ectopic (high) from adrenal (suppressed). Treatment removes the source surgically; drugs that inhibit steroid synthesis bridge.

4. **Aldosterone and catecholamine excess are curable hypertension, and both come from the adrenal.** Aldosterone retains sodium and excretes potassium; an adenoma or bilateral hyperplasia secreting it autonomously (primary aldosteronism) causes hypertension with low renin and often low potassium, and is found in perhaps 5–10% of hypertensives when sought ([hypertension](../01-cardiology/hypertension-and-vascular-disease.md)). A phaeochromocytoma of the adrenal medulla secretes catecholamines, causing paroxysms of headache, sweating and palpitations on a background of hypertension; plasma or urine metanephrines diagnose it, and alpha-blockade (phenoxybenzamine) must precede surgery or the handling of the tumour releases a lethal surge. About a third are inherited, so genetic testing is routine.

5. **Pituitary tumours cause harm by what they make and what they crush.** Adenomas are common (found in ~10% of autopsies) and mostly inert. Secreting ones produce prolactin (galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, hypogonadism), growth hormone (acromegaly: enlarging hands, feet and jaw, sweating, diabetes, cardiomyopathy) or corticotropin. Large tumours of any kind compress the optic chiasm above (bitemporal hemianopia) and the normal gland beside them, so hormones are lost in a typical order, growth hormone and gonadotropins first, corticotropin and TSH last ([head and neck anatomy](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/head-neck-and-spine.md)). Magnetic resonance imaging shows the tumour and its relation to the chiasm ([MRI](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/magnetic-resonance-imaging.md)). Prolactinomas shrink with dopamine agonists (cabergoline), because dopamine is the hypothalamic brake on prolactin; most others need transsphenoidal surgery, with radiotherapy and somatostatin analogues for residual acromegaly. Sudden haemorrhage into an adenoma (apoplexy) causes headache, visual loss and acute cortisol deficiency, and is a neurosurgical and endocrine emergency.

6. **The gonadal axis fails at either end, and the pattern of gonadotropins says which.** Low oestrogen or testosterone with high luteinising and follicle-stimulating hormones means the gonad has failed (menopause, Turner and Klinefelter syndromes, chemotherapy, autoimmune oophoritis); low with low means the hypothalamus or pituitary has, from tumour, prolactin excess, extreme weight loss or exercise, or systemic illness ([reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md)). Polycystic ovary syndrome, the commonest endocrine disorder of women (~8–13%), is a different problem: insulin resistance and excess ovarian androgen disrupt follicle maturation, causing oligomenorrhoea, hirsutism and infertility, and linking the gonadal axis to the [metabolic syndrome](obesity-lipids-and-metabolic-syndrome.md). Replacement of sex steroids treats symptoms and protects bone; restoring fertility requires gonadotropins or the treatment of the cause ([fertility and menopause](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/fertility-contraception-and-menopause.md)).

7. **Water balance belongs to the posterior pituitary, and its failure is a sodium disorder.** Antidiuretic hormone from the hypothalamus is stored in the posterior pituitary; its loss after surgery, trauma or infiltration causes cranial diabetes insipidus, litres of dilute urine and hypernatraemia if the patient cannot drink; its inappropriate excess from the brain, lung or drugs causes hyponatraemia ([electrolyte disorders](../03-nephrology/electrolyte-and-acid-base-disorders.md)). A water deprivation test distinguishes true deficiency (urine stays dilute until synthetic desmopressin is given) from renal resistance and from habitual over-drinking.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Neurosurgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) removes pituitary tumours and [ophthalmology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md) maps the visual fields before and after. [Endocrine surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) removes adrenal tumours after endocrine preparation. [Anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) covers the steroid-dependent and the phaeochromocytoma patient. [Cardiology](../01-cardiology/hypertension-and-vascular-disease.md) refers resistant or hypokalaemic hypertension. [Obstetrics and gynaecology](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) share polycystic ovary syndrome, menopause, infertility and the pituitary in pregnancy. [Oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) sends ectopic hormone syndromes and the hypophysitis and adrenalitis caused by immune checkpoint inhibitors; [clinical genetics](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/README.md) is involved in the inherited tumour syndromes. [Psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) shares the depression of Cushing's and the amenorrhoea of eating disorders.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dynamic test | Suppressing or stimulating an axis to test whether it is still under control |
| Adrenal crisis | Acute cortisol deficiency with hypotension, hyponatraemia and hypoglycaemia; fatal if untreated |
| Addison's disease | Primary adrenal failure, usually autoimmune, losing cortisol and aldosterone |
| Cushing's syndrome | Cortisol excess from any source; Cushing's disease is the pituitary form |
| Primary aldosteronism | Autonomous aldosterone secretion causing hypertension with low renin |
| Phaeochromocytoma | Catecholamine-secreting tumour of the adrenal medulla |
| Incidentaloma | A gland mass found by imaging done for another reason; common, mostly harmless |
| Hypopituitarism | Deficiency of one or more anterior pituitary hormones, usually from tumour or its treatment |
| Hypogonadism | Sex steroid deficiency; hypergonadotropic (gonadal) or hypogonadotropic (central) |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A random cortisol diagnoses adrenal disease."** Cortisol is circadian and stress-responsive; deficiency and excess are shown by dynamic tests, not single values.
- **"Steroid tablets can be stopped when the illness is better."** After weeks of treatment the adrenals are suppressed; abrupt withdrawal or an intercurrent illness without cover can cause fatal crisis.
- **"A pituitary tumour on a scan needs removing."** Most incidental adenomas neither secrete nor grow; surgery is for secretion that cannot be controlled or for mass effect.
- **"Low testosterone in an older man means his testes have failed."** Obesity, illness and opioids suppress the hypothalamus; gonadotropins must be measured to tell.
