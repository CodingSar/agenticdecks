## What this area is about

Nephrology is the medicine of the organ that keeps the internal environment constant. The kidney filters about 180 litres of plasma a day and reabsorbs 99% of it, adjusting what it keeps so that the volume, osmolality, potassium, acid and calcium of the extracellular fluid stay within narrow limits whatever the diet and whatever the other organs do. It therefore fails in two ways: it stops filtering (acute injury or chronic loss of nephrons), or it keeps filtering but regulates wrongly (electrolyte and acid–base disorders, which are as often caused by other organs, drugs and hormones as by the kidney itself). The question the specialty answers is: is the kidney the victim, the culprit, or the messenger?

The unifying idea is **the kidney as the body's compensator, and compensation as the thing that fails last and shows least**. A patient can lose half of all nephrons and have a normal creatinine, because the survivors filter more; a failing heart or a cirrhotic liver keeps its blood pressure by making the kidney retain salt; a lung that retains carbon dioxide is rescued by a kidney that retains bicarbonate. Because the kidney hides other organs' failures and its own, nephrology reads small numbers (creatinine, urine albumin, potassium, bicarbonate) with unusual care and asks what they imply about the whole patient.

   perfusion falls (shock, heart, liver, drugs) --> ACUTE KIDNEY INJURY --> recovery or scar
   immune attack on glomerulus / tubular defect  --> GLOMERULAR & TUBULAR DISEASE --+
   diabetes, hypertension, ageing --------------------> nephron loss ---------------+--> CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE --> dialysis, transplant

Read acute kidney injury first: it introduces the filtration rate, creatinine as its marker, and the pre-renal, renal and post-renal logic that organises the whole specialty. Chronic kidney disease is what follows when nephrons are not recovered, and introduces the long-term compensations (hyperfiltration, secondary hyperparathyroidism, anaemia) and their replacement by dialysis. The glomerular and tubular leaf is the intrinsic pathology that causes both. The electrolyte leaf stands apart: it is the regulation the kidney does for the whole body and the place where every other specialty's patients end up on a nephrologist's list.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md):** the kidney sets blood pressure and volume; heart failure and kidney failure cause each other (cardiorenal syndrome), and cardiovascular disease kills most patients with chronic kidney disease before they need dialysis.
- **[Endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/README.md):** diabetes is the leading cause of kidney failure worldwide; the kidney makes erythropoietin, activates vitamin D and clears insulin, so its failure causes anaemia, bone disease and hypoglycaemia; aldosterone, antidiuretic hormone and parathyroid hormone act on it.
- **[Pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md):** the two organs share acid–base control and are jointly attacked in the pulmonary–renal vasculitides.
- **[Gastroenterology and hepatology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md):** cirrhosis causes a functional renal failure (hepatorenal syndrome) by the same haemodynamic logic as heart failure; vomiting and diarrhoea are the commonest causes of electrolyte disorders.
- **[Rheumatology and clinical immunology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md):** lupus, vasculitis and the immune glomerulonephritides are rheumatology's diseases in nephrology's organ, and immunosuppression is jointly managed.
- **[Haematology](../06-haematology/README.md):** myeloma damages tubules with light chains; the kidney's erythropoietin deficit causes anaemia; thrombotic microangiopathies injure both.
- **[Infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md):** sepsis is the commonest cause of acute kidney injury in hospital, and dialysis access is a route for infection.

## Interfaces with other specialties

Nephrology's defining hand-overs are with [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md), where acute kidney injury affects about half of patients and continuous [renal replacement](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/organ-support-and-icu-syndromes.md) is delivered by intensivists with nephrology advice on timing and dose, and with [urology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md), which owns the obstructed kidney: a dilated collecting system on ultrasound is a surgical problem until it is drained, and only then a medical one. [Transplant surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) provides the kidney; nephrology selects the recipient, manages the immunosuppression and the rejection, and the two share the patient for life. [Vascular surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) creates and repairs the arteriovenous fistula that makes haemodialysis possible. [Anaesthesia and perioperative medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) asks about renal reserve before surgery and contrast, because a kidney with no reserve is one hypotensive episode from dialysis. [Emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) treats the hyperkalaemia and the pulmonary oedema of renal failure before nephrology arrives, and [poisoning](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/poisoning-and-overdose.md) uses dialysis to remove some toxins.

In layer 14, [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) manages the large majority of chronic kidney disease, which is mostly mild and mostly about controlling blood pressure, diabetes and cardiovascular risk; referral is for a falling filtration rate, heavy proteinuria or an unexplained cause. [Obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) shares pre-eclampsia (a glomerular disease of pregnancy) and the pregnant woman with kidney disease. [Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) owns congenital and childhood nephrotic disease. [Geriatrics and palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) are partners in the decision, common in people over 80, that conservative management without dialysis is a better choice than a machine three times a week. The emergency that crosses every boundary is hyperkalaemia, which any specialty can cause and nephrology is called to fix.

## Connections across layers

From [06 Physiology](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/README.md) nephrology takes filtration, tubular transport, volume regulation and the handling of potassium, calcium and acid; from [06 integrative principles](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/README.md) the fluid compartments and acid–base; from [07](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/README.md) the immune complex and autoantibody mechanisms of glomerulonephritis; from [08 Pathology](../../08-pathology/README.md) shock, ischaemic necrosis and fibrosis; from [09 Pharmacology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/elimination-half-life-and-dosing.md) the fact that the kidney eliminates most drugs, so its failure changes every dose; from [11](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/clinical-chemistry-and-reference-ranges.md) the chemistry that is the specialty's main instrument. It gives [13](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/README.md) the physiology of fluid, electrolyte and renal support.

- [Gradients and flow](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#gradients-and-flow): filtration is a pressure gradient across a membrane; the countercurrent system builds an osmotic one.
