# Glomerular and tubular diseases

## Essence

These are the diseases of the nephron itself rather than of its blood supply or outflow. The glomerulus is a filter made of fenestrated endothelium, a basement membrane and podocytes with slit diaphragms, built to pass water and small solutes while holding back cells and albumin; its diseases are mostly immune, and they announce themselves by what leaks through (protein, blood) rather than by pain. The tubule is a chain of segment-specific transporters; its diseases, mostly genetic or toxic, announce themselves by what is lost or retained in the urine. Two syndromes organise the glomerular diseases: nephrotic (the filter is leaky to protein) and nephritic (the filter is inflamed and bleeds), and a biopsy read by light, immunofluorescence and electron microscopy names the cause. The unifying idea is that the urine is a direct read-out of which part of the nephron is damaged.

## Core tenets

1. **The glomerular barrier is selective by size and charge, so damage to it is read in the urine.** The basement membrane and podocyte slit diaphragms exclude albumin (molecular mass ~66 kDa) by size and by negative charge ([filtration](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/glomerular-filtration-and-clearance.md)). Podocyte injury lets albumin through (proteinuria, measured as the albumin-to-creatinine ratio); capillary wall rupture lets red cells through, which are deformed on their way down the tubule and may be moulded into red-cell casts. Proteinuria without casts means a podocyte problem; dysmorphic red cells and casts mean glomerular inflammation. A urine dipstick and a microscope do most of the localisation before any biopsy.

2. **Nephrotic syndrome is the consequences of losing protein in the urine.** More than ~3.5 g of protein a day leaves the plasma, albumin falls, plasma oncotic pressure falls and fluid moves into the interstitium ([oedema](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/edema-and-fluid-imbalance.md)), aided by primary renal sodium retention. The liver responds by over-producing lipoproteins (hyperlipidaemia), and the loss of antithrombin and immunoglobulins in the urine causes thrombosis and infection. The causes are podocyte diseases: minimal change disease (the commonest in children, responsive to corticosteroids), focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, membranous nephropathy (autoantibodies against a podocyte antigen, the phospholipase A2 receptor, in most adult cases), and diabetic nephropathy, which is the commonest cause of all.

3. **Nephritic syndrome is inflammation inside the glomerulus.** Immune complexes deposited or formed in the capillary wall activate complement and recruit neutrophils ([type III hypersensitivity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)); antibodies against the basement membrane or against neutrophil cytoplasmic antigens injure it directly ([autoimmunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md)). The inflamed glomerulus bleeds (haematuria), filters less (rising creatinine, oliguria), and retains sodium (hypertension, oedema). Immunoglobulin A nephropathy is the commonest glomerulonephritis worldwide; post-streptococcal nephritis is the classic immune-complex disease; lupus nephritis, anti-glomerular basement membrane disease and the vasculitides are the forms that destroy a kidney in weeks (rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis, seen on biopsy as crescents) and demand immunosuppression at once.

4. **The biopsy names the disease by three microscopies, and the name decides the treatment.** Light microscopy shows the pattern of injury (proliferation, sclerosis, crescents); immunofluorescence shows which immunoglobulins and complement are deposited and where (granular immune complexes versus linear anti-basement-membrane antibody); electron microscopy shows the deposits' position and the podocyte foot-process effacement of nephrotic disease ([histopathology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/histopathology-cytology-and-molecular-diagnostics.md)). Serology narrows the field first: complement levels, antinuclear and anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies, anti-phospholipase A2 receptor antibodies. Because the treatments range from nothing to cytotoxic immunosuppression, the diagnosis must be precise.

5. **Treatment suppresses the immune injury, lowers glomerular pressure, and treats the consequences.** Corticosteroids, cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate, calcineurin inhibitors and B-cell-depleting antibodies (rituximab) each interrupt a step of the immune attack ([immunomodulatory drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anti-inflammatory-and-immunomodulatory-drugs.md)); plasma exchange removes circulating antibody in anti-basement-membrane disease. In every proteinuric disease, blockade of the renin–angiotensin system reduces glomerular pressure and protein leak and slows progression toward [chronic kidney disease](chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md). Diuretics treat oedema, statins the lipids, anticoagulation the thrombotic risk when albumin is very low. The harm of immunosuppression is weighed against the harm of the disease, which the biopsy's activity and chronicity scores estimate.

6. **Tubular diseases are failures of specific transporters, and the urine shows which.** The proximal tubule reabsorbs glucose, phosphate, amino acids and bicarbonate; its generalised failure (Fanconi syndrome, from myeloma light chains, drugs or inherited defects) spills them all ([tubular transport](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/tubular-transport-and-the-countercurrent-system.md)). Inherited loss of the thick ascending limb or distal transporters (Bartter and Gitelman syndromes) mimics loop and thiazide diuretics, with salt wasting and low potassium. Failure of distal acid secretion or proximal bicarbonate reabsorption causes renal tubular acidosis: a metabolic acidosis with a normal anion gap and an inappropriately alkaline urine. The collecting duct's insensitivity to antidiuretic hormone (nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, from lithium or inherited mutations) wastes water. Each is diagnosed by comparing what the urine contains with what the plasma chemistry says it should.

7. **Interstitial nephritis and the cystic diseases injure the tubule from outside.** Drugs (proton-pump inhibitors, non-steroidal anti-inflammatories, antibiotics) can provoke a hypersensitivity infiltrate of the interstitium, causing acute kidney injury with sterile pyuria that resolves when the drug stops. Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, the commonest single-gene cause of kidney failure (mutations in the polycystin genes, ~1 in 1,000), grows fluid-filled cysts from tubular epithelium that compress and replace the parenchyma over decades ([single-gene disorders](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/single-gene-and-chromosomal-disorders.md)); a vasopressin receptor antagonist slows cyst growth, the first mechanism-based treatment for it.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Rheumatology and clinical immunology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/connective-tissue-disease-and-vasculitis.md) co-own lupus nephritis and the vasculitides, and the joint clinic is standard. [Haematology](../06-haematology/haematological-malignancies.md) is involved when light chains from myeloma cause tubular or glomerular disease. [Infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) matters for the hepatitis B and C, HIV and streptococcal infections that trigger glomerulonephritis, and for infections under immunosuppression. [Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) manages most nephrotic syndrome in children and the inherited tubulopathies. [Clinical genetics](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/README.md) and [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) are involved in polycystic disease and in pre-eclampsia, a podocyte disease of pregnancy.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Podocyte | Epithelial cell whose interdigitating foot processes form the glomerular slit diaphragm |
| Nephrotic syndrome | Heavy proteinuria, low albumin, oedema and hyperlipidaemia from a leaky filter |
| Nephritic syndrome | Haematuria, hypertension, oliguria and reduced filtration from an inflamed glomerulus |
| Red-cell cast | Cylinder of red cells moulded in a tubule; proof that bleeding is glomerular |
| Crescent | Proliferation of cells in Bowman's space in rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis |
| Immune complex deposition | Antigen–antibody aggregates lodged in the glomerular wall, activating complement |
| Renal tubular acidosis | Metabolic acidosis from failure of tubular acid handling, with a normal anion gap |
| Fanconi syndrome | Generalised proximal tubular dysfunction with loss of glucose, phosphate, amino acids and bicarbonate |
| Interstitial nephritis | Inflammation of the tubulointerstitium, often a drug hypersensitivity |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Blood in the urine means a kidney infection or a stone."** Dysmorphic red cells and casts point to the glomerulus; infections and stones give normally shaped cells and are painful.
- **"Proteinuria is always kidney disease."** Fever, exercise and upright posture cause transient or orthostatic proteinuria in healthy people; persistence on repeat testing is what matters.
- **"Glomerulonephritis is one disease."** It is a dozen with different mechanisms, prognoses and treatments, and only the biopsy and serology distinguish them.
- **"Oedema in nephrotic syndrome is just low albumin."** Primary renal sodium retention contributes substantially, which is why diuretics work even before albumin recovers.
