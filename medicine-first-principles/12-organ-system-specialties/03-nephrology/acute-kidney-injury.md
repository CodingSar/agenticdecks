## Essence

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) is a fall in glomerular filtration over hours to days, detected as a rising creatinine or a falling urine output. It is common (10–20% of hospital admissions, 2010s–2020s data), usually caused by something other than kidney disease, and usually reversible if that something is corrected quickly. The organising idea is anatomical: blood must reach the glomerulus (pre-renal), the nephron must work (intrinsic renal), and urine must leave (post-renal). Most AKI is pre-renal, the kidney's response to low perfusion from bleeding, sepsis, heart failure, dehydration or drugs that disable its own autoregulation; if the insult persists, the tubular cells, which have the highest oxygen demand of any in the body outside the brain and heart, die, and pre-renal becomes intrinsic. The treatment reverses the cause and protects the kidney from further insult while it recovers; nothing yet speeds the recovery itself.

## Core tenets

1. **Filtration is a pressure gradient the kidney defends, and the defence has a limit.** The glomerulus filters because capillary hydrostatic pressure exceeds plasma oncotic pressure plus Bowman's capsule pressure; the kidney holds that pressure constant across a wide range of arterial pressure by dilating the afferent arteriole (prostaglandins) and constricting the efferent (angiotensin II) ([glomerular filtration](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/glomerular-filtration-and-clearance.md)). Below a mean arterial pressure of roughly 65 mmHg autoregulation fails and filtration falls with pressure. Two drug classes disable the defence: non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs remove the afferent dilatation, and angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors remove the efferent constriction. Each is safe alone and dangerous in a dehydrated patient, which is the commonest preventable AKI.

2. **Pre-renal AKI is a healthy kidney doing what evolution asked.** Low perfusion activates renin, sympathetic drive and antidiuretic hormone; the tubules reabsorb sodium and water avidly, so the urine is concentrated and nearly sodium-free. Filtration falls because flow has fallen, not because the nephron is damaged, and restoring volume or output restores it within hours. The cause is outside the kidney: [haemorrhage, sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md), vomiting, diarrhoea, [heart failure](../01-cardiology/heart-failure.md), [cirrhosis](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md) (hepatorenal syndrome is the extreme case).

3. **Sustained ischaemia kills the tubule, and intrinsic AKI follows.** The proximal tubule and thick ascending limb consume oxygen reabsorbing sodium in the poorly oxygenated outer medulla. When perfusion stays low, or a toxin (aminoglycosides, radiographic contrast, myoglobin from crushed muscle, cisplatin) injures them directly, the cells lose polarity, detach and die by [necrosis and apoptosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/cell-injury-adaptation-and-death.md). Dead cells block tubules, filtrate leaks back across the denuded wall, and tubuloglomerular feedback shuts filtration down. The urine now contains sodium (the tubule cannot reabsorb it) and granular casts (shed cells). Recovery requires the surviving cells to divide and re-line the tubule, which takes one to three weeks; dialysis may bridge that interval.

4. **Obstruction causes AKI by raising the pressure downstream, and it is the cause not to miss.** Urine that cannot leave raises pressure in Bowman's capsule until it cancels the filtration gradient. Prostatic enlargement, stones, tumours and a blocked catheter are the causes; both kidneys, or the only functioning one, must be obstructed for creatinine to rise. Ultrasound shows the dilated collecting system in minutes and relief of obstruction (a catheter, a nephrostomy) reverses the injury, which makes it the first test in any unexplained AKI ([ultrasound](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md)).

5. **Creatinine is a late and lagging marker; urine output and urine chemistry are faster.** Creatinine is produced at a steady rate from muscle and cleared by filtration, so its concentration rises only after filtration has fallen and only as fast as production allows: a kidney that stops entirely raises creatinine by perhaps 100 micromol/L a day, so the first day's value underestimates the injury ([clinical chemistry](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/clinical-chemistry-and-reference-ranges.md)). Staging by the proportional rise in creatinine and by hours of oliguria is the convention. Urine sodium and osmolality separate pre-renal (concentrated, sodium-retaining) from tubular injury; microscopy shows casts; blood shows the dangers: potassium, acid, urea, fluid.

6. **The symptoms are few and the dangers are chemical.** Most AKI is silent and found on a blood test. What kills is hyperkalaemia (the kidney excretes 90% of dietary potassium; failure plus tissue breakdown plus acidosis raises it to levels that stop the heart), metabolic acidosis (acid produced by metabolism cannot be excreted), pulmonary oedema (retained fluid), and uraemia (retained nitrogenous waste causing encephalopathy, pericarditis and bleeding). These four are the indications for emergency dialysis when they cannot be controlled otherwise ([electrolyte and acid–base disorders](electrolyte-and-acid-base-disorders.md)).

7. **Treatment restores perfusion, removes the insult and adjusts every drug.** Intravenous fluid corrects hypovolaemia and is harmful in overload, so the volume state is assessed before it is given ([fluids](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/fluids-nutrition-and-blood-products.md)); sepsis is treated; nephrotoxic and autoregulation-blocking drugs are stopped; obstruction is relieved. Because the kidney eliminates most drugs, every renally cleared drug's dose must fall with the filtration rate or it accumulates ([elimination and dosing](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/elimination-half-life-and-dosing.md)). Dialysis replaces the lost functions temporarily. An episode of AKI, even with full recovery, roughly doubles the long-term risk of [chronic kidney disease](chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md), because recovery is incomplete at the nephron level.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) sees the sickest half and runs continuous renal replacement; [urology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) relieves obstruction; [cardiology](../01-cardiology/heart-failure.md) and [hepatology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/liver-disease.md) send the cardiorenal and hepatorenal patients, in whom the kidney is the messenger of another organ's failure. [Anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/perioperative-risk-and-complications.md) and [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) cause a large share through hypotension, blood loss and contrast, and [pharmacists](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md) find the drug causes; [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) shares myeloma and thrombotic microangiopathy. [Geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) matters because the old have less reserve and more drugs.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Glomerular filtration rate | Volume of plasma filtered per unit time; ~120 mL/min in a healthy adult |
| Creatinine | Muscle metabolite cleared by filtration; its rise lags the fall in filtration |
| Oliguria | Urine output below ~0.5 mL/kg/h; an early functional sign of AKI |
| Pre-renal | Reduced filtration from reduced perfusion with an intact nephron |
| Acute tubular injury | Death and shedding of tubular cells after ischaemia or toxin; the commonest intrinsic AKI |
| Post-renal | Reduced filtration from obstruction to urine outflow |
| Autoregulation | Afferent dilatation and efferent constriction that keep filtration constant across arterial pressures |
| Granular casts | Cylinders of shed tubular cells in urine; a sign of tubular injury |
| Renal replacement therapy | Dialysis or filtration to substitute for the kidney's excretory function |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A normal creatinine means normal kidneys."** Creatinine rises only after half or more of filtration is lost and lags by a day or more; it also depends on muscle mass, so a frail old woman with a "normal" value may have severely reduced filtration.
- **"AKI is a kidney disease."** Most AKI is the kidney's correct response to a circulatory, septic, obstructive or pharmacological insult elsewhere; the treatment is of the cause.
- **"Giving fluid always helps the kidneys."** It helps a hypovolaemic kidney and drowns an overloaded one; in established tubular injury the kidney cannot excrete what is given.
