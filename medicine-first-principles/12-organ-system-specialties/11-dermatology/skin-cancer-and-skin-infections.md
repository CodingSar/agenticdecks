# Skin cancer and skin infections

## Essence

The skin faces the two external hazards no other organ meets directly: ultraviolet radiation and the microbial world. Ultraviolet light damages keratinocyte and melanocyte DNA every day of life, and the skin cancers are the cumulative failure of repair: basal cell carcinoma, which is the commonest human cancer and almost never spreads; squamous cell carcinoma, which can; and melanoma, which is rarer but accounts for most skin-cancer deaths because it metastasises early. Skin infections are the breach of the barrier by organisms that normally live on it or arrive from outside: bacteria causing impetigo, cellulitis and the surgical emergency of necrotising fasciitis; viruses causing warts, herpes and the exanthems; fungi living on keratin; and parasites burrowing in it. The two halves share a logic: the skin's defence is a physical barrier plus surveillance, cancer and infection are both failures of that defence, and immunosuppression unmasks both at once.

## Core tenets

1. **Ultraviolet B writes a specific signature into DNA, and the skin cancers carry it.** Ultraviolet B photons (280–315 nm) are absorbed by adjacent pyrimidines and fuse them into cyclobutane dimers; nucleotide excision repair removes most, but the errors that remain are cytosine-to-thymine transitions at dipyrimidine sites, the ultraviolet mutational signature found in TP53 of squamous and basal cell carcinomas and throughout melanoma genomes ([waves, sound, light and radiation](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md), [DNA replication and repair](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/03-genetic-information-flow/dna-replication-and-repair.md), [causes of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md)). Xeroderma pigmentosum, in which excision repair is inherited defective, produces skin cancers in childhood and proves the mechanism. Melanin absorbs ultraviolet light, so risk falls steeply with darker skin; fair skin, freckling, many moles, intermittent intense burning exposure and sunbeds raise melanoma risk, while cumulative exposure drives the keratinocyte cancers.

2. **The three cancers differ in cell of origin, and that predicts their behaviour.** Basal cell carcinoma arises from the basal layer or follicle under Hedgehog-pathway activation (PTCH1 loss), grows slowly as a pearly nodule with rolled edge and surface vessels, destroys locally and metastasises almost never; excision cures, and inhibitors of the pathway treat the rare advanced case. Squamous cell carcinoma arises from suprabasal keratinocytes, often from a precursor actinic keratosis, grows as a scaly or ulcerated nodule, and metastasises in a few percent, more on the ear and lip, in large or deep tumours, and in the immunosuppressed, in whom it is the dominant cancer after organ transplantation ([transplant immunology](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md)). Melanoma arises from melanocytes, often with BRAF or NRAS mutations, spreads first within the epidermis then invades the dermis, and once in the dermis gains access to lymphatics and blood ([invasion and metastasis](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/invasion-metastasis-and-the-tumour-microenvironment.md)).

3. **In melanoma, depth is destiny.** The ABCDE features (asymmetry, border irregularity, colour variation, diameter over 6 mm, evolution) and the "ugly duckling" that differs from a patient's other moles identify the suspect; dermoscopy sharpens the call ([skin lesion morphology](skin-lesion-morphology-and-diagnosis.md)). Once excised, the Breslow thickness, the vertical depth of invasion in millimetres, is the strongest predictor of survival: thin melanomas under a millimetre are cured in over 95% of cases by excision alone, thick ones carry a substantial risk of spread. Sentinel node biopsy stages the regional nodes; for metastatic disease, melanoma's high ultraviolet mutation burden makes it one of the most immunogenic tumours, and checkpoint inhibitors and BRAF/MEK inhibitors have raised five-year survival from under 10% to around half (2010s–2020s) ([systemic anticancer therapy](../07-oncology/systemic-anticancer-therapy.md)).

4. **Primary prevention and early detection work because the cause and the organ are both accessible.** Sun protection, shade and sunscreen reduce keratinocyte cancers and, in randomised trials, melanoma; sunbed bans reduce exposure in the young ([levels of prevention](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/levels-of-prevention.md)). Population screening for melanoma has not been shown to reduce mortality and risks overdiagnosis of indolent lesions, but surveillance of high-risk individuals with total-body photography and dermoscopy finds thin melanomas ([screening principles and biases](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md)). Incidence of melanoma has risen several-fold in fair-skinned populations since the 1970s while mortality has risen far less, which is partly earlier detection and partly overdiagnosis.

5. **Bacterial skin infection is depth plus organism, and the deepest is a surgical emergency.** Impetigo is *Staphylococcus aureus* or *Streptococcus pyogenes* in the superficial epidermis, crusted and contagious. Cellulitis is streptococcal or staphylococcal infection of dermis and subcutaneous fat, spreading, hot and tender, entering through a crack, ulcer or fungal toe web; it is usually unilateral, and bilateral "cellulitis" is almost always venous eczema or oedema ([acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md)). Necrotising fasciitis is infection racing along the fascial plane beneath, where it is invisible from the surface: pain out of proportion to the signs, rapid systemic toxicity and later skin necrosis mark it, and only immediate surgical debridement, not antibiotics alone, saves the patient ([fever and sepsis](../08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md), [trauma and haemorrhage control](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md)). Toxin-mediated syndromes (staphylococcal scalded skin, toxic shock) show that a local infection can injure the whole skin or body through a circulating toxin ([pathogenesis, virulence and toxins](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/pathogenesis-virulence-and-toxins.md)).

6. **Viral, fungal and parasitic infections exploit the skin's own biology.** Human papillomaviruses infect basal keratinocytes and drive the benign hyperproliferation of warts, while high-risk types cause anogenital and some cutaneous squamous carcinomas ([viruses](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/viruses.md)). Herpes simplex and varicella-zoster establish latency in sensory ganglia and return along the nerve, which is why zoster is dermatomal and its pain can persist as post-herpetic neuralgia ([HIV and chronic viral infections](../08-infectious-diseases/hiv-and-chronic-viral-infections.md)). Dermatophyte fungi digest keratin and so live only in the dead layers of skin, hair and nail, producing scaling rings and crumbling nails that are confirmed by microscopy of scrapings ([fungi, protozoa and helminths](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/fungi-protozoa-and-helminths.md)). The scabies mite burrows in the stratum corneum and its itch is a delayed hypersensitivity to the mite, which is why it begins weeks after infestation and persists after cure.

7. **Immunosuppression unmasks both halves at once.** Transplant recipients develop squamous cell carcinoma at some 65–250 times the population rate, together with widespread warts, because T-cell surveillance normally clears both papillomavirus-infected and ultraviolet-mutated keratinocytes ([transplant and tumour immunology](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md), [immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)). HIV brings Kaposi sarcoma, severe herpes and fungal disease; biologics bring reactivated zoster. This is the clearest demonstration in medicine that the immune system suppresses cancer as well as infection, and it makes skin surveillance a routine part of care for every immunosuppressed patient.

8. **Who else is involved.** Skin cancer is a multidisciplinary disease: dermatology diagnoses and excises most lesions, [plastic and reconstructive surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) reconstructs the face and performs wide excisions and sentinel node biopsy, [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) gives immunotherapy and targeted therapy for melanoma, [clinical oncology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/radiotherapy-and-radiation-biology.md) irradiates inoperable keratinocyte cancers, and histopathology reports the Breslow depth that decides everything. [Transplant physicians](../03-nephrology/README.md), [rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) and [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) refer immunosuppressed patients for surveillance. Cellulitis is treated in [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) and [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md); necrotising fasciitis belongs to [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md), [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) and [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md); [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) sees impetigo, warts and the viral exanthems; and [public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/environmental-and-planetary-health.md) runs sun-protection and papillomavirus vaccination campaigns.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ultraviolet signature | Cytosine-to-thymine mutations at dipyrimidine sites from pyrimidine dimers |
| Actinic keratosis | Sun-induced dysplastic keratinocyte lesion; precursor of squamous cell carcinoma |
| Basal cell carcinoma | Commonest cancer; locally destructive, almost never metastatic; Hedgehog-pathway driven |
| Breslow thickness | Depth of melanoma invasion in millimetres; the dominant prognostic factor |
| ABCDE | Asymmetry, border, colour, diameter, evolution: features of a suspicious pigmented lesion |
| Sentinel node biopsy | Sampling the first draining node to stage melanoma |
| Cellulitis | Spreading bacterial infection of dermis and subcutis, usually streptococcal |
| Necrotising fasciitis | Rapidly spreading infection along fascia requiring immediate debridement |
| Dermatophyte | Keratin-digesting fungus confined to dead skin, hair and nail |
| Latency and reactivation | Persistence of herpesviruses in ganglia with return along the nerve as zoster |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Skin cancer is not serious."** Basal cell carcinoma rarely kills, but melanoma causes most of the roughly 60,000 skin-cancer deaths a year worldwide (2020), and squamous cell carcinoma kills the immunosuppressed and the neglected.
- **"Sunscreen prevents melanoma, so burning is the only risk."** Sunscreen helps, but cumulative dose, skin type, mole count and genetics matter; and sunscreen used to prolong exposure can increase total dose.
- **"Bilateral red legs are cellulitis."** Infection is almost always unilateral; bilateral redness is venous stasis, eczema or oedema, and treating it with antibiotics is a common error.
