## Essence

The skin faces the two external hazards no other organ meets directly: ultraviolet radiation and the microbial world. Ultraviolet light damages skin DNA every day of life, and the skin cancers are the cumulative failure of repair: basal cell carcinoma, the commonest human cancer, which almost never spreads; squamous cell carcinoma, which can; and melanoma, rarer but responsible for most skin-cancer deaths because it metastasises early. Skin infections are breaches of the barrier by bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites, and their depth decides their danger. Both are failures of the same defence, barrier plus immune surveillance, and immunosuppression unmasks both at once.

## Core tenets

1. **Ultraviolet B writes a specific signature into DNA, and the skin cancers carry it.** Ultraviolet B photons (280–315 nm) fuse adjacent pyrimidines into dimers; nucleotide excision repair removes most, and the errors that remain are cytosine-to-thymine transitions at dipyrimidine sites, the ultraviolet signature found in TP53 of keratinocyte cancers and throughout melanoma genomes ([waves, sound, light and radiation](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md), [DNA replication and repair](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/03-genetic-information-flow/dna-replication-and-repair.md), [causes of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md)). Xeroderma pigmentosum, inherited failure of excision repair, produces skin cancers in childhood and proves the mechanism. Melanin absorbs ultraviolet light, so risk falls steeply with darker skin; intermittent burning exposure drives melanoma, cumulative exposure the keratinocyte cancers.

2. **The three cancers differ in cell of origin, and that predicts their behaviour.** Basal cell carcinoma arises from the basal layer under Hedgehog-pathway activation (PTCH1 loss), grows slowly as a pearly nodule, destroys locally and metastasises almost never; excision cures. Squamous cell carcinoma arises from suprabasal keratinocytes, often from a precursor actinic keratosis, and metastasises in a few percent, more in large or deep tumours and in the immunosuppressed, in whom it is the dominant cancer after transplantation ([transplant immunology](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md)). Melanoma arises from melanocytes, often with BRAF mutations, spreads first within the epidermis and, once in the dermis, gains access to lymphatics and blood ([invasion and metastasis](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/invasion-metastasis-and-the-tumour-microenvironment.md)). Transplant recipients develop squamous cell carcinoma at some 65–250 times the population rate, together with widespread warts, because T-cell surveillance normally clears both ultraviolet-mutated and papillomavirus-infected keratinocytes: the clearest proof that immunity suppresses cancer as well as infection ([immunodeficiency](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)).

3. **In melanoma, depth is destiny.** The ABCDE features (asymmetry, border irregularity, colour variation, diameter over 6 mm, evolution) identify the suspect and dermoscopy sharpens the call ([skin lesion morphology](skin-lesion-morphology-and-diagnosis.md)). Once excised, the Breslow thickness, the vertical depth of invasion in millimetres, is the strongest predictor of survival: thin melanomas under a millimetre are cured in over 95% of cases by excision alone, thick ones carry a substantial risk of spread. For metastatic disease, melanoma's high ultraviolet mutation burden makes it one of the most immunogenic tumours, and checkpoint inhibitors and BRAF/MEK inhibitors have raised five-year survival from under 10% to around half (2010s–2020s) ([systemic anticancer therapy](../07-oncology/systemic-anticancer-therapy.md)).

4. **Primary prevention and early detection work because the cause and the organ are both accessible.** Sun protection and sunscreen reduce keratinocyte cancers and, in randomised trials, melanoma ([levels of prevention](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/levels-of-prevention.md)). Population screening for melanoma has not been shown to reduce mortality and risks overdiagnosis, but surveillance of high-risk individuals finds thin melanomas ([screening principles and biases](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md)). Melanoma incidence has risen several-fold in fair-skinned populations since the 1970s while mortality has risen far less: partly earlier detection, partly overdiagnosis.

5. **Bacterial skin infection is depth plus organism, and the deepest is a surgical emergency.** Impetigo is *Staphylococcus aureus* or *Streptococcus pyogenes* in the superficial epidermis. Cellulitis is streptococcal or staphylococcal infection of dermis and fat, entering through a crack, ulcer or fungal toe web; it is almost always unilateral ([acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md)). Necrotising fasciitis races along the fascial plane beneath, invisible from the surface: pain out of proportion to the signs and rapid toxicity mark it, and only immediate debridement saves the patient ([fever and sepsis](../08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md), [trauma and haemorrhage control](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md)). Staphylococcal scalded skin and toxic shock show a local infection injuring the whole body through a circulating toxin ([pathogenesis, virulence and toxins](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/pathogenesis-virulence-and-toxins.md)).

6. **Viral, fungal and parasitic infections exploit the skin's own biology.** Human papillomaviruses drive the benign hyperproliferation of warts, and high-risk types cause anogenital squamous carcinoma ([viruses](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/viruses.md)). Herpes simplex and varicella-zoster lie latent in sensory ganglia and return along the nerve, which is why zoster is dermatomal ([HIV and chronic viral infections](../08-infectious-diseases/hiv-and-chronic-viral-infections.md)). Dermatophyte fungi digest keratin and so live only in dead skin, hair and nail, producing scaling rings confirmed by microscopy of scrapings ([fungi, protozoa and helminths](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/fungi-protozoa-and-helminths.md)). The itch of scabies is delayed hypersensitivity to the mite, so it begins weeks after infestation and outlasts cure.

7. **Who else is involved.** Dermatology excises most skin cancers; [plastic surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) reconstructs the face and performs sentinel node biopsy; [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) treats metastatic melanoma; histopathology reports the Breslow depth that decides everything. [Transplant physicians](../03-nephrology/README.md), [rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) and [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) refer immunosuppressed patients for surveillance. Cellulitis is treated in [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) and [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md); necrotising fasciitis belongs to [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md), [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) and [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md); [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) sees impetigo, warts and exanthems; [public health](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/environmental-and-planetary-health.md) runs sun-protection and papillomavirus vaccination.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ultraviolet signature | Cytosine-to-thymine mutations at dipyrimidine sites from pyrimidine dimers |
| Actinic keratosis | Sun-induced dysplastic keratinocyte lesion; precursor of squamous cell carcinoma |
| Basal cell carcinoma | Commonest cancer; locally destructive, almost never metastatic; Hedgehog-pathway driven |
| Breslow thickness | Depth of melanoma invasion in millimetres; the dominant prognostic factor |
| ABCDE | Asymmetry, border, colour, diameter, evolution: features of a suspicious pigmented lesion |
| Cellulitis | Spreading bacterial infection of dermis and subcutis, usually streptococcal |
| Necrotising fasciitis | Rapidly spreading infection along fascia requiring immediate debridement |
| Dermatophyte | Keratin-digesting fungus confined to dead skin, hair and nail |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Skin cancer is not serious."** Basal cell carcinoma rarely kills, but melanoma causes most of the roughly 60,000 skin-cancer deaths a year worldwide (2020), and squamous cell carcinoma kills the immunosuppressed and the neglected.
- **"Bilateral red legs are cellulitis."** Infection is almost always unilateral; bilateral redness is venous stasis, eczema or oedema, and treating it with antibiotics is a common error.
