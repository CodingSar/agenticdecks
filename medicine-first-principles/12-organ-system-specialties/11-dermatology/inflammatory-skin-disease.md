## Essence

The inflammatory skin diseases are what happens when the skin's two jobs, barrier and immune sentinel, go wrong together. Atopic eczema is a leaky barrier that lets antigens in and an immune system biased to respond with the allergic (type 2) arm; psoriasis is an intact barrier with an immune system driven by the interleukin-23 and interleukin-17 axis to make the epidermis proliferate ten times too fast; urticaria is mast-cell release in the dermis; contact dermatitis is a T-cell response to a chemical that has crossed the barrier and bound to self-proteins. Each has a characteristic morphology, each is chronic and relapsing, and each is now treatable at the level of its mechanism, because the cytokines that drive them have been identified and blocked one at a time. The lesson of the field is that the skin was the first organ in which immunology was turned into targeted therapy at scale, and that quality of life, not survival, is the outcome that matters.

## Core tenets

1. **Atopic eczema starts with a defective barrier.** The stratum corneum is a brick wall of flattened keratinocytes mortared with lipids; filaggrin, the protein that organises keratin and generates the skin's natural moisturisers, is mutated in a large fraction of patients, and the barrier is further weakened by the inflammation itself ([the skin as barrier and sensor](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/the-skin-as-barrier-and-sensor.md), [barriers and compartments](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#barriers-and-compartments)). Water leaves and allergens, irritants and *Staphylococcus aureus* enter. The response is a type 2 immune reaction (interleukins 4, 13 and 31, immunoglobulin E) that produces itch, and scratching further breaks the barrier ([hypersensitivity and allergy](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)). The itch–scratch cycle is the disease; emollients that repair the barrier are therefore treatment, not cosmetics, and the condition begins in infancy, affecting roughly one in five children in high-income countries, and often resolves as the barrier matures.

2. **Psoriasis is an immune-driven acceleration of epidermal turnover.** Dendritic cells releasing interleukin-23 sustain T helper 17 cells that release interleukin-17, which drives keratinocytes to proliferate and transit the epidermis in days rather than weeks ([T cells](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/t-cells.md), [cytokines](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/02-innate-immunity/cytokines-interferons-and-nk-cells.md)). The result is the thick, well-demarcated, silvery-scaled plaque on elbows, knees, scalp and sacrum, with pinpoint bleeding when scale is removed because the dermal capillaries reach almost to the surface. Strong HLA associations (HLA-C*06:02), triggers such as streptococcal infection, stress and beta-blockers, and new lesions at sites of injury (Koebner phenomenon) reflect an immune system primed to react in the skin. Psoriasis is systemic: a third develop psoriatic arthritis ([inflammatory arthritis](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/inflammatory-arthritis.md)), and cardiovascular and metabolic disease are more frequent in proportion to its severity.

3. **Urticaria is mast-cell degranulation in the dermis and lasts hours; its timing distinguishes it from everything else.** Histamine and other mediators from mast cells dilate dermal vessels and leak plasma, producing itchy wheals that come and go within 24 hours, often moving around the body; deeper leakage is angio-oedema ([hypersensitivity and allergy](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)). Acute urticaria is often allergic or infective; chronic spontaneous urticaria, lasting over six weeks, is usually autoimmune, with antibodies activating the mast cell directly, and is not an allergy to anything. Antihistamines, often at several times standard dose, and anti-immunoglobulin E antibody treat it; allergy testing is rarely useful. A wheal that lasts days and leaves bruising is urticarial vasculitis and needs biopsy.

4. **Contact dermatitis is delayed-type hypersensitivity to a small molecule, and its pattern maps the exposure.** Nickel, fragrances, preservatives, rubber chemicals and plant allergens penetrate the skin, bind to self-proteins and are presented to T cells; on re-exposure, sensitised T cells produce an eczematous reaction over one to three days (type IV hypersensitivity) exactly where the chemical touched ([antigen presentation and MHC](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/antigen-presentation-and-mhc.md)). Irritant contact dermatitis, from detergents, water and solvents, needs no sensitisation and affects anyone whose barrier is overwhelmed, which makes it the commonest occupational skin disease. Patch testing, applying standardised allergens to the back for two days, identifies the allergen, and avoidance cures ([sports, occupational and lifestyle medicine](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/sports-occupational-and-lifestyle-medicine.md)).

5. **Acne is a disease of the pilosebaceous unit under androgen drive.** At puberty, androgens enlarge sebaceous glands and increase sebum; follicular keratinocytes shed abnormally and block the duct; *Cutibacterium acnes* proliferates in the trapped lipid and triggers inflammation ([reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md), [the human microbiome](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/the-human-microbiome.md)). Treatments act at each step: topical retinoids normalise keratinisation, benzoyl peroxide and antibiotics reduce the bacteria, hormonal therapy reduces sebum, and oral isotretinoin shrinks the gland itself, which is why it is the only treatment that produces lasting remission and also why it is teratogenic. Acne scars and its psychological toll are the reason to treat it early rather than wait for it to pass.

6. **Treatment is matched to mechanism and to the surface area involved.** Topical glucocorticoids suppress inflammation locally but thin skin with prolonged use; topical calcineurin inhibitors avoid that cost; ultraviolet phototherapy induces local immunosuppression and apoptosis of infiltrating T cells ([radiotherapy and radiation biology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/radiotherapy-and-radiation-biology.md)). For widespread disease, systemic immunosuppressants such as methotrexate and ciclosporin give way to biologics that block tumour necrosis factor, interleukin-17 or interleukin-23 in psoriasis, and interleukin-4/13 or Janus kinase signalling in eczema, each clearing the skin in most patients ([anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anti-inflammatory-and-immunomodulatory-drugs.md), [biologics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)). The specificity of the response confirms the mechanism: interleukin-17 blockers clear psoriasis and worsen inflammatory bowel disease, where the same cytokine protects the gut barrier ([specificity and recognition](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#specificity-and-recognition)).

7. **Who else is involved.** [Primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) manages most eczema, acne and psoriasis and teaches the daily self-care these chronic diseases depend on ([chronic disease and self-management](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/chronic-disease-and-self-management.md)). [Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) shares psoriatic arthritis and the biologics; [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) shares the inflammatory bowel disease that accompanies psoriasis and the cytokine trade-offs; [clinical immunology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/allergy-and-immunodeficiency-in-practice.md) the atopic march and food allergy; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) infantile eczema; occupational medicine contact dermatitis; [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) the staphylococcal and herpetic infections that complicate eczema and the screening before biologics; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) the depression, anxiety and social withdrawal that visible skin disease causes and that worsen it; [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) the teratogenic drugs (isotretinoin, methotrexate) in women of reproductive age.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stratum corneum | Outer layer of dead keratinocytes and lipid forming the water and antigen barrier |
| Filaggrin | Structural protein of the barrier; loss-of-function variants predispose to eczema |
| Type 2 inflammation | Interleukin-4, -13 and -31 driven response with IgE and eosinophils; the eczema and allergy pathway |
| Interleukin-23/17 axis | Cytokine pathway driving psoriatic keratinocyte proliferation |
| Wheal | Transient dermal oedema from mast-cell release; the lesion of urticaria |
| Angio-oedema | Deeper swelling of dermis and subcutis, often of lips and eyelids |
| Type IV hypersensitivity | Delayed T-cell-mediated reaction, as in allergic contact dermatitis |
| Patch test | Application of allergens to skin for two days to identify contact sensitisation |
| Pilosebaceous unit | Hair follicle with its sebaceous gland; the site of acne |
| Phototherapy | Controlled ultraviolet exposure as an immunosuppressive treatment |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Eczema is an allergy."** It is a barrier defect with a type 2 immune bias; most flares are not caused by an identifiable allergen, and allergy testing rarely changes management.
- **"Psoriasis is a skin disease."** It is a systemic inflammatory disease with joint, vascular and metabolic consequences, and it is treated accordingly.
- **"Chronic urticaria means you are allergic to something."** Chronic spontaneous urticaria is autoimmune mast-cell activation; searching for an allergen is almost always futile.
