## Essence

The skin is the only organ examined directly, in its entirety, with the naked eye, and dermatology is built on a disciplined way of describing what is seen. A lesion's morphology, arrangement and distribution each encode where in the skin the pathology sits and what process produced it: a blister means the layers have separated, and the level of the split names the disease; a scaly plaque means the epidermis is proliferating too fast; a dermatomal band means a nerve. Thousands of named diseases reduce to a few dozen reaction patterns, and the method is to describe, infer the level and process, and biopsy when the stakes demand it. Because the skin mirrors systemic disease and drug reactions, the method is a window onto the rest of medicine.

## Core tenets

1. **Morphology encodes depth, and depth encodes mechanism.** The skin is layered: a keratinised epidermis a tenth of a millimetre thick, a collagen dermis with vessels, nerves and appendages, and subcutaneous fat ([the skin as barrier and sensor](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/the-skin-as-barrier-and-sensor.md), [epithelium](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/01-tissues/epithelium.md)). A macule (flat colour change) is pigment or vessel change without altered thickness; a papule or plaque (raised, solid) is thickening of epidermis, infiltration of dermis, or both; a vesicle or bulla (fluid-filled) is a split between or within layers; a nodule is deep dermal or subcutaneous. Scale is disordered keratinisation; crust is dried exudate; lichenification is thickening from rubbing. The primary lesion, before scratching alters it, is the thing to find.

2. **The level of a blister is diagnostic, and immunology explains it.** Pemphigus vulgaris autoantibodies target desmogleins, the adhesion molecules between keratinocytes, so the split is within the epidermis: the roof breaks at once and the patient presents with erosions and mouth ulcers ([cell adhesion](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/cell-adhesion-and-the-extracellular-matrix.md), [autoimmunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md)). Bullous pemphigoid antibodies target the hemidesmosomes anchoring epidermis to dermis, so the blister is tense and survives. Direct immunofluorescence on biopsy shows where the antibody sits.

3. **Distribution is the second axis, and it tells you the cause.** Photo-exposed sites implicate ultraviolet light, in lupus, drug photosensitivity and skin cancer. Flexures suggest atopic eczema; extensors, psoriasis; a single dermatome, herpes zoster ([segmental innervation and dermatomes](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)); a contact pattern, contact dermatitis; symmetry an internal cause and asymmetry an external one. Arrangement adds more: grouped vesicles are herpetic; annular lesions with an active edge suggest fungus.

4. **Colour is physics and chemistry read through the skin.** Red is dilated dermal vessels and blanches on pressure; purple that does not blanch is extravasated blood (purpura), pointing to platelet, coagulation or vessel-wall disease ([bleeding and clotting disorders](../06-haematology/bleeding-and-clotting-disorders.md), [vasculitis](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/connective-tissue-disease-and-vasculitis.md)). Brown is epidermal melanin; blue-grey is dermal melanin scattered by the Tyndall effect ([waves, sound, light and radiation](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/waves-sound-light-and-radiation.md)); yellow is lipid or bilirubin; white is absent melanin or blood. Every colour sign must be read against baseline skin tone: erythema and jaundice are harder to see and purpura easier to miss in darker skin, a persistent source of diagnostic inequity.

5. **The skin reports internal disease and drug reactions, often first.** Pruritus without rash suggests cholestasis, kidney failure, iron deficiency or lymphoma. Specific eruptions announce systemic disease: the malar rash of lupus, the heliotrope rash of dermatomyositis, acanthosis nigricans in insulin resistance, erythema nodosum in sarcoidosis and inflammatory bowel disease. Drug eruptions range from the benign maculopapular rash a week or two after a new drug to the T-cell-mediated emergencies of Stevens–Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis, in which the epidermis dies in sheets ([adverse drug reactions](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md), [hypersensitivity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)). The timing from drug start to rash is the key history.

6. **Biopsy is cheap and definitive, so the threshold for it is low when the differential includes cancer or a blistering disease.** A punch biopsy takes minutes and shows the level and type of inflammation, atypical cells and, with immunofluorescence, the antibody involved ([histopathology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/histopathology-cytology-and-molecular-diagnostics.md)). Dermoscopy, a hand-held polarised-light magnifier, improves the sensitivity and specificity of melanoma diagnosis and reduces unnecessary excisions ([diagnostic accuracy studies](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/diagnostic-accuracy-studies.md)). Scrapings confirm fungi and scabies; photography over time is the test for change in a pigmented lesion.

7. **Who else is involved.** Most skin disease is seen in [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), where teledermatology now triages referrals by photograph. The dermatologist reads the skin for [rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) (lupus, vasculitis), [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) (systemic infection), [haematology](../06-haematology/README.md) (purpura, cutaneous lymphoma), [oncology](../07-oncology/README.md) (drug rashes, paraneoplastic signs), [endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/README.md) and [gastroenterology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md). Severe drug eruptions are managed with [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) and burns units; [paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) shares the exanthems; histopathologists are the other half of every difficult diagnosis.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Macule and patch | Flat change in colour, small and large respectively |
| Papule, plaque and nodule | Raised solid lesions: small, broad and flat-topped, or deep |
| Vesicle and bulla | Fluid-filled blisters, small and large |
| Scale, crust and lichenification | Excess keratin, dried exudate and thickened skin markings |
| Purpura | Non-blanching discolouration from extravasated blood |
| Primary versus secondary lesion | The original lesion versus changes from scratching, infection or healing |
| Distribution | Where on the body lesions occur; encodes cause (light, contact, nerve, flexure) |
| Dermoscopy | Magnified polarised-light examination improving pigmented-lesion diagnosis |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A rash is a diagnosis."** It is a description that has not yet been made; the same red scaly plaque may be psoriasis, eczema, fungus or a cutaneous lymphoma, and the method exists to tell them apart.
- **"Blanching is a trivial sign."** It is the fastest way to tell inflamed vessels from leaked blood, and non-blanching purpura in a feverish patient is meningococcal sepsis until proven otherwise.
- **"Skin signs look the same in everyone."** Erythema, cyanosis and jaundice are muted in darker skin and purpura is easier to miss; textbooks built on pale skin have taught a biased pattern recognition.
