## What this area is about

Neurology is the medicine of the organ that cannot be biopsied, barely regenerates and does something different in every cubic centimetre. Because function is localised, a lesion's position can be inferred from what has been lost, and the neurological examination is the most precise piece of bedside reasoning in medicine: it answers "where" before "what". The second question, "what", is then answered largely by tempo. Seconds means vascular or electrical; days means inflammatory; months to years means degenerative or neoplastic. The five children are organised by these two axes, and each adds one further principle: time as brain in stroke, excitation and inhibition in epilepsy, the pain system as the disease in headache, protein misfolding in degeneration, and the vulnerable links of the motor and sensory chain in demyelinating and neuromuscular disease.

The unifying idea is **localisation plus tempo**: where in the nervous system, and over what time course. Together they generate the differential diagnosis before any test, and they decide which tests are worth ordering, because imaging and electrophysiology confirm a hypothesis rather than generate one.

Read stroke first: it teaches localisation by vascular territory and the logic of a time-critical reversible mechanism. Epilepsy adds the electrical dimension and the problem of diagnosing an event from a witness's account. Headache is the exercise in pattern and probability that most of neurology outside hospital consists of. Neurodegenerative disease and demyelinating and neuromuscular disease are the deliberate pair: the first is where the neuron dies and treatment lags, the second where the neuron survives and treatment works.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Cardiology](../01-cardiology/README.md):** atrial fibrillation, patent foramen ovale and endocarditis cause embolic stroke; syncope is the main mimic of seizure; hypertension drives small-vessel disease and haemorrhage.
- **[Pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md):** neuromuscular respiratory failure, sleep apnoea as a cause of headache and stroke, home ventilation in motor neuron disease.
- **[Nephrology](../03-nephrology/README.md):** hyponatraemia and uraemia cause seizures and encephalopathy; dialysis patients have high stroke risk.
- **[Gastroenterology and hepatology](../04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md):** hepatic encephalopathy, B12 and thiamine deficiency after gut disease or surgery, dysphagia in stroke and degeneration.
- **[Endocrinology](../05-endocrinology/README.md):** diabetic neuropathy is the commonest neuropathy; hypoglycaemia mimics stroke; thyroid disease causes myopathy and cognitive change; pituitary tumours present with visual field loss.
- **[Haematology](../06-haematology/README.md):** anticoagulation decisions after stroke, thrombophilia in young stroke, sickle cell stroke, B12 deficiency affecting cord and marrow.
- **[Oncology](../07-oncology/README.md):** brain metastases, cord compression, paraneoplastic syndromes and chemotherapy neuropathy.
- **[Infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md):** meningitis, encephalitis, abscess, HIV and syphilis of the nervous system, and the infections that follow lymphocyte-depleting therapy.
- **[Rheumatology and clinical immunology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md):** vasculitic neuropathy, giant cell arteritis, neuropsychiatric lupus, myositis, and the shared immunosuppressive drugs.
- **[Dermatology](../11-dermatology/README.md):** shingles along a dermatome, the skin signs of neurocutaneous syndromes, dermatomyositis.

## Interfaces with other specialties

Neurology **receives** the acute brain from [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md): stroke, first seizure, thunderclap headache and acute weakness are triaged there and handed over within the hour. It **shares** the operating theatre's problems with [neurosurgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md), which evacuates haematomas, secures aneurysms, resects epileptic foci and tumours and implants stimulators; the division is roughly medical mechanism versus surgical lesion, and tumours, hydrocephalus and head injury sit on the border. It **depends on** [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) for status epilepticus, neuromuscular respiratory failure and the comatose patient, and on [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) to manage patients with myasthenia, dystrophy and epilepsy safely through surgery. [Ophthalmology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) and neurology share optic neuritis, papilloedema, visual field defects and the eye signs of myasthenia; ENT shares vertigo, where the distinction between inner ear and brainstem is a daily problem.

**Border conditions** with layer 14 are extensive. [Psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) is the historic twin: functional neurological disorder, non-epileptic attacks, the depression of chronic neurological disease and the behavioural symptoms of dementia are managed jointly, and neither specialty can do without the other. [Geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) carries most dementia and much stroke; [palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) manages the end of motor neuron disease and advanced dementia. [Paediatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/README.md) has its own neurology because the developing brain has its own epilepsies, dystrophies and metabolic diseases, and transition at adolescence is a recognised hazard. [Obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) shares epilepsy, migraine and multiple sclerosis in pregnancy and the headache of pre-eclampsia. [Rehabilitation medicine](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md) takes over after stroke, brain injury and in progressive disease, and [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) manages most headache, drives secondary prevention after stroke and monitors anti-seizure drugs.

**Emergencies** are defined by minutes: stroke reperfusion, status epilepticus, cord compression, meningitis and the respiratory failure of Guillain–Barré syndrome and myasthenic crisis all require the receiving team to act before the neurologist arrives, which is why these pathways are written as shared protocols.

## Connections across layers

Neurology rests on [nervous tissue](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/01-tissues/nervous-tissue.md) and [head, neck and spine](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/head-neck-and-spine.md) for localisation, on the whole of the [nervous system physiology](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/README.md) for mechanism, on [thrombosis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md), [autoimmunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md) and [degeneration](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/diseases-of-aging-and-degeneration.md) for disease, on [central nervous system drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/central-nervous-system-drugs.md) and [biologics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md) for treatment, and on [magnetic resonance imaging](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/magnetic-resonance-imaging.md) and [electrophysiological testing](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/electrocardiography-and-electrophysiological-testing.md) for confirmation. It gives upward the resuscitation targets of [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/triage-and-the-abcde-approach.md), the patients of [rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md) and [geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md), and the questions of capacity and personhood that [ethics and law](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md) must answer.

## Cross-cutting threads

- [Structure determines function](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#structure-determines-function): localisation is the whole method; territory, tract and nucleus predict deficit.
- [Time-courses](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#time-courses): tempo of onset is the second diagnostic axis, from the seconds of stroke to the decades of degeneration.
- [Supply and demand](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#supply-and-demand): the brain's lack of fuel reserve makes ischaemia and status epilepticus emergencies.
- [Compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation): the penumbra, the junction's safety factor, and the decade of silent Parkinson pathology before symptoms.
- [Signalling and information](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#signalling-and-information): seizures as loss of inhibitory control; demyelination as loss of signal fidelity.
- [Injury and repair](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#injury-and-repair): remyelination and plasticity recover; neurons do not regenerate.
