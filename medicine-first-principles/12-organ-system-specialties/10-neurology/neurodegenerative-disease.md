## Essence

The neurodegenerative diseases are the slow death of selected populations of neurons, and nearly all of them are diseases of protein folding. A normally soluble protein (amyloid-beta and tau in Alzheimer's disease, alpha-synuclein in Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia, TDP-43 in most motor neuron disease, huntingtin in Huntington's disease) misfolds, aggregates, spreads from cell to cell along connected pathways, and kills the neurons in which it accumulates. Which neurons are vulnerable decides the syndrome: hippocampal and cortical neurons give amnesia and dementia, substantia nigra neurons give parkinsonism, motor neurons give weakness. The time course is the defining feature: pathology begins one to two decades before the first symptom, progression is relentless, and treatment so far mostly replaces a lost neurotransmitter or supports the person rather than halting the process. Age is the dominant risk factor, so as populations age these are becoming the leading cause of disability in high-income countries.

## Core tenets

1. **Misfolded protein aggregates are the common lesion, and they propagate.** Each disease has a signature protein that adopts a beta-sheet-rich conformation, seeds further misfolding of its normal counterpart, and accumulates when the proteasome and autophagy systems cannot clear it ([proteins, structure and folding](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/01-macromolecules/proteins-structure-and-folding.md), [protein quality control and degradation](../../03-cell-biology/01-cell-structure/protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md)). The seeds move between connected neurons, so the pathology spreads along anatomical pathways in a predictable sequence (Braak staging), which is why symptoms evolve in a predictable order. The prion diseases, in which the misfolded protein is literally infectious, are the extreme case that revealed the mechanism ([the cellular stress response](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/the-cellular-stress-response.md)).

2. **Selective vulnerability maps protein to syndrome.** Alzheimer pathology (extracellular amyloid-beta plaques, intracellular tau tangles) begins in the entorhinal cortex and hippocampus, so the first symptom is loss of the ability to form new memories, followed by language, visuospatial and executive decline as it spreads to association cortex ([higher functions](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/higher-functions-memory-language-and-consciousness.md)). Alpha-synuclein in the substantia nigra removes dopaminergic input to the striatum, so Parkinson's disease is bradykinesia, rigidity and rest tremor ([motor control](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/motor-control.md)); the same protein spreading to cortex gives Lewy body dementia with hallucinations and fluctuation. Motor neuron disease kills upper and lower motor neurons, so the picture is progressive weakness with both spasticity and wasting, sparing sensation and, usually, cognition. Frontotemporal dementias attack behaviour and language first.

3. **Genes reveal the mechanism even when most cases are sporadic.** Rare autosomal dominant mutations in amyloid precursor protein or the presenilins that process it cause early-onset Alzheimer's disease, establishing amyloid as upstream; the common APOE e4 allele raises late-onset risk several-fold by impairing amyloid clearance ([mendelian inheritance](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/mendelian-inheritance-and-pedigrees.md), [complex traits](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/complex-traits-and-heritability.md)). Mutations in alpha-synuclein, LRRK2 and the lysosomal enzyme glucocerebrosidase cause or predispose to Parkinson's disease, pointing to synuclein handling. Huntington's disease is a CAG repeat expansion that lengthens across generations (anticipation) and allows predictive testing decades before onset, with all the ethical weight that carries.

4. **Diagnosis is clinical phenotype plus the exclusion of treatable mimics, now supported by biomarkers.** Cognitive testing shows the profile of deficits; examination shows parkinsonism or motor neuron signs. Blood tests exclude thyroid disease, B12 deficiency and syphilis; imaging excludes tumour, hydrocephalus and subdural haematoma and shows the pattern of atrophy. Cerebrospinal fluid amyloid and tau, amyloid and tau positron emission tomography ([nuclear medicine](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/nuclear-medicine-and-molecular-imaging.md)) and, increasingly, blood phospho-tau now detect Alzheimer pathology in life, and dopamine transporter imaging confirms nigral loss. Depression and delirium are the common reversible causes of apparent dementia and must be sought first ([geriatric syndromes](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md)).

5. **Treatment so far mostly replaces the missing transmitter or removes the protein modestly, and the natural history continues.** Levodopa, converted to dopamine in surviving nigral terminals, restores movement in Parkinson's disease for years, but as terminals are lost the response fluctuates and dyskinesias appear; deep brain stimulation of basal ganglia nuclei treats these by modulating the circuit ([central nervous system drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/central-nervous-system-drugs.md), [devices](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/devices-implants-and-biomaterials.md)). Cholinesterase inhibitors give a small symptomatic benefit in Alzheimer's disease by boosting the acetylcholine that degenerating basal forebrain neurons no longer supply. Monoclonal antibodies that clear amyloid (lecanemab, donanemab, approved 2023–2024 in the United States) slow decline by roughly a quarter to a third over eighteen months in early disease, at the cost of brain oedema and microhaemorrhage; they are the first disease-modifying treatments and a test of the amyloid hypothesis rather than its vindication. Riluzole adds months in motor neuron disease.

6. **Vascular and modifiable factors contribute, so some dementia is preventable.** Cerebrovascular disease ([stroke](stroke.md)) coexists with Alzheimer pathology in most old brains and lowers the threshold for dementia; midlife hypertension, diabetes, smoking, inactivity, hearing loss, low education and social isolation together account for an estimated 40% of dementia risk (Lancet Commission, 2020). Age-specific dementia incidence has fallen in several high-income countries over recent decades, consistent with better vascular health ([multifactorial disease and risk](../../08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/multifactorial-disease-and-risk.md)).

7. **Who else is involved.** These are diseases of years, lived mostly outside neurology. [Geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) and [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) carry most dementia care; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) (old-age psychiatry) manages the behavioural and psychotic symptoms and the depression that mimics and accompanies dementia; [neurosurgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) implants deep brain stimulators and shunts hydrocephalus; [rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md), speech therapy and [ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) manage swallowing in Parkinson's and motor neuron disease; [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) and [palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) manage the respiratory failure that ends motor neuron disease and the decisions about ventilation and feeding; [genetics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/congenital-and-genetic-disease-in-childhood.md) services counsel families in Huntington's disease; and the law ([consent, capacity and confidentiality](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md)) governs capacity, driving and advance decisions.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proteinopathy | Disease caused by misfolding and aggregation of a specific protein |
| Amyloid-beta and tau | The plaque and tangle proteins of Alzheimer's disease |
| Alpha-synuclein | Protein of Lewy bodies in Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia |
| Selective vulnerability | Susceptibility of particular neuronal populations to a given proteinopathy |
| Braak staging | Sequence of anatomical spread of tau or synuclein pathology |
| Dementia | Acquired decline in multiple cognitive domains sufficient to impair independence |
| Parkinsonism | Bradykinesia with rigidity or rest tremor, from nigrostriatal dopamine loss |
| Motor neuron disease | Degeneration of upper and lower motor neurons; amyotrophic lateral sclerosis is the main form |
| Anticipation | Earlier onset in successive generations as a repeat expansion lengthens |
| Disease-modifying therapy | Treatment that slows the underlying process rather than its symptoms |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Dementia is normal ageing."** Ageing slows recall; dementia is a disease with specific pathology, and most people reach ninety without it.
- **"Parkinson's disease is a tremor."** Bradykinesia is the defining feature; a third of patients have no tremor, and the non-motor features (constipation, loss of smell, sleep disorder, depression, later dementia) often precede the motor ones by years.
- **"Nothing can be done."** Symptomatic treatment transforms Parkinson's disease for a decade, reversible mimics are common, risk is partly modifiable, and planning while capacity remains is itself a treatment.
