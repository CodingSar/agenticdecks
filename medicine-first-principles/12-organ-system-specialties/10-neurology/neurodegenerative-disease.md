## Essence

The neurodegenerative diseases are the slow death of selected neurons, and nearly all are diseases of protein folding. A normally soluble protein (amyloid-beta and tau in Alzheimer's disease, alpha-synuclein in Parkinson's disease, TDP-43 in most motor neuron disease, huntingtin in Huntington's disease) misfolds, aggregates, spreads along connected pathways and kills the neurons in which it accumulates. Which neurons are vulnerable decides the syndrome: hippocampus and cortex give dementia, substantia nigra gives parkinsonism, motor neurons give weakness. Pathology begins one to two decades before the first symptom, progression is relentless, and treatment so far mostly replaces a lost transmitter rather than halting the process. Age is the dominant risk factor, so these are becoming the leading cause of disability in ageing populations.

## Core tenets

1. **Misfolded protein aggregates are the common lesion, and they propagate.** Each disease has a signature protein that adopts a beta-sheet-rich conformation, seeds further misfolding of its normal counterpart, and accumulates when the proteasome and autophagy systems cannot clear it ([proteins, structure and folding](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/01-macromolecules/proteins-structure-and-folding.md), [protein quality control and degradation](../../03-cell-biology/01-cell-structure/protein-quality-control-and-degradation.md)). The seeds move between connected neurons, so pathology spreads along anatomical pathways in a predictable sequence (Braak staging) and symptoms evolve in a predictable order. The prion diseases, in which the misfolded protein is literally infectious, revealed the mechanism ([the cellular stress response](../../03-cell-biology/03-cells-in-community/the-cellular-stress-response.md)).

2. **Selective vulnerability maps protein to syndrome.** Alzheimer pathology (amyloid-beta plaques, tau tangles) begins in the hippocampus, so the first symptom is failure to form new memories, followed by language and visuospatial decline as it spreads to association cortex ([higher functions](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/higher-functions-memory-language-and-consciousness.md)). Alpha-synuclein in the substantia nigra removes dopaminergic input to the striatum, so Parkinson's disease is bradykinesia, rigidity and rest tremor ([motor control](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/motor-control.md)); the same protein in cortex gives Lewy body dementia with hallucinations. Motor neuron disease kills upper and lower motor neurons, so the picture is progressive weakness with both spasticity and wasting, sparing sensation.

3. **Genes reveal the mechanism even when most cases are sporadic.** Rare dominant mutations in amyloid precursor protein or the presenilins that process it cause early-onset Alzheimer's disease, placing amyloid upstream; the common APOE e4 allele raises late-onset risk several-fold ([mendelian inheritance](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/mendelian-inheritance-and-pedigrees.md), [complex traits](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/complex-traits-and-heritability.md)). Mutations in alpha-synuclein and LRRK2 cause Parkinson's disease. Huntington's disease is a CAG repeat expansion that lengthens across generations (anticipation) and allows predictive testing decades before onset, with all the ethical weight that carries.

4. **Diagnosis is clinical phenotype plus the exclusion of treatable mimics, now supported by biomarkers.** Cognitive testing shows the profile of deficits; blood tests exclude thyroid disease and B12 deficiency; imaging excludes tumour, hydrocephalus and subdural haematoma. Cerebrospinal fluid amyloid and tau, amyloid positron emission tomography ([nuclear medicine](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/nuclear-medicine-and-molecular-imaging.md)) and, increasingly, blood phospho-tau now detect Alzheimer pathology in life. Depression and delirium are the common reversible mimics and must be sought first ([geriatric syndromes](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md)).

5. **Treatment so far mostly replaces the missing transmitter or removes the protein modestly, and the natural history continues.** Levodopa, converted to dopamine in surviving nigral terminals, restores movement in Parkinson's disease for years, but as terminals are lost the response fluctuates and dyskinesias appear; deep brain stimulation treats these by modulating the circuit ([central nervous system drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/central-nervous-system-drugs.md), [devices](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/devices-implants-and-biomaterials.md)). Cholinesterase inhibitors give a small symptomatic benefit in Alzheimer's disease. Monoclonal antibodies that clear amyloid (lecanemab, donanemab, approved 2023–2024 in the United States) slow decline by roughly a quarter to a third over eighteen months in early disease, at the cost of brain oedema and microhaemorrhage; they are the first disease-modifying treatments and a test of the amyloid hypothesis rather than its vindication.

6. **Vascular and modifiable factors contribute, so some dementia is preventable.** Cerebrovascular disease ([stroke](stroke.md)) coexists with Alzheimer pathology in most old brains and lowers the threshold for dementia; midlife hypertension, diabetes, smoking, inactivity, hearing loss and low education together account for an estimated 40% of dementia risk (Lancet Commission, 2020), and age-specific incidence has fallen in several high-income countries ([multifactorial disease and risk](../../08-pathology/05-disease-as-process/multifactorial-disease-and-risk.md)).

7. **Who else is involved.** These are diseases of years, lived mostly outside neurology. [Geriatrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) and [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) carry most dementia care; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) manages the behavioural symptoms and the depression that mimics dementia; [neurosurgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md) implants stimulators; [rehabilitation](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md) and [ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) manage swallowing; [pulmonology](../02-pulmonology/README.md) and [palliative care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) the respiratory failure that ends motor neuron disease; [genetics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/01-paediatrics/congenital-and-genetic-disease-in-childhood.md) services counsel Huntington families; and the law ([consent, capacity and confidentiality](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md)) governs capacity, driving and advance decisions.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proteinopathy | Disease caused by misfolding and aggregation of a specific protein |
| Amyloid-beta and tau | The plaque and tangle proteins of Alzheimer's disease |
| Alpha-synuclein | Protein of Lewy bodies in Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia |
| Selective vulnerability | Susceptibility of particular neuronal populations to a given proteinopathy |
| Dementia | Acquired decline in multiple cognitive domains sufficient to impair independence |
| Parkinsonism | Bradykinesia with rigidity or rest tremor, from nigrostriatal dopamine loss |
| Motor neuron disease | Degeneration of upper and lower motor neurons; amyotrophic lateral sclerosis is the main form |
| Anticipation | Earlier onset in successive generations as a repeat expansion lengthens |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Dementia is normal ageing."** Ageing slows recall; dementia is a disease with specific pathology, and most people reach ninety without it.
- **"Parkinson's disease is a tremor."** Bradykinesia is the defining feature; a third of patients have no tremor, and the non-motor features (constipation, loss of smell, sleep disorder, depression, later dementia) often precede the motor ones by years.
- **"Nothing can be done."** Symptomatic treatment transforms Parkinson's disease for a decade, reversible mimics are common, risk is partly modifiable, and planning while capacity remains is itself a treatment.
