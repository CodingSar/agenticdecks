## Essence

Headache is the commonest neurological complaint and one of the few symptoms where the brain itself is never the source: the brain has no pain receptors, so all head pain comes from the meninges, blood vessels, muscles, sinuses, eyes, teeth and the nerves that supply them, almost all routed through the trigeminal nerve. More than nine in ten headaches are primary, meaning the pain system itself is the disorder, and migraine, a genetically determined disorder of sensory processing, is the one that matters most because it is common, disabling and treatable. The rare secondary headaches, from bleeding, infection, raised pressure, arteritis or a mass, kill or blind, and the whole skill of headache medicine is a history structured to separate them: the pattern over time and a short list of red flags, not a scan.

## Core tenets

1. **Head pain is referred from pain-sensitive structures through the trigeminovascular system.** Nociceptors in the dura, the large cerebral and meningeal arteries and venous sinuses send fibres through the trigeminal nerve (and, for the posterior fossa, the upper cervical roots) to the trigeminal nucleus caudalis, which also receives input from face, scalp, neck, sinuses and eye ([pain](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/pain.md), [head, neck and spine](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/head-neck-and-spine.md)). Convergence in this nucleus is why pain is poorly localised, why neck and sinus pain are felt as headache, and why migraine pain is felt in the face and neck as well as the head ([segmental innervation and referred pain](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)).

2. **Migraine is a disorder of brain excitability and sensory gain, not of blood vessels.** The aura that precedes about a third of attacks is cortical spreading depression, a slow wave of depolarisation followed by silence that crosses the cortex at a few millimetres a minute, which is why visual aura marches across the field over twenty minutes, in contrast to the seconds of a seizure or the instant of a stroke. The headache follows activation of trigeminovascular afferents, release of calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) and sensitisation of the brainstem relays, so that light, sound, smell and movement, normally neutral, become painful (photophobia, phonophobia, allodynia) ([sensory systems](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/sensory-systems.md)). Migraine runs in families, is three times commoner in women after puberty, and is triggered by anything that perturbs homeostasis: sleep loss, missed meals, hormonal change, stress and its release.

3. **The pattern over time is the diagnosis.** Primary headaches are recurrent and stereotyped: migraine as episodic, often unilateral, throbbing pain with nausea and sensitivity to light lasting hours to days; tension-type as a featureless bilateral pressure; cluster as excruciating unilateral orbital pain with tearing, nasal congestion and restlessness, in attacks of under three hours clustered over weeks, with a strong circadian rhythm ([sleep and circadian rhythms](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/sleep-and-circadian-rhythms.md)). Secondary headaches break a pattern: a first or worst headache, a change in character, or a headache that progresses. Frequent use of acute painkillers converts episodic migraine into a daily medication-overuse headache, the commonest cause of chronic daily headache and reversible only by withdrawal ([tolerance, dependence and withdrawal](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/tolerance-dependence-and-withdrawal.md)).

4. **Red flags are the probes for the dangerous mechanisms, and each one maps to one.** Thunderclap onset (maximal within a minute) suggests subarachnoid haemorrhage ([stroke](stroke.md)); fever and neck stiffness, meningitis ([fever and sepsis](../08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md)); headache worse lying flat, on waking, or with coughing, with papilloedema, raised intracranial pressure from a mass or venous thrombosis; a new headache over fifty with jaw claudication and a raised inflammatory marker, giant cell arteritis ([connective tissue disease and vasculitis](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/connective-tissue-disease-and-vasculitis.md)); a new focal deficit, a lesion; a painful red eye with a fixed mid-dilated pupil, acute glaucoma ([the eye and vision loss](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md)). The cerebrospinal fluid pressure is a hydrostatic column, so posture-dependence tells you whether pressure is high or low ([fluids, pressure and flow](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/fluids-pressure-and-flow.md)).

5. **Imaging is for red flags, not reassurance.** In a patient with a typical migraine history and normal examination, the chance of a significant finding on brain imaging is below 1%, similar to the incidental findings in the general population; scanning everyone finds aneurysms and cysts that cause anxiety and intervention without benefit ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md), [clinical reasoning and cognitive bias](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/clinical-reasoning-and-cognitive-bias.md)). Where thunderclap headache is present, computed tomography within hours is highly sensitive for subarachnoid blood, and lumbar puncture follows if it is negative and the suspicion remains.

6. **Treatment acts at the identified step of the mechanism.** Acute migraine therapy uses triptans, serotonin 5-HT1B/1D agonists that constrict meningeal vessels and inhibit trigeminal CGRP release, and newer gepants that block the CGRP receptor directly; non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs reduce the neurogenic inflammation ([central nervous system drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/central-nervous-system-drugs.md)). Prevention lowers excitability (beta-blockers, antiepileptics such as topiramate, amitriptyline) or removes the peptide (monoclonal antibodies against CGRP or its receptor, [biologics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)). Cluster headache responds to high-flow oxygen and triptans acutely and to verapamil for prevention. Secondary headache is treated by treating its cause, and the headache's resolution confirms the diagnosis.

7. **Who else is involved.** Most headache is managed in [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), whose task is to recognise migraine and the red flags. Thunderclap headache goes to [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and then [neurosurgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md). [Ophthalmology](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) sees papilloedema, glaucoma and the visual loss of arteritis; [ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) sees the sinus pain often misattributed to sinusitis. [Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) shares giant cell arteritis; [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) meningitis; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) the depression and anxiety that are comorbid with chronic migraine and amplify it; [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) migraine in pregnancy, where it usually improves, and the headache of pre-eclampsia, which must not be mistaken for it.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary headache | Headache in which the pain system itself is the disorder (migraine, tension-type, cluster) |
| Secondary headache | Headache caused by another disease (haemorrhage, infection, pressure, arteritis) |
| Trigeminovascular system | Trigeminal afferents from meninges and vessels converging in the brainstem |
| Cortical spreading depression | Slow wave of neuronal depolarisation and silence underlying migraine aura |
| Aura | Transient focal neurological symptoms, usually visual, preceding migraine headache |
| CGRP | Calcitonin gene-related peptide; neuropeptide released in migraine and a drug target |
| Allodynia | Pain from a normally innocuous stimulus; sign of central sensitisation |
| Thunderclap headache | Severe headache maximal within a minute; subarachnoid haemorrhage until excluded |
| Medication-overuse headache | Chronic daily headache sustained by frequent acute analgesic use |
| Papilloedema | Optic disc swelling from raised intracranial pressure |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Migraine is a vascular headache."** The vessels are the messenger, not the cause; the disorder is in the brain's sensory processing, which is why triggers are physiological and why drugs acting on neurons and neuropeptides work.
- **"Severe pain means serious cause."** Migraine and cluster headache are among the most painful conditions known and are benign; a mild, new, progressive headache with a red flag is more worrying.
- **"Sinus headache is common."** Most recurrent "sinus headache" with facial pressure and nasal symptoms is migraine; true sinusitis headache accompanies acute infection with fever and purulent discharge.
