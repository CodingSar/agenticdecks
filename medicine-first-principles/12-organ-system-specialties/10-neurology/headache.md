## Essence

Headache is the commonest neurological complaint and the brain itself is never its source: the brain has no pain receptors, so head pain comes from meninges, vessels, muscles, sinuses, eyes and teeth, almost all routed through the trigeminal nerve. More than nine in ten headaches are primary, meaning the pain system itself is the disorder, and migraine, a genetic disorder of sensory processing, matters most because it is common, disabling and treatable. The rare secondary headaches, from bleeding, infection, pressure, arteritis or a mass, kill or blind, and the skill of headache medicine is a history structured to separate them: the pattern over time and a short list of red flags, not a scan.

## Core tenets

1. **Head pain is referred from pain-sensitive structures through the trigeminovascular system.** Nociceptors in the dura and the meningeal arteries and venous sinuses send fibres through the trigeminal nerve (and the upper cervical roots) to the trigeminal nucleus caudalis, which also receives input from face, neck, sinuses and eye ([pain](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/pain.md), [head, neck and spine](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/head-neck-and-spine.md)). Convergence here is why head pain is poorly localised and why neck and sinus pain are felt as headache ([segmental innervation and referred pain](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/segmental-innervation-dermatomes-and-referred-pain.md)).

2. **Migraine is a disorder of brain excitability and sensory gain, not of blood vessels.** The aura preceding about a third of attacks is cortical spreading depression, a slow wave of depolarisation then silence crossing the cortex at a few millimetres a minute, which is why visual aura marches across the field over twenty minutes, unlike the seconds of a seizure or the instant of a stroke. The headache follows activation of trigeminovascular afferents, release of calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) and sensitisation of brainstem relays, so that light, sound and movement become painful ([sensory systems](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/sensory-systems.md)). It runs in families, is three times commoner in women after puberty, and is triggered by anything that perturbs homeostasis: sleep loss, missed meals, hormonal change, stress.

3. **The pattern over time is the diagnosis.** Primary headaches are recurrent and stereotyped: migraine as episodic, often unilateral, throbbing pain with nausea and photophobia lasting hours to days; tension-type as featureless bilateral pressure; cluster as excruciating unilateral orbital pain with tearing and restlessness, in attacks under three hours clustered over weeks with a circadian rhythm ([sleep and circadian rhythms](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/sleep-and-circadian-rhythms.md)). Secondary headaches break a pattern: a first or worst headache, a change in character, progression. Frequent acute painkillers convert episodic migraine into daily medication-overuse headache, reversible only by withdrawal ([tolerance, dependence and withdrawal](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/tolerance-dependence-and-withdrawal.md)).

4. **Red flags are the probes for the dangerous mechanisms, and each one maps to one.** Thunderclap onset (maximal within a minute) suggests subarachnoid haemorrhage ([stroke](stroke.md)); fever and neck stiffness, meningitis ([fever and sepsis](../08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md)); headache worse lying flat, on waking, or with coughing, with papilloedema, raised intracranial pressure from a mass or venous thrombosis; a new headache over fifty with jaw claudication and a raised inflammatory marker, giant cell arteritis ([connective tissue disease and vasculitis](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/connective-tissue-disease-and-vasculitis.md)); a new focal deficit, a lesion; a painful red eye with a fixed mid-dilated pupil, acute glaucoma ([the eye and vision loss](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md)). Cerebrospinal fluid is a hydrostatic column, so posture-dependence tells you whether pressure is high or low ([fluids, pressure and flow](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/fluids-pressure-and-flow.md)).

5. **Imaging is for red flags, not reassurance.** With a typical migraine history and normal examination, the chance of a significant finding on imaging is below 1%; scanning everyone finds incidental aneurysms and cysts that cause anxiety and intervention without benefit ([using tests](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/using-tests-pretest-probability-and-thresholds.md), [clinical reasoning and cognitive bias](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/clinical-reasoning-and-cognitive-bias.md)). In thunderclap headache, computed tomography within hours is highly sensitive for subarachnoid blood, and lumbar puncture follows if it is negative.

6. **Treatment acts at the identified step of the mechanism.** Acute migraine therapy uses triptans, serotonin 5-HT1B/1D agonists that inhibit trigeminal CGRP release, gepants that block the CGRP receptor, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs ([central nervous system drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/central-nervous-system-drugs.md)). Prevention lowers excitability (beta-blockers, topiramate, amitriptyline) or removes the peptide (monoclonal antibodies against CGRP, [biologics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)). Cluster headache responds to high-flow oxygen acutely and verapamil for prevention. Secondary headache is treated by treating its cause.

7. **Who else is involved.** Most headache is managed in [primary care](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), whose task is to recognise migraine and the red flags. Thunderclap headache goes to [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and [neurosurgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md). [Ophthalmology and ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) see papilloedema, glaucoma, arteritic visual loss and the facial pain misattributed to sinusitis. [Rheumatology](../09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) shares giant cell arteritis; [infectious diseases](../08-infectious-diseases/README.md) meningitis; [psychiatry](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/03-psychiatry/README.md) the depression and anxiety that amplify chronic migraine; [obstetrics](../../14-life-course-mind-and-integrative-care/02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) the headache of pre-eclampsia, which must not be mistaken for migraine.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary headache | Headache in which the pain system itself is the disorder (migraine, tension-type, cluster) |
| Secondary headache | Headache caused by another disease (haemorrhage, infection, pressure, arteritis) |
| Trigeminovascular system | Trigeminal afferents from meninges and vessels converging in the brainstem |
| Cortical spreading depression | Slow wave of neuronal depolarisation and silence underlying migraine aura |
| Aura | Transient focal neurological symptoms, usually visual, preceding migraine headache |
| CGRP | Calcitonin gene-related peptide; neuropeptide released in migraine and a drug target |
| Thunderclap headache | Severe headache maximal within a minute; subarachnoid haemorrhage until excluded |
| Medication-overuse headache | Chronic daily headache sustained by frequent acute analgesic use |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Migraine is a vascular headache."** The vessels are the messenger, not the cause; the disorder is in the brain's sensory processing, which is why triggers are physiological and why drugs acting on neurons and neuropeptides work.
- **"Severe pain means serious cause."** Migraine and cluster headache are among the most painful conditions known and are benign; a mild, new, progressive headache with a red flag is more worrying.
- **"Sinus headache is common."** Most recurrent "sinus headache" with facial pressure and nasal symptoms is migraine; true sinusitis headache accompanies acute infection with fever and purulent discharge.
