## Essence

Pregnancy is a nine-month physiological experiment in which one organism grows another inside itself, and most of its complications are the experiment's control systems pushed past their limits. Plasma volume rises by about half, cardiac output by a third or more, glomerular filtration by half, insulin resistance climbs through the third trimester and coagulation tilts towards clotting, all to supply a placenta and protect against haemorrhage at birth. Each adaptation carries a disease: the heart that cannot raise its output, the pancreas that cannot match the insulin resistance, the clotting that becomes thrombosis, and above all the placenta that implants shallowly and poisons the maternal endothelium (pre-eclampsia). Worldwide about 287,000 women died of pregnancy-related causes in 2020, most from haemorrhage, hypertensive disease, sepsis and unsafe abortion, almost all preventable. The unifying idea is **the placenta as the organ that sets the terms**: how well it implants decides the fate of mother and fetus.

## Core tenets

1. **Maternal physiology is reset, so normal values change.** Progesterone relaxes smooth muscle and lowers systemic vascular resistance, so blood pressure falls to a nadir in mid-pregnancy and rises back towards term; the renin–angiotensin system is activated yet pressure stays low because the vessels are refractory to angiotensin II ([hemodynamics and blood pressure regulation](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md)). Plasma expands more than red cell mass, so haemoglobin falls (physiological anaemia); filtration rises, so creatinine and urea fall; tidal volume rises under progesterone, so a mild respiratory alkalosis is normal ([pregnancy, placenta and lactation](../../06-physiology/09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/pregnancy-placenta-and-lactation.md)). A "normal" creatinine or blood pressure for a non-pregnant woman can therefore be abnormal in pregnancy, and every reference range must be re-read.

2. **Pre-eclampsia is a placental disease that presents in the mother's endothelium.** In normal pregnancy trophoblast invades and remodels the uterine spiral arteries into wide, low-resistance channels. When invasion is shallow the placenta is underperfused and, from about 20 weeks, releases anti-angiogenic factors (soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 and soluble endoglin) that neutralise vascular endothelial growth factor and placental growth factor in the maternal circulation. Endothelium everywhere then becomes leaky and vasoconstricted: hypertension, proteinuria (glomerular endotheliosis), oedema, raised liver enzymes and low platelets (the HELLP variant), cerebral oedema and seizures (eclampsia). It affects 2–8% of pregnancies. Antihypertensives treat the pressure and magnesium sulfate the seizures, but the only cure is delivery of the placenta, which is why obstetrics so often trades fetal maturity against maternal safety. Low-dose aspirin from early pregnancy reduces the risk in women at high risk, probably by shifting the thromboxane–prostacyclin balance.

3. **Pregnancy is a glucose stress test.** Placental hormones (human placental lactogen, progesterone, cortisol, growth hormone variants) raise insulin resistance to divert glucose to the fetus ([glucose homeostasis](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/glucose-homeostasis.md)). A beta-cell that cannot compensate produces gestational diabetes, which affects a growing fraction of pregnancies (regionally 5–15% or more, 2020s) as obesity rises. Maternal glucose crosses the placenta, insulin does not, so the fetus becomes hyperinsulinaemic: macrosomia, shoulder dystocia at birth, neonatal hypoglycaemia, and a lifelong higher risk of obesity. Women with gestational diabetes have a several-fold higher lifetime risk of type 2 diabetes, so the diagnosis is also a screening result for the future ([diabetes mellitus](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/diabetes-mellitus.md)).

4. **Pregnancy is prothrombotic by design, and thrombosis is a leading cause of maternal death.** Fibrinogen and factors VII, VIII and X rise, protein S falls, fibrinolysis is suppressed and the gravid uterus compresses the pelvic veins; all three of Virchow's elements are present ([haemostasis and coagulation](../../06-physiology/08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md); [thrombosis, embolism and infarction](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/thrombosis-embolism-and-infarction.md)). Venous thromboembolism risk is several times that outside pregnancy, highest after delivery, and in high-income countries it is among the commonest direct causes of maternal death. Low-molecular-weight heparin, which does not cross the placenta, is the anticoagulant; warfarin does cross and is teratogenic.

5. **Bleeding in pregnancy is placental location or separation.** Early bleeding is miscarriage (10–20% of recognised pregnancies, mostly from chromosomal aneuploidy) or ectopic pregnancy (about 1–2%, the leading cause of first-trimester death because a tube ruptures into the peritoneum). Later bleeding is placenta praevia (a placenta over the cervical os, painless) or abruption (premature separation, painful, with a tense uterus and fetal distress, and consumptive coagulopathy when thromboplastin enters the maternal circulation). Ultrasound distinguishes them ([ultrasound](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/ultrasound.md)), and blood group matters because fetal red cells entering a Rhesus-negative mother sensitise her against the next pregnancy, preventable with anti-D immunoglobulin ([blood groups and transfusion compatibility](../../06-physiology/08-blood/blood-groups-and-transfusion-compatibility.md)).

6. **The fetus is a second patient reached only through the mother.** Growth is assessed by ultrasound biometry and placental function by Doppler of the umbilical artery; a fetus that is small because the placenta is failing redistributes flow to the brain ("brain sparing") and, if left, dies in utero. Teratogens act by gestational window: the embryo is most vulnerable in weeks 3–8 when organs form ([congenital malformations and teratogens](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/03-development/congenital-malformations-and-teratogens.md)), so sodium valproate, isotretinoin, warfarin and angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors are stopped before conception, and maternal infection (rubella, cytomegalovirus, syphilis, Zika) crosses the placenta. Prescribing in pregnancy weighs a known maternal benefit against fetal risk that is often uncertain because trials exclude pregnant women ([adverse drug reactions and pharmacovigilance](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md)).

7. **Pregnancy unmasks future disease.** Pre-eclampsia roughly doubles later cardiovascular risk, gestational diabetes predicts type 2 diabetes, and a pregnancy that goes badly in a woman with a heart lesion, lupus or kidney disease reveals how little reserve she had ([compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation)). Obstetric history is therefore part of a woman's cardiovascular risk assessment for life.

8. **Who else is involved.** Obstetric medicine shares the pregnant woman with [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md) (valve disease, cardiomyopathy, congenital heart disease survivors), [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md) (lupus nephritis, chronic kidney disease, pre-eclampsia versus glomerulonephritis), [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md) (diabetes, thyroid disease), [haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/README.md) (anticoagulation, sickle cell, alloimmunisation), [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) (epilepsy and its drugs), [psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md) (perinatal mental illness), neonatology for the planned preterm delivery, and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) and [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) for eclampsia, haemorrhage and sepsis.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Spiral artery remodelling | Trophoblast conversion of uterine arterioles into low-resistance vessels; its failure underlies pre-eclampsia |
| sFlt-1 | Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1, the placental anti-angiogenic factor that neutralises maternal vascular endothelial growth factor |
| HELLP | Haemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets: the hepatic and haematological face of pre-eclampsia |
| Eclampsia | Generalised seizures in pre-eclampsia, from cerebral endothelial failure and oedema |
| Gestational diabetes | Hyperglycaemia first recognised in pregnancy, from insulin resistance exceeding beta-cell reserve |
| Ectopic pregnancy | Implantation outside the uterine cavity, usually in the fallopian tube |
| Abruption | Premature separation of a normally sited placenta, with concealed or revealed bleeding |
| Rhesus alloimmunisation | Maternal antibodies against fetal red cell antigens, causing haemolytic disease in later pregnancies |
| Umbilical artery Doppler | Ultrasound measure of placental resistance; absent or reversed end-diastolic flow signals placental failure |
| Teratogenic window | The gestational period, mainly weeks 3–8, during which an exposure can disrupt organ formation |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Pre-eclampsia is a hypertensive disorder."** Hypertension is a sign; the disease is placental, and treating pressure alone does not stop its progression, which only delivery does.
- **"Normal lab values in pregnancy mean normal."** Haemoglobin, creatinine, bicarbonate and alkaline phosphatase all have pregnancy-specific ranges; a creatinine normal for a non-pregnant woman may signify kidney injury in a pregnant one.
- **"Drugs are avoided in pregnancy, so untreated disease is safer."** Uncontrolled epilepsy, hypertension, diabetes or depression harms the fetus more than most treatments; the decision is a balance, not an abstention.
