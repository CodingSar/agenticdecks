## Essence

The female reproductive tract is an organ system that rebuilds itself every month under hormonal command, which makes its diseases unusually legible: most are either tissue responding to oestrogen in the wrong place or amount (fibroids, endometriosis, endometrial cancer), a hormonal axis that has stopped cycling (polycystic ovary syndrome, amenorrhoea), an infection ascending from the vagina (pelvic inflammatory disease, and the papillomavirus behind cervical cancer), or a structural failure of the pelvic floor after childbirth. Gynaecology is therefore applied reproductive endocrinology, applied microbiology and applied surgery, sharing its borders with urology, gastroenterology, oncology and general surgery. The unifying idea is **hormone-responsive tissue and the consequences of its response**, in the right place and the wrong.

## Core tenets

1. **Abnormal bleeding is read against the cycle.** The endometrium proliferates under oestrogen, is stabilised by progesterone after ovulation and sheds when both fall ([gametogenesis and the menstrual cycle](../../06-physiology/09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/gametogenesis-and-the-menstrual-cycle.md)). Heavy regular bleeding usually means a structural cause (fibroids, adenomyosis, polyps) or a bleeding disorder such as von Willebrand disease ([bleeding and clotting disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/bleeding-and-clotting-disorders.md)); irregular bleeding means anovulation, with unopposed oestrogen building an endometrium that sheds unpredictably; postmenopausal bleeding is cancer until proved otherwise. Iron-deficiency anaemia is the commonest systemic consequence and menstrual loss the commonest cause of iron deficiency in young women ([anaemias](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/anaemias.md)).

2. **Fibroids and endometriosis are oestrogen-dependent tissue growing where it should not.** Fibroids (leiomyomas) are benign monoclonal smooth-muscle tumours of the myometrium present in most women by age 50; they grow under oestrogen and progesterone, shrink after the menopause, and cause bleeding, pressure and subfertility according to where they sit. Endometriosis, affecting roughly 10% of women of reproductive age, is endometrium-like tissue outside the uterus (ovary, peritoneum, bowel, bladder) that bleeds and inflames with each cycle, producing cyclical pain, scarring and infertility ([chronic inflammation and granulomas](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/chronic-inflammation-and-granulomas.md)). Both respond to the same logic: suppress the ovarian cycle (combined hormonal contraception, progestogens, gonadotrophin-releasing hormone analogues) or remove the tissue surgically. Diagnosis of endometriosis is typically delayed for years because pelvic pain is attributed to the bowel or to the mind.

3. **Polycystic ovary syndrome is a metabolic disease that presents through the ovary.** Insulin resistance and raised luteinising hormone drive ovarian androgen production; androgen excess (hirsutism, acne) and arrested follicle maturation (anovulation, oligomenorrhoea, the "polycystic" ovary of many small follicles) follow ([reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md); [glucose homeostasis](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/glucose-homeostasis.md)). It affects about 8–13% of women, is the commonest cause of anovulatory infertility, and carries the long-term risks of insulin resistance (type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia) and of unopposed oestrogen on the endometrium. Weight loss, insulin sensitisers and ovulation induction act on different links of the same chain.

4. **Ascending infection scars tubes.** *Chlamydia trachomatis* and *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* ascend from the cervix to the endometrium and fallopian tubes (pelvic inflammatory disease); chlamydia is often silent and the damage is discovered years later as tubal infertility, ectopic pregnancy or chronic pain ([transmission and the chain of infection](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/01-microbes-and-infection/transmission-and-the-chain-of-infection.md)). Opportunistic screening of young adults and partner notification act on the chain; antibiotics treat the infection but not the scar.

5. **Cervical cancer is a viral disease with a long, screenable preinvasive phase.** Persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus types (16 and 18 cause about 70%) integrates viral oncoproteins E6 and E7, which inactivate p53 and retinoblastoma protein in the cervical transformation zone ([causes of cancer](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/causes-of-cancer.md); [oncogenes, tumour suppressors and genomic instability](../../08-pathology/03-neoplasia/oncogenes-tumour-suppressors-and-genomic-instability.md)). Progression from infection through cervical intraepithelial neoplasia to invasion takes a decade or more, which is why cytological screening (and now primary testing for the virus) prevents most cases, and why vaccination before exposure prevents most of the rest ([screening principles and biases](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md)). Cervical cancer remains a leading cause of cancer death among women in low-income countries, where neither exists, with about 350,000 deaths worldwide in 2022.

6. **Endometrial and ovarian cancers obey oestrogen and silence respectively.** Endometrial cancer is the commonest gynaecological cancer in high-income countries and is driven by unopposed oestrogen: obesity (aromatase in fat converts androgens to oestrogen), anovulation, late menopause, oestrogen-only replacement and tamoxifen; it presents early with postmenopausal bleeding and is usually curable by hysterectomy. Ovarian cancer, mostly high-grade serous carcinoma arising in the fallopian tube epithelium with near-universal *TP53* mutation and a strong link to *BRCA1* and *BRCA2*, has no early symptom and presents in most cases with ascites and peritoneal spread, which is why it kills more women than the other gynaecological cancers combined and why no screening test has yet reduced its mortality ([common solid tumours](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/common-solid-tumours.md)).

7. **The pelvic floor is a load-bearing structure that childbirth damages.** The levator ani and endopelvic fascia hold the bladder, uterus and rectum against intra-abdominal pressure; vaginal delivery stretches and denervates them, and oestrogen withdrawal at the menopause weakens the connective tissue ([biomechanics of the musculoskeletal system](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/02-body-organization/biomechanics-of-the-musculoskeletal-system.md); [abdomen and pelvis](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/03-regional-anatomy/abdomen-and-pelvis.md)). The result is prolapse and stress urinary incontinence (leakage when pressure exceeds urethral closure), as against urge incontinence (detrusor overactivity), which has a different mechanism and treatment. Pelvic floor training strengthens what is weak; surgery resuspends what has fallen.

8. **Who else is involved.** Pelvic pain is shared with [gastroenterology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) (irritable bowel, inflammatory bowel disease) and general [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) (appendicitis versus ovarian torsion or ruptured ectopic in [the acute abdomen](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/the-acute-abdomen.md)); incontinence with urology; gynaecological cancer with [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md), radiotherapy and histopathology in a mandatory multidisciplinary team; polycystic ovary syndrome with [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md) and [dermatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/11-dermatology/README.md); heavy bleeding with [haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/README.md); sexually transmitted infection with sexual health and [infectious diseases](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md); and screening, contraception and most first presentations with [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anovulatory bleeding | Irregular bleeding from an endometrium exposed to oestrogen without progesterone |
| Leiomyoma (fibroid) | Benign hormone-dependent smooth-muscle tumour of the myometrium |
| Endometriosis | Endometrium-like tissue outside the uterus responding to the cycle with bleeding and inflammation |
| Polycystic ovary syndrome | Hyperandrogenism with ovulatory dysfunction and polycystic ovarian morphology, rooted in insulin resistance |
| Pelvic inflammatory disease | Ascending infection of the upper genital tract, usually chlamydial or gonococcal |
| Transformation zone | The cervical region where columnar epithelium becomes squamous; the site of papillomavirus-driven neoplasia |
| Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia | Preinvasive dysplasia of the cervical epithelium, graded 1–3, detectable by screening |
| Unopposed oestrogen | Oestrogen exposure without progesterone, the driver of endometrial hyperplasia and cancer |
| Stress incontinence | Leakage when intra-abdominal pressure exceeds urethral closure pressure, from pelvic floor weakness |
| Ovarian torsion | Twisting of the ovary on its pedicle, an ischaemic surgical emergency |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Pelvic pain in a young woman is usually gynaecological."** It is often bowel, bladder or musculoskeletal, and conversely endometriosis is frequently mislabelled as irritable bowel syndrome; the cycle-relatedness of pain is the discriminating question.
- **"Cervical screening detects cancer."** It detects the preinvasive phase and prevents cancer; a programme that finds many invasive cancers is failing, not succeeding.
- **"Ovarian cysts are dangerous."** Functional cysts are a normal product of the cycle and resolve; the concern is a complex mass in a postmenopausal woman, or torsion of any cyst.
