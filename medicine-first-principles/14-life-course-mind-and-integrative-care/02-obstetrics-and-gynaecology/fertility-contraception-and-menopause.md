## Essence

Reproduction is controlled by one hormonal axis, hypothalamus to pituitary to gonad, and this leaf is about managing that axis in three directions: switching conception on when it fails (fertility medicine), switching it off reliably when it is unwanted (contraception), and managing its permanent shutdown (menopause). All three rest on the same mechanisms, negative feedback of sex steroids on gonadotrophins, the monthly selection of one follicle, and the finite ovarian reserve that runs out in the fifth decade. Contraception is among the most consequential technologies in medicine's history, and the menopause is the only endocrine failure that every woman who lives long enough will experience. The unifying idea is **one axis, deliberately steered**.

## Core tenets

1. **Fecundability is low by mammalian standards and falls with the age of the oocyte.** A healthy couple has roughly a 20–25% chance of conception per cycle, so about 85% conceive within a year; infertility is defined as failure after a year and affects roughly one in six people at some point (World Health Organization, 2023). Oocytes are not replenished: a woman is born with her lifetime supply, their number falls steadily and their rate of meiotic error rises steeply from the mid-thirties, so both conception and the aneuploidy behind miscarriage track maternal age ([gametogenesis and the menstrual cycle](../../06-physiology/09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/gametogenesis-and-the-menstrual-cycle.md); [chromosomes and cytogenetics](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/chromosomes-and-cytogenetics.md)). Sperm quality declines more slowly. Male factors contribute in about 40–50% of infertile couples, so the man is investigated first and cheaply with a semen analysis.

2. **Infertility is localised to a link in the chain.** Conception needs ovulation, a patent tube, adequate sperm and a receptive endometrium. Anovulation (polycystic ovary syndrome, hypothalamic amenorrhoea from low energy availability, hyperprolactinaemia, thyroid disease) is identified by a mid-luteal progesterone that never rises; tubal damage (previous chlamydia, endometriosis) by imaging or laparoscopy; male factor by semen analysis ([gynaecological disease](gynaecological-disease.md)). Each has its own remedy: ovulation induction with an anti-oestrogen such as clomifene (which blocks oestrogen's negative feedback so gonadotrophins rise) or with exogenous gonadotrophins, surgery for tubes, and intracytoplasmic sperm injection for severe male factor. Unexplained infertility, about a quarter of cases, is a statement about the limits of the tests.

3. **In-vitro fertilisation bypasses the tube and multiplies the odds, at a price set by age.** Controlled ovarian stimulation recruits many follicles rather than one, oocytes are retrieved, fertilised and cultured, and an embryo is transferred; freezing lets surplus embryos be used later. Live birth per embryo transfer is around a third for women under 35 and falls to a few percent beyond the early forties (UK data, 2020s) because the limiting factor is oocyte quality, which stimulation cannot fix. Multiple pregnancy is the chief iatrogenic harm, prevented by transferring a single embryo; ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome is the other. Preimplantation genetic testing can select against a known familial mutation ([Mendelian inheritance and pedigrees](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/mendelian-inheritance-and-pedigrees.md)).

4. **Hormonal contraception works by borrowing the body's own negative feedback.** A combined oestrogen–progestogen preparation holds gonadotrophins low so no follicle matures and no luteinising hormone surge occurs; the progestogen also thickens cervical mucus and thins the endometrium ([reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md); [feedback and homeostasis](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#feedback-and-homeostasis)). Progestogen-only methods rely more on mucus and endometrium, with variable ovulation suppression. The oestrogen component raises hepatic synthesis of clotting factors and venous thromboembolism risk roughly two- to four-fold from a low baseline, and raises blood pressure slightly, which is why age, smoking, migraine with aura and thrombophilia are the contraindications ([haemostasis and coagulation](../../06-physiology/08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md)). Non-contraceptive benefits (lighter periods, less endometrial and ovarian cancer) follow from the same suppression.

5. **Effectiveness is a property of the method and of the user.** The copper intrauterine device is spermicidal and inflammatory to the endometrium; the levonorgestrel intrauterine system atrophies the endometrium locally; the implant releases progestogen for years. These long-acting reversible methods fail in under 1% of users per year because they remove adherence from the equation, whereas the pill fails in about 7% per year with typical use against under 1% with perfect use ([behaviour change and the placebo effect](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/behaviour-change-and-the-placebo-effect.md)). Emergency contraception (a progesterone-receptor modulator such as ulipristal, or a copper device) works by delaying ovulation or preventing implantation, not by ending an established pregnancy. Sterilisation and vasectomy remove the gamete pathway mechanically.

6. **Abortion is a pharmacological and surgical procedure with a defined mechanism.** The anti-progestogen mifepristone withdraws the hormonal support of the decidua and sensitises the uterus to prostaglandins; misoprostol then produces contraction and expulsion. It is among the safest procedures in medicine where legal and supervised, and unsafe abortion causes a significant fraction of maternal deaths where it is not ([global health and pandemics](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/global-health-and-pandemics.md)); the law and ethics belong to layer 15 ([medical ethics](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/medical-ethics.md)).

7. **The menopause is follicular exhaustion, and its symptoms are oestrogen withdrawal.** When the follicle pool is depleted (median age about 51), oestrogen and inhibin fall, negative feedback is lost and follicle-stimulating hormone rises, which is the diagnostic signature. Vasomotor symptoms (hot flushes, night sweats) follow a narrowing of the hypothalamic thermoneutral zone in which kisspeptin–neurokinin B neurons, freed from oestrogen's restraint, become hyperactive ([thermoregulation and energy balance](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/thermoregulation-and-energy-balance.md)); neurokinin-3 receptor antagonists treat flushes without hormones, a direct test of the mechanism. Urogenital atrophy, sleep disturbance and mood change follow. Loss of oestrogen's restraint on osteoclasts accelerates bone loss for several years ([bone remodelling and joints](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/bone-remodelling-and-joints.md); [bone and calcium disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/bone-and-calcium-disorders.md)), and cardiovascular risk rises towards that of men.

8. **Hormone replacement trades symptom relief against measured risks, and timing changes the balance.** Oestrogen (with a progestogen to protect the endometrium in women with a uterus) abolishes vasomotor symptoms and prevents bone loss. The Women's Health Initiative trials (2002 onward) showed small absolute increases in breast cancer with combined therapy, venous thromboembolism and stroke, and no cardiovascular protection in the older women they enrolled; later analyses suggest that starting near the menopause carries less cardiovascular risk than starting a decade later ([randomized controlled trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md); [harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)). Transdermal oestrogen avoids the first-pass hepatic effect on clotting factors. The decision is individual and shared.

9. **Who else is involved.** Fertility medicine shares patients with [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md) (thyroid, prolactin, androgen disorders), urology (male factor, varicocele, obstruction), clinical genetics (preimplantation testing, recurrent miscarriage), [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md) (fertility preservation before chemotherapy, hormone-sensitive cancers) and [psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md); contraception and the menopause are mostly managed in [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md), with [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md), [haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/README.md) and [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) consulted for the woman with hypertension, thrombophilia or migraine, and bone health shared with [rheumatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) and [geriatrics](../04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fecundability | The probability of conception in one menstrual cycle |
| Ovarian reserve | The remaining follicle pool, estimated by anti-Müllerian hormone and antral follicle count |
| Anovulation | Failure to release an oocyte; the commonest treatable cause of female infertility |
| Ovulation induction | Raising gonadotrophin drive, by blocking oestrogen feedback or giving gonadotrophins, to mature a follicle |
| Intracytoplasmic sperm injection | Injection of a single sperm into an oocyte, bypassing severe male factor infertility |
| Long-acting reversible contraception | Implants and intrauterine methods whose effectiveness does not depend on daily adherence |
| Pearl index | Pregnancies per 100 woman-years of method use; typical-use and perfect-use rates differ |
| Perimenopause | The years of erratic cycles and fluctuating hormones before the final menstrual period |
| Vasomotor symptoms | Hot flushes and sweats from a narrowed hypothalamic thermoneutral zone after oestrogen withdrawal |
| Timing hypothesis | The proposal that hormone replacement started near the menopause has a different risk balance from late starting |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Infertility is a woman's problem."** Male factors contribute in nearly half of couples, and the first test is a semen analysis.
- **"Hormone replacement is dangerous."** The absolute risks found in trials are small, differ by preparation, route and timing, and must be set against severe symptoms and bone loss; the blanket conclusion drawn in 2002 has been substantially revised.
- **"Emergency contraception is abortion."** It acts before implantation, mainly by delaying ovulation, and does not disrupt an established pregnancy.
