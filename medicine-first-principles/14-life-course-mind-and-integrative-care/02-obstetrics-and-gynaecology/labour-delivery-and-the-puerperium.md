## Essence

Labour is a positive-feedback process that expels a fetus through a bony pelvis that evolution made barely wide enough, followed by the most dangerous hour of a woman's reproductive life, when a placental bed the size of a dinner plate must stop bleeding by muscular contraction alone. Obstetrics manages three risks in sequence: that labour will not progress (obstruction, dystocia), that the fetus will not tolerate it (hypoxia from cord or placental compromise), and that the mother will bleed, clot or become infected afterwards. The puerperium, the six weeks of reversal, adds thrombosis, sepsis and the mood disorders that follow the steepest hormonal fall in human physiology. The unifying idea is **a mechanical and haemodynamic process run on feedback loops**: contractions beget contractions, and the uterus is its own tourniquet.

## Core tenets

1. **Labour is a positive-feedback loop that must be switched on, and progress is a product of three variables.** Fetal and placental signals (cortisol, prostaglandins, the withdrawal of progesterone's quiescence) ripen the cervix and sensitise the myometrium to oxytocin; stretch of the cervix triggers oxytocin release (the Ferguson reflex), which produces stronger contractions and more stretch ([feedback and control](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/03-systems-and-models/feedback-and-control.md)). Progress depends on the powers (contraction strength and frequency), the passage (pelvic dimensions) and the passenger (fetal size, position and presentation). Failure to progress is a failure of one of the three: weak contractions are augmented with oxytocin; a malposition may rotate or be delivered instrumentally; a true disproportion needs caesarean section. Obstructed labour that is not relieved kills by uterine rupture, sepsis and fistula where surgery is unavailable, and remains a major cause of maternal death globally.

2. **The fetal heart rate is a window on fetal oxygenation, and a noisy one.** Fetal hypoxia triggers chemoreceptor-mediated vagal slowing; cord compression produces variable decelerations, uteroplacental insufficiency produces decelerations that lag each contraction, and loss of beat-to-beat variability suggests a depressed fetal brain ([control of breathing](../../06-physiology/04-respiratory-system/control-of-breathing.md) and [the autonomic nervous system](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/the-autonomic-nervous-system.md) supply the reflexes). Continuous electronic monitoring is sensitive but poorly specific for acidosis, so it raises caesarean rates without clearly reducing cerebral palsy ([diagnostic accuracy studies](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/diagnostic-accuracy-studies.md)); fetal scalp blood sampling or lactate adds specificity. The trade-off between a false-positive operation and a missed asphyxia is the central decision of the labour ward.

3. **Haemostasis after birth is muscular before it is chemical.** The placental bed is supplied by spiral arteries that run through interlacing myometrial fibres; contraction compresses them ("living ligatures") and only then does the hypercoagulable state of pregnancy seal them ([haemostasis and coagulation](../../06-physiology/08-blood/haemostasis-and-coagulation.md)). Postpartum haemorrhage (blood loss above about 500 mL vaginally, 1,000 mL at caesarean) is the leading direct cause of maternal death worldwide and follows the four Ts: tone (atony, by far the commonest, favoured by an overdistended or exhausted uterus), tissue (retained placenta), trauma (tears) and thrombin (coagulopathy, including that of abruption and amniotic fluid embolism). Treatment follows the mechanism in order: uterotonics (oxytocin, ergometrine, prostaglandin analogues such as carboprost and misoprostol) restore tone, manual removal and repair fix tissue and trauma, tranexamic acid and blood products fix thrombin, and balloon tamponade, uterine artery occlusion or hysterectomy are the mechanical last resorts ([haemorrhage, shock and sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md)). Active management of the third stage, giving oxytocin as the baby delivers, halves the incidence.

4. **A young woman compensates for haemorrhage until she does not.** The expanded blood volume of pregnancy and a healthy heart hold blood pressure through a litre or more of loss; tachycardia, pallor and oliguria come before hypotension, and bleeding may be concealed in the uterus or peritoneum. The obstetric haemorrhage protocol, like the trauma one ([trauma and haemorrhage control](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/trauma-and-haemorrhage-control.md)), replaces what is lost with blood and clotting factors rather than crystalloid and treats the source in parallel.

5. **Delivery mode is a trade-off, and the caesarean rate is rising everywhere.** Caesarean section rescues obstructed labour, fetal distress, praevia and malpresentation, and about 21% of births worldwide were caesarean in 2021, from under 5% in parts of sub-Saharan Africa to above 40% in parts of Latin America. Above a population rate of roughly 10–15% there is no further fall in maternal or neonatal mortality, while each caesarean raises the risk of placenta praevia and accreta (placenta invading the scar), uterine rupture and adhesions in later pregnancies ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)). Anaesthesia for caesarean is usually neuraxial because pregnancy makes the airway difficult and aspiration likely, and the supine gravid uterus compresses the vena cava, so the woman is tilted ([general and regional anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/general-and-regional-anaesthesia.md)).

6. **Preterm birth is the main cause of neonatal death and is partly an inflammatory process.** Spontaneous preterm labour often follows subclinical intrauterine infection or inflammation activating the same prostaglandin pathways as term labour ([acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md)); cervical insufficiency and multiple pregnancy are the other main routes. Tocolytics buy at most days; what changes outcome is giving the mother corticosteroids to mature fetal lungs, magnesium sulfate for fetal neuroprotection, and delivering in a unit with neonatal intensive care ([the newborn and neonatology](../01-paediatrics/the-newborn-and-neonatology.md)).

7. **The puerperium reverses everything, and three things go wrong.** Thrombosis risk peaks in the first weeks as the clotting changes persist while mobility is reduced ([pregnancy and its complications](pregnancy-and-its-complications.md)). Sepsis, classically with group A streptococcus entering the raw placental bed, was the great killer of the pre-antiseptic era (Semmelweis, 1847) and still kills where hand hygiene and antibiotics are absent ([fever and sepsis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/fever-and-sepsis.md)). And the abrupt fall of oestrogen and progesterone, sleep loss and the social upheaval of a new infant produce postnatal depression in roughly 10–15% of women and postpartum psychosis in about 1–2 per 1,000, the latter a psychiatric emergency with risk to mother and infant ([mood and anxiety disorders](../03-psychiatry/mood-and-anxiety-disorders.md); [psychosis](../03-psychiatry/psychosis.md)). Lactation, meanwhile, is prolactin-driven synthesis and oxytocin-driven ejection, with the same positive feedback from suckling that labour used ([pregnancy, placenta and lactation](../../06-physiology/09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/pregnancy-placenta-and-lactation.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** Midwifery leads normal birth; [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) provides analgesia and operative conditions; neonatology attends every anticipated problem; [haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/README.md) and transfusion supply the massive haemorrhage response; [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) takes eclampsia, haemorrhage and sepsis; interventional radiology and [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) assist with accreta and bowel or bladder injury; perinatal [psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md) manages postpartum illness; and [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) runs the postnatal check and the years after.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ferguson reflex | Cervical stretch triggering oxytocin release: the positive feedback of labour |
| Stages of labour | First: cervical dilatation; second: descent and delivery of the fetus; third: delivery of the placenta |
| Dystocia | Abnormal or obstructed progress from powers, passage or passenger |
| Cardiotocography | Continuous recording of fetal heart rate and uterine contractions |
| Postpartum haemorrhage | Blood loss above about 500 mL after vaginal birth; primary within 24 hours, secondary up to 12 weeks |
| Uterine atony | Failure of myometrial contraction after delivery, the commonest cause of postpartum haemorrhage |
| Uterotonic | A drug that contracts the uterus: oxytocin, ergometrine, prostaglandin analogues |
| Placenta accreta | Placenta invading the myometrium, usually into a caesarean scar, with catastrophic bleeding at separation |
| Amniotic fluid embolism | Amniotic contents entering the maternal circulation, causing collapse and consumptive coagulopathy |
| Puerperium | The six weeks after birth during which pregnancy changes reverse |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A normal fetal heart trace means a healthy fetus, and an abnormal one means a compromised one."** The trace is sensitive but non-specific; most abnormal traces end in a well baby, and the trade-off is the point.
- **"Postpartum bleeding is a clotting problem."** It is usually a muscle problem; the uterus stops bleeding by contracting, which is why the first drug is a uterotonic.
- **"Caesarean section is the safe option."** It is safer for some indications and raises the risks of every subsequent pregnancy; above a modest population rate it saves no lives.
