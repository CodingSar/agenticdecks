## Essence

Three groups of disorders are defined less by episodes than by the shape of a person: neurodevelopmental conditions (autism, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, intellectual disability) in which the brain develops along an atypical path from early life; personality disorders in which enduring patterns of feeling, relating and behaving cause persistent distress or harm; and eating disorders in which the control of eating and weight becomes the organising principle of a life and starvation does the physical damage. They share a developmental origin, high heritability modified by early environment, a dimensional rather than categorical nature, and a tendency to be dismissed as character rather than treated as illness. The unifying idea is **traits at the tail of a distribution**: each is an extreme of a variation present in everyone, and the clinical question is where impairment begins.

## Core tenets

1. **Neurodevelopmental conditions are lifelong trait patterns of brain organisation, not childhood illnesses.** Autism (about 1% of the population, perhaps more with broader criteria) is a pattern of differences in social communication and of restricted, repetitive interests and sensory sensitivities, present from early childhood and stable; attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (about 5% of children and 2–3% of adults) is persistent inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity beyond what development expects; intellectual disability (1–2%) is global cognitive impairment with an intelligence quotient roughly two standard deviations below the mean plus adaptive impairment ([growth, development and the child as patient](../01-paediatrics/growth-development-and-the-child-as-patient.md); [distributions and descriptive statistics](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/01-probability-and-measurement/distributions-and-descriptive-statistics.md)). Heritability of autism and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder is around 70–80%, polygenic with rare large-effect variants, and the conditions co-occur with each other and with epilepsy, anxiety and depression far more than chance ([complex traits and heritability](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/complex-traits-and-heritability.md)).

2. **Stimulants treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder by raising prefrontal catecholamines.** Methylphenidate and amfetamines block dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake (amfetamines also release them) and improve attention, impulse control and working memory in most patients within an hour, consistent with hypoactive prefrontal and striatal catecholamine signalling in the condition ([central nervous system drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/central-nervous-system-drugs.md)). The paradox that a stimulant calms is resolved by the inverted-U relation between catecholamine level and prefrontal function. The effect on long-term outcomes (education, injury, criminality) is real but smaller than the effect on symptoms, and appetite suppression and growth slowing are the main costs in children.

3. **Autism is not treated; its disabilities and comorbidities are.** No drug alters the core features. Intervention is environmental and educational: structured, predictable settings, communication support, reasonable adjustments at school and work, and treatment of the anxiety, depression, epilepsy and sleep disorders that accompany it. The neurodiversity framing, that autism is a variation to be accommodated rather than a disease to be cured, is accurate for many and insufficient for those with severe intellectual disability and no speech; both are true at different points of the same distribution ([rehabilitation and disability](../05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md)).

4. **Personality disorder is an enduring, pervasive pattern that is now classified by severity and trait.** The ICD-11 abandoned categorical types for a severity rating (mild, moderate, severe) with trait qualifiers (negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia), recognising that the old categories overlapped and that impairment is dimensional ([measurement error and biological variation](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/01-probability-and-measurement/measurement-error-and-biological-variation.md)). The borderline pattern (emotional instability, fear of abandonment, unstable relationships and self-image, impulsivity, recurrent self-harm) is the most clinically prominent, is associated with childhood trauma in a majority, and carries a suicide rate near 10%. Its core is emotional dysregulation, a fast and intense limbic response with slow prefrontal recovery, plus invalidating early environments that failed to teach regulation.

5. **Personality disorders are treatable, mainly by structured psychotherapy.** Dialectical behaviour therapy and mentalisation-based treatment reduce self-harm and hospitalisation in borderline personality disorder by teaching emotion regulation, distress tolerance and the capacity to think about one's own and others' mental states; drugs treat comorbid depression or psychosis but not the disorder itself, and polypharmacy is a common harm. Most people with the borderline pattern no longer meet criteria a decade later, a prognosis better than the label implies. Antisocial personality disorder, with its forensic consequences, is less treatable and overlaps with substance use ([addiction and substance use](addiction-and-substance-use.md)).

6. **Anorexia nervosa is a psychiatric disorder whose physiology is starvation, and it kills.** Restriction of intake driven by an overvalued idea about shape and weight produces the physiology of famine: bradycardia and hypotension (reduced metabolic rate and sympathetic tone), hypothermia, lanugo hair, amenorrhoea from hypothalamic suppression of gonadotrophins (the body refusing to reproduce in famine), osteoporosis, loss of cardiac muscle, and hypoglycaemia ([metabolic integration and the fed–fasted cycle](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/02-metabolism/metabolic-integration-and-the-fed-fasted-cycle.md); [nutritional disease, obesity and malnutrition](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/nutritional-disease-obesity-and-malnutrition.md); [reproductive endocrinology](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/reproductive-endocrinology.md)). It has the highest mortality of any psychiatric disorder (standardised mortality ratio around 5–6), from the cardiac effects of starvation and from suicide. Heritability is around 50–60% and genetic correlations with metabolic traits suggest it is partly a metabolic as well as a psychiatric disorder. Treatment is refeeding plus family-based or individual psychotherapy, and weight restoration is the precondition for the mind to change, because starvation itself produces rigidity, obsessionality and depression.

7. **Refeeding and purging have their own electrolyte physics.** When a starved body is fed, insulin surges and drives phosphate, potassium and magnesium into cells; the fall in serum phosphate can cause heart failure, arrhythmia and seizures (refeeding syndrome), so refeeding is gradual and monitored, with thiamine given because glucose metabolism demands it ([fluids, nutrition and blood products](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/fluids-nutrition-and-blood-products.md); [potassium, calcium, phosphate and renal acid–base](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/potassium-calcium-phosphate-and-renal-acid-base.md)). Bulimia nervosa (binge eating with compensatory vomiting or laxatives, at normal weight) causes hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis from gastric acid loss, dental erosion and parotid swelling; the hypokalaemia is the cardiac danger. Binge eating disorder without purging is the commonest eating disorder and presents through obesity.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Paediatrics](../01-paediatrics/README.md) and child psychiatry assess neurodevelopment and most new eating disorders; [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) shares epilepsy and tics; [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md) and [gastroenterology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) manage the metabolic and nutritional consequences and are consulted for organic mimics of weight loss; [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md) monitors the starved heart; dietetics directs refeeding; [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) receives self-harm; forensic psychiatry and the justice system share antisocial personality disorder; education and social care support neurodevelopmental disability; and [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) holds the long course of all three groups.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Neurodevelopmental disorder | A lifelong condition arising from atypical brain development, present from early childhood |
| Executive function | Prefrontal processes of attention, working memory, planning and inhibition impaired in attention deficit hyperactivity disorder |
| Inverted-U | The relation in which prefrontal performance is best at intermediate catecholamine levels |
| Neurodiversity | The framing of neurodevelopmental conditions as natural variation deserving accommodation |
| Personality disorder severity | ICD-11's primary axis, replacing categorical types with mild, moderate and severe |
| Emotional dysregulation | Rapid, intense emotional responses with slow return to baseline, the core of the borderline pattern |
| Mentalisation | The capacity to understand one's own and others' behaviour in terms of mental states |
| Overvalued idea | A belief held with unreasonable intensity and dominance, short of a delusion; the shape-and-weight idea of anorexia |
| Refeeding syndrome | Dangerous falls in phosphate, potassium and magnesium when a starved patient is fed, from insulin-driven cellular uptake |
| Standardised mortality ratio | Observed deaths divided by those expected in the general population |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder is a modern invention or an excuse."** The syndrome was described in the nineteenth century, is highly heritable, has consistent neuroimaging and pharmacological correlates, and raises the risk of injury, school failure and early death when untreated.
- **"Personality disorder is untreatable and a diagnosis of exclusion from care."** Structured psychotherapies have trial evidence and most patients improve over a decade; the label has been misused to deny care.
- **"Anorexia is about vanity and the patient could just eat."** Starvation changes the brain in ways that entrench the disorder; the standardised mortality ratio is the highest in psychiatry, and refeeding is a medical procedure with its own dangers.
