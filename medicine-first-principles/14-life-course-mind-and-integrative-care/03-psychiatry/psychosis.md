## Essence

Psychosis is a loss of contact with shared reality: beliefs held with certainty against all evidence (delusions), perceptions without stimulus (hallucinations, most often voices) and disorganised thought. It is a syndrome with many causes, from delirium, drugs and epilepsy to the primary psychotic illnesses of which schizophrenia is the archetype, a disorder of late adolescence and early adulthood affecting roughly 0.5–1% of people over a lifetime. The best-supported mechanism is excess presynaptic dopamine in the striatum, which gives ordinary stimuli an abnormal salience that the mind then explains with a delusion, and every effective antipsychotic drug blocks dopamine D2 receptors. But the dopamine excess is itself the final common path of a neurodevelopmental vulnerability shaped by hundreds of genes and by early environment, and the negative and cognitive symptoms that determine long-term function do not respond to dopamine blockade. The unifying idea is **aberrant salience on a neurodevelopmental substrate**.

## Core tenets

1. **Psychosis is a syndrome and the first task is to find its cause.** Delirium (fluctuating attention, visual hallucinations, a physical trigger), intoxication with stimulants, cannabis or hallucinogens, alcohol withdrawal, temporal lobe epilepsy, autoimmune encephalitis (anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor antibodies in a young person with a rapid onset), dementia with Lewy bodies, and steroid or dopaminergic drugs all produce psychosis ([geriatric syndromes](../04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/geriatric-syndromes.md); [epilepsy](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/epilepsy.md); [autoimmunity](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/autoimmunity.md)). Severe depression and mania produce psychosis congruent with the mood. Only when these are excluded and the picture has persisted for months is schizophrenia diagnosed, and the label commits the patient to long treatment, so it is given carefully.

2. **The dopamine hypothesis rests on convergent evidence and explains the positive symptoms.** Amphetamine, which releases dopamine, causes psychosis; every antipsychotic's clinical potency correlates with its affinity for the D2 receptor ([receptor theory, agonists and antagonists](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/receptor-theory-agonists-and-antagonists.md)); and positron emission tomography shows increased dopamine synthesis and release in the striatum of patients and of people in the prodrome who go on to develop illness ([nuclear medicine and molecular imaging](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/03-imaging-and-physiological-measurement/nuclear-medicine-and-molecular-imaging.md)). Dopamine normally signals that a stimulus matters; released inappropriately it makes a passing car or a stranger's glance feel charged with meaning, and the delusion is the explanation the patient constructs. This "aberrant salience" account links the chemistry to the phenomenology without claiming to explain everything ([signalling and information](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#signalling-and-information)).

3. **Negative and cognitive symptoms are the disabling core and respond poorly to drugs.** Blunted affect, poverty of speech, loss of motivation and social withdrawal, together with impaired working memory and attention, precede the first psychotic episode, persist after it, and predict employment and independence far better than the delusions do. They are associated with reduced prefrontal dopamine and glutamatergic N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor hypofunction (ketamine, an antagonist at that receptor, reproduces positive, negative and cognitive symptoms in volunteers) and with reduced synaptic density on imaging. No drug reliably treats them; psychosocial rehabilitation, supported employment and cognitive remediation do a little ([rehabilitation and disability](../05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md)).

4. **Schizophrenia is highly heritable and polygenic, with a neurodevelopmental history.** Heritability is around 80%; risk is spread across hundreds of common variants of small effect, each shared with bipolar disorder and other conditions, with a few rare copy-number variants (such as the 22q11.2 deletion) of large effect ([complex traits and heritability](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/complex-traits-and-heritability.md); [genomic variation and mutation](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/genomic-variation-and-mutation.md)). The strongest common-variant signal lies in the major histocompatibility complex and involves complement component 4, which tags synapses for pruning, consistent with the observation that illness begins when adolescent synaptic pruning peaks. Obstetric complications, winter birth, childhood adversity, migration, urban upbringing and heavy use of high-potency cannabis each raise risk modestly, the last by perhaps two- to five-fold in daily users ([the host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment)).

5. **Antipsychotics are D2 antagonists, and their adverse effects are the same pharmacology in other pathways.** Blocking D2 in the mesolimbic pathway reduces positive symptoms within days to weeks; blocking it in the nigrostriatal pathway causes parkinsonism, dystonia, akathisia and, after years, tardive dyskinesia; blocking it in the tuberoinfundibular pathway raises prolactin ([the autonomic nervous system](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/the-autonomic-nervous-system.md); [adverse drug reactions and pharmacovigilance](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md)). Second-generation drugs add serotonin 5-HT2A antagonism and cause fewer movement disorders but more weight gain, insulin resistance and dyslipidaemia through histamine H1 and serotonin receptor blockade, with olanzapine and clozapine the worst offenders. Clozapine is uniquely effective in treatment-resistant illness through a mechanism still unexplained, and uniquely dangerous (agranulocytosis, myocarditis), so it is used with mandatory blood monitoring. Partial agonists such as aripiprazole dampen rather than block.

6. **Relapse follows discontinuation, and the illness kills through the body.** Most patients relapse within a year or two of stopping medication; long-acting injectable preparations remove adherence as a variable ([elimination, half-life and dosing](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/elimination-half-life-and-dosing.md)). Life expectancy is reduced by 15–20 years, mostly from cardiovascular disease, smoking (which most patients do, partly as self-medication), metabolic effects of treatment, and poor access to physical care; suicide accounts for about 5% of deaths. Monitoring weight, glucose and lipids, and treating what is found, is part of the treatment ([obesity, lipids and metabolic syndrome](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/obesity-lipids-and-metabolic-syndrome.md)).

7. **Early intervention changes the trajectory.** The duration of untreated psychosis predicts outcome, and services that identify people in the prodrome or first episode and provide drugs at low dose with family intervention and vocational support reduce relapse and hospitalisation. Cognitive behavioural therapy for psychosis reduces the distress of persistent voices and delusions by changing their appraisal rather than their presence.

8. **Who else is involved.** [Neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) shares the organic boundary (epilepsy, encephalitis, dementia with Lewy bodies, Parkinson disease psychosis from dopaminergic drugs); [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) and [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) manage acute disturbance, neuroleptic malignant syndrome and clozapine toxicity; [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md), [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md) and [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) carry the metabolic and cardiovascular burden; [haematology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/README.md) monitors clozapine; [obstetrics](../02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) manages postpartum psychosis; [geriatrics](../04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) manages late-onset psychosis and the psychosis of dementia; and the law ([consent, capacity and confidentiality](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md)) governs the compulsory treatment that psychosis more than any other condition requires.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Positive symptoms | Delusions, hallucinations and disorganised thought: experiences added to normal function |
| Negative symptoms | Blunted affect, alogia, avolition and withdrawal: functions subtracted |
| Aberrant salience | Inappropriate dopamine-mediated marking of stimuli as significant, proposed as the root of delusion formation |
| Prodrome | The months or years of subtle change before the first psychotic episode |
| Duration of untreated psychosis | Time from onset of psychotic symptoms to treatment; longer predicts worse outcome |
| D2 receptor occupancy | The fraction of striatal D2 receptors blocked; about 60–80% is the therapeutic window for most antipsychotics |
| Extrapyramidal symptoms | Parkinsonism, dystonia and akathisia from nigrostriatal dopamine blockade |
| Tardive dyskinesia | Late, often irreversible involuntary movements after prolonged D2 blockade |
| Clozapine | The antipsychotic effective in treatment resistance, requiring blood monitoring for agranulocytosis |
| Neuroleptic malignant syndrome | A rare, life-threatening reaction to dopamine blockade with rigidity, fever and autonomic instability |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Schizophrenia is a split personality."** It is a disorder of perception, belief and thought, not of multiple identities; the misreading comes from the word's etymology.
- **"Antipsychotics cure schizophrenia."** They suppress positive symptoms and prevent relapse while taken; negative and cognitive symptoms persist and function depends on rehabilitation and social support.
- **"People with psychosis are dangerous."** The absolute risk of violence is low and is mostly explained by comorbid substance use; people with psychosis are far more often victims than perpetrators.
