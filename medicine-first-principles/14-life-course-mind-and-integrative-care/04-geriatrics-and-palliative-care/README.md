## What this area is about

This area is the medicine of the end of the life course: the older person whose reserve has run low, the person of any age whose illness will not be cured, and the process of dying itself. It answers a question the organ-system specialties cannot: what is the right care for a person with several diseases, little reserve and a limited time, when every guideline was written for a patient with one disease and a long one? Populations are ageing everywhere, and most people in high-income countries now die after years of multimorbidity, so this is no longer a niche: it is the modal patient on a medical ward.

The unifying idea is **the person over the disease**. Geriatrics treats function, independence and what matters to the patient as the outcomes, with the diseases as means; palliative care treats symptoms and experience as the outcomes, with the disease's course as context. Both reason about reserve, trajectory and time-to-benefit, both are delivered by multidisciplinary teams, and both are where the decision to do less is as deliberate and evidence-based as the decision to do more.

Read frailty first: it defines reserve, which every other leaf uses. Geriatric syndromes are its clinical face. Palliative care applies to the slow trajectory of frailty as much as to cancer, and death and dying is where all three converge.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Paediatrics](../01-paediatrics/README.md):** the two ends of the life course share the logic of reserve (the newborn has little, the frail elder has lost it), weight- and function-based prescribing, the triangular consultation with a carer, and the ethics of limiting treatment at the margins of viability.
- **[Obstetrics and gynaecology](../02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md):** the postmenopausal decades deliver osteoporosis, prolapse, incontinence and gynaecological cancers into the older woman's multimorbidity.
- **[Psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md):** old age psychiatry is a joint territory: delirium, dementia, late-life depression, the psychosis of Lewy body disease, and capacity assessment; palliative care shares depression, anxiety and terminal agitation.
- **[Primary care and integration](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md):** general practice and community nursing deliver most care of the frail and most palliative care, hold the advance care plans, and manage the care home; rehabilitation is the geriatric syndromes' treatment.

## Interfaces with other specialties

Geriatric medicine was built on the organ-system specialties of layer 12 and now works inside them. It **receives** the older patient from [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md), where frailty units at the front door assess the fall, the confusion and the "off legs" presentation, and from every medical specialty when a single-organ problem turns out to be one of six. It **co-manages** with [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) as orthogeriatrics (hip fracture, where joint care reduces mortality) and perioperative medicine for the older surgical patient (delirium prevention, medication review, deconditioning), with [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) over who benefits from admission and over delirium, and with [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) in stroke units (the stroke unit model is a geriatric invention), memory clinics and Parkinson services. [Cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md) shares heart failure, atrial fibrillation (anticoagulation against falls), syncope and the question of valve intervention in the frail; [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md) shares chronic kidney disease and the decision between dialysis and conservative management; [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md) shares diabetes with relaxed targets and osteoporosis; [ophthalmology and ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) share the sensory impairments that drive falls and isolation; urology and [gynaecology](../02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) share incontinence; and pharmacy is a core member of the team.

Palliative care's primary partner is [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md), in which it is now embedded from diagnosis; it extends to [pulmonology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/README.md) (end-stage lung disease), cardiology (advanced heart failure, device deactivation), nephrology (conservative kidney management), neurology (motor neuron disease, dementia), [gastroenterology and hepatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/04-gastroenterology-and-hepatology/README.md) (end-stage liver disease), [infectious diseases](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md) (advanced HIV where treatment fails), paediatrics (children's hospices), anaesthesia and pain medicine (interventional analgesia), [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and interventional radiology (palliative stents, bypasses and drains), and radiotherapy for bone pain and cord compression.

**Disputed territory:** the boundary between "acute medicine" and geriatrics for the older medical admission; between geriatrics and old age psychiatry for dementia with behavioural symptoms; between oncology and palliative care over when disease-directed treatment stops; between critical care and the ward over who is admitted; and between palliative care and every specialty over who has the conversation about dying. **Multidisciplinary decisions** are the comprehensive geriatric assessment itself, the orthogeriatric ward round, the memory clinic, the cancer multidisciplinary team's palliative arm, the intensive-care family meeting, and the best-interests meeting when capacity is lost. **Emergencies that cross the boundary** are delirium (any ward), hip fracture (emergency, orthopaedics, anaesthesia, geriatrics), malignant spinal cord compression (oncology, radiotherapy, spinal surgery, palliative care), hypercalcaemia (medicine, oncology) and the cardiac arrest call in a patient for whom resuscitation was never going to work.
