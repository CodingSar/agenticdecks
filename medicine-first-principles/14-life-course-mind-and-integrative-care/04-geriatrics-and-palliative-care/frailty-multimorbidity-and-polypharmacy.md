## Essence

Ageing narrows the margin between the body's capacity and the demands placed on it, in every organ at once. Frailty is the name for the state in which that margin has become so thin that a minor insult (a urinary infection, a new tablet, a day in bed) produces a disproportionate collapse (delirium, a fall, immobility, death). Multimorbidity, the coexistence of several chronic diseases, is the norm after 65 and the rule after 85, and it generates polypharmacy, which in a frail body is itself a disease. Geriatric medicine is the specialty of the whole older person rather than of any one of their conditions, and its central instrument, the comprehensive geriatric assessment, has trial evidence that it keeps people alive and at home. The unifying idea is **the loss of reserve across systems**: frailty is compensation with nothing left to compensate with.

## Core tenets

1. **Ageing is accumulated molecular and cellular damage that reduces reserve in every system.** Senescent cells, shortened telomeres, mitochondrial decline, protein misfolding, stem cell exhaustion and chronic low-grade inflammation ("inflammaging") reduce maximal function while resting function is preserved ([senescence and telomeres](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/senescence-and-telomeres.md); [diseases of aging and degeneration](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/diseases-of-aging-and-degeneration.md)). Maximal heart rate, glomerular filtration, vital capacity, muscle mass and the speed of baroreceptor and thermoregulatory reflexes all fall by roughly half between 30 and 80, yet a healthy 80-year-old at rest is indistinguishable from a 30-year-old on most tests. The gap between resting and maximal function is reserve, and homeostasis becomes "homeostenosis": the set point is held, but only over a narrower range of perturbation ([homeostasis and set points](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/homeostasis-and-set-points.md)).

2. **Frailty is a measurable state, not a synonym for old.** Two operational models converge: the phenotype (unintentional weight loss, exhaustion, weakness measured by grip, slow walking speed, low activity; three or more defines frailty) and the deficit-accumulation index (the proportion of a long list of symptoms, diseases and disabilities a person has, which predicts death smoothly up to a ceiling near 0.7 beyond which no one survives). Around 10% of people over 65 and a quarter to half of those over 85 are frail (community studies, high-income countries, 2010s). Frailty predicts mortality, delirium, falls, surgical complications and institutionalisation better than chronological age, and it is partly reversible with resistance exercise and nutrition ([exercise and stress physiology](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/exercise-and-stress-physiology.md)).

3. **Sarcopenia is the physical core of frailty.** Muscle mass falls from the fourth decade and the loss accelerates after 70, through motor neuron loss, anabolic resistance to protein, reduced sex hormones and growth hormone, inflammation and disuse ([skeletal muscle contraction and energetics](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/skeletal-muscle-contraction-and-energetics.md)). Weak muscle means slow gait, falls, inability to rise from a chair, and a small protein reserve to draw on in illness. Bed rest costs an older person strength at a rate of several percent a week, which is why hospital admission is itself a cause of disability and why mobilisation is a treatment ([geriatric syndromes](geriatric-syndromes.md)).

4. **Multimorbidity is the normal state and single-disease guidelines do not add up.** More than half of people over 65 and most over 75 have two or more chronic conditions, and in deprived areas multimorbidity arrives 10–15 years earlier ([social determinants and health inequity](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/social-determinants-and-health-inequity.md)). Each guideline is written for a patient with one disease, tested in trials that excluded the old and the multimorbid ([randomized controlled trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md); [grading evidence and guidelines](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/grading-evidence-and-guidelines.md)). Applied together they produce a dozen drugs, conflicting advice (anticoagulate for atrial fibrillation, avoid falls) and a treatment burden the patient cannot carry. Geriatric medicine prioritises by what matters to the patient and by time-to-benefit: a statin whose benefit appears after five years has little to offer a person with a two-year prognosis.

5. **Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics both shift against the older patient.** Renal clearance falls with age even when creatinine looks normal, because muscle mass (the source of creatinine) has fallen too ([glomerular filtration and clearance](../../06-physiology/05-renal-system/glomerular-filtration-and-clearance.md)); hepatic blood flow and first-pass metabolism fall; body fat rises and water falls, so lipophilic drugs such as benzodiazepines accumulate and water-soluble ones reach higher concentrations; albumin falls, raising free fractions ([elimination, half-life and dosing](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/elimination-half-life-and-dosing.md); [distribution and protein binding](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/distribution-and-protein-binding.md)). Independently, the ageing brain is more sensitive to sedatives and anticholinergics, baroreflexes buffer antihypertensives less well, and the kidney conserves sodium less well under diuretics. The same dose therefore produces a larger and longer effect, and adverse drug reactions cause around a tenth of hospital admissions in older people.

6. **Polypharmacy produces cascades, and deprescribing is a treatment.** A drug causes a symptom, the symptom is read as a new disease and a second drug is given: a calcium-channel blocker causes ankle oedema, a diuretic follows, which causes incontinence and hypokalaemia, which bring a third and fourth drug (the prescribing cascade). Anticholinergic burden adds up across unrelated drugs to produce confusion, constipation, retention and falls. Structured review against explicit criteria for drugs that are inappropriate in older people, with stopping of those whose indication has lapsed or whose harm now exceeds their benefit, reduces falls and admissions without worsening disease control ([adverse drug reactions and pharmacovigilance](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/02-pharmacodynamics/adverse-drug-reactions-and-pharmacovigilance.md); [harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)).

7. **Disease presents atypically because the signalling systems have aged too.** Pneumonia without fever or cough, myocardial infarction without chest pain, sepsis presenting as a fall or confusion, hyperthyroidism as apathy, depression as physical complaints: blunted immune, autonomic and pain signalling, and the brain's vulnerability as the weakest link, mean that the first sign of almost anything in a frail person is a functional change ([compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation)). "Off legs" and "acute confusion" are presentations of the whole body, and the search for the cause is systematic.

8. **Comprehensive geriatric assessment is the treatment with the evidence.** A multidimensional, multidisciplinary assessment of medical conditions, medications, function, cognition, mood, nutrition, social circumstances and goals, followed by a coordinated plan, increases the probability that an older person admitted to hospital is alive and in their own home a year later (number needed to treat around 20 in meta-analysis, Cochrane 2017). It is the structural answer to multimorbidity: one assessment of the person instead of many assessments of diseases.

9. **Who else is involved.** Geriatric medicine is embedded in [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) as perioperative medicine for the older surgical patient and as orthogeriatrics for hip fracture ([orthopaedics, fractures and joints](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md)), in [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) as frailty units at the front door, and in [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) and care homes as the main site of long-term management; it shares patients with every organ-system specialty of layer 12, especially [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md), [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md) and [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md), with pharmacy for medication review, and with [palliative care](palliative-care-and-symptom-control.md) as frailty approaches its end.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reserve | The difference between resting and maximal function of a system; the capacity to meet a demand |
| Homeostenosis | The age-related narrowing of the range of perturbation over which homeostasis is maintained |
| Frailty phenotype | Fried's five criteria (weight loss, exhaustion, weakness, slowness, low activity); three or more defines frailty |
| Frailty index | The proportion of a list of health deficits present; a continuous measure of frailty |
| Sarcopenia | Age-related loss of muscle mass and strength |
| Multimorbidity | The coexistence of two or more chronic conditions in one person |
| Polypharmacy | The use of multiple medicines, conventionally five or more; "problematic" when harm exceeds benefit |
| Prescribing cascade | Treating a drug's adverse effect as a new disease with another drug |
| Anticholinergic burden | The cumulative antimuscarinic load of all a patient's drugs, linked to confusion and falls |
| Time-to-benefit | The time before a preventive treatment's benefit exceeds its harm; must be shorter than life expectancy |
| Comprehensive geriatric assessment | Multidimensional, multidisciplinary assessment and plan for an older person |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Frailty is just old age."** Many 90-year-olds are not frail and some 65-year-olds are; frailty is a measurable physiological state with its own prognosis and is partly reversible.
- **"A normal creatinine means normal kidneys in an older person."** Creatinine depends on muscle mass, which has fallen; estimated filtration is often half that of a young adult at the same creatinine.
- **"Stopping drugs is giving up."** Deprescribing is an active treatment with trial evidence for fewer falls and admissions; continuing a drug past its benefit is the passive choice.
