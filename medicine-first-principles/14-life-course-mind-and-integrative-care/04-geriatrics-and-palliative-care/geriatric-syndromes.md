## Essence

A geriatric syndrome is a clinical presentation in an older person that has many contributing causes across several systems and does not fit a single organ's disease: delirium, falls, incontinence, immobility, pressure injury and the cognitive decline of dementia. Each is what frailty looks like when a stressor arrives, and each is a final common pathway: a fall is the output of weak muscle, poor vision, a drooping blood pressure, a sedative, a loose rug and an infection, none of which alone would have caused it. Because the causes are multiple, the treatment is multifactorial, and because the brain and the musculoskeletal system are the weakest links, the syndromes present through them. The unifying idea is **many small causes, one presentation**, which is why the geriatric method is a systematic search rather than a single diagnosis.

## Core tenets

1. **Delirium is acute brain failure, and it is a medical emergency presenting as a psychiatric one.** An acute, fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness, with disorganised thinking, altered arousal (hyperactive or, more often and more often missed, hypoactive) and perceptual disturbance, delirium affects 20–30% of older hospital inpatients and most ventilated patients in intensive care ([organ support and ICU syndromes](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/organ-support-and-icu-syndromes.md)). It is precipitated by infection, drugs (especially anticholinergics, opioids and sedatives), dehydration, pain, urinary retention, constipation, hypoxia, metabolic derangement and the ward environment, acting on a vulnerable brain (age, dementia, sensory impairment). The mechanism is incompletely understood but involves cholinergic deficit, inflammatory cytokines crossing the blood–brain barrier and network disconnection ([higher functions](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/higher-functions-memory-language-and-consciousness.md); [barriers and compartments](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#barriers-and-compartments)). Treatment is to find and reverse every precipitant and to reorient, mobilise, hydrate and restore sleep; antipsychotics do not shorten it. Delirium doubles mortality and accelerates subsequent dementia, so prevention programmes that act on the precipitants reduce its incidence by about a third.

2. **Dementia is chronic brain failure and must be separated from delirium and depression.** Dementia is acquired, progressive cognitive decline across more than one domain, sufficient to impair function, with preserved attention early and a course of months to years; 55 million people live with it worldwide (2023). Alzheimer disease (amyloid and tau pathology), vascular dementia (small-vessel ischaemia), Lewy body dementia (alpha-synuclein, with visual hallucinations, parkinsonism and fluctuations) and frontotemporal dementia each have a mechanism described in [neurodegenerative disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/neurodegenerative-disease.md). The geriatric contribution is the differential: delirium is acute and inattentive, depression is low mood with intact but effortful cognition ("I don't know" rather than wrong answers), and reversible causes (hypothyroidism, B12 deficiency, normal-pressure hydrocephalus, drugs) are sought once. Behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia (agitation, wandering, aggression) are usually communications of unmet need (pain, constipation, fear) and respond to its removal better than to drugs, whose use raises stroke and death rates.

3. **Falls are the output of a control system that has lost its margin.** Standing balance integrates vision, vestibular and proprioceptive input through a slowed central processor to drive weak muscles ([motor control](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/motor-control.md); [sensory systems](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/sensory-systems.md)). About a third of people over 65 fall each year and half of those over 80. Risk factors add: previous falls, gait and balance impairment, sarcopenia, visual loss, postural hypotension (an aged baroreflex plus antihypertensives, [hemodynamics and blood pressure regulation](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/hemodynamics-and-blood-pressure-regulation.md)), psychotropic drugs, cognitive impairment, incontinence (rushing at night), footwear and environment. Multifactorial intervention (strength and balance training, medication review, vision correction, home modification) reduces falls by around a quarter; strength and balance exercise alone is the single most effective component.

4. **Hip fracture is the fall meeting osteoporosis, and its outcome depends on the whole person.** Bone loses mass from mid-life and faster after the menopause ([bone remodelling and joints](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/bone-remodelling-and-joints.md); [bone and calcium disorders](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/bone-and-calcium-disorders.md)), so a fall from standing height that would bruise a young adult breaks an old hip. About a fifth to a third of patients die within a year and many survivors never regain their previous mobility; early surgery, orthogeriatric co-management, mobilisation the next day and secondary prevention (bone protection, falls assessment) each improve outcome ([orthopaedics, fractures and joints](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md)). The fracture is treated by the surgeon; the reasons for the fall and the fragility are treated by the geriatrician.

5. **Immobility is both a syndrome and a cause of every other one.** Bed rest in an older person produces loss of muscle strength at a rate of several percent per week, postural hypotension through plasma volume loss and baroreflex deconditioning, venous thrombosis, constipation, urinary retention, pressure injury, pneumonia from basal atelectasis, delirium and depression ([supply and demand](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#supply-and-demand)). Hospital-acquired deconditioning is a major reason an older person admitted with pneumonia goes home unable to climb the stairs. Getting patients up and dressed is a treatment with the same standing as the antibiotic ([rehabilitation and disability](../05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md)).

6. **Incontinence has four mechanisms, and most are treatable.** Urge incontinence is detrusor overactivity (idiopathic, or from stroke, Parkinson disease or bladder irritation); stress incontinence is a weak pelvic floor or sphincter ([gynaecological disease](../02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/gynaecological-disease.md)); overflow is retention from outflow obstruction (prostate) or an underactive bladder (neuropathy, anticholinergic drugs); functional incontinence is a working bladder in a person who cannot reach the toilet in time for cognitive or mobility reasons ([the autonomic nervous system](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/the-autonomic-nervous-system.md)). A history, a bladder scan for residual volume and a drug review distinguish them, and the treatments differ: bladder training and antimuscarinic or beta-3 agonist drugs for urge, pelvic floor training for stress, relief of obstruction for overflow, and timed toileting and mobility for functional. Incontinence is a leading precipitant of care-home admission and is under-reported from shame.

7. **Pressure injury is physics applied to tissue with no reserve.** Sustained external pressure above capillary closing pressure (roughly 30 mmHg) over a bony prominence stops perfusion; shear forces tear the microcirculation; moisture macerates the skin; and malnutrition, immobility, sensory loss and incontinence remove the normal defences of movement and repair ([microcirculation and capillary exchange](../../06-physiology/03-cardiovascular-system/microcirculation-and-capillary-exchange.md); [tissue repair, regeneration and fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)). Ischaemia for as little as two hours can start an injury that takes months to heal. Prevention is redistribution of pressure (repositioning, pressure-relieving surfaces), skin care and nutrition; it is almost entirely a nursing achievement, and a pressure ulcer in hospital is a measure of care quality ([patient safety and quality improvement](../../15-medicine-in-society/01-health-systems/patient-safety-and-quality-improvement.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** [Neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) and old-age [psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md) share dementia, delirium and the memory clinic; urology and gynaecology share incontinence; [ophthalmology and ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) correct the vision and vestibular contributors to falls; orthopaedics and [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) share hip fracture; [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) shares delirium; physiotherapy, occupational therapy, nursing, dietetics and social care deliver most of the treatment; and [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) and care homes manage the syndromes where most older people live.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Geriatric syndrome | A multifactorial presentation in older people not attributable to one organ's disease |
| Delirium | Acute, fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness with a physical precipitant |
| Hypoactive delirium | The quiet, withdrawn form, commoner than the agitated form and more often missed |
| Dementia | Chronic, progressive, acquired decline in multiple cognitive domains impairing function |
| Behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia | Agitation, aggression, wandering and psychosis in dementia, usually signalling unmet need |
| Postural hypotension | A fall in blood pressure on standing from a blunted baroreflex, drugs or volume depletion |
| Multifactorial falls intervention | Combined strength and balance training, medication review, vision and home assessment |
| Orthogeriatrics | Joint surgical and geriatric care of older patients with fractures |
| Deconditioning | Loss of muscle, cardiovascular and functional capacity from inactivity, especially during admission |
| Capillary closing pressure | The external pressure (roughly 30 mmHg) above which capillary flow stops, the basis of pressure injury |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Confusion in an older patient is probably dementia."** Acute confusion is delirium until proved otherwise, has a physical cause and is reversible; the mistake is lethal because the cause goes untreated.
- **"Falls are accidents."** A fall in an older person is a symptom with a differential diagnosis, from arrhythmia and postural hypotension to sedatives and sarcopenia; the second fall is predictable from the first.
- **"Bed rest is safe for a sick older person."** Immobility causes deconditioning, thrombosis, pressure injury, delirium and pneumonia; mobilisation is a treatment.
