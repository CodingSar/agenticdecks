## Essence

A child is an organism under construction, so the central paediatric question is not only "is this child ill?" but "is this child growing and developing along its expected path?" Growth (gain in size) and development (gain in function) follow predictable trajectories whose deviation is the earliest sign of chronic disease, neglect or genetic disorder, often long before any symptom. The same incompleteness makes the child a different patient: drug handling, fluid balance, vital signs and the capacity to describe symptoms all change with age, and the history usually comes from a parent. The unifying idea is **trajectory over snapshot**: a single measurement means little; a measurement that crosses centiles means a great deal.

## Core tenets

1. **Growth is hormonally staged and nutritionally gated.** Fetal and infant growth depends on nutrition and insulin-like growth factors; mid-childhood growth is driven by growth hormone through insulin-like growth factor 1 and by thyroid hormone; the pubertal spurt adds sex steroids, which first accelerate growth and then fuse the epiphyses and end it ([growth, puberty and aging](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/03-development/growth-puberty-and-aging.md); [hormone axes](../../06-physiology/07-endocrine-system/hormone-classes-axes-and-feedback.md)). Each stage has its own failure modes: undernutrition and chronic illness slow infant growth, endocrine deficiency slows mid-childhood growth with preserved weight, and early or late puberty shifts the spurt. Final height is largely heritable, so a child is judged against its parents' heights, not only the population.

2. **The growth chart turns one child into a hypothesis test.** Centile charts are population distributions of weight, height and head circumference by age ([distributions and descriptive statistics](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/01-probability-and-measurement/distributions-and-descriptive-statistics.md)). A healthy child tracks a centile; crossing two major centiles downward ("faltering growth") is a signal regardless of the absolute value, and a head circumference diverging from the body signals hydrocephalus or microcephaly. Weight falls first in undernutrition, height first in endocrine disease, and both with a head spared in placental insufficiency. Growth is therefore the cheapest chronic-disease screen in medicine, which is why it is measured at every contact.

3. **Development proceeds in a fixed order along four domains, at variable speed.** Gross motor, fine motor and vision, hearing and language, and social skills each unfold cranio-caudally and from reflex to voluntary control as myelination and synaptic pruning proceed ([nervous tissue](../../05-tissues-and-anatomy/01-tissues/nervous-tissue.md); [higher functions](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/higher-functions-memory-language-and-consciousness.md)). Most children sit by about 8 months, walk by 18 and say single words around 12; the limit ages, not the medians, define delay. Delay in one domain suggests a focal problem (deafness for language, cerebral palsy for motor); global delay suggests a genetic, metabolic or environmental cause. Regression, the loss of acquired skills, is always abnormal and points to neurodegenerative or metabolic disease ([inborn errors of metabolism](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/inborn-errors-of-metabolism.md)).

4. **Children are not small adults pharmacologically or physiologically.** Total body water is about 75% of weight in a newborn against 60% in an adult, so water-soluble drugs distribute into a larger volume; hepatic enzymes and glomerular filtration mature over the first one to two years, so clearance is low then rises; and drug exposure is scaled by weight or body surface area rather than fixed ([elimination, half-life and dosing](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/elimination-half-life-and-dosing.md); [scale and allometry](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/scale-and-allometry.md)). Fluid turnover per kilogram is several times the adult's, so a day of vomiting dehydrates an infant that would barely trouble an adult. Normal heart and respiratory rates fall with age (a newborn's resting heart rate of 120–160 per minute is an adult's tachycardia), so every vital sign must be read against an age-specific range.

5. **The child compensates, then collapses.** A child's circulation holds blood pressure through tachycardia and vasoconstriction until a large fraction of volume is lost ([haemorrhage, shock and sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md)), so hypotension is a late and ominous sign, and the respiratory system fatigues rather than stiffens. Recognition therefore rests on work of breathing, capillary refill, mental state and parental concern more than on numbers that look reassuring until they do not ([compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation)).

6. **The consultation is triangular.** The history comes from a parent, the examination is opportunistic (listen to the chest while the child is calm, leave the throat for last) and the child's assent matters increasingly with age ([history taking and communication](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/history-taking-and-communication.md)). Parental concern is a validated predictor of serious illness, and the social history (housing, who cares for the child, school) carries diagnostic weight it rarely has in adults because the child's environment is its exposure ([the host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment)).

7. **Prevention is most of paediatrics.** Immunisation ([immunological memory and vaccination](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/03-adaptive-immunity/immunological-memory-and-vaccination.md)) has removed most of the diseases that once filled children's wards, and surveillance of growth, hearing, vision and development catches the remainder early. The return on prevention is highest here because a lifetime of benefit follows.

8. **Who else is involved.** Developmental concerns pull in community paediatrics, audiology and ophthalmology ([the ear, hearing and balance](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-ear-hearing-and-balance.md)), speech and language therapy, child psychiatry ([neurodevelopmental, personality and eating disorders](../03-psychiatry/neurodevelopmental-personality-and-eating-disorders.md)) and clinical genetics; growth failure pulls in paediatric endocrinology and gastroenterology; and the health visitor and general practitioner ([family medicine and the generalist](../05-primary-care-and-integration/family-medicine-and-the-generalist.md)) see the child far more often than any specialist and hold the chart.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Centile | The percentage of a reference population at or below a measurement for that age and sex |
| Faltering growth | Weight falling across centile lines, usually from inadequate intake, absorption or increased demand |
| Mid-parental height | The average of parental heights adjusted for sex, the child's expected genetic target |
| Bone age | Skeletal maturity on a hand radiograph; delayed in constitutional delay and endocrine disease, advanced in precocious puberty |
| Developmental milestone | An age by which a skill is acquired by most children; the limit age defines delay |
| Global developmental delay | Significant delay in two or more domains, implying a diffuse cause |
| Regression | Loss of previously acquired skills; always pathological |
| Body surface area | The scaling variable for many paediatric doses and fluid prescriptions |
| Assent | A child's agreement to care, sought alongside parental consent as understanding grows |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A child on the 3rd centile is abnormal."** By definition 3% of healthy children are there; a child who has always tracked the 3rd centile and matches small parents is normal, while a child falling from the 75th to the 25th is not.
- **"Normal blood pressure means the child is not shocked."** Children maintain pressure until late; a tachycardic, cool, drowsy child with normal pressure is in compensated shock.
- **"Late talkers catch up, so wait."** Many do, but isolated language delay is the commonest presentation of hearing loss and autism, both of which benefit from early identification.
