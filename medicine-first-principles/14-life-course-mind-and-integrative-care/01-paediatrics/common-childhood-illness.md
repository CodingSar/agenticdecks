## Essence

Most childhood illness is infection meeting a host that has not yet met the organism: a child acquires six to ten respiratory infections a year while its adaptive immune memory is being written. The clinical task is one of signal detection, finding the few children with bacterial sepsis, meningitis, pneumonia or dehydration among the many with self-limiting viral illness, using the physiological signs of decompensation rather than the diagnosis label. Small anatomy explains why the same virus that gives an adult a cold gives an infant bronchiolitis or croup, and vaccination explains why the ward of the 2020s looks nothing like the ward of the 1970s. The unifying idea is **a self-limiting illness in a body with small airways, high water turnover and little reserve**.

## Core tenets

1. **Small airways amplify ordinary inflammation.** Resistance to laminar flow rises with the fourth power of falling radius ([fluids, pressure and flow](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/fluids-pressure-and-flow.md)), so a millimetre of mucosal oedema that is trivial in an adult trachea halves the lumen of an infant's. Respiratory syncytial virus inflames the bronchioles of infants under a year (bronchiolitis: wheeze, crackles, feeding difficulty, apnoea in the youngest), parainfluenza inflames the subglottis of toddlers (croup: barking cough, inspiratory stridor). Treatment is largely supportive, with oxygen and feeding support in bronchiolitis and a single dose of corticosteroid in croup that shrinks the oedema, because the mechanism is geometry, not bacteria.

2. **Water turnover makes dehydration fast and electrolyte disturbance common.** An infant exchanges a far larger fraction of its body water each day than an adult and cannot seek fluid itself ([body fluid compartments](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/body-fluid-compartments-and-electrolytes.md)). Rotavirus or norovirus gastroenteritis therefore causes dehydration within a day: dry mucosae, sunken eyes and fontanelle, reduced skin turgor, tachycardia, and in the worst cases shock. Oral rehydration solution works because glucose-coupled sodium absorption in the small intestine continues during secretory diarrhoea ([secretion, digestion and absorption](../../06-physiology/06-gastrointestinal-system/secretion-digestion-and-absorption.md)); it is among the cheapest life-saving interventions known. Diarrhoeal disease still kills around 400,000 children under five a year worldwide (2021), nearly all where rehydration and clean water are unavailable.

3. **Fever is a sign, not a disease, and most of it is viral.** Pyrogenic cytokines reset the hypothalamic set point ([thermoregulation](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/thermoregulation-and-energy-balance.md); [acute inflammation](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/acute-inflammation.md)). The height of the fever discriminates poorly between viral and bacterial illness; what discriminates is the child's physiology: colour, activity, respiratory effort, circulation and hydration (the basis of structured "traffic-light" assessments). Febrile seizures affect roughly 2–5% of children between six months and five years and are a benign response of the immature brain to a rapid temperature rise, not epilepsy, though they frighten parents more than almost anything else.

4. **The dangerous few declare themselves by decompensation.** Bacterial meningitis, septicaemia (classically meningococcal, with a non-blanching rash from endotoxin-driven vascular injury and consumptive coagulopathy, [haemorrhage, shock and sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md)), pneumonia and urinary infection in infants present with the same early signs as benign illness. The distinguishing features are the ones that show reserve running out: grunting, recession, tachycardia out of proportion to fever, prolonged capillary refill, drowsiness, a bulging fontanelle, and the parent saying "this is different". The safety net (what to watch for, when to return) is as much a treatment as the antibiotic, because the test is time.

5. **Vaccination rewrote the epidemiology.** Conjugate vaccines against *Haemophilus influenzae* type b, pneumococcus and meningococcus removed most bacterial meningitis and epiglottitis from high-coverage countries; measles, which once killed through pneumonia and encephalitis, returns wherever coverage drops below the roughly 95% herd-immunity threshold its high transmissibility demands ([infectious disease dynamics](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/01-epidemiology/infectious-disease-dynamics.md)). Rotavirus vaccine halved gastroenteritis admissions where it is used. The residual burden is now viral respiratory illness, which no vaccine yet prevents well, and, globally, the pneumonia, diarrhoea and malaria of poverty.

6. **Childhood asthma, eczema and allergy are one family.** Atopic disease follows a sensitising exposure in a genetically susceptible, often barrier-defective host ([hypersensitivity and allergy](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md); [the skin as barrier and sensor](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/the-skin-as-barrier-and-sensor.md)). Preschool wheeze is mostly viral and resolves; asthma persisting into school age is eosinophilic airway inflammation with reversible obstruction, whose treatment logic is that of [asthma in adults](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/asthma-and-copd.md). Anaphylaxis to food is the acute face of the same immunology and adrenaline reverses the vasodilatation and bronchospasm that mast-cell mediators produce.

7. **Some rare childhood conditions matter because they are treatable only if recognised.** Intussusception (a bowel segment telescoping, with colic, pallor and red-currant-jelly stool) and pyloric stenosis (projectile vomiting with hypochloraemic alkalosis) are surgical; Kawasaki disease (prolonged fever, mucosal and skin changes) damages the coronary arteries unless treated with intravenous immunoglobulin; diabetic ketoacidosis presents as abdominal pain and heavy breathing ([diabetes mellitus](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/diabetes-mellitus.md)). Each is an organ-system disease in a child, and each has a window.

8. **Who else is involved.** Primary care sees almost all of this illness and does the signal detection ([family medicine and the generalist](../05-primary-care-and-integration/family-medicine-and-the-generalist.md)); emergency medicine receives the decompensating child ([triage and the ABCDE approach](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/triage-and-the-abcde-approach.md)); paediatric intensive care, paediatric surgery, ENT ([nose, throat and head and neck](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/nose-throat-and-head-and-neck.md), for airway and otitis media) and public health (outbreaks, vaccination) take the rest.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bronchiolitis | Viral small-airway inflammation of infancy, usually respiratory syncytial virus |
| Croup | Viral laryngotracheobronchitis with subglottic oedema, barking cough and stridor |
| Oral rehydration solution | A glucose–sodium solution that exploits intact coupled absorption during diarrhoea |
| Febrile seizure | A brief generalised seizure with fever in a child aged six months to five years, without intracranial infection |
| Non-blanching rash | Purpura or petechiae not disappearing under pressure; meningococcal sepsis until excluded |
| Recession | Indrawing of the chest wall on inspiration, a sign of increased work of breathing |
| Herd immunity threshold | The vaccinated fraction above which an infection cannot sustain transmission |
| Safety-netting | Explicit advice on what to watch for and when to return, making time a diagnostic test |
| Atopy | The tendency to produce Immunoglobulin E (IgE) against common environmental antigens |

## Common misconceptions

- **"The higher the fever, the more serious the infection."** Fever height correlates weakly with bacterial disease; the child's physiology and behaviour are far better discriminators.
- **"Febrile seizures mean epilepsy."** Simple febrile seizures carry only a small increase in later epilepsy and cause no brain damage.
- **"Antibiotics are safer than waiting."** Most childhood infections are viral; unnecessary antibiotics drive resistance and side effects without changing the course, and the safety net catches the few bacterial cases that evolve.
