## What this area is about

Paediatrics is medicine for a body that is still being built. Every organ system of layer 12 and every acute problem of layer 13 occurs in children, but in a host whose physiology, anatomy, pharmacology, immunity and capacity to consent change month by month. The question the area answers is: how does disease present, progress and respond in an organism whose normal state is itself a moving target, and how do we tell illness from a variation in the timetable?

The unifying idea is **development as the reference frame**. A newborn's circulation, a toddler's airways, a school child's immune memory and an adolescent's brain are each normal for their age and abnormal for any other. The paediatrician therefore reasons about trajectories (growth charts, milestones, pubertal staging) rather than fixed reference ranges, and treats the family and environment as part of the patient because the child cannot choose either.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Obstetrics and gynaecology](../02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md):** the fetus is the shared patient. Antenatal diagnosis, timing of delivery, antenatal steroids and the management of labour decide the neonatologist's caseload; maternal diabetes, infection and drugs act on the child.
- **[Psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md):** neurodevelopmental disorders and most adult mental illness begin in childhood and adolescence; child and adolescent psychiatry sits on both areas.
- **[Geriatrics and palliative care](../04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md):** paediatric palliative care uses the same symptom-control principles for children with life-limiting disease; the ethics of withholding treatment at the limit of viability mirror those at the end of life.
- **[Primary care and integration](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md):** general practice and health visiting see children far more often than paediatricians do, deliver immunisation and surveillance, and hold the child's chart across the whole life course.

## Interfaces with other specialties

Paediatrics is organised like adult medicine in miniature, so its borders are with almost everyone. It **receives** from [obstetrics](../02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/README.md) (every newborn, planned or emergency), from [emergency medicine](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/04-emergency-medicine/README.md) (the febrile, injured or decompensating child) and from [primary care](../05-primary-care-and-integration/README.md) (faltering growth, developmental concern, recurrent infection, the child who worries the general practitioner). It **refers** to [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and its paediatric subspecialties (pyloric stenosis, intussusception, appendicitis, malformations, fractures), to [anaesthesia](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/02-anaesthesia-and-perioperative-medicine/README.md) (small airways, weight-based dosing, induction without cooperation), to [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) (paediatric and neonatal intensive care are its own units), to [ophthalmology and ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) (retinopathy of prematurity, squint, hearing screening, otitis media, tonsillectomy) and to [dermatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/11-dermatology/README.md) (eczema, birthmarks, infections).

Each adult organ-system specialty has a paediatric counterpart that shares its physiology but not its diseases: paediatric [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md) is congenital rather than atherosclerotic, paediatric [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md) is nephrotic syndrome and reflux rather than diabetic kidney disease, paediatric [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md) is leukaemia and embryonal tumours with cure rates above 80% rather than carcinoma, paediatric [endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md) is growth, puberty and type 1 diabetes, paediatric [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) is epilepsy and cerebral palsy, and paediatric [infectious diseases](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md) is the immunologically naive host. **Transition** at 16–18 years, from each of these to its adult twin, is a formal hand-over and a known point of loss to follow-up.

**Disputed and shared territory:** the adolescent between paediatric and adult services; the child with a functional disorder between paediatrics and [psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md); the febrile child in an emergency department staffed by either specialty; and the newborn with a surgical malformation, jointly held by neonatology, paediatric surgery and cardiology. **Multidisciplinary decisions** include the fetal medicine meeting (obstetrics, neonatology, genetics, surgery), the cleft, neuromuscular and oncology teams, and the safeguarding strategy meeting with social care and police. **Emergencies that cross the boundary** are the collapsed neonate (neonatology and cardiology), the child with a compromised airway (ENT, anaesthesia, paediatrics) and major trauma (emergency medicine, surgery, paediatric intensive care).

## Connections across layers

From below, paediatrics takes [development](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/README.md) (the timetable), [fetal and neonatal physiology](../../06-physiology/09-reproduction-and-the-life-course/fetal-and-neonatal-physiology.md) and [growth and puberty](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/03-development/growth-puberty-and-aging.md), [immunology](../../07-microbiology-and-immunology/README.md) (the naive host and the vaccine), [pathology](../../08-pathology/README.md) (genetic, metabolic and malformation disease), [pharmacokinetics](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/01-pharmacokinetics/README.md) (why dose scales with size and age) and [screening theory](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/README.md). To the layers above it gives adults: survivors of congenital disease to layer 12, and the evidence for prevention, immunisation and social determinants that [medicine in society](../../15-medicine-in-society/README.md) builds policy on.

## Cross-cutting threads

- [Scale and surface-to-volume](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#scale-and-surface-to-volume): heat loss, water turnover, airway resistance and dosing all follow from the child's size.
- [Time courses](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#time-courses): growth and development are trajectories; deviation from the trajectory is the signal.
- [Compensation and decompensation](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#compensation-and-decompensation): the child holds blood pressure until late, then collapses.
- [Redundancy and reserve](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#redundancy-and-reserve): the newborn has little, the healthy child much, which is why children recover from illnesses that kill adults.
- [The host and its environment](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#the-host-and-its-environment): the child cannot choose its home, food or carers; safeguarding and social history are clinical data.
- [Harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit): vaccination and newborn screening are the clearest cases of population benefit from intervention in the healthy.
