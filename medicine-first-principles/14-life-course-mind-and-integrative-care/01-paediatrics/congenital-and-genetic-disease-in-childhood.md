# Congenital and genetic disease in childhood

## Essence

About 2–3% of babies are born with a major structural malformation, and a comparable fraction carry a single-gene or chromosomal disorder that will declare itself in childhood. These conditions are where development (layer 04) and pathology (layer 08) meet the patient: a gene that fails to pattern the heart, an enzyme that cannot clear a metabolite, a chromosome present in triplicate. Paediatrics adds what the mechanism alone does not give: when the condition presents, how it is found before it harms, how it is managed across every organ it touches, and how a child with it becomes an adult with it. The unifying idea is **a fixed defect meeting a growing body**: the lesion does not change, but its consequences do as the circulation transitions, the diet changes and the organs grow.

## Core tenets

1. **Timing of presentation follows physiology, not the lesion's severity.** A ventricular septal defect is silent at birth because fetal pulmonary resistance keeps the shunt small; as resistance falls over weeks the left-to-right flow grows, the lungs are overperfused and the infant breathes fast, sweats on feeding and fails to grow, which is heart failure by the mechanism of [heart failure](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/heart-failure.md) with a volume load. A coarctation or hypoplastic left heart presents with collapse when the duct closes ([the newborn](the-newborn-and-neonatology.md)). Tetralogy of Fallot presents with cyanosis as right-sided outflow obstruction forces blood right-to-left. Congenital heart disease affects roughly 8–9 per 1,000 live births and the anatomy predicts the week of presentation.

2. **Chromosomal disorders are dosage disorders of many genes at once.** Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome, about 1 in 700 births, rising steeply with maternal age) produces a recognisable pattern (hypotonia, characteristic facies, atrioventricular septal defects, duodenal atresia, hypothyroidism, early Alzheimer pathology) because hundreds of genes are overexpressed by half ([chromosomes and cytogenetics](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/chromosomes-and-cytogenetics.md)). Microdeletions such as 22q11 (cardiac outflow defects, hypocalcaemia, immunodeficiency, later psychosis) show the same logic at smaller scale. Dysmorphology, the systematic reading of face, hands and growth, is the clinical method that points to which test to order.

3. **Single-gene disorders present through the physiology of the missing protein.** Cystic fibrosis (about 1 in 2,500–3,000 births of Northern European ancestry) is a chloride channel failure that dehydrates every secretion: thick mucus obstructs airways and invites chronic infection, pancreatic ducts block so fat is malabsorbed and the child fails to thrive, and sweat is salty, which is the diagnostic test ([membrane transport and ion gradients](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/04-cell-signalling/membrane-transport-and-ion-gradients.md)). Modulator drugs that correct the folding and gating of the mutant channel now change the course for most genotypes, a direct example of therapy aimed at mechanism. Sickle cell disease is a haemoglobin that polymerises when deoxygenated ([anaemias](../../12-organ-system-specialties/06-haematology/anaemias.md)); Duchenne muscular dystrophy is a missing cytoskeletal protein in muscle, presenting as a boy who cannot climb stairs by age five.

4. **Inborn errors of metabolism present when the diet or the stress changes.** A block in a pathway causes disease by the substrate that accumulates, the product that is missing or the toxic side-product that forms ([inborn errors of metabolism](../../08-pathology/04-genetic-metabolic-and-environmental-disease/inborn-errors-of-metabolism.md)). Phenylketonuria harms the brain only after protein feeding begins; medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency kills only during fasting, when fat oxidation is needed; urea cycle defects present with encephalopathy as ammonia rises. Each is rare, but together they are about 1 in 1,500–2,500 births, and most that matter are treatable by removing the substrate, supplying the product or avoiding the stress, which is the case for screening them at birth ([screening principles](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/screening-principles-and-biases.md)).

5. **Diagnosis has moved from phenotype to genome, and that changes what a diagnosis means.** Antenatal ultrasound at 18–22 weeks finds most major structural anomalies; cell-free fetal DNA in maternal blood screens for the common trisomies with high sensitivity; chromosomal microarray and exome or genome sequencing now find a cause in a substantial fraction of children with unexplained developmental disorders ([histopathology, cytology and molecular diagnostics](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/histopathology-cytology-and-molecular-diagnostics.md)). A genomic result is a probability statement about one child and a statement about relatives and future pregnancies, with variants of uncertain significance, incomplete penetrance and incidental findings as the costs ([genomic variation and mutation](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/genomic-variation-and-mutation.md)).

6. **The pedigree is the first genetic test and the family is the patient.** Inheritance pattern (autosomal recessive in consanguineous families, X-linked in affected boys with carrier mothers, dominant with variable expression) predicts recurrence risk ([Mendelian inheritance and pedigrees](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/mendelian-inheritance-and-pedigrees.md)). Genetic counselling converts a mechanism into a decision: carrier testing, prenatal diagnosis, preimplantation genetic testing or acceptance. The ethics (whose information is it, testing children for adult-onset disease, termination) are those of [consent, capacity and confidentiality](../../15-medicine-in-society/02-ethics-law-and-the-profession/consent-capacity-and-confidentiality.md).

7. **Survival has turned childhood diseases into lifelong ones.** Median survival in cystic fibrosis in well-resourced countries has risen from early childhood in the 1960s to beyond 50 years for children born in the 2020s (projected); most children with congenital heart disease now reach adulthood with repaired or palliated circulations that need surveillance for arrhythmia, ventricular failure and pregnancy risk. Transition to adult services, from paediatric cardiology to [adult congenital cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/valvular-and-structural-heart-disease.md), from paediatric to adult respiratory medicine, is a structured hand-over and a recognised point of failure.

8. **Who else is involved.** Fetal medicine and obstetrics find and plan for the lesion before birth ([pregnancy and its complications](../02-obstetrics-and-gynaecology/pregnancy-and-its-complications.md)); paediatric surgery and cardiac surgery repair what can be repaired ([surgical subspecialties](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/surgical-subspecialties.md)); clinical genetics interprets and counsels; the organ-system specialties of layer 12 inherit the adult; community paediatrics, education and social care manage disability ([rehabilitation and disability](../05-primary-care-and-integration/rehabilitation-and-disability.md)); and primary care coordinates a family whose child has a dozen appointments.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Malformation | A structural defect from abnormal development; distinguished from deformation (mechanical) and disruption (destruction of normal tissue) |
| Dysmorphology | Systematic examination for the pattern of minor anomalies that suggests a syndrome |
| Left-to-right shunt | Flow from the systemic to the pulmonary side through a defect, causing pulmonary overcirculation and heart failure |
| Cyanotic heart disease | Lesions in which deoxygenated blood reaches the systemic circulation |
| Chromosomal microarray | Genome-wide detection of deletions and duplications too small for a karyotype |
| Exome sequencing | Sequencing of the protein-coding 1–2% of the genome, where most known Mendelian variants lie |
| Variant of uncertain significance | A sequence change whose effect on function is unknown; a cost of broad testing |
| Penetrance | The fraction of carriers of a genotype who show the phenotype |
| Transition | The planned move of a young person with a chronic condition from paediatric to adult services |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Congenital means present at birth, so it presents at birth."** Many malformations are silent until the circulation, diet or demand changes; some single-gene disorders present in adulthood.
- **"A genetic disease cannot be treated."** Dietary restriction, enzyme replacement, channel modulators, transfusion and surgery each reverse a step of the mechanism; gene therapy now does so for a few ([biologics and advanced therapies](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/biologics-and-advanced-therapies.md)).
- **"Finding the variant explains the child."** Penetrance, expressivity and variants of uncertain significance mean a genotype is a probability, not a verdict.
