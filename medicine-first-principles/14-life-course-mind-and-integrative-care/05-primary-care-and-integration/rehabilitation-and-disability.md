## Essence

Rehabilitation is the part of medicine that works on what a person can do rather than on what disease they have. After a stroke, a fracture, a heart attack, an amputation, a spinal cord injury or a stay in intensive care, the lesion may be fixed but the loss of function is not: the nervous system can re-learn, muscle can be rebuilt, tasks can be re-engineered and the environment changed so that an impairment does not become a disability. Rehabilitation medicine's conceptual tool is the World Health Organization's framework separating body structure and function, activity and participation, and its evidence base shows that organised, goal-directed, multidisciplinary rehabilitation after stroke, cardiac events and lung disease reduces death and dependency. Disability, meanwhile, is as much a property of the environment and society as of the body. The unifying idea is **function as the outcome**: the same lesion produces very different lives depending on what is done afterward.

## Core tenets

1. **Impairment, activity limitation and participation restriction are three different things, and interventions target each.** The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (2001) distinguishes body structure and function (a paralysed arm), activity (dressing oneself) and participation (returning to work), each shaped by environmental and personal factors. Physiotherapy acts on the impairment, occupational therapy on the activity, vocational and social intervention on participation, and a ramp or a wheelchair on the environment. A person with complete paraplegia has an unchangeable impairment and, with a wheelchair, an accessible home and an employer who adapts, little participation restriction; the social model of disability states this as a principle ([structure-determines-function](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#structure-determines-function)).

2. **Neurological recovery is use-dependent plasticity, so rehabilitation is training.** After a stroke, surviving cortex and spared pathways take over lost functions through synaptic strengthening, unmasking of latent connections and cortical map reorganisation ([motor control](../../06-physiology/02-nervous-system/motor-control.md); [stroke](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/stroke.md)). This plasticity is driven by repetition of the specific task, by intensity, and by the period of heightened plasticity in the first weeks to months; it is suppressed by disuse, which is why "learned non-use" of a weak limb entrenches the deficit and constraint of the good limb can reverse it. Spontaneous recovery and training interact: most recovery occurs in the first three to six months, and the slope depends on the dose of practice. The same principles apply after spinal cord injury, brain injury and in cerebral palsy.

3. **Organised stroke unit care saves lives and independence, and it is mostly rehabilitation.** Meta-analysis of trials of stroke units (geographically defined wards with a multidisciplinary team, early mobilisation, swallowing assessment and goal-setting) shows fewer deaths, less dependency and less institutional care than general wards, with a number needed to treat of around 20 for death or dependency, an effect size larger than that of thrombolysis across the whole stroke population ([randomized controlled trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md)). The active ingredients are the team, the early start and the prevention of complications (aspiration pneumonia, pressure injury, thrombosis, contracture, depression) rather than any single technique.

4. **Deconditioning is a disease of inactivity and reconditioning is its treatment.** Bed rest produces muscle wasting, cardiovascular deconditioning (reduced plasma volume, stroke volume and orthostatic tolerance), bone loss, insulin resistance, joint stiffness and low mood within days to weeks ([exercise and stress physiology](../../06-physiology/01-integrative-principles/exercise-and-stress-physiology.md); [skeletal muscle contraction and energetics](../../06-physiology/10-musculoskeletal-system-and-skin/skeletal-muscle-contraction-and-energetics.md)). Survivors of critical illness lose a large fraction of their muscle mass in a week and many have weakness, cognitive impairment and post-traumatic symptoms a year later (post-intensive-care syndrome, [organ support and ICU syndromes](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/organ-support-and-icu-syndromes.md)). Progressive resistance and aerobic training reverse the physiology at any age; cardiac rehabilitation after myocardial infarction reduces cardiovascular mortality and readmission, and pulmonary rehabilitation in chronic lung disease improves exercise capacity and breathlessness more than any drug ([asthma and COPD](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/asthma-and-copd.md); [supply and demand](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#supply-and-demand)).

5. **Musculoskeletal rehabilitation is the management of tissue healing under load.** Bone, tendon, ligament and muscle heal through inflammation, proliferation and remodelling ([tissue repair, regeneration and fibrosis](../../08-pathology/01-cell-and-tissue-injury/tissue-repair-regeneration-and-fibrosis.md)), and the remodelled tissue is only as strong as the loads it has been asked to bear: immobilisation produces weak, stiff tissue, while graded loading within the tissue's current tolerance stimulates collagen alignment and strength ([mechanics and materials](../../00-physical-and-chemical-foundations/01-physics-of-the-body/mechanics-and-materials.md)). Rehabilitation after fracture fixation or joint replacement ([orthopaedics, fractures and joints](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/orthopaedics-fractures-and-joints.md)) is therefore the controlled application of load over time, and for most chronic back pain active rehabilitation outperforms rest and imaging.

6. **Prosthetics, orthotics and assistive technology replace or augment function.** An amputee's prosthesis must transfer load across a socket to residual bone and soft tissue without injuring the skin, and microprocessor knees, powered ankles and osseointegrated implants have changed what is achievable ([devices, implants and biomaterials](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/devices-implants-and-biomaterials.md)). Wheelchairs, communication aids, environmental controls, hearing aids and cochlear implants ([the ear, hearing and balance](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-ear-hearing-and-balance.md)) act at the activity and participation levels; the World Health Organization estimates that over 2.5 billion people need one or more assistive products and that most in low-income countries have no access (2022).

7. **Goals, measurement and the team are the method.** Rehabilitation is organised around goals the patient sets (walk to the shop, return to work), broken into measurable steps and reviewed; standardised function scales (the Barthel index, the modified Rankin scale, the Functional Independence Measure) make progress and outcomes comparable. The team (physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy, psychology, nursing, social work, rehabilitation medicine, prosthetics and orthotics) meets, sets and reviews the goals together, and the patient and family are members of it ([prognosis and shared decision-making](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/prognosis-and-shared-decision-making.md)).

8. **Disability is a social and economic fact as much as a medical one.** Around 1.3 billion people, about 16% of the world's population, live with significant disability (World Health Organization, 2023), and disabled people have worse access to healthcare, education and employment, and die earlier, from the barriers they meet rather than only from their impairments ([social determinants and health inequity](../../15-medicine-in-society/03-populations-and-the-future/social-determinants-and-health-inequity.md)). Rehabilitation medicine's claim is that disability is partly a design choice made by societies, and legislation on accessibility and discrimination is a health intervention.

9. **Who else is involved.** Rehabilitation receives from [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) (stroke, multiple sclerosis, brain and spinal injury), [surgery](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/01-surgery/README.md) and orthopaedics (fracture, joint replacement, amputation), [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md) and [pulmonology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/README.md) (cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation), [critical care](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/03-critical-care/README.md) (post-intensive-care syndrome), [oncology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/07-oncology/README.md) (cancer survivorship), [rheumatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) (arthritis, back pain), [geriatrics](../04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/README.md) (deconditioning, falls), [paediatrics](../01-paediatrics/README.md) (cerebral palsy, neurodevelopmental disability), [psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md) (psychosocial rehabilitation in psychosis) and [ophthalmology and ENT](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/README.md) (vision and hearing rehabilitation); it hands back to [primary care](family-medicine-and-the-generalist.md) and to the social and employment services that decide participation.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Impairment | Loss or abnormality of body structure or function |
| Activity limitation | Difficulty executing a task (walking, dressing, speaking) |
| Participation restriction | Difficulty taking part in life situations (work, relationships, community) |
| Social model of disability | The view that disability arises from the interaction of impairment with an environment that excludes |
| Use-dependent plasticity | Reorganisation of neural circuits driven by repeated, task-specific practice |
| Learned non-use | Suppression of a weak limb's use that entrenches deficit beyond the lesion's effect |
| Deconditioning | Loss of muscular, cardiovascular and metabolic fitness from inactivity |
| Stroke unit | A geographically defined ward with a coordinated multidisciplinary rehabilitation team |
| Goal setting | Patient-centred, measurable targets that organise the rehabilitation programme |
| Assistive technology | Devices and systems that maintain or improve function and participation |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Recovery after stroke is spontaneous, so therapy only helps the patient cope."** Recovery is use-dependent plasticity; the dose and specificity of practice change the slope, and organised rehabilitation reduces death and dependency in trials.
- **"Rest heals."** Beyond the first days, rest deconditions; tissue, muscle, bone and brain all remodel to the loads and tasks they are given, and graded activity is the treatment for most musculoskeletal and cardiorespiratory conditions.
- **"Disability is a medical condition."** The impairment is; the disability is the product of impairment and an environment, and changing the environment is often more effective than changing the body.
