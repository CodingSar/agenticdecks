## Essence

Non-communicable chronic diseases (cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic lung disease, cancer, chronic kidney disease, depression) cause about three-quarters of deaths worldwide (2019) and most of the work of health systems, yet almost all of their management happens outside any clinic: in the patient's decisions about food, activity, smoking, tablets and when to seek help. Chronic disease care is therefore a control problem in which the patient is the controller, the clinician sets targets and supplies tools and feedback, and the system is organised to make the right action the easy one. Its logic runs from the risk factor through the surrogate marker to the hard outcome, and its evidence comes from trials that measure how much a sustained change in a marker changes events years later. The unifying idea is **the patient as the primary clinician**, with professionals as consultants to a process they do not control.

## Core tenets

1. **Chronic disease is managed through surrogate markers whose link to outcomes is established by trials.** Blood pressure, glycated haemoglobin, low-density-lipoprotein cholesterol, forced expiratory volume, estimated glomerular filtration rate and viral load are measured because trials have shown that moving them moves events: each 10 mmHg reduction in systolic pressure lowers major cardiovascular events by about a fifth (meta-analysis, 2016), and each 1 mmol/L reduction in low-density-lipoprotein cholesterol by a similar fraction over five years ([randomized controlled trials](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/02-evidence-based-medicine/randomized-controlled-trials.md); [hypertension and vascular disease](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/hypertension-and-vascular-disease.md)). A surrogate is only as good as that link: drugs that improved a marker but worsened outcomes (some anti-arrhythmics, some glucose-lowering agents) are the warning ([cause and mechanism](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#cause-and-mechanism)).

2. **Benefit is proportional to absolute risk, so the same treatment is worth different amounts to different people.** A statin that cuts relative risk by a quarter prevents one event in about 20 people over five years at 20% baseline risk and one in 200 at 2% ([measures of frequency and association](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/01-epidemiology/measures-of-frequency-and-association.md)). Risk scores combining age, sex, pressure, lipids, smoking and diabetes estimate the baseline and so the number needed to treat ([risk prediction and stratification](../../10-epidemiology-and-evidence/03-screening-and-prevention/risk-prediction-and-stratification.md)). Treatment thresholds are therefore value judgements about how many people should take a tablet for one to benefit, which is why they shift with guidelines, cost and the patient's preferences ([harm and benefit](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#harm-and-benefit)).

3. **Adherence is the rate-limiting step, and it is about 50%.** Roughly half of patients with chronic disease do not take medicines as prescribed (World Health Organization, 2003), and the proportion falls with the number of drugs, the complexity of the regimen, the absence of symptoms and the presence of adverse effects, depression or cost. A drug that works in a trial, where adherence is monitored, works less well in practice for this reason alone ([behaviour change and the placebo effect](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/04-non-drug-therapeutics/behaviour-change-and-the-placebo-effect.md)). Simplifying regimens, once-daily and fixed-dose combinations, asking about adherence without judgement and addressing beliefs about the illness and the drug improve it more than exhortation.

4. **Self-management is a feedback loop the patient closes.** A person with diabetes who measures glucose and adjusts insulin, with asthma who steps up treatment when peak flow falls, with heart failure who weighs themselves and takes an extra diuretic, or with hypertension who records home readings is running a control loop: measure, compare with target, act ([feedback and control](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/03-systems-and-models/feedback-and-control.md); [feedback and homeostasis](../../CROSS-CUTTING.md#feedback-and-homeostasis)). Structured education that teaches the loop (problem-solving, action plans, confidence) improves glycaemic control, reduces asthma admissions and heart failure readmissions. Home measurement removes the white-coat and regression-to-the-mean artefacts of clinic readings ([measurement error and biological variation](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/01-probability-and-measurement/measurement-error-and-biological-variation.md)).

5. **The system must be designed for chronic care, because the acute model fails it.** The Chronic Care Model (Wagner, 1998) specifies what works: a disease register that identifies everyone with the condition, planned recall rather than reactive visits, decision support embedded in the record, a team in which nurses and pharmacists do most routine review, self-management support, and links to community resources. Pay-for-performance schemes that reward recorded targets improve measurement and modestly improve intermediate outcomes, but they also shift attention to what is measured and away from what is not, and can penalise appropriate deviation in the frail ([patient safety and quality improvement](../../15-medicine-in-society/01-health-systems/patient-safety-and-quality-improvement.md)).

6. **One patient, many diseases: the diabetic is the paradigm of integration.** A person with type 2 diabetes needs glycaemic, blood pressure and lipid control (primary care), annual retinal photography ([the eye and vision loss](../../13-acute-perioperative-and-procedural-care/05-ophthalmology-and-ent/the-eye-and-vision-loss.md)), urine albumin and kidney function with nephrology referral when filtration falls ([chronic kidney disease and dialysis](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/chronic-kidney-disease-and-dialysis.md)), foot examination with podiatry and vascular surgery for ulcers, cardiology for the coronary disease that kills most of them, obstetrics if pregnant and psychiatry for the depression that doubles poor outcomes ([diabetes mellitus](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/diabetes-mellitus.md)). Each specialty sees one complication; the register, the recall and the person who reads all the letters sit in primary care ([how the specialties interact](how-the-specialties-interact.md)).

7. **Multimorbidity changes the goal from each disease's target to the person's priorities.** Guidelines for six conditions prescribe a dozen drugs and dozens of actions a day; treatment burden competes with life and with each other (a beta-blocker for the heart worsens the lungs; tight glucose control raises hypoglycaemia in the frail). The negotiation of what matters most, which targets to relax and which drugs to stop is shared decision-making applied to a whole life ([prognosis and shared decision-making](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/01-the-clinical-encounter/prognosis-and-shared-decision-making.md); [frailty, multimorbidity and polypharmacy](../04-geriatrics-and-palliative-care/frailty-multimorbidity-and-polypharmacy.md)).

8. **Who else is involved.** Every organ-system specialty of layer 12 manages the complicated end of a chronic disease and sets the targets ([endocrinology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/05-endocrinology/README.md), [cardiology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/01-cardiology/README.md), [pulmonology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/02-pulmonology/README.md), [nephrology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/03-nephrology/README.md), [neurology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/10-neurology/README.md) for epilepsy and Parkinson disease, [rheumatology](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/README.md) for inflammatory arthritis, [infectious diseases](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/README.md) for HIV as a chronic disease); [psychiatry](../03-psychiatry/README.md) for the depression and severe mental illness that are themselves chronic diseases and worsen the others; nursing, pharmacy, dietetics and podiatry deliver most review; [rehabilitation](rehabilitation-and-disability.md) restores function; and [primary care](family-medicine-and-the-generalist.md) holds the register and the person.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Non-communicable disease | Chronic disease not transmitted between people: cardiovascular, cancer, respiratory, diabetes and others |
| Surrogate marker | A measurable variable whose change is taken, on trial evidence, to predict change in outcomes |
| Absolute risk reduction | The difference in event rate between treated and untreated; its reciprocal is the number needed to treat |
| Treatment threshold | The level of risk or marker above which treatment is offered; a value judgement informed by evidence |
| Adherence | The extent to which a patient takes treatment as agreed; about 50% in chronic disease |
| Self-management | The patient's monitoring and adjustment of their own treatment and behaviour according to a plan |
| Action plan | Written instructions for what to do when a measurement or symptom crosses a threshold |
| Chronic Care Model | Wagner's design: register, planned care, decision support, team, self-management support, community links |
| Treat-to-target | Adjusting treatment until a marker reaches a trial-validated goal |
| Treatment burden | The work and cost to the patient of following all their treatments |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A lower marker is always better."** The link between marker and outcome is specific to the drug and population tested; intensive glucose lowering in frail older people increased harm, and some drugs improved markers while raising mortality.
- **"Non-adherence is the patient's failure."** It is the predictable result of asymptomatic disease, complex regimens, adverse effects and cost; it is a design problem for the clinician and the system as much as a behaviour of the patient.
- **"Chronic disease management is giving the right drug."** The drug is one component; the register, the recall, the education, the feedback loop and the relationship determine whether it is taken and whether it works.
