## What this area is about

Physics sets the forces; chemistry supplies the molecules those forces act on. This area answers: *what chemical facts must be true for a body to exist, stay within the narrow conditions that keep it alive, extract energy from food, and pass signals between molecules?* It is deliberately not a biochemistry course. It stops where the molecules become the body's own (proteins, nucleic acids, metabolic pathways belong to [02 Biochemistry](../../02-biochemistry-and-molecular-biology/README.md)) and says instead what any molecule in water must obey.

The unifying idea is **the weak bond at body temperature**. Thermal energy at 37 °C (about 2.6 kJ/mol) is far below the strength of a covalent bond and close to the strength of a hydrogen bond or an ionic contact in water. Covalent bonds therefore fix what a molecule *is*; weak bonds, forming and breaking constantly, decide what it *does*: whether it dissolves, which compartment it enters, whether it is charged at blood pH, which electrons it gives up, how fast it reacts and which partner it recognises. Medicine's most used ideas, from blood pH to receptor affinity to the oxygen debt of a hypoxic cell, are weak-bond chemistry.

Read atoms and bonds first, then water: together they say what a molecule is and where it goes. Kinetics and equilibrium then supply the constants (K, Kd, Km, half-life) that the next three leaves use. Acids and bases and redox are the two transfer chemistries, of protons and of electrons; they are coupled in the mitochondrion and in the red cell, and each has a clinical arithmetic (the blood gas, the respiratory chain's ATP yield). Carbon chemistry and recognition closes the area by naming the building blocks and stating the principle, complementary surfaces held by many weak bonds, on which enzymes, receptors, antibodies and drugs all rest.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Physics of the body](../01-physics-of-the-body/README.md):** every chemical quantity here is a physical one in disguise. The equilibrium constant is free energy ([thermodynamics](../01-physics-of-the-body/energy-and-thermodynamics.md)); concentration and osmolality are what [diffusion and osmosis](../01-physics-of-the-body/diffusion-osmosis-and-transport.md) move; reduction potential and the mitochondrial proton gradient are the voltages of [excitable membranes](../01-physics-of-the-body/electricity-and-excitable-membranes.md); the hydrophobic effect that shapes membranes and proteins is entropy. Where physics gives the body its gradients, chemistry gives it the molecules that build and spend them.

From [01 Quantitative and Scientific Foundations](../../01-quantitative-and-scientific-foundations/README.md) this area takes exponential decay (half-life), the logarithm (pH, reduction potential), and the idea of signal fidelity against noise. [09 Pharmacology](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/README.md) is this area's most direct heir: absorption is pKa and polarity, receptor theory is Kd, elimination is first-order and saturable kinetics, and side effects are imperfect recognition. [11 Clinical Method and Diagnostics](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/README.md) reads concentrations, gaps and blood gases as the arithmetic of this layer.
