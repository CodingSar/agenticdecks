## Essence

A living body is an open system held far from equilibrium. It takes in chemical energy as food and oxygen, uses a fraction to build and maintain order (gradients, macromolecules, movement) and releases the rest as heat and low-grade waste. Two laws bound everything it does: energy is conserved (the first law), so every calorie eaten is stored, worked or radiated; and useful work can only be extracted while a gradient exists (the second law), so order inside the body is paid for by greater disorder outside it. The quantity that tells you whether a biological process can run on its own is the free energy change, and the body's whole metabolic design is a way of coupling processes that release free energy to processes that need it.

## Core tenets

1. **Energy is conserved, so energy balance is bookkeeping.** Intake equals heat produced plus external work plus stored energy. An adult at rest dissipates about 80–100 W, roughly 7–8 MJ (1,700–2,000 kcal) a day, nearly all as heat. A sustained surplus of a few hundred kilojoules a day is stored as fat; this is why body weight changes slowly and why obesity and starvation are, at bottom, first-law problems.

2. **Free energy, not energy, decides direction.** For a process at constant temperature and pressure, the Gibbs free energy change is `ΔG = ΔH − TΔS`; it runs spontaneously only if `ΔG < 0`. A reaction can absorb heat yet proceed if it raises entropy enough (dissolving a salt), or release heat yet stall if it orders the system too much. Hydrolysis of Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) has `ΔG` of about −50 kJ/mol under cellular conditions, which is the currency that drives otherwise uphill reactions.

3. **Life maintains order by coupling.** The second law forbids a decrease in total entropy, but not a local one paid for elsewhere. Every pump, synthesis and contraction is coupled to an exergonic reaction (usually ATP hydrolysis, ultimately food oxidation) so that the sum of the two has `ΔG < 0`. A cell that stops coupling, through hypoxia or poisoning of its mitochondria, runs down its gradients within minutes and dies when they are flat.

4. **Concentration gradients are stored free energy.** Moving a mole of solute from concentration `C1` to `C2` costs or yields `RT ln(C2/C1)`, about 5.7 kJ/mol per tenfold ratio at body temperature; for an ion a term for the voltage is added. The sodium gradient across a cell membrane is a battery charged by the sodium pump and discharged to import glucose and amino acids and to fire action potentials. Gradients, not bonds, are where much of the body's immediately usable energy sits.

5. **Metabolic rate sets heat production, and heat must leave at the same rate.** Because almost all metabolic energy ends as heat, the body's core temperature is a balance between production (about 1 W/kg at rest, ten times more in hard exercise) and loss by radiation, convection, conduction and evaporation. Evaporation of 1 L of sweat removes about 2.4 MJ. A fever is a raised set point, not a failure of this balance; hyperthermia is the balance lost.

6. **Efficiency is bounded, so work always costs heat.** Muscle converts chemical to mechanical energy at about 20–25% efficiency; the rest is heat, which is why exercise warms you. Oxidative phosphorylation captures roughly 40% of the free energy of glucose oxidation as ATP. No biological engine can beat the second law, so any claim of "energy from nothing" in physiology or in a treatment is wrong.

7. **Temperature sets rates.** Reaction rates rise roughly 2–3-fold per 10 °C (the Q10 rule) because more molecules exceed the activation barrier. This is why hypothermia protects the brain during cardiac surgery and some cardiac arrests (less oxygen demand per minute), and why fever speeds both immune reactions and metabolic demand.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| First law | Energy is conserved; intake = heat + work + storage |
| Second law | Total entropy never decreases; local order is paid for by disorder elsewhere |
| Gibbs free energy (`ΔG`) | `ΔH − TΔS`; negative means a process can run without input |
| Enthalpy (`ΔH`) | Heat exchanged at constant pressure |
| Entropy (`S`) | A measure of the number of microscopic arrangements; disorder |
| Coupling | Linking an exergonic reaction to an endergonic one so the pair runs |
| Open system | Exchanges matter and energy with surroundings; the body is one |
| Basal metabolic rate | Energy expenditure at rest, ~80–100 W in an adult |
| Q10 | Factor by which a rate rises for a 10 °C increase, ~2–3 for biology |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Living things violate the second law by creating order."** They export more entropy (heat, carbon dioxide, urea) than they remove internally; the total always rises.
- **"ATP stores energy in a 'high-energy bond'."** The energy is in the reaction, not the bond: ATP hydrolysis is favourable because the products are more stable and more hydrated than the reactant, and because cells keep ATP far above its equilibrium concentration.
- **"Calories in, calories out is wrong because metabolism varies."** It is exactly right; what varies is the "out" term (metabolic rate, absorption, activity), which depends on hormones, gut flora and behaviour.
