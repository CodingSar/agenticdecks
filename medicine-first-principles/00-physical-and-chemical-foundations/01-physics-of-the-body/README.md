## What this area is about

The body is matter, and it obeys the same laws as a steam engine, a river or a loudspeaker. This area answers: *which physical laws constrain what a living body can be and do, and what do they predict about how it is built and how it fails?* A reader who knows these seven leaves can see the circulation as a pump and resistors, the lung as a diffusion membrane, a nerve as a leaky cable, a bone as a hollow beam and a newborn as a small object with too much surface, before learning any physiology.

The unifying idea is **gradients and the cost of maintaining them**. Energy flows down gradients of temperature, pressure, concentration and voltage; that flow does the body's work; and the body spends chemical energy to rebuild the gradients it has spent. Each leaf is one kind of gradient (pressure, concentration, electrical, mechanical stress, wave energy) with its own law relating flow to driving force and resistance, and one more leaf, scale, explains why the body's size dictates which laws dominate.

Read thermodynamics first: everything else is a special case of energy moving down a gradient. Scale comes next because it says which gradients matter at which size. Then the three transport leaves, diffusion (across walls), fluids (along tubes) and electricity (ions across membranes), which together describe how substance and signal move. Mechanics covers the solid structures that contain the flows, and waves covers energy that passes through the body from outside, or is emitted from it, which is the basis of the senses and of every imaging method.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Chemistry of life](../02-chemistry-of-life/README.md):** physics supplies the forces, chemistry the molecules they act on. Thermodynamics becomes [kinetics and equilibrium](../02-chemistry-of-life/kinetics-equilibrium-and-catalysis.md) when the gradient is one of chemical potential; diffusion and osmosis presuppose [water and solutions](../02-chemistry-of-life/water-solutions-and-concentration.md); the free energy that pumps spend is released by [redox](../02-chemistry-of-life/redox-and-electron-transfer.md); the acid–base chemistry of [buffers](../02-chemistry-of-life/acids-bases-and-buffers.md) is equilibrium thermodynamics with protons; and the properties of collagen and elastin that mechanics relies on come from [molecular shape](../02-chemistry-of-life/carbon-chemistry-and-molecular-recognition.md). The two areas are read together.
