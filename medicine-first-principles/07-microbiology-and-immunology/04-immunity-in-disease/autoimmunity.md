## Essence

Autoimmune disease is an adaptive immune response against the body's own antigens that causes damage. It affects perhaps 5 to 8% of people in high-income countries (2020s), is commoner in women, and takes some eighty named forms that differ in which self-antigen is attacked and which hypersensitivity mechanism does the damage. Because the antigen can never be cleared, the response never ends, and chronicity is the defining property. The disease arises when several layers of tolerance fail together: a permissive Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) type, variants in checkpoint genes, and an environmental trigger such as infection, smoking or tissue injury. Treatment has moved from blunting the whole immune system to blocking the cytokine or cell driving each disease.

## Core tenets

1. **Autoimmunity is tolerance failing at several checkpoints at once.** Every healthy repertoire contains self-reactive clones and every healthy serum contains some autoantibodies; disease needs those clones to be activated, helped, expanded and not suppressed ([tolerance and self-recognition](../03-adaptive-immunity/tolerance-and-self-recognition.md)). Single-gene failures prove each checkpoint matters (AIRE: thymic deletion; FOXP3: regulatory T cells; Fas: deletion of activated cells; C1q: clearance of dead cells) and are all rare and severe. Common autoimmune disease is the polygenic, multifactorial version: many small failures.

2. **Genes load the gun; HLA is the heaviest.** Monozygotic twin concordance is roughly 15 to 50% depending on the disease (around 30 to 50% for type 1 diabetes, about 15% for rheumatoid arthritis), so inheritance is substantial but not sufficient ([complex traits](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/complex-traits-and-heritability.md)). The strongest loci are in the HLA region: a given groove presents a given self or modified peptide (HLA-DQ2/DQ8 and deamidated gluten in coeliac disease, HLA-DR4 and citrullinated peptides in rheumatoid arthritis, HLA-B27 in spondyloarthritis). Most other loci (PTPN22, CTLA4, IL23R) set immune signalling thresholds and are shared across diseases, which is why autoimmune diseases cluster in families and individuals.

3. **Environment pulls the trigger, often through infection or injury.** A microbe can break tolerance by molecular mimicry (streptococcal M protein resembling cardiac myosin in rheumatic fever; *Campylobacter* lipo-oligosaccharide resembling nerve gangliosides in Guillain-Barré syndrome), by supplying the co-stimulation that a tissue presenting self-antigen otherwise lacks, or by damaging tissue and releasing hidden antigens. Smoking citrullinates lung proteins and precedes rheumatoid arthritis by years; ultraviolet light kills skin cells and flares lupus; Epstein-Barr virus infection precedes multiple sclerosis in almost every case. Female sex is the strongest demographic risk, through X-linked immune genes and oestrogen's effects on B cells.

4. **The damage uses the hypersensitivity mechanisms, and the antigen's distribution sets the disease.** Antibody against a cell-surface molecule (type II) gives organ-specific disease: Graves' disease, myasthenia gravis, autoimmune haemolysis, pernicious anaemia. Immune complexes (type III) give systemic disease wherever they deposit: lupus nephritis, vasculitis. T cells (type IV) destroy tissue directly: beta cells in type 1 diabetes, thyroid in Hashimoto's disease, myelin in multiple sclerosis, synovium in rheumatoid arthritis. Organ-specific diseases attack an antigen found in one tissue; systemic diseases attack ubiquitous antigens such as nucleic acids and nuclear proteins ([hypersensitivity](hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md)).

5. **Lupus shows how a defect in waste disposal becomes autoimmunity.** Cells die constantly by apoptosis and are normally cleared before they leak. When clearance is slow (C1q deficiency, defective phagocyte receptors), nuclear material persists, is bound by autoantibody and taken up by plasmacytoid dendritic cells, where DNA and RNA engage Toll-like receptors 7 and 9 and drive interferon-alpha; interferon activates more B cells, which make more antibody, which delivers more nucleic acid. The loop is self-sustaining, and the interferon signature is the molecular fingerprint of lupus ([apoptosis](../../03-cell-biology/02-the-cell-life-cycle/cell-death-apoptosis-necrosis-and-autophagy.md), [pattern recognition](../02-innate-immunity/barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md)).

6. **Autoantibodies are both weapons and signposts, and they appear years before symptoms.** Some cause disease directly (anti-thyrotropin receptor, anti-acetylcholine receptor, anti-desmoglein in pemphigus); some are markers of a process they do not drive (anti-nuclear antibodies, present at low titre in 5 to 15% of healthy people, rising with age). Anti-citrullinated protein antibodies and anti-nuclear antibodies are detectable five to ten years before rheumatoid arthritis and lupus declare themselves, revealing a long preclinical phase during which tolerance is already lost ([serology](../../11-clinical-method-and-diagnostics/02-laboratory-medicine/microbiological-and-serological-diagnosis.md)).

7. **Chronicity, relapse and feed-forward loops distinguish autoimmunity from infection.** Because self-antigen is never cleared, inflammation persists, epitopes spread as damaged tissue exposes new antigens, and tissue ends in fibrosis or loss (a destroyed thyroid, a burnt-out joint, a kidney in failure). Flares follow infections that transiently raise innate activation. Treatment therefore aims at the driving loop: anti-tumour necrosis factor in rheumatoid arthritis, B-cell depletion in several antibody-mediated diseases, interferon or interleukin-6 blockade, co-stimulation blockade, Janus kinase inhibitors; and replacement of what has been destroyed (insulin, thyroxine). Each targeted drug reveals a cost that mirrors the pathway's job (tuberculosis reactivation, hypogammaglobulinaemia) ([immunomodulatory drugs](../../09-pharmacology-and-therapeutics/03-drug-classes-by-mechanism/anti-inflammatory-and-immunomodulatory-drugs.md)). The reverse experiment, blocking checkpoints to treat cancer, produces thyroiditis, colitis and diabetes in weeks, confirming that autoimmunity is the price of releasing the brakes.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Autoantigen | Self-molecule targeted by an immune response |
| Autoantibody | Antibody against a self-antigen; pathogenic or merely a marker |
| Organ-specific versus systemic | Attack on an antigen confined to one tissue, or one present everywhere |
| HLA association | Raised disease risk with particular HLA alleles, through peptide presentation |
| Molecular mimicry | Cross-reaction between a microbial and a self-antigen |
| Epitope spreading | Extension of the response to new self-antigens as tissue is damaged |
| Interferon signature | Expression pattern of interferon-stimulated genes typical of lupus |
| Citrullination | Post-translational change of arginine to citrulline that creates neoantigens in rheumatoid arthritis |
| Preclinical autoimmunity | Autoantibodies present years before symptoms |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Autoimmunity means an overactive immune system."** It is a misdirected one; patients are not better protected against infection, and many are immunodeficient by disease or treatment.
- **"A positive autoantibody test makes the diagnosis."** Anti-nuclear antibodies are found in up to 15% of healthy people; a test result is interpreted against pre-test probability and the clinical picture.
- **"Autoimmune disease is caused by a single trigger."** It needs genetic susceptibility, a permissive HLA groove and usually more than one environmental event; the same infection causes disease in a minority.
