## Essence

Hypersensitivity is an adaptive immune response that damages the host out of proportion to the threat. Nothing in it is new: the mechanisms are the same antibodies, complement, mast cells and T cells that protect against worms, bacteria and viruses, aimed at a harmless antigen (pollen, peanut, penicillin, nickel) or at self. Gell and Coombs (1963) sorted the damage into four types by effector, and the classification still works because the effector sets the tempo, the tissue pattern and the treatment. Allergy, the type I form, is the commonest immune disease in wealthy countries: the anti-helminth programme firing at the wrong target.

## Core tenets

1. **Four mechanisms, four time courses.**

   | Type | Effector | Onset | Examples |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | I (immediate) | Immunoglobulin E (IgE) on mast cells | Minutes | Anaphylaxis, hay fever, urticaria, food allergy, allergic asthma |
   | II (antibody to cells) | Immunoglobulin G (IgG) or M against cell-surface or matrix antigen | Hours to days | Haemolytic transfusion reaction, haemolytic disease of the newborn, Graves' disease, myasthenia gravis, Goodpasture's disease |
   | III (immune complex) | Soluble antigen-antibody complexes depositing in vessels | Hours to days | Serum sickness, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, lupus nephritis |
   | IV (delayed, cellular) | T cells, with macrophages or cytotoxic cells | 2 to 3 days | Contact dermatitis, tuberculin test, drug rashes, type 1 diabetes |

   The first three are transferable by serum; the fourth only by cells.

2. **Type I: IgE arms mast cells, and cross-linking fires them.** Sensitisation comes first: an allergen drives a Th2 response, interleukin-4 switches B cells to IgE, and IgE binds high-affinity receptors on mast cells, where it persists for weeks. On re-exposure, allergen bridging two IgE molecules degranulates the cell within minutes: histamine and tryptase cause vasodilation, leak, itch, bronchoconstriction and mucus; leukotrienes and prostaglandins prolong them; cytokines bring eosinophils for a late phase at 4 to 8 hours. Anaphylaxis is this response in every tissue at once, and adrenaline reverses it by constricting vessels, dilating airways and raising cardiac output ([type 2 immunity](../01-microbes-and-infection/fungi-protozoa-and-helminths.md)).

3. **Atopy is a tendency, set by genes and the route of first exposure.** Twenty to thirty percent of people in high-income countries have IgE to environmental allergens (2010s to 2020s), far more than a century ago or in rural low-income settings; the hygiene hypothesis attributes the rise to loss of early microbial exposures that calibrate regulation. Where antigen is first met matters: filaggrin mutations weaken the skin barrier, and food proteins met through inflamed skin tend to sensitise, whereas the same proteins eaten early tend to tolerise (early peanut introduction cut peanut allergy by about 80% in a 2015 trial). The atopic march (eczema, then food allergy, asthma and rhinitis) follows from this.

4. **Type II: antibody against a cell either destroys it or changes its function.** IgG on a red cell fixes complement and is eaten by splenic macrophages (autoimmune haemolysis, transfusion reactions, anti-D haemolytic disease of the newborn). Antibody against the glomerular basement membrane recruits neutrophils to the kidney. Antibody against a receptor can block it (myasthenia gravis, acetylcholine receptor) or stimulate it (Graves' disease, thyrotropin receptor), causing disease with no inflammation at all. Drugs such as penicillin can bind to red cells and make them antibody targets.

5. **Type III: complexes deposit where blood is filtered and leak.** Soluble antigen with antibody at roughly equivalent amounts forms complexes too small to be cleared and too large to stay dissolved; they lodge in glomeruli, small vessels, joints and skin, fix complement and summon neutrophils. Serum sickness (fever, rash, arthritis, nephritis) appears 7 to 10 days after a foreign protein such as antivenom or antithymocyte globulin, as antibody first appears while antigen persists. Lupus is the chronic version, with self nucleic acid as the antigen ([autoimmunity](autoimmunity.md)).

6. **Type IV: T cells take days and need no antibody.** Th1 cells recognising antigen on tissue cells recruit and activate macrophages (tuberculin skin test, granulomas); contact dermatitis follows haptens such as nickel or urushiol binding skin proteins and being presented as neoantigens. Cytotoxic T cells cause the severe drug eruptions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis) that are tightly linked to particular Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) alleles, because the drug alters which self-peptides a given groove presents ([antigen presentation](../03-adaptive-immunity/antigen-presentation-and-mhc.md)). The 48- to 72-hour delay is the time for memory T cells to be recruited and expand.

7. **Diagnosis and treatment follow the mechanism.** Skin-prick testing and specific IgE read type I in 15 minutes; patch testing reads type IV at 48 to 72 hours; complement levels and tissue biopsy reveal type III. Antihistamines and mast-cell stabilisers address type I mediators, adrenaline the systemic form, anti-IgE antibodies the arming step, and allergen immunotherapy (graded exposure that shifts the response toward IgG and regulatory T cells) the sensitisation itself. Avoidance remains the only certain measure ([allergy in practice](../../12-organ-system-specialties/09-rheumatology-and-clinical-immunology/allergy-and-immunodeficiency-in-practice.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypersensitivity | Adaptive response causing tissue damage disproportionate to the threat |
| Allergen | Harmless environmental antigen that elicits IgE |
| Mast cell | Tissue cell loaded with histamine granules and IgE receptors |
| Anaphylaxis | Systemic type I reaction with hypotension and airway compromise |
| Atopy | Inherited tendency to make IgE against common environmental antigens |
| Immune complex | Soluble antigen-antibody aggregate that deposits in vessels |
| Hapten | Small molecule that becomes antigenic by binding a host protein |
| Delayed-type hypersensitivity | T-cell-mediated reaction peaking at 48 to 72 hours |
| Allergen immunotherapy | Graded antigen exposure to induce tolerance |

## Common misconceptions

- **"An allergy is a weak immune system."** It is a vigorous, well-targeted response to the wrong thing; allergic people are not immunodeficient.
- **"A positive allergy test means allergy."** Specific IgE shows sensitisation; many sensitised people eat the food or breathe the pollen without symptoms, so tests are interpreted against history.
