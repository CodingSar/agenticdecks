## Essence

Immunodeficiency is the absence or failure of a component of immunity, and it is the experiment that reveals what each component is for. Lose antibody and encapsulated bacteria take over; lose T cells and viruses, fungi and intracellular organisms do; lose neutrophils and the patient's own skin and gut flora invade; lose terminal complement and only the meningococcus benefits. The pattern of infection therefore points to the missing part. Inherited (primary) immunodeficiencies are rare and instructive; acquired (secondary) ones, from Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), malnutrition, cancer and the drugs medicine itself uses, are common. Because the same cells and genes that fight infection also restrain autoimmunity and surveil tumours, immunodeficiency often brings autoimmune disease and virus-driven cancer with it.

## Core tenets

1. **The organisms name the defect.**

   | Missing component | Typical infections | Why |
   |---|---|---|
   | Antibody (B cells) | *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Giardia*, enteroviruses; sinopulmonary infection | Encapsulated bacteria need opsonising antibody; IgA guards mucosa |
   | T cells | Viruses (cytomegalovirus, herpes), *Candida*, *Pneumocystis*, mycobacteria, *Cryptococcus*; plus poor antibody | Intracellular pathogens need cytotoxic and helper cells; B cells need help |
   | Neutrophils (number or function) | *Staphylococcus aureus*, Gram-negative gut bacteria, *Aspergillus*, *Candida*; abscesses | Extracellular bacteria and fungi are killed by phagocytosis |
   | Early complement (C3) | Pyogenic bacteria | Opsonisation lost |
   | Terminal complement (C5-C9) | *Neisseria* only | Only thin-walled Gram-negatives need lysis |
   | Interferon-gamma / interleukin-12 axis | Mycobacteria, *Salmonella* | Macrophage activation lost |
   | Spleen | Encapsulated bacteria, overwhelming sepsis | Clearance of opsonised bacteria from blood lost |

   The clinician reasons backwards: recurrent pneumococcal pneumonia prompts antibody tests, *Pneumocystis* an HIV test.

2. **Primary immunodeficiencies are inborn errors, and age of onset is a clue.** Nearly 500 single-gene defects were catalogued by 2022, together affecting perhaps 1 in 1,000 to 2,000 people (far more if asymptomatic IgA deficiency is counted). Severe combined immunodeficiency (no T cells; defects in the common gamma chain, adenosine deaminase or the RAG recombinases) presents within months and is fatal without stem-cell transplantation or gene therapy. X-linked agammaglobulinaemia (no B cells, from a defective BTK kinase) presents after about six months, when maternal Immunoglobulin G (IgG) has decayed. Common variable immunodeficiency, the commonest symptomatic form, presents in adolescence or adulthood. Most follow X-linked or autosomal recessive [inheritance](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/01-inheritance/mendelian-inheritance-and-pedigrees.md), so a family history of early infant deaths is a warning sign; newborn screening by counting T-cell receptor excision circles now detects severe combined immunodeficiency before infection.

3. **Secondary immunodeficiency is far commoner, and much of it is iatrogenic.** HIV infects and depletes CD4 T cells; about 39 million people were living with it in 2023 (UNAIDS), and the opportunistic infections of the acquired immunodeficiency syndrome appear as the CD4 count falls below roughly 200 cells per microlitre ([HIV](../../12-organ-system-specialties/08-infectious-diseases/hiv-and-chronic-viral-infections.md)). Protein-energy malnutrition, with zinc and vitamin A deficiency, is the leading cause worldwide, impairing T cells and barriers. Corticosteroids, cytotoxic chemotherapy (neutropenia), transplant immunosuppressants, B-cell-depleting and cytokine-blocking antibodies, and splenectomy each remove a defined component. Malignancy, diabetes, organ failure and old age impair several at once.

4. **Opportunists are the organisms that healthy immunity handles invisibly.** *Pneumocystis*, *Candida*, cytomegalovirus, *Aspergillus* and non-tuberculous mycobacteria are met by everyone and controlled without symptoms; their appearance as disease is diagnostic of a hole in defence. Latent viruses (cytomegalovirus, Epstein-Barr virus, varicella zoster, JC virus, hepatitis B) are held in check by continuous T-cell surveillance, not eliminated, so T-cell suppression reactivates them: shingles in the elderly, cytomegalovirus after transplantation, fatal brain demyelination from JC virus with some biologics.

5. **Neutropenia is a numbers game with a threshold.** Risk of serious infection rises steeply once the neutrophil count falls below 0.5 x 10^9 per litre and with the duration spent there; the invaders are the patient's own gut and skin flora, and because neutrophils produce the signs of infection, a febrile neutropenic patient may have no localising features at all. Fever in neutropenia is treated as bacteraemia until proven otherwise.

6. **Immunodeficiency, autoimmunity and cancer travel together.** Common variable immunodeficiency brings autoimmune cytopenias and granulomas as often as infection; the regulatory defects FOXP3, CTLA4 and AIRE deficiency cause autoimmunity as their main feature; T-cell deficiency allows Epstein-Barr-virus-driven lymphoma, Kaposi sarcoma from its herpesvirus, and cervical and anal cancer from papillomavirus. One system does three jobs, defence, restraint and surveillance, with the same parts ([autoimmunity](autoimmunity.md), [tumour immunology](transplant-and-tumour-immunology.md)).

7. **Treatment replaces, prevents, reconstitutes or removes the cause.** Pooled immunoglobulin replaces antibody; prophylaxis covers the predictable organisms; vaccines are given, but live vaccines are contraindicated where T cells are deficient, because the attenuated organism becomes the disease. Haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation cures severe combined immunodeficiency and gene therapy now cures some forms (adenosine deaminase deficiency, approved 2016). For secondary immunodeficiency the cure is removing the cause: antiretroviral therapy restores CD4 counts and has turned HIV into a chronic condition.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary immunodeficiency | Inborn, usually monogenic, defect in an immune component |
| Secondary immunodeficiency | Acquired loss of immune function from disease, malnutrition or treatment |
| Severe combined immunodeficiency | Absence of functional T cells, with or without B and NK cells |
| Opportunistic infection | Disease from an organism controlled asymptomatically by normal immunity |
| Neutropenia | Neutrophil count below 1.5 x 10^9/L; severe below 0.5 |
| Reactivation | Return of a latent virus when T-cell surveillance falls |
| Live vaccine contraindication | Attenuated organisms are unsafe when T-cell immunity is deficient |
| CD4 count | Helper T-cell number; the staging measure in HIV |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Frequent colds mean immunodeficiency."** Children have six to ten viral upper respiratory infections a year normally; the warning signs are infections that are serious, persistent, unusual in organism or site, or recurrent in the same pattern.
- **"Immunodeficient patients catch exotic organisms."** They are mostly infected by ordinary organisms, including their own flora and the latent viruses everyone carries; what is unusual is the severity and the site.
