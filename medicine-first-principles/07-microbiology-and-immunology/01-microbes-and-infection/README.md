## What this area is about

What lives on us, in us and against us, and how a few of those organisms cause disease. The immune system can only be understood as an answer to the microbes it evolved alongside, so this area comes first in the layer: the structure of a bacterial wall explains why there is a receptor for lipopolysaccharide, the intracellular life of a virus explains why there are cytotoxic T cells, the size of a worm explains why there is Immunoglobulin E (IgE).

The unifying idea is that **pathogenicity is a relationship, not a property of a species**. Almost all microbes are harmless or useful; disease is what happens when a particular organism, in a particular dose, reaches a particular site in a particular host. The three organism leaves describe what the agents are made of and how they differ from us (which decides what drugs can touch them); the microbiome leaf describes the normal state; pathogenesis describes what tips the relationship into disease; and transmission describes how the agent reaches the next host, which is the point at which infection becomes a population problem.

```
   organism biology                 relationship with the host
   ----------------                 --------------------------
   bacteria  ---+
   viruses   ---+--> microbiome (health) --> pathogenesis (disease) --> transmission (next host)

                          |                         v
```

Read the three organism leaves first, in order, because each is defined partly by contrast with the previous one: bacteria have their own metabolism and wall, viruses have neither, eukaryotic parasites have ours. Then the microbiome, which establishes the normal state against which infection is defined. Pathogenesis then asks what distinguishes the organisms that cause disease, and transmission closes the loop by asking how they persist in a population.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Innate immunity](../02-innate-immunity/README.md):** every pattern the innate system recognises (lipopolysaccharide, flagellin, double-stranded Ribonucleic Acid (RNA), beta-glucan) is a structure described here, and endotoxin is the molecule that most directly links microbial structure to septic shock.
- **[Adaptive immunity](../03-adaptive-immunity/README.md):** intracellular pathogens are why T cells exist, antigenic variation is why memory sometimes fails, toxoids and attenuated organisms are the raw material of vaccines, and the microbiome is the largest antigen load that tolerance must handle.
- **[Immunity in disease](../04-immunity-in-disease/README.md):** the opportunistic organisms of immunodeficiency are catalogued here, type 2 immunity against helminths is the mechanism of allergy, and molecular mimicry by microbes is a trigger of autoimmunity.
