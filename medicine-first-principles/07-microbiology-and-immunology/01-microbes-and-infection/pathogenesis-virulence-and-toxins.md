## Essence

Pathogenesis is the sequence by which a microbe causes disease: it must reach a tissue, attach, obtain nutrients, avoid being killed, multiply, damage the host and leave to infect someone else. Each step is carried out by specific molecules (virulence factors), and each is a point of intervention. Disease is never a property of the microbe alone; it is the product of the microbe's weapons, the dose and the host's response, and much of the damage in infection is inflicted by the host's own defences. Toxins are the clearest weapons: secreted proteins that act on host cells with enzymatic precision, and the shed lipopolysaccharide of Gram-negative bacteria that the immune system over-reacts to.

## Core tenets

1. **Colonisation, infection and disease are different states.** A microbe may live on a surface (colonisation), multiply and provoke a response (infection), or cause symptoms (disease). Koch's postulates (1884) asked that the organism be found in every case, isolated, reproduce the disease and be re-isolated; molecular Koch's postulates (Falkow, 1988) ask the same of a gene: disease should fall when it is removed and return when it is restored.

2. **Disease is dose times virulence divided by host resistance.** The infectious dose varies by a million-fold: ~10 to 100 *Shigella* cells cause dysentery, while *Vibrio cholerae* needs ~10^6 or more because stomach acid kills most of it (fewer in people with low acid). The same dose produces nothing in one host and death in another, depending on barriers, genetics, immune state, age and microbiome. Casadevall and Pirofski's damage-response framework states this directly: disease is host damage, from whatever source.

3. **Pathogenesis is a sequence of molecular steps.** *Attachment* uses adhesins (pili, surface proteins) binding host receptors. *Invasion* uses invasins or, in many Gram-negatives, a type III secretion system, a molecular syringe that injects effector proteins to rearrange the host cytoskeleton and let the bacterium in. *Nutrient acquisition* is a battle for iron: the host keeps free iron near zero (nutritional immunity), so pathogens secrete siderophores that strip it from transferrin. *Immune evasion* uses capsules that block complement and phagocytosis, proteases that cleave Immunoglobulin A (IgA), intracellular residence and antigenic variation. *Damage* comes from toxins, direct invasion, or the host response. *Transmission* closes the loop ([transmission and the chain of infection](transmission-and-the-chain-of-infection.md)).

4. **Exotoxins are secreted enzymes with exact targets.** Most are A-B toxins: a B subunit binds a host receptor and delivers an enzymatic A subunit. Cholera toxin adds an ADP-ribose to the G protein Gs, locking adenylyl cyclase on; the resulting cyclic AMP opens chloride channels and secretes litres of fluid a day. Diphtheria toxin ADP-ribosylates elongation factor 2 and stops protein synthesis. Tetanus and botulinum toxins are proteases that cleave the SNARE proteins of vesicle fusion, blocking neurotransmitter release at inhibitory (tetanus: spasm) or motor (botulinum: paralysis) synapses. Superantigens (staphylococcal toxic shock syndrome toxin) cross-link Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) class II to T-cell receptors outside the peptide groove, activating up to a fifth of all T cells at once and causing shock by cytokine release. Because exotoxins are proteins, they can be denatured into toxoids that induce neutralising antibody (tetanus, diphtheria vaccines), and disease can occur without the organism (food-borne botulism).

5. **Endotoxin is structural, not secreted, and the host does the damage.** Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is the outer-membrane lipid of Gram-negative bacteria, released when they divide or die. Its lipid A is sensed by Toll-Like Receptor 4 (TLR4) at picomolar concentrations and triggers tumour necrosis factor and interleukin-1 release. In small amounts this gives fever; in large amounts, vasodilation, capillary leak, disseminated clotting and septic shock. Endotoxin cannot be made into a toxoid, and the therapeutic target is the host response, not the molecule ([hemorrhage, shock and sepsis](../../08-pathology/02-hemodynamic-disorders/hemorrhage-shock-and-sepsis.md)).

6. **Much of the damage in infection is immunopathology.** Tuberculosis destroys lung by the granulomas and cavities the immune system builds to contain it; hepatitis B virus does not kill hepatocytes, cytotoxic T cells do; the lethal phase of severe influenza, dengue and COVID-19 is a host inflammatory response. So treatment of severe infection sometimes suppresses immunity (corticosteroids in bacterial meningitis and severe COVID-19) while killing the organism.

7. **Virulence genes travel in packages and are shaped by selection.** Toxins and secretion systems are often encoded on pathogenicity islands, plasmids or bacteriophage: cholera toxin, diphtheria toxin, Shiga toxin and botulinum toxin are all phage genes, so a harmless strain can become a pathogen by infection with a virus. Virulence is not fixed by natural law at "as harmful as possible": a pathogen that needs its host mobile (a cold) is selected to be mild; one spread by vectors, water or from the dying (malaria, cholera, smallpox) can afford to be severe ([host-pathogen coevolution](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/host-pathogen-coevolution-and-resistance.md)).

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Virulence | The degree of damage a pathogen causes; measured by infectious or lethal dose |
| Virulence factor | A molecule that contributes to a step of pathogenesis |
| Infectious dose | The number of organisms needed to establish infection (ID50: in half of hosts) |
| Type III secretion system | Needle-like apparatus injecting bacterial effectors into host cells |
| Siderophore | Secreted iron-chelating molecule that steals iron from host proteins |
| Capsule | Polysaccharide coat that blocks complement deposition and phagocytosis |
| Exotoxin | Secreted protein toxin with a specific enzymatic action |
| Superantigen | Toxin that activates large numbers of T cells irrespective of antigen |
| Endotoxin | Lipopolysaccharide of the Gram-negative outer membrane; acts via TLR4 |
| Toxoid | Chemically inactivated toxin that still induces neutralising antibody |

## Common misconceptions

- **"A pathogen is a species."** Strains of *Escherichia coli* range from harmless commensal to the Shiga-toxin producer that causes haemolytic uraemic syndrome, differing by a few mobile genes.
- **"The sicker the patient, the more organisms there are."** Toxic shock and tetanus can follow trivial local infections; severity tracks toxin and host response, not bacterial load.
- **"Pathogens evolve to be harmless."** Selection acts on transmission, not on kindness; where transmission does not need a healthy host, virulence can persist or rise.
