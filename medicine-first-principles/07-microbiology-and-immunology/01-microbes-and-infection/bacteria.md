## Essence

Bacteria are single-celled prokaryotes: a chromosome without a nucleus, a cytoplasm without organelles, a ribosome built differently from ours, and in most species a rigid cell wall that no human cell possesses. Two consequences of this design dominate medicine. First, the structures that differ from human cells (the wall, the 70S ribosome, bacterial enzymes of folate and nucleic acid synthesis) are exactly where antibiotics act selectively. Second, their enormous numbers, short generation times and habit of swapping genes mean that they evolve on a timescale of weeks, which is why resistance follows every new antibiotic.

## Core tenets

1. **A bacterium is a self-sufficient cell built on a different plan from ours.** A single circular chromosome (0.5 to 10 million base pairs; *Escherichia coli* ~4.6 million, ~4,300 genes) lies free in the cytoplasm, so transcription and translation are coupled and a gene can be expressed within minutes of a signal. Energy is made across the plasma membrane itself. Ribosomes are 70S rather than our 80S, which lets aminoglycosides, macrolides and tetracyclines poison bacterial protein synthesis while sparing ours.

2. **The cell wall is the defining structure and the first antibiotic target.** Peptidoglycan, a mesh of sugar chains cross-linked by short peptides, resists the osmotic pressure of a cytoplasm far more concentrated than its surroundings. Cut the cross-links (beta-lactams block the transpeptidases; glycopeptides such as vancomycin bind the precursor) and a growing cell bursts. Human cells have no peptidoglycan, which is why penicillin kills bacteria at doses that leave the patient untouched.

3. **The Gram stain is a structural classification that predicts behaviour.** Gram-positive bacteria have a thick wall and retain the crystal violet dye; Gram-negative bacteria have a thin wall wrapped in an outer membrane whose outer leaflet is Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), and the dye washes out. The outer membrane excludes many drugs, and LPS is endotoxin, the molecule the innate immune system reacts to most violently ([pathogenesis, virulence and toxins](pathogenesis-virulence-and-toxins.md)). Mycobacteria wrap their wall in waxy mycolic acids that resist staining, drugs and digestion inside macrophages; *Mycoplasma* has no wall at all and is untouched by beta-lactams.

4. **Growth is exponential and stops only when something runs out.** Under good conditions *E. coli* doubles every ~20 minutes; *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* takes ~15 to 20 hours, which is why tuberculosis is slow and its treatment long. A single cell can become 10^9 overnight. Oxygen requirement decides where an organism can live: anaerobes dominate the colon and abscesses.

5. **Bacteria live in communities, not as free cells.** Attached to a surface (a tooth, a heart valve, a catheter, the lung of a patient with cystic fibrosis) they build a biofilm: cells embedded in a self-made polymer matrix, coordinated by quorum-sensing signals. Cells deep in a biofilm grow slowly and are shielded from antibodies, phagocytes and drugs, tolerating antibiotic concentrations 10 to 1,000 times those that kill the same cells in suspension. This is why device infections usually require removing the device.

6. **Bacterial genomes are fluid, and that is why resistance is inevitable.** Beyond mutation (roughly one error per 10^9 to 10^10 base pairs per generation, but multiplied by 10^9 cells it happens daily), bacteria acquire whole genes horizontally: conjugation transfers plasmids between cells of different species, transformation takes up naked DNA, and transduction carries genes in bacteriophage. Antibiotic exposure does not create resistance; it selects for it ([host-pathogen coevolution and resistance](../../04-genetics-evolution-and-development/02-evolution/host-pathogen-coevolution-and-resistance.md)).

7. **Some bacteria hide: spores outside the body, intracellular life inside it.** *Clostridium* and *Bacillus* form endospores that survive boiling, drying and disinfectants for years, so tetanus persists in soil and *Clostridioides difficile* on hospital surfaces. Others survive inside cells: *M. tuberculosis*, *Salmonella* and *Listeria* resist or escape the phagolysosome; *Chlamydia* and *Rickettsia* cannot grow outside a cell at all. Antibodies cannot reach them, so clearance depends on [T cells](../03-adaptive-immunity/t-cells.md) and activated macrophages.

8. **Pathogen is a relationship, not a species.** Of the ~10^13 bacteria living on and in a human, almost all are harmless or useful ([the human microbiome](the-human-microbiome.md)). The same *E. coli* that is normal in the colon causes cystitis in the bladder and peritonitis in the abdominal cavity; *Staphylococcus epidermidis* is skin flora until it coats a prosthetic joint. Disease depends on where the bacterium is, how many there are and how the host responds.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Prokaryote | Cell without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles |
| Peptidoglycan | Cross-linked sugar-peptide polymer of the bacterial wall; target of beta-lactams and glycopeptides |
| Gram stain | Dye-retention test that separates thick-walled (positive) from outer-membrane (negative) bacteria |
| Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) | Outer-membrane lipid of Gram-negatives; the endotoxin sensed by the innate immune system |
| Binary fission | Division of one cell into two identical daughters; the basis of exponential growth |
| Biofilm | Surface-attached community in a polymer matrix with high drug tolerance |
| Plasmid | Self-replicating extrachromosomal DNA that often carries resistance or virulence genes |
| Horizontal gene transfer | Conjugation, transformation and transduction: moving genes between cells and species |
| Endospore | Dormant, highly resistant survival form of some Gram-positive genera |
| Obligate intracellular bacterium | One that can replicate only inside a host cell (*Chlamydia*, *Rickettsia*) |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Bacteria are germs, so fewer is better."** The body carries as many bacterial cells as human ones, and removing them with antibiotics opens niches for pathogens such as *C. difficile*.
- **"The Gram stain identifies the organism."** It sorts bacteria into structural classes that predict antibiotic access and the inflammatory response; species identification needs culture, mass spectrometry or sequencing.
- **"Antibiotic resistance is caused by patients not finishing their course."** Resistance arises by mutation and gene transfer and is selected by any exposure; total antibiotic consumption, in people and in agriculture, drives it more than the completeness of individual courses.
