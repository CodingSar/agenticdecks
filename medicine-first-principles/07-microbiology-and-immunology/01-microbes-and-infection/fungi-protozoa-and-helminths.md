# Fungi, protozoa and helminths

## Essence

Fungi, protozoa and helminths are eukaryotes. Their cells are built like ours, so the chemical differences that make antibiotics selective mostly do not exist, and drugs against them are fewer and more toxic. Fungi are opportunists that mostly wait for immunity to fail. Protozoa are single cells with elaborate life cycles, often passing through insects and hiding inside human cells or changing their coats to outlast antibody. Helminths are visible worms that do not multiply inside the host and provoke a distinctive type 2 immune response, built for expelling things too large to swallow, that is the same response misfired in allergy.

## Core tenets

1. **Eukaryotic pathogens share our machinery, so selective toxicity is hard.** Antifungals exploit the one clear difference: fungal membranes use ergosterol where ours use cholesterol (azoles block its synthesis, amphotericin binds it) and fungal walls contain beta-glucan and chitin (echinocandins block glucan synthesis). Antiprotozoal and anthelminthic drugs rely on subtler differences (the malaria parasite's haem detoxification, the worm's neuromuscular receptors), and many are old, toxic or failing to resistance.

2. **Fungi are everywhere and mostly harmless; disease marks a failure of the host.** Yeasts (*Candida*, *Cryptococcus*) grow as single cells; moulds (*Aspergillus*) as branching hyphae. *Candida* is a gut and skin commensal that invades when barriers break or neutrophils fall; *Aspergillus* spores are inhaled daily by everyone and cause invasive disease only in prolonged neutropenia or heavy immunosuppression; *Cryptococcus* and *Pneumocystis* are diseases of lost T-cell immunity, so their appearance in a previously healthy adult almost always means Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection ([immunodeficiency](../04-immunity-in-disease/immunodeficiency.md)). Fungal disease is estimated to kill on the order of 1.5 to 3.8 million people a year worldwide (2020s estimates), largely unrecognised.

3. **Protozoa are single cells with life cycles, and the cycle is the target.** Each has stages adapted to different hosts and tissues: *Plasmodium* passes from mosquito salivary gland to human liver cell to red cell to mosquito gut; *Toxoplasma* alternates between cat, prey and cyst; *Trypanosoma* and *Leishmania* move between insect and human blood or macrophage; *Giardia* and *Entamoeba* alternate between resistant cysts in water and active forms in the gut. A stage inside a red cell or a macrophage is invisible to antibody, and a cyst in water is indifferent to drugs, so control works by breaking the cycle (bed nets, clean water, cooked meat) as much as by treating the patient.

4. **Protozoa defeat adaptive immunity by variation and hiding.** African trypanosomes wear a coat of a single variant surface glycoprotein and switch to one of hundreds of alternatives each time antibody catches up, producing waves of parasitaemia. *Plasmodium falciparum* cycles through ~60 variant genes for the protein it displays on the red-cell surface, and that protein makes infected cells stick to endothelium, the mechanism of cerebral malaria. Immunity to malaria is therefore partial, slow to acquire and quickly lost, which is why it kills mainly young children: roughly 250 million cases and 600,000 deaths a year (World Health Organization estimates, 2022), most in African children under five.

5. **Helminths are macroscopic, long-lived and do not usually multiply in the host.** Nematodes (roundworms), cestodes (tapeworms) and trematodes (flukes) are animals with organs. Because eggs or larvae must leave the body and return, the worm burden is the sum of exposures, so disease is chronic and dose-dependent (anaemia from hookworm blood loss, liver fibrosis from schistosome eggs, lymphatic obstruction from filariae). Soil-transmitted helminths infect on the order of 1.5 billion people (World Health Organization, 2020s), almost all where sanitation is poor.

6. **The immune system has a dedicated response for things too big to eat.** Helminth antigens drive a type 2 response: interleukin-4, -5 and -13 from helper T cells, Immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibody, eosinophils, mast cells and goblet-cell hyperplasia. The logic is expulsion (mucus, smooth-muscle contraction, toxic granule proteins poured onto a surface), not phagocytosis. Eosinophilia and raised IgE are therefore the laboratory signature of a worm, and the same machinery pointed at pollen or peanut is [allergy](../04-immunity-in-disease/hypersensitivity-and-allergy.md). Helminths actively dampen host immunity to survive for years, and the loss of worms has been proposed as one reason allergic disease has risen in wealthy countries; the hypothesis is plausible and unproven.

7. **Vector and geography define these diseases as much as biology does.** Malaria, leishmaniasis, trypanosomiasis, filariasis and schistosomiasis each need an arthropod or snail with its own climate, so their maps are the maps of their vectors ([transmission and the chain of infection](transmission-and-the-chain-of-infection.md)). Travel history is the most useful question in diagnosing them in a temperate hospital.

## Key concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ergosterol | Fungal membrane sterol replacing cholesterol; target of azoles and amphotericin |
| Opportunistic infection | Disease by an organism that the intact immune system handles without symptoms |
| Life cycle | The ordered series of forms a parasite passes through in different hosts and tissues |
| Antigenic variation | Programmed switching of surface antigens to outrun antibody |
| Vector | An arthropod or other animal that carries a pathogen between hosts |
| Type 2 immunity | The interleukin-4/5/13, IgE, eosinophil and mast-cell response to helminths and allergens |
| Eosinophilia | Raised blood eosinophils; the usual marker of tissue-invading helminths or allergy |
| Worm burden | The number of adult worms, which sets the severity of helminth disease |

## Common misconceptions

- **"Fungal infections are superficial nuisances."** Invasive candidiasis, aspergillosis and cryptococcal meningitis have mortality of tens of percent even when treated; they are among the commonest killers of patients with leukaemia or advanced HIV.
- **"Parasites are a tropical problem."** *Toxoplasma* infects a third of humanity, *Giardia* is worldwide, and travel brings every parasite to every hospital.
- **"Worms multiply like bacteria."** Most helminths cannot complete their cycle inside one person; *Strongyloides* is the exception and can overwhelm an immunosuppressed host.
