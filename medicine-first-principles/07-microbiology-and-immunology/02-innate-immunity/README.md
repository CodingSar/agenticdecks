# Innate immunity

> **Children:** [Barriers and pattern recognition](barriers-and-pattern-recognition.md) · [Complement, phagocytes and the inflammatory cascade](complement-phagocytes-and-the-inflammatory-cascade.md) · [Cytokines, interferons and NK cells](cytokines-interferons-and-nk-cells.md)

## What this area is about

How the body responds to a microbe in the first minutes and hours, before any cell specific for that microbe exists. The answer is a system that is inherited rather than learned: barriers that exclude, receptors that recognise molecular patterns shared by whole classes of microbe, a plasma protein cascade that coats and lyses, phagocytes that eat, and secreted signals that recruit help and warn neighbours. It is the only immunity most animals have, and in humans it still handles nearly every encounter alone.

The unifying idea is **speed through pattern recognition**. Because the receptors are encoded in the germline and present on every relevant cell from birth, recognition is immediate; because they bind conserved motifs (lipopolysaccharide, flagellin, viral Ribonucleic Acid (RNA)) rather than individual antigens, a few dozen receptors cover most threats. The cost is that the system cannot distinguish one bacterium from another, cannot improve with experience, and reads the body's own damage with the same receptors, so that injury without infection still inflames. Inflammation itself is not a disease but the delivery mechanism that brings these components from blood to tissue, and most of what pathology calls inflammation is this area seen under a microscope.

```
   barrier breached
        |
   PRR senses PAMP / DAMP  ----->  cytokines (TNF, IL-1, IL-6; interferons)
        |                                 |               |
   complement deposits  <---- endothelium activated    fever, acute phase,
        |                        |                      dendritic cell maturation
   opsonised microbe ----> neutrophils recruited ----> phagocytosis, killing
                                                            |
                                            resolution  or  chronic inflammation
```

Read pattern recognition first: it states what the system detects and why the detection is immediate. Complement and phagocytes is the effector arm, and the inflammatory cascade that delivers it; this is the leaf that the pathology of inflammation and the clinical signs of infection rest on. Cytokines, interferons and NK cells explains how the parts coordinate, how the type of threat is encoded into a type of response, and how the same signals produce fever, shock and the maturation of dendritic cells that starts adaptive immunity.

## Connections to sibling areas

- **[Microbes and infection](../01-microbes-and-infection/README.md):** the patterns recognised here are the conserved structures described there; endotoxin is the Gram-negative outer membrane; interferon exists because viruses replicate inside cells; the microbiome is tolerated by keeping it behind barriers.
- **[Adaptive immunity](../03-adaptive-immunity/README.md):** innate recognition licenses the adaptive response (dendritic cell maturation and co-stimulation), the first cytokines commit it to a type, and adaptive products (antibody) feed back to direct complement and phagocytes. Adjuvants are innate signals added to vaccines.
- **[Immunity in disease](../04-immunity-in-disease/README.md):** complement, neutrophil and interferon defects are immunodeficiencies with characteristic organisms; complement and phagocytes are the effectors of type II and III hypersensitivity; the interferon loop drives lupus; cytokine blockade is the main class of biologic drug.
